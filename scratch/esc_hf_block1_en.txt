## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Major Paradigm Shifts in the 2026 ESC Heart Failure Guidelines
The 2026 ESC Guidelines introduce profound structural and conceptual updates reflecting landmark clinical trials published since 2021:

1. **Redefined LVEF Phenotypes — Expansion of HFrEF and Abolition of HFmrEF:**
   - **Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF)** is now expanded to encompass all patients with **LVEF $\le 50\%$** (previously $< 40\%$). Post hoc analyses and dedicated trials demonstrate that patients with LVEF $41\text{--}49\%$ derive substantial survival and morbidity benefit from the same foundational neurohormonal therapies as those with LVEF $\le 40\%$.
   - **Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFmrEF)** has been **officially abolished** as a distinct entity, streamlining clinical decision-making.
   - **Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF)** is now defined as symptoms/signs of HF with **LVEF $> 50\%$**, accompanied by objective evidence of elevated filling pressures or structural alterations.

2. **Adoption of the Universal Stages of Heart Failure (Stages A to D):**
   - **Stage A (At Risk for HF):** Individuals with risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, ASCVD, obesity, cardiotoxic exposure) but no current or prior symptoms, physical signs, or structural/functional cardiac disease or biomarker elevation.
   - **Stage B (Pre-Heart Failure):** Asymptomatic individuals with objective evidence of structural heart disease (e.g., LV hypertrophy, chamber dilatation, wall motion abnormalities, valvular disease), increased filling pressures, or persistently elevated natriuretic peptides / cardiac troponins.
   - **Stage C (Heart Failure):** Patients with current or previous symptoms and signs caused by structural/functional cardiac abnormalities.
   - **Stage D (Advanced Heart Failure):** Patients with severe, refractory symptoms (NYHA class III–IV) despite optimal therapy, frequent hospitalizations, and persistent intolerance to neurohormonal blockers, requiring specialized interventions (LVAD, heart transplantation, palliative inotropes).

3. **Decompensated Heart Failure (DHF) Replaces "Acute Heart Failure":**
   - The term **Decompensated Heart Failure (DHF)** officially supersedes "acute heart failure", recognizing that almost all acute presentations represent worsening of an underlying, chronic progressive syndrome rather than isolated acute episodes.

4. **New Nomenclature for Heart Failure Therapies:**
   - **Foundational Medical Therapy (FMT):** The four core pharmacological pillars ("quad-therapy") proven to modify disease trajectory and improve survival: ARNI/ACEi, evidence-based beta-blocker, steroidal MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor.
   - **Additional Medical Therapy (AMT):** Tailored agents based on specific clinical profiles (vericiguat, ivabradine, GLP-1 receptor agonists, non-steroidal MRAs, digoxin, potassium binders).
   - **Guideline-Directed Device and Interventional Therapy (GDIT):** CRT, ICD, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER), catheter ablation, and mechanical circulatory support.

5. **Breakthrough Evidence in Obesity and Cardiometabolic HFpEF:**
   - **GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide)** are given high-priority recommendations for patients with HFpEF and obesity (BMI $\ge 30\text{ kg/m}^2$) based on STEP-HFpEF and SUMMIT trials, demonstrating marked symptom reduction, functional improvement, and reduced HF hospitalization.

6. **Temporary Mechanical Circulatory Support (tMCS) in Cardiogenic Shock:**
   - Following the **DanGer Shock** trial, routine use of microaxial flow pump (Impella) in selected patients with STEMI-related cardiogenic shock demonstrates reduced 180-day mortality, whereas routine IABP is Class III (no benefit).

7. **Revolution in Cardiac Amyloidosis (ATTR-CA):**
   - Expanded Class I recommendations for **TTR silencers (vutrisiran)** and **stabilizers (tafamidis, acoramidis)** to reduce cardiovascular hospitalization and all-cause mortality across NYHA classes I–III.

## 1. Preamble</summary><div class="details-content">

Clinical practice guidelines summarize and evaluate all available evidence on a particular issue at the time of the writing process, with the overarching aim of assisting healthcare professionals in selecting the best management strategies for an individual patient suffering from a given condition.

These 2026 Guidelines on the management of Heart Failure were developed by an interdisciplinary Task Force appointed by the European Society of Cardiology (ESC), with direct contribution from the Heart Failure Association (HFA) of the ESC, and endorsed by the European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (EACTS).

The recommendations are grounded on an extensive synthesis of randomized clinical trials, observational cohorts, systematic reviews, and international registry data published up to 2026. Great care has been taken to avoid biases, manage potential conflicts of interest, and formulate concise, practical guidance. The Guideline provides actionable clinical algorithms structured around the patient pathway, ensuring equitable, evidence-based, and multidisciplinary delivery of cardiovascular healthcare.

## 2. Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

Heart failure (HF) remains one of the greatest global healthcare challenges of the 21st century. Despite major advances in medical and device therapies, HF is associated with substantial mortality, frequent hospitalizations, and severe impairment of quality of life.

### 2.1 Rationale for the 2026 Revision
Since the release of the 2021 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of acute and chronic heart failure and the 2023 Focused Update, transformative evidence has reshaped clinical practice:
1. **Therapeutic parity between mildly reduced and reduced LVEF:** Re-evaluations of the major neurohormonal trials (PARADIGM-HF, DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER) demonstrated consistent benefit across the ejection fraction continuum up to $50\%$.
2. **Metabolic pharmacotherapies in HFpEF and obesity:** Landmark trials (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER, STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT) established SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists as disease-modifying therapies in HFpEF.
3. **Acute care transitions:** The terminology of "acute heart failure" has been replaced by Decompensated Heart Failure (DHF), emphasizing aggressive early decongestion, inpatient optimization of foundational therapies, and structured pre-discharge planning.
4. **Mechanical circulatory support trials:** The DanGer Shock trial demonstrated that microaxial flow pumps reduce 180-day mortality in STEMI-related cardiogenic shock, redefining the role of temporary MCS.
5. **Genetic and infiltrative cardiomyopathies:** Breakthrough amyloidosis therapies (tafamidis, vutrisiran, acoramidis) and expanding genetics require early systematic screening.

## 3. Definition, Epidemiology, and Classifications</summary><div class="details-content">

### 3.1 Definition of Heart Failure
Heart failure is a complex clinical syndrome comprising cardinal symptoms (breathlessness, fatigue, ankle swelling) and physical signs (elevated jugular venous pressure, pulmonary crackles, peripheral oedema) caused by structural and/or functional abnormalities of the heart, resulting in elevated intracardiac pressures and/or inadequate cardiac output at rest and/or during physical exertion.

To establish a definitive diagnosis of heart failure, clinical symptoms must be corroborated by:
1. **Objective evidence of cardiac dysfunction:** Assessed primarily via transthoracic echocardiography (abnormal ejection fraction, chamber enlargement, diastolic dysfunction, significant valve disease).
2. **Elevated circulating biomarkers:** Natriuretic peptides (B-type natriuretic peptide [BNP] $\ge 35\text{ pg/mL}$ or N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide [NT-proBNP] $\ge 125\text{ pg/mL}$ in outpatients).
3. **Objective evidence of congestion:** Pulmonary or systemic congestion documented by chest radiography, lung ultrasound (B-lines), or invasive hemodynamic assessment.

Asymptomatic individuals with cardiac dysfunction or elevated biomarkers do not have heart failure; they are classified as having **pre-heart failure (stage B)**.

### 3.2 Epidemiology of Heart Failure
- **Prevalence:** Approximately $1\%\text{--}3\%$ of the adult population in high-income nations, rising steeply to $> 10\%$ in individuals aged $\ge 70$ years.
- **Incidence:** Estimated at $1\text{--}5$ cases per 1,000 person-years. While age-adjusted incidence of HFrEF has stabilized or slightly declined due to improved post-MI care, the incidence of HFpEF continues to escalate rapidly due to population aging and the obesity/metabolic syndrome epidemic.
- **Prognosis:** Despite therapeutic strides, 1-year and 5-year mortality following an initial diagnosis remain approximately $15\%\text{--}20\%$ and $50\%$, respectively. Hospitalization for decompensation marks a critical inflection point, carrying a $10\%\text{--}15\%$ in-hospital mortality and a $25\%\text{--}30\%$ 30-day readmission rate.

### 3.3 Classifications of Heart Failure

#### 3.3.1 Classification Based on Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF)
The 2026 Guidelines introduce a modernized, binary ejection fraction framework:

| Phenotype | LVEF Range | Key Diagnostic Criteria | Core Therapeutic Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HFrEF** (Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction) | **$\le 50\%$** | Symptoms $\pm$ signs; LVEF $\le 50\%$ on echocardiography | Universal Foundational Medical Therapy (**FMT quad-therapy**: ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i), loop diuretics for congestion, device therapy (ICD, CRT). |
| **HFpEF** (Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction) | **$> 50\%$** | Symptoms $\pm$ signs; LVEF $> 50\%$; objective evidence of structural/functional abnormality (LVH, LAE, elevated $E/e'$) and/or elevated natriuretic peptides | **SGLT2 inhibitors** (Class I), GLP-1 RA for obesity, MRA/ARNI, loop diuretics, aggressive comorbidity management. |

> [!NOTE]
> **Abolition of HFmrEF:** The previously designated intermediate category of "heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction" (LVEF $41\text{--}49\%$) has been merged into HFrEF. Clinical trials demonstrated identical response to foundational neurohormonal blockade in this group, eliminating artificial therapeutic barriers.

#### 3.3.2 Classification Based on Heart Failure Stages
The ESC officially adopts the universal four-stage classification (A–D):
- **Stage A (At Risk):** Individuals at high risk for HF (hypertension, diabetes, ASCVD, obesity, cardiotoxin exposure) but without structural heart disease, elevated biomarkers, or symptoms.
- **Stage B (Pre-Heart Failure):** Asymptomatic individuals with structural heart disease (e.g., LVEF $\le 50\%$, LVH, regional wall motion abnormality, moderate/severe valvular disease), increased filling pressures, or elevated natriuretic peptides / troponins.
- **Stage C (Symptomatic Heart Failure):** Current or previous symptoms and signs caused by structural/functional cardiac abnormalities.
- **Stage D (Advanced Heart Failure):** Severe symptoms (NYHA III–IV) refractory to guideline-directed therapies, marked functional limitation, recurrent hospitalizations, and medication intolerance.

#### 3.3.3 New Nomenclature for Heart Failure Therapies
To foster structured prescribing and prevent clinical inertia, therapies are categorized into three hierarchical tiers:
1. **Foundational Medical Therapy (FMT):** Universal first-line "quad-therapy" proven to reduce mortality and hospitalizations in HFrEF:
   - Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitor: Angiotensin Receptor–Neprilysin Inhibitor (**ARNI**) preferred, or ACE inhibitor (**ACE-I**), or ARB if intolerant.
   - Beta-Blocker: Evidence-based agents (**bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate**, nebivolol).
   - Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist (**MRA**): Steroidal (spironolactone, eplerenone).
   - Sodium–Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitor (**SGLT2-I**): **Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin**.
2. **Additional Medical Therapy (AMT):** Tailored add-on pharmacotherapies:
   - **Loop Diuretics:** Titrated to achieve and maintain euvolemia.
   - **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide):** Indicated for HFpEF with obesity.
   - **Vericiguat:** Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator for worsening HFrEF despite FMT.
   - **Ivabradine:** For sinus rhythm with resting HR $\ge 70\text{ b.p.m.}$ despite maximally tolerated beta-blockade.
   - **Cardiac Glycosides (digoxin, digitoxin):** In persistent symptoms or AF with rapid ventricular rate.
   - **Potassium Binders (patiromer, sodium zirconium cyclosilicate):** Enabling continuation of RAASi and MRA in hyperkalaemia.
3. **Guideline-Directed Device and Interventional Therapy (GDIT):**
   - **ICD:** Primary and secondary prevention of sudden cardiac death.
   - **CRT:** Resynchronization for wide QRS / LBBB and high-degree AV block.
   - **Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (TEER):** For persistent severe secondary mitral regurgitation.
   - **Catheter Ablation:** For atrial fibrillation in heart failure.
   - **Mechanical Circulatory Support & Transplantation:** For end-stage advanced HF.
