## 5. Prevention and Monitoring of Cardiovascular Complications During Cancer Therapy</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardioprotection strategies during cancer therapy comprise primary prevention (interventions initiated in patients without pre-existing CVD or prior CTR-CVT) and secondary prevention (interventions in patients with active or pre-existing CVD, including prior CTRCD). Routine surveillance protocols aim to identify subclinical cardiovascular toxicity at an early, reversible stage before clinical heart failure or irreversible vascular occlusion ensues ([Fig. 9](#fig-09)).

<span id="fig-09"></span>![](fig09_cardioprotection_strategies.jpg)

**FIGURE 9.** Primary and secondary cancer therapy-related cardiovascular toxicity prevention and monitoring pathways across the cancer care continuum.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5.1 General Principles of Cardioprotection</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiovascular toxicity prevention requires an aggressive, multi-targeted approach:
* **Optimization of Cardiovascular Risk Factors:** Aggressive control of arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, and smoking cessation according to ESC general prevention guidelines.
* **Cardioprotective Pharmacotherapy:** Neurohormonal antagonists (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors [ACEI] or angiotensin receptor blockers [ARB], and beta-blockers) and statins have demonstrated cardioprotective efficacy in reducing the incidence of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) decline during anthracycline and HER2-targeted therapies.
* **Oncologic Drug Selection and Formulation Modification:** Utilizing liposomal anthracycline formulations or dexrazoxane in patients at high and very high risk of cardiotoxicity.
* **Radiotherapy Optimization:** Employing advanced radiation planning techniques (intensity-modulated radiotherapy [IMRT], deep inspiration breath-hold [DIBH], proton beam therapy) to minimize mean heart dose (MHD).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5.2 Primary Prevention Strategies</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 5.2.1 Primary Prevention During Anthracycline Chemotherapy
Primary prevention is specifically recommended for patients categorized as high and very high cardiovascular toxicity risk prior to anthracycline therapy:
* **Dexrazoxane:** An intracellular iron-chelating agent and topoisomerase II beta inhibitor that significantly reduces free radical generation and anthracycline-induced double-strand DNA breaks. Dexrazoxane is recommended in high- and very high-risk patients, or when high cumulative doses of doxorubicin ($\ge 250\ 	ext{mg/m}^2$) or epirubicin ($\ge 600\ 	ext{mg/m}^2$) are planned.
* **Liposomal Anthracyclines:** Non-pegylated or pegylated liposomal doxorubicin alters tissue biodistribution, dramatically reducing myocardial drug accumulation while preserving antineoplastic efficacy. Recommended in patients at high or very high cardiotoxicity risk.
* **Neurohormonal Therapy and Statins:** Prophylactic administration of ACEI/ARB (enalapril, perindopril, candesartan) and beta-blockers (carvedilol, nebivolol) should be considered in high- and very high-risk patients. Statins (atorvastatin) reduce reactive oxygen species and preserve LVEF during anthracycline treatment.

#### 5.2.2 Primary Prevention of Radiation-Induced Cardiovascular Toxicity
Radiation-induced heart disease (RIHD) encompasses accelerated coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, pericardial disease, conduction abnormalities, and myocardial fibrosis:
* **Dose Constraints:** Minimizing the mean heart dose (MHD) is the primary determinant of long-term risk. Every $1\ 	ext{Gy}$ increase in MHD increases the relative rate of major adverse coronary events by $7.4\%$ linearly without a safe lower threshold.
* **Modern Radiotherapy Techniques:** Deep inspiration breath-hold (DIBH) displaces the heart caudally and dorsally away from anterior chest wall radiation fields in left-sided breast cancer, reducing cardiac exposure by up to $50	ext{--}70\%$. IMRT and proton therapy enable highly conformal dose distributions avoiding cardiac substructures (left anterior descending coronary artery [LAD], cardiac valves).

### 5.3 Secondary Prevention Strategies</summary><div class="details-content">

Secondary prevention applies to cancer patients with established pre-existing CVD or prior CTR-CVT:
* **Continuation of Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT):** In patients with pre-existing heart failure, CAD, or hypertension, baseline cardioprotective medications (ACEI/ARNi, beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists [MRA], SGLT2 inhibitors, statins, antiplatelet/anticoagulant drugs) should be continued and uptitrated to target doses unless clinically contraindicated.
* **Multidisciplinary Cardio-Oncology Review:** Any dose adjustment, temporary pause, or drug change must be discussed within the cardio-oncology team to ensure uninterrupted cancer therapy while mitigating cardiovascular decompensation.

### 5.4 Cardiovascular Surveillance During Cancer Therapies</summary><div class="details-content">

Serial cardiovascular surveillance combines clinical evaluations, serial 12-lead ECGs, serum biomarker tracking (cardiac troponins and natriuretic peptides), and transthoracic echocardiography (TTE with 3D-LVEF and GLS).

#### 5.4.1 Cardiac Serum Biomarkers
* **Serial Troponin Testing:** Troponin release reflects acute or ongoing cardiomyocyte injury. Measurements should be obtained at baseline, prior to cycles 2, 4, and 6 of anthracycline chemotherapy, and periodically during targeted therapies in high-risk patients. A new troponin elevation above the laboratory 99th percentile ULN indicates subclinical cardiac injury, triggering cardiology review and consideration of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy.
* **Natriuretic Peptides (NP):** BNP and NT-proBNP reflect ventricular wall stress and volume overload. Serial measurements identify hemodynamic compromise during proteasome inhibitor and targeted therapy regimens.

#### 5.4.2 Cardiac Imaging Modalities
* **TTE Monitoring Protocol:** Echocardiography is the primary imaging modality. Transthoracic echocardiography should be scheduled at baseline, at mid-treatment, at completion of anthracycline therapy, and every 3 months during HER2-targeted therapy.
* **GLS Monitoring:** A relative percentage reduction in GLS $> 15\%$ from baseline indicates subclinical CTRCD even if LVEF remains $\ge 50\%$.

</div></details>

Autologous and allogeneic HSCT patients face severe multi-system cardiovascular hazards: high-dose conditioning chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, melphalan, busulfan), total body irradiation (TBI), fluid overload, graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), and accelerated atherosclerosis ([Fig. 24](#fig-24)).
