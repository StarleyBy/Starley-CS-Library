### 6.9 Pulmonary Hypertension and 6.10 Pericardial Diseases</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 6.9 Pulmonary Hypertension (PH)
All five clinical groups of pulmonary hypertension can occur in patients with cancer:
* **Group 1 (Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension [PAH]):** Associated with targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (especially **dasatinib**, bosutinib, ponatinib), proteasome inhibitors (carfilzomib), interferon-alpha, and alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide, mitomycin C, which frequently induce pulmonary veno-occlusive disease [PVOD]). In the DASISION trial, $5\%$ of patients on dasatinib developed PH compared with $0.4\%$ on imatinib.
* **Group 2 (PH Due to Left Heart Disease):** Secondary to cancer therapy-induced heart failure with reduced or preserved ejection fraction (anthracyclines, HER2-targeted agents, radiation).
* **Group 3 (PH Due to Lung Diseases and/or Hypoxia):** Secondary to antineoplastic drug-induced interstitial lung disease (bleomycin, checkpoint inhibitors) or thoracic radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis.
* **Group 4 (Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension [CTEPH]):** Occurs as a chronic sequel of cancer-associated venous thromboembolism or from recurrent thrombotic occlusions around long-term indwelling central venous access catheters. Malignant obstruction of pulmonary arteries can also arise from angiosarcoma, renal cell carcinoma, or testicular germ cell metastases.
* **Group 5 (PH with Unclear or Multifactorial Mechanisms):** Includes tumoral pulmonary microembolism, pulmonary tumour thrombotic microangiopathy (PTTM), and myeloproliferative neoplasms (myelofibrosis with extramedullary hematopoiesis).

##### Diagnostic and Hemodynamic Stratification
Transthoracic echocardiography is the frontline screening tool:
* **Low PH Probability:** Peak tricuspid regurgitation velocity ($TRV$) $\le 2.8\ 	ext{m/s}$ (estimated systolic PAP $\le 35\ 	ext{mmHg}$) without secondary signs of right ventricular pressure overload.
* **Intermediate PH Probability:** Peak $TRV\ 2.9	ext{--}3.4\ 	ext{m/s}$. In patients receiving dasatinib, dose reduction and monthly echocardiographic surveillance are indicated.
* **High PH Probability:** Peak $TRV > 3.4\ 	ext{m/s}$ (estimated systolic PAP $> 50\ 	ext{mmHg}$) or significant right ventricular dysfunction. Immediate discontinuation of dasatinib and prompt right-heart catheterization (RHC) are mandatory to confirm precapillary hemodynamics (mean PAP $> 20\ 	ext{mmHg}$, PAWP $\le 15\ 	ext{mmHg}$, and pulmonary vascular resistance $> 2\ 	ext{Wood Units}$).

#### 6.10 Pericardial Diseases
Pericarditis and pericardial effusion can stem from thoracic radiation, cytotoxic agents (anthracyclines, bleomycin, high-dose cyclophosphamide, cytarabine), molecular targeted inhibitors (dasatinib, ATRA, arsenic trioxide), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).

##### 6.10.1 Acute Pericarditis
* **Presentation and Diagnosis:** Often atypical in cancer patients; diagnosed by classic criteria (pericarditic chest pain, friction rub, widespread ST-segment elevation or PR depression, and new pericardial effusion).
* **Treatment:** First-line therapy consists of NSAIDs (e.g. ibuprofen $600\ 	ext{mg}$ t.i.d.) plus colchicine ($0.5\ 	ext{mg}$ b.i.d. or $0.5\ 	ext{mg}$ daily if $< 70\ 	ext{kg}$) for 3 months to prevent recurrences. Low-to-moderate doses of systemic corticosteroids are reserved for NSAID-refractory cases or severe thrombocytopenia.
* **ICI-Associated Pericarditis:** Carries high mortality and frequently coexists with myocarditis. Requires immediate interruption of ICI therapy, hospitalization, telemetry, and high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($1	ext{--}2\ 	ext{mg/kg/day}$) combined with colchicine.

##### 6.10.2 Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade
* **Etiology:** Malignant effusions (direct invasion by lung, breast, esophageal cancers; or metastasis from melanoma and lymphomas) represent $> 30\%$ of tamponade cases and carry a grave prognosis.
* **Monitoring:** Small-to-moderate asymptomatic effusions ($< 20\ 	ext{mm}$) can be monitored with serial echocardiograms every 1–2 weeks initially, then every 4–6 weeks.
* **Intervention:** In hemodynamically compromising effusions or overt tamponade, urgent percutaneous echocardiography-guided pericardiocentesis with prolonged catheter drainage is the gold standard. Intrapericardial instillation of cytostatic or sclerosing agents (bleomycin, cisplatin, thiotepa) or creation of a surgical/balloon pericardial window is recommended for recurrent malignant effusions.

## 7. End-of-Cancer Therapy Cardiovascular Risk Assessment</summary><div class="details-content">

The end of active cancer therapy represents a critical transition point. Cardiovascular risk must be systematically re-evaluated to detect early asymptomatic ventricular injury, optimize long-term cardioprotective regimens, coordinate rehabilitation, and establish a tailored survivorship surveillance trajectory.

### 7.1 Risk Stratification at the End of Cancer Therapy
All cancer patients treated with potentially cardiotoxic therapies should undergo structured clinical evaluation, blood pressure and lipid profiling, ECG, and review of cardiotoxic exposures within the first 12 months after completing cancer treatment ([Table 10](#table-10)).

### 7.2 Surveillance Protocols and Weaning of Cardioprotective Medications
* **Asymptomatic High-Risk Patients:** Transthoracic echocardiography and cardiac serum biomarkers (cTn, NP) are recommended at **3 and 12 months** following treatment completion.
* **Asymptomatic Moderate-Risk Patients:** Echocardiography and biomarkers should be considered within **12 months** after completion.
* **Asymptomatic Low-Risk Patients:** Re-evaluation within 12 months may be considered.
* **Management of Heart Failure Medications ([Fig. 37](#fig-37)):**
  * **Long-Term Continuation:** In patients with moderate or severe symptomatic CTRCD, or asymptomatic $LVEF < 40\%$, continuation of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT: ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) is recommended indefinitely due to high rates of recurrent HF upon withdrawal.
  * **Trial of Weaning:** In selected patients with mild or moderate asymptomatic CTRCD (e.g. secondary to trastuzumab without anthracyclines) who have achieved full functional normalization ($LVEF \ge 50\%$, normal GLS, normal biomarkers), a gradual trial of weaning off cardioprotective therapy under multidisciplinary supervision may be considered, accompanied by serial echocardiographic and biomarker reassessment at 3, 6, and 12 months post-weaning.

### 7.3 Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing (CPET) and Cardiac Rehabilitation
Cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF), quantified as peak oxygen uptake ($	ext{VO}_{2	ext{peak}}$), is severely impaired across cancer survivors. A $1\ 	ext{MET}$ ($3.5\ 	ext{mL O}_2/	ext{kg/min}$) increment in CRF confers a $14\%$ reduction in cardiovascular mortality.
* **CPET:** Indicated in survivors experiencing persistent exertional intolerance, fatigue, or dyspnea to differentiate cardiac, pulmonary, musculoskeletal, and deconditioning components.
* **Cardiac Rehabilitation:** Supervised individualized aerobic and resistance training, including high-intensity interval training (HIIT), is safe, improves CRF, restores endothelial function, reduces metabolic risk factors, and decreases long-term cardiovascular mortality.

Mediastinal radiation induces ostial and proximal epicardial coronary stenoses, predominantly involving the left main and LAD coronary arteries. 
* **Surveillance:** In asymptomatic adult cancer survivors who received thoracic radiation exposing the heart, non-invasive stress testing (stress echocardiography, CMR perfusion, SPECT/PET) or coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) should be considered every 5–10 years.
* **Revascularization:** Revascularization decisions require MDT consensus. Pre-operative assessment must evaluate internal mammary artery patency, extensive chest wall adhesions, and the presence of a "porcelain" calcified ascending aorta, which can make cross-clamping impossible and favor percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) over coronary artery bypass graft (CABG).

## 9. Special Populations</summary><div class="details-content">

### 9.1 Cardiac Tumours
Cardiac masses encompass non-neoplastic pseudotumours (thrombi, vegetations, annular calcification), primary benign neoplasms ($75\%$), primary malignant neoplasms ($10	ext{--}25\%$), and secondary metastatic tumours ($20	ext{--}30$ times more common than primary neoplasms) ([Fig. 39](#fig-39)).

**FIGURE 39.** Anatomical predilection and frequency of primary and secondary cardiac tumours across cardiac chambers.

#### Diagnostic Algorithm
Comprehensive non-invasive multimodality imaging is the diagnostic cornerstone ([Fig. 40](#fig-40)):
1. **Transthoracic and Transesophageal Echocardiography:** Rapidly identifies mobility, stalk attachment, hemodynamic obstruction, and vascularity with myocardial contrast agents.
2. **Cardiac Magnetic Resonance (CMR):** Provides definitive tissue characterization based on $T_1$, $T_2$, fat suppression, perfusion, and early/late gadolinium enhancement ($LGE$) to distinguish avascular thrombi from vascularized neoplasms.
3. **Computed Tomography (CT) and PET:** Delineates calcification, extracardiac staging, and FDG-avidity for metabolic differentiation between benign and aggressive malignant neoplasms.

| | **Secondary Metastatic** | 20–30 times more frequent than primary tumours (lung, breast, melanoma, hematologic). | Systemic anticancer therapy; palliative surgical resection or debulking is considered only for severe intractable hemodynamic obstruction or tamponade. |

### 9.2 Pregnant Patients with Cancer
Cancer diagnosed during pregnancy poses profound clinical challenges, requiring careful balance between maternal survival and fetal teratogenicity ([Fig. 41](#fig-41)).

<span id="fig-41"></span>![](fig41_pregnant_cancer_monitoring_protocol.jpg)

**FIGURE 41.** Cardiovascular monitoring and therapeutic protocol for pregnant women receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy.

#### Principles of Therapy in Pregnancy
* **Anthracycline Chemotherapy:** Can be administered during the second and third trimesters ($> 14$ weeks' gestation) with close fetal and maternal monitoring (TTE every 2 cycles). Anthracyclines are contraindicated during the first trimester.
* **Trastuzumab and Targeted HER2 Agents:** Strictly contraindicated throughout pregnancy due to risk of severe oligohydramnios and fetal renal agenesis.
* **Heart Failure GDMT:** ACEI, ARB, ARNi, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitors are strictly contraindicated during pregnancy due to fetotoxicity. Beta-blockers (metoprolol succinate, carvedilol) and hydralazine/nitrates are the agents of choice.
* **Anticoagulation:** DOACs and warfarin (in the first trimester) are contraindicated. Therapeutic low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) with anti-Xa monitoring is the standard of care for acute VTE in pregnancy.

### 9.3 Carcinoid Valvular Heart Disease
Carcinoid heart disease develops in up to $50\%$ of patients with carcinoid syndrome, typically when neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) metastasize to the liver, releasing high circulating levels of serotonin ($5	ext{-HT}$), bradykinin, and tachykinins into the systemic circulation ([Fig. 42](#fig-42)).

<span id="fig-42"></span>![](fig42_carcinoid_heart_disease_workup.jpg)

**FIGURE 42.** Diagnostic and clinical assessment algorithm for carcinoid valvular heart disease: biomarker triggers, echocardiographic features, and surgical intervention criteria.

#### Clinical and Diagnostic Characteristics
* **Pathophysiology:** Serotonin stimulates 5-$	ext{HT}_{2	ext{B}}$ receptors on subendocardial interstitial cells, inducing plaque-like fibrous deposits that fuse and retract the tricuspid and pulmonary valves, causing severe tricuspid regurgitation and pulmonary stenosis/regurgitation. Left-sided valve involvement occurs in $< 10\%$ (patent foramen ovale or bronchial NETs).
* **Biomarkers:** Elevated **urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA)** excretion ($> 300\ \mu	ext{mol}/24\ 	ext{h}$) and elevated NT-proBNP ($> 260\ 	ext{pg/mL}$).
* **Echocardiography:** Thickened, shortened, rigid tricuspid leaflets with failure of coaptation (free-floating regurgitation) and restricted pulmonary valve leaflets.
* **Surgical Management:** Valve replacement (bioprosthetic valves preferred) is indicated in patients with severe right-sided carcinoid valve disease and refractory right heart failure, prior to or concurrently with liver metastases debulking or PRRT. Perioperative octreotide infusion is mandatory to prevent fatal carcinoid crisis.

### 9.4 Amyloid Light-Chain (AL) Cardiac Amyloidosis
AL cardiac amyloidosis arises from plasma cell dyscrasias that produce misfolded monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains that deposit as amyloid fibrils in the myocardial interstitium ([Fig. 43](#fig-43)).

<span id="fig-43"></span>![](fig43_al_cardiac_amyloidosis_diagnosis.jpg)

**FIGURE 43.** Non-invasive and invasive diagnostic pathway for amyloid light-chain (AL) cardiac amyloidosis: monoclonal screening, multimodality imaging, and biomarker staging.

#### Diagnostic Pathway and Staging
1. **Monoclonal Protein Screening:** Mandatory trifecta: serum free light chains (sFLC, kappa/lambda ratio), serum immunofixation electrophoresis (IFE), and urine IFE.
2. **Echocardiography and CMR:** Concentric biventricular thickening, granular myocardial sparkling, biatrial enlargement, restrictive filling, apical sparing strain pattern, and diffuse transmural late gadolinium enhancement with elevated extracellular volume ($ECV > 40\%$).
3. **Biopsy:** Abdominal fat pad or bone marrow biopsy with Congo red staining; endomyocardial biopsy if non-invasive testing or extracardiac biopsy is non-diagnostic.
4. **Mayo Biomarker Staging:** Based on troponin T/I, NT-proBNP, and difference between involved and uninvolved free light chains ($	ext{dFLC}$). Stage IV patients carry severe median survival ($4	ext{--}6$ months) without urgent therapy.
5. **Therapy:** Frontline quadruplet therapy with **daratumumab, bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone (Dara-CyBorD)** rapidly clears circulating amyloidogenic light chains.

### 9.5 Cardiac Implantable Electronic Devices (CIED) During Radiotherapy
Patients with permanent pacemakers (PPM), implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICD), or cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) undergoing radiation therapy are at risk of device malfunction, software reset, circuit corruption, or lead damage ([Fig. 44](#fig-44)).

<span id="fig-44"></span>![](fig44_cied_radiotherapy_risk_stratification.jpg)

**FIGURE 44.** Risk stratification for patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIED) undergoing radiotherapy: beam energy, dose exposure, and pacing dependence.

#### Mechanisms and Risk Factors
* **Direct Ionizing Radiation:** Cumulative dose absorption damages complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) components, causing battery depletion, charge loss, and irreversible hardware reset.
* **Neutron Beam Scatter:** High-energy photon beams ($> 6\ 	ext{MV}$ or $> 10\ 	ext{MV}$) produce photoneutron scatter that induces single-event upsets (SEU), RAM corruption, and software reset.
* **Electromagnetic Interference (EMI):** May trigger inappropriate inhibition of pacing in pacemaker-dependent patients or inappropriate defibrillation shocks in ICD carriers.

#### Risk Categories and Management Protocols ([Fig. 45](#fig-45), [Fig. 46](#fig-46))
* **Low Risk:** Cumulative device dose $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$, photon energy $\le 6\ 	ext{MV}$, non-pacemaker-dependent. Interrogation pre- and post-RT regimen.
* **Intermediate Risk:** Cumulative dose $2	ext{--}10\ 	ext{Gy}$ or pacing-dependent (dose $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$). Continuous visual/audio monitoring and weekly interrogation.
* **High Risk:** Cumulative dose $> 10\ 	ext{Gy}$ (pacemaker), cumulative dose $> 1\ 	ext{Gy}$ (ICD), pacing-dependent with dose $2	ext{--}10\ 	ext{Gy}$, or use of neutron-producing beams ($> 6\ 	ext{MV}$). Continuous ECG/telemetry during fractions, weekly interrogation, and surgical relocation of the CIED prior to RT if the generator lies directly within the primary radiation beam.

## 10. Patient Information, Communication, and Self-Management</summary><div class="details-content">

Effective patient-centered cardio-oncology care requires clear communication, shared decision-making, and structured self-management education throughout the cancer continuum ([Fig. 47](#fig-47)).

<span id="fig-47"></span>![](fig47_patient_information_communication.jpg)

**FIGURE 47.** Structured domains for patient information, communication, and self-management in cardio-oncology care.

### Core Components of Patient Self-Management
1. **Pre-Treatment Education:** Informing patients regarding potential cardiovascular toxicities, the importance of maintaining cancer treatment adherence, and early symptom recognition.
2. **Red Flag Symptom Awareness:** Immediate reporting of chest tightness, exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, palpitations, syncope, and rapid peripheral edema.
3. **Home Monitoring:** Daily self-monitoring of blood pressure and heart rate for patients receiving VEGFi or TKIs; regular body weight recording for early heart failure detection.
4. **Lifestyle Modification:** Smoking cessation, Mediterranean diet, alcohol moderation, and structured physical exercise tailored to patient capacity.

## 12. Key Messages</summary><div class="details-content">

1. **Cardiovascular Risk Assessment:** Baseline risk stratification using HFA-ICOS tools is recommended before starting potentially cardiotoxic anticancer treatment.
2. **Primary and Secondary Prevention:** Aggressive management of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors and continuation/optimization of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy reduces CTRCD.
3. **Cardioprotective Therapy:** Statins, ACEI/ARBs, and beta-blockers prevent or mitigate CTRCD in high- and very high-risk patients receiving anthracyclines or targeted therapies.
4. **Multidisciplinary Decision-Making:** Cardiotoxicity management requires close collaboration between cardiologists, oncologists, and hematologists to avoid unnecessary cancer treatment interruptions.
5. **Surveillance During Therapy:** Serial echocardiography (3D LVEF, GLS) and cardiac serum biomarkers (cTn, NP) detect subclinical myocardial damage early.
6. **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Myocarditis:** Rare but highly lethal; requires immediate permanent ICI discontinuation and immediate high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($500	ext{--}1000\ 	ext{mg/day}$).
7. **Anticoagulation in Active Cancer:** DOACs are preferred for both atrial fibrillation and VTE in cancer patients without gastrointestinal/genitourinary bleeding risks or drug-drug interactions.
8. **End-of-Therapy Assessment:** Evaluation within 12 months post-treatment identifies survivors at high future risk who require lifelong cardiology follow-up.
9. **Long-Term Survivorship:** High-risk survivors of childhood, adolescent, and adult cancers require lifelong surveillance every 2 to 5 years for late cardiomyopathy, premature CAD, and valvular heart disease.
10. **Patient-Centered Education:** Empowering patients through shared decision-making, red flag symptom awareness, and exercise rehabilitation significantly improves long-term outcomes.

## 13. Gaps in Evidence</summary><div class="details-content">

Despite major advances, critical knowledge gaps remain across cardio-oncology:
* **Precision Risk Models:** Lack of prospectively validated multi-omic and artificial intelligence risk prediction algorithms integrating genomic susceptibility and clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP).
* **Cardioprotection Efficacy:** Optimal duration of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy in asymptomatic CTRCD and long-term benefit of primary prevention in moderate-risk cohorts.
* **ICI Myocarditis Therapies:** Comparative efficacy and sequencing of second-line immunosuppressive agents (abatacept, infliximab, alemtuzumab, tofacitinib) in steroid-refractory cases.
* **CAR-T Cell Toxicity Mechanisms:** Incomplete understanding of early cytokine-mediated myocardial injury vs late immune-mediated vascular remodelling.
* **Survivorship Outcomes:** Scarcity of prospective randomized trials evaluating the optimal frequency of imaging surveillance and preventive pharmacotherapy in cancer survivors 10–30 years post-cure.

## 14. "What to Do" and "What Not to Do" Messages from the Guidelines</summary><div class="details-content">

The following compilation summarizes the strong Class I (recommended/indicated) and Class III (not recommended/contraindicated) directives across the 2022 ESC Guidelines on cardio-oncology:

### What to Do (Class I Recommendations)
* **Baseline Assessment:** Perform baseline clinical risk assessment, ECG, and cardiotoxicity risk stratification using HFA-ICOS tools in all patients scheduled for potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapy.
* **Imaging Modality:** Use 3D echocardiography as the preferred first-line modality for LVEF assessment, and global longitudinal strain (GLS) for subclinical CTRCD detection.
* **High-Dose Anthracycline Protection:** Use dexrazoxane and/or liposomal anthracyclines in patients with high baseline risk or high cumulative doses.
* **Symptomatic CTRCD:** Initiate guideline-directed heart failure medical therapy (ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) in all patients who develop symptomatic CTRCD.
* **ICI Myocarditis:** Immediately hospitalize, initiate continuous telemetry, discontinue ICI permanently, and administer immediate high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($500	ext{--}1000\ 	ext{mg/day}$ for 3 days).
* **Anticoagulation in Cancer:** Use DOACs as first-line therapy for non-valvular AF and acute VTE in cancer patients without gastrointestinal/mucosal lesions, severe thrombocytopenia ($< 50	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$), or drug interactions.
* **CIED Protection During RT:** Limit cumulative absorbed dose to CIED generators $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$, avoid photon energies $> 6\ 	ext{MV}$, and monitor pacing-dependent patients continuously.
* **Long-Term Survivorship:** Re-stratify cardiovascular risk 5 years after treatment completion and conduct regular echocardiographic surveillance in high-risk adult and childhood cancer survivors.

### What Not to Do (Class III Directives)
* **Unnecessary Interruption:** Do not permanently discontinue life-saving cancer therapy for mild asymptomatic CTRCD ($LVEF \ge 50\%$, $\Delta GLS > 15\%$, or biomarker rise).
* **Avoid Obsolete Thresholds:** Do not withhold ACEI/ARB or beta-blocker therapy in asymptomatic patients who develop significant $LVEF$ drops below $50\%$.
* **Avoid DOACs in High Bleeding Risk:** Do not administer DOACs in patients with active gastrointestinal bleeding, severe thrombocytopenia ($< 25	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$), or unoperated luminal GI/GU tumours.
* **Avoid Low-Risk Over-Surveillance:** Routine periodic echocardiography surveillance is not recommended in asymptomatic low-risk cancer survivors.
* **Contraindicated Medications in Pregnancy:** Do not prescribe ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNi, MRAs, or DOACs during pregnancy.
* **HER2 Targeted Agents in Pregnancy:** Do not administer trastuzumab or other HER2-targeted agents during pregnancy due to high risk of fetal oligohydramnios and death.

</div></details>

## 15. Quality Indicators for Cardio-Oncology</summary><div class="details-content">

To facilitate clinical governance, continuous audit, and benchmark quality of care across healthcare institutions, the European Society of Cardiology established five key quality indicator domains:

### Domain 1: Structural and Multidisciplinary Organization
* **QI 1.1:** Availability of a dedicated, specialized cardio-oncology multidisciplinary team and structured weekly MDT consultation pathways.
* **QI 1.2:** Written institutional clinical protocols for cardiotoxicity risk assessment, monitoring, and expedited cardiology referral within 48–72 hours for acute CTR-CVT.

### Domain 2: Baseline Cardiovascular Risk Assessment
* **QI 2.1:** Proportion of patients receiving potentially cardiotoxic therapies who undergo documented baseline HFA-ICOS cardiovascular risk assessment before treatment initiation.
* **QI 2.2:** Proportion of patients undergoing baseline ECG, transthoracic echocardiography, and cardiac serum biomarker measurement according to protocol.

### Domain 3: Monitoring During Cancer Treatment
* **QI 3.1:** Adherence rate to guideline-recommended surveillance imaging and biomarker intervals during active cancer therapy.
* **QI 3.2:** Proportion of patients who undergo echocardiographic GLS evaluation in addition to LVEF.

### Domain 4: Management of Acute and Subacute Toxicities
* **QI 4.1:** Proportion of patients diagnosed with symptomatic CTRCD who are prescribed guideline-directed heart failure medical therapy (ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i).
* **QI 4.2:** Time to initiation of high-dose corticosteroids in patients with suspected or confirmed ICI-associated myocarditis (target: $< 24\ 	ext{hours}$ from clinical presentation).
* **QI 4.3:** Proportion of cancer patients with acute VTE or AF who receive appropriate guideline-concordant anticoagulation.

### Domain 5: End-of-Therapy and Long-Term Survivorship Care
* **QI 5.1:** Proportion of high- and moderate-risk cancer patients who receive a documented end-of-cancer therapy cardiovascular risk evaluation within 12 months of completing therapy.
* **QI 5.2:** Implementation of structured long-term survivorship surveillance plans for high-risk childhood and adult cancer survivors.
