## 4. Prevention of Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Prevention of heart failure requires identifying individuals before overt symptoms emerge, categorizing them into Stage A (at risk) or Stage B (pre-heart failure), and implementing evidence-based lifestyle and pharmacological interventions.

### 4.1 Patients at Risk for Heart Failure (Stage A)
Stage A includes individuals with cardiometabolic risk factors, cardiotoxic exposures, or systemic diseases known to precipitate cardiac dysfunction, but without current or prior HF symptoms, physical signs, structural/functional heart disease, or biomarker elevation.

#### 4.1.1 Cardiometabolic Risk Factors
- **Hypertension:** The single most prevalent attributable risk factor. Long-term observational studies and randomized trials confirm that aggressive blood pressure reduction substantially reduces incident HF. The 2026 Guidelines recommend strict blood pressure control with a **systolic target $< 130\text{ mmHg}$** for patients with hypertension or Stage B HF (**Class I, Level A**).
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) reduce HF hospitalizations and cardiovascular mortality across high-risk diabetes cohorts (**Class I, Level A**). In diabetic kidney disease, the non-steroidal MRA **finerenone** significantly reduces incident HF events (**Class I, Level A**). **GLP-1 receptor agonists** should be considered in T2DM with additional CV risk factors to reduce HF or CV death (**Class IIa, Level A**).
- **Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD):** Statin therapy is recommended for patients with or at high risk of ASCVD to prevent incident HF (**Class IIa, Level A**).
- **Lifestyle & Behavioural Modification:** Structured counselling on smoking cessation, weight management, Mediterranean-style diet, avoidance of heavy alcohol consumption, and physical activity is recommended for all Stage A/B patients (**Class I, Level C**).

### Recommendation Table 1 — Recommendations for Prevention of Heart Failure (Stage A)

| Recommendations | Class | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Strict blood pressure control (systolic blood pressure target $< 130\text{ mmHg}$) is recommended in patients with hypertension or stage B HF to reduce the risk of HF. | **I** | **A** |
| An SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended in patients with T2DM at risk of HF (multiple ASCVD risk factors or established ASCVD) to reduce the risk of HF and CV death. | **I** | **A** |
| An SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended in patients with T2DM and CKD to reduce the risk of HF or CV death. | **I** | **A** |
| Finerenone is recommended in patients with T2DM and CKD (eGFR $25\text{--}75\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$, UACR $\ge 30\text{ mg/g}$) to reduce the risk of HF. | **I** | **A** |
| Counselling on healthy lifestyle choices including maintaining a healthy weight, consuming a well-balanced diet, avoiding sedentary behaviour, abstaining from smoking and heavy alcohol consumption, as well as counselling against the use of illicit cardiotoxic drugs (cocaine, amphetamines, anabolic steroids) is recommended for all patients with stage A or B HF to reduce the risk of HF progression. | **I** | **C** |
| Treatment with a statin should be considered in people at high risk of, or with established ASCVD, to reduce the risk of HF. | **IIa** | **A** |
| A GLP-1 receptor agonist should be considered in patients with T2DM and at least one other additional CV risk factor to reduce the risk of HF or CV death. | **IIa** | **A** |

### 4.2 Patients with Stage B Heart Failure (Pre-Heart Failure)
Stage B encompasses asymptomatic patients with structural heart disease (asymptomatic LV systolic dysfunction with LVEF $\le 50\%$, LV hypertrophy, chamber dilatation, regional wall motion abnormality, or significant valvular disease), elevated filling pressures, or persistently elevated natriuretic peptides or cardiac troponins.

| Eplerenone is recommended in patients with LVEF $\le 40\%$ after myocardial infarction with signs of HF or diabetes to reduce the risk of HF hospitalization and death. | **I** | **B1** |

## 5. Diagnosis of Chronic Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

### 5.1 Clinical Presentation and Diagnostic Algorithm
The diagnosis of chronic heart failure requires a comprehensive clinical evaluation integrating history, physical examination, resting 12-lead ECG, circulating natriuretic peptide measurement, and transthoracic echocardiography.

#### Table 7: Symptoms and Signs of Heart Failure
| Category | Typical / More Specific | Less Typical / Less Specific |
|---|---|---|
| **Symptoms** | Breathlessness (dyspnoea on exertion), orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, reduced exercise tolerance, fatigue, tiredness, increased time to recover after exercise, ankle swelling | Nocturnal cough, wheezing, weight gain ($> 2\text{ kg/week}$), weight loss (in advanced HF/cachexia), bloating, early satiety, loss of appetite, confusion/dizziness, syncope, bendopnoea |
| **Signs** | Elevated jugular venous pressure (JVP), hepatojugular reflux, third heart sound ($S_3$ gallop), laterally displaced apical impulse | Peripheral oedema (ankle, sacral, scrotal), pulmonary crackles (rales), bilateral pleural effusions, tachycardia, irregular pulse, tachypnoea, hepatomegaly, ascites, cold extremities, oliguria, narrow pulse pressure |

#### Table 8: New York Heart Association (NYHA) Functional Classification
| Class | Degree of Limitation | Clinical Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Class I** | None | Ordinary physical activity does not cause undue breathlessness, fatigue, or palpitations. |
| **Class II** | Slight | Comfortable at rest. Ordinary physical activity results in fatigue, palpitations, or breathlessness. |
| **Class III** | Marked | Comfortable at rest. Less than ordinary physical activity causes fatigue, palpitations, or breathlessness. |
| **Class IV** | Severe | Inability to carry on any physical activity without discomfort. Symptoms of heart failure at rest. |

#### 5.1.1 Natriuretic Peptides in Diagnosis
Circulating natriuretic peptides have high negative predictive value ($> 95\%$) for excluding HF. Recommended diagnostic thresholds in non-acute outpatient settings:
- **NT-proBNP:** $< 125\text{ pg/mL}$ renders HF unlikely.
- **BNP:** $< 35\text{ pg/mL}$ renders HF unlikely.

#### Table 9: Causes for Increase or Decrease in Natriuretic Peptide Levels
| Mechanism | Clinical Causes |
|---|---|
| **Cardiac Causes of Elevation** | Heart failure, acute coronary syndromes, valvular heart disease, cardiomyopathies, atrial fibrillation and flutter, myocarditis, cardiac surgery, cardioversion, cardiac amyloidosis |
| **Non-Cardiac Causes of Elevation** | Advanced age, chronic kidney disease (reduced renal clearance), ischaemic stroke, subarachnoid haemorrhage, pulmonary embolism, pulmonary hypertension, sepsis, severe burns, critical illness, severe anaemia, cirrhosis |
| **Causes of Decreased Levels (False Low)** | **Obesity (BMI $\ge 30\text{ kg/m}^2$):** Circulating BNP and NT-proBNP levels are reduced by $30\%\text{--}50\%$ due to increased clearance receptor expression and blunted adipocyte synthesis; **pericardial constriction, acute "flash" pulmonary oedema** (very early before release). |

![Figure 4: Diagnostic algorithm for chronic heart failure](images/fig04_diagnostic_algorithm_chronic_hf.jpg)

**Figure 4: Diagnostic algorithm for chronic heart failure presenting in the outpatient or community setting.** Stepwise evaluation: (1) Clinical history, symptoms, physical examination, resting ECG; (2) Natriuretic peptide testing (NT-proBNP $\ge 125\text{ pg/mL}$ or BNP $\ge 35\text{ pg/mL}$); (3) Transthoracic echocardiography to establish LVEF phenotype (HFrEF $\le 50\%$ vs HFpEF $> 50\%$); (4) Aetiological workup and therapeutic initiation.

#### 5.1.2 Echocardiographic Criteria for HFpEF
#### Table 10: Simplified Echocardiographic Criteria Supporting Objective Evidence of HFpEF (LVEF $> 50\%$)
| Echocardiographic Parameter | Abnormal Threshold Supporting HFpEF |
|---|---|
| **Left Ventricular Mass Index (LVMI)** | $\ge 95\text{ g/m}^2$ (women), $\ge 115\text{ g/m}^2$ (men) |
| **Relative Wall Thickness (RWT)** | $> 0.42$ (concentric remodeling / hypertrophy) |
| **Left Atrial Volume Index (LAVI)** | $> 34\text{ mL/m}^2$ (sinus rhythm), $> 40\text{ mL/m}^2$ (atrial fibrillation) |
| **Mitral Diastolic Filling Ratio ($E/e'$)** | Average $E/e' > 9$ at rest |
| **Mitral Annular Early Diastolic Velocity ($e'$)** | Septal $e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$, Lateral $e' < 10\text{ cm/s}$ |
| **Tricuspid Regurgitation Peak Velocity (TRV)** | $> 2.8\text{ m/s}$ at rest (estimated PASP $> 35\text{ mmHg}$) |

### 5.2 Aetiological Workup of Heart Failure
Identifying the primary underlying aetiology is critical, as disease-specific targeted therapies (e.g., revascularization, amyloid stabilizers, tafamidis, immunosuppression, enzyme replacement) significantly alter outcomes.

## 6. Management of Chronic Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

### 6.1 Pharmacological Management
The treatment of chronic heart failure is structured around disease-modifying **Foundational Medical Therapy (FMT)** for HFrEF, phenotype-specific evidence in HFpEF, and tailored **Additional Medical Therapy (AMT)**.

#### 6.1.1 Foundational Medical Therapy (FMT Quad-Therapy) in HFrEF (LVEF $\le 50\%$)
All four foundational pillars should be initiated rapidly—ideally during hospitalization or within the first 4–6 weeks of outpatient care—and uptitrated every 1–2 weeks to target or maximally tolerated doses:
1. **ARNI / ACE-I:** Sacubitril/valsartan is preferred over ACE inhibitors due to superior reduction in CV death and HF hospitalizations. For patients naïve to ARNI or intolerant, ACE inhibitors (or ARBs if cough/angioedema occurs) are recommended.
2. **Evidence-Based Beta-Blocker:** Bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or nebivolol reduce all-cause mortality, CV mortality, and sudden cardiac death in stable patients.
3. **Steroidal MRA:** Spironolactone or eplerenone reduces mortality and hospitalizations across the entire HFrEF spectrum.
4. **SGLT2 Inhibitor:** Dapagliflozin ($10\text{ mg}$ o.d.) or empagliflozin ($10\text{ mg}$ o.d.) rapidly improves symptoms, decreases HF hospitalizations, and reduces CV mortality, with minimal blood pressure or renal clearance constraints.
