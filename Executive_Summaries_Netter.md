## Chapter 1: OVERVIEW OF THORACIC ANATOMY</summary>

### Anterior Thoracic Wall
* **Key surface anatomy:** Pectoralis major/minor, rectus abdominis, external oblique, serratus anterior, latissimus dorsi; neurovascular supply includes perforating branches of internal thoracic artery and anterior cutaneous branches of intercostal nerves.
* **Clinical relevance:** Landmark for thoracotomy incisions and chest tube placement.

### Thoracic Cage
* **Bony framework:** Sternum, ribs, costal cartilages, thoracic vertebrae; contains mediastinum and pleural cavities.
* **Key relations:** Diaphragm, pericardium, parietal pleura attachments; important for understanding surgical access to heart and lungs.

### Great Vessels of Superior Mediastinum
* **Major structures:** Aortic arch, brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid and subclavian arteries, superior vena cava, pulmonary trunk and arteries, thoracic duct, vagus and phrenic nerves.
* **Surgical significance:** Critical for cannulation, cross-clamping, and dissection during cardiac and aortic procedures.

### Lymph Vessels and Nodes of Lung
* **Right lung drainage:** Pulmonary → bronchopulmonary (hilar) → inferior tracheobronchial (carinal) → right superior tracheobronchial → right paratracheal nodes → bronchomediastinal trunk or scalene node → brachiocephalic vein.
* **Left lung drainage:** Superior lobe follows left-sided route via left superior tracheobronchial and paratracheal nodes plus aortic arch node; inferior lobe crosses to right superior tracheobronchial nodes before entering systemic veins.
* **Clinical implication:** Predicts pathways of lymphatic metastasis in lung carcinoma.

### Lymph Vessels and Nodes of Esophagus
* **Drainage pattern:** Paratracheal, superior/inferior tracheobronchial, posterior mediastinal/intercostal, juxtaesophageal, celiac and superior phrenic nodes; ultimately to thoracic duct.
* **Surgical relevance:** Determines extent of lymphadenectomy in esophagectomy.

### Veins of Esophagus
* **Venous plexuses:** Submucosal venous plexus, esophageal tributaries to azygos, hemiazygos and accessory hemiazygos systems; porto-systemic anastomoses via left gastric vein.
* **Clinical importance:** Site of varices in portal hypertension.

### Thorax: Four Chambers of Heart
* **Transverse section anatomy:** Atria, ventricles, valves, coronary sinus, pericardial reflections, thoracic duct, azygos vein at T7-T8 level.
* **Surgical landmarks:** Sternum, intercostal spaces, triangle of auscultation, relations to esophagus and descending aorta.

## Chapter 3: PULMONARY ANATOMY AND VARIANTS</summary>

### Inferior Accessory Fissure
* **Anatomy:** Thin, well-defined linear structure that segregates the medial basal segment of either lower lobe from the remaining basal segments.
* **Incidence and significance:** Common incidental finding; generally of no clinical significance.

### Azygos Fissure and Azygos Lobe
* **Anatomy:** Thin, curvilinear structure through the medial right upper lobe formed by four layers of pleura (2 visceral, 2 parietal); the azygos vein courses through the fissure; the subtended lung is the azygos lobe.
* **Embryology and incidence:** Forms when the azygos vein extends into the right upper lobe during fetal development; found in 0.3% to 0.5% of the population.
* **Significance:** Usually an incidental anomaly without clinical significance.

### Tracheal Bronchus
* **Anatomy:** Anomalous bronchus originating directly from the right lateral tracheal wall; supplies all or a portion of the apical segment of the right upper lobe.
* **Incidence and significance:** Found in 0.1% of the population; usually incidental.

### Coronal Projection
* **Imaging correlation:** Coronal view demonstrates the right upper lobe tracheal bronchus to advantage; confirms relationship to right upper lobe bronchi and carina.

## Chapter 4: THORACIC LYMPH NODES</summary>

### Role of Thoracic CT in Lymph Node Evaluation
* **Clinical importance:** Major tool for detection of hilar, mediastinal, and intrapulmonary lymph node enlargement in suspected pulmonary malignancy and other thoracic diseases.
* **Staging significance:** Accurate identification and classification of lymph node stations is critical for lung cancer staging (AJCC/UICC system).

### AJCC/UICC Lymph Node Classification
* **Nomenclature:** Stations numbered 1–14; “R” and “L” denote right and left of midline.
* **Key stations illustrated:** Upper paratracheal (station 2), lower paratracheal (station 4R/4L), subaortic (station 5), subcarinal (station 7), hilar (station 10R), interlobar (station 11R/11L), lobar (station 12R), segmental (station 13R), pulmonary ligament (station 9L).

### Anatomic Landmarks and Accessibility
* **Ligamentum arteriosum:** Critical landmark; paratracheal nodes (medial) accessible via cervical mediastinoscopy; prevascular, subaortic, and aortopulmonary window nodes (lateral) are not.
* **Hilar vs mediastinal nodes:** Stations 10–14 lie within the lung (distal to mediastinal pleural reflection); stations 1–9 lie within the mediastinum.
* **Prognostic implication:** Mediastinal (stations 1–9) involvement in lung carcinoma confers worse prognosis than purely intrapulmonary nodes.

### Sarcoidosis Example
* **Imaging features:** Calcified enlarged nodes (high-attenuation foci) in paratracheal, hilar, interlobar, subcarinal, and pulmonary ligament stations.
* **Clinical note:** Common incidental finding; demonstrates typical distribution and calcification pattern.

## Chapter 5: 5 CISTERNA CHYLI AND THORACIC DUCT</summary>

### Cisterna Chyli
* **Location:** Typically between the distal thoracic/upper abdominal aorta and azygos vein in the right posterior mediastinum or retrocrural space, posterior to the right diaphragmatic crus.
* **Tributaries:** Receives lymph from right and left lumbar lymphatic trunks, intestinal lymphatic trunk, and inferior intercostal lymph nodes.
* **Clinical note:** Inconsistently visualized on thoracic CT; well seen in this patient.

### Thoracic Duct
* **Origin and course:** Arises from cisterna chyli; ascends in posterior mediastinum, usually on the right side of midline adjacent to azygos vein and posterior to esophagus.
* **Midline crossing:** Typically crosses to the left of midline at approximately the level of the 5th thoracic vertebral body (may occur more inferiorly).
* **Termination:** Empties into the junction of the left subclavian and left internal jugular veins; drains lymph from the entire body except the right head/neck, right thorax, and right arm.
* **Imaging appearance:** Not always visible on CT but clearly demonstrated here; courses adjacent to azygos vein and descending thoracic aorta.

### Coronal Anatomy Correlation
* **Key relations:** Seen to the left of midline superiorly, crossing midline inferiorly; close proximity to esophagus, azygos vein, and descending thoracic aorta throughout its course.
* **Surgical/clinical relevance:** Critical landmark for thoracic duct injuries, chylothorax, and lymphatic drainage pathways.

## Chapter 6: VENOUS ANATOMY AND VARIANTS</summary>

### Left Superior Intercostal Vein
* **Normal anatomy:** Courses along the lateral aspect of the aortic arch and empties into the left brachiocephalic vein; typically drains the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th left intercostal veins and connects the left brachiocephalic and accessory hemiazygos veins.
* **Clinical role:** Provides collateral pathway in upper extremity or thoracic venous stenosis/obstruction.

### Persistent Left Superior Vena Cava
* **Anatomy:** Most common thoracic venous anomaly (prevalence ~0.3%); arises from confluence of left internal jugular and subclavian veins, descends along left aspect of aortic arch and mediastinum, passes anterior to left hilum, crosses posterior wall of left atrium, and empties into coronary sinus.
* **Rare variant:** Direct drainage into left atrium (right-to-left shunt).
* **Association:** May coexist with other congenital cardiovascular anomalies.

### Collateral Venous Pathways in Obstruction
* **Pathologic process:** Right subclavian venous stenosis leads to rerouting of blood via chest wall and thoracic veins (intercostal, azygos system).
* **Azygos vein:** Courses in posterior mediastinum on the right, receives intercostal, mediastinal, bronchial, esophageal, and paravertebral plexuses; arches superior to right lung root to enter superior vena cava.
* **Imaging opportunity:** Contrast injection in obstructed upper extremity demonstrates intercostal and azygos systems clearly.

### Imaging Correlation
* **Axial, coronal, and sagittal views:** Demonstrate normal and variant venous anatomy with volume-rendered and CT correlation.

## Chapter 7: OVERVIEW OF CARDIAC ANATOMY</summary>

### Heart: Anterior Exposure
* **Position and pericardium:** Heart lies in mediastinum; pericardium opened at reflections from great vessels; transverse pericardial sinus visible between aorta/pulmonary trunk and atria.
* **Great vessels:** Ascending aorta, pulmonary trunk, superior vena cava, brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid and subclavian arteries.
* **Chambers and sulci:** Right atrium (auricle), right ventricle, left ventricle (apex), coronary sulcus with right coronary artery.
* **Neurovascular and adjacent structures:** Phrenic nerves, vagus nerves, recurrent laryngeal nerves, internal thoracic vessels; hila of right and left lungs, mediastinal pleura.

### Coronary Arteries and Cardiac Veins
* **Right coronary artery (RCA):** Atrial branch, right (acute) marginal branch, anterior cardiac veins, small cardiac vein, interventricular septal branches.
* **Left coronary artery (LCA):** Anterior interventricular (left anterior descending) branch with diagonal branches, circumflex branch with left marginal and posterior left ventricular branches.
* **Venous drainage:** Great cardiac vein, middle cardiac vein, small cardiac vein, posterior vein of left ventricle, oblique vein of left atrium (of Marshall).
* **Key landmarks:** Sinoatrial (SA) nodal branch, coronary sinus; shown on sternocostal (anterior) and diaphragmatic (inferior) surfaces.

### Right Atrium and Right Ventricle
* **Right atrium:** Superior and inferior vena cava, coronary sinus opening, fossa ovalis with limbus, crista terminalis, pectinate muscles, right auricle.
* **Right ventricle:** Tricuspid valve (anterior, septal, posterior cusps), chordae tendineae, anterior and posterior papillary muscles, trabeculae carneae, septomarginal trabecula (moderator band), conus arteriosus.
* **Pulmonary valve:** Three semilunar cusps; membranous and muscular interventricular septum.
* **Views:** Opened right atrium (right lateral); opened right ventricle (anterior).

### Left Atrium and Left Ventricle
* **Left atrium:** Left auricle, four pulmonary vein openings, valve of foramen ovale, oblique vein of left atrium.
* **Left ventricle:** Mitral valve (anterior and posterior cusps), anterior and posterior papillary muscles, chordae tendineae; aortic valve (left, right, posterior/noncoronary cusps).
* **Interventricular septum:** Membranous and muscular parts, atrioventricular part.
* **Views:** Flap opened in posterolateral wall of left ventricle; section through left atrium and ventricle (mitral valve cut away).

### Atria, Ventricles, and Interventricular Septum
* **Four-chamber anatomy:** All four chambers, mitral and tricuspid valves, aortic and pulmonary valves, papillary muscles.
* **Interventricular septum:** Muscular part, membranous part, atrioventricular part.
* **Outflow tracts:** Ascending aorta, pulmonary trunk; right and left ventricular outflow.
* **Special features:** Right anterior, posterior and septal papillary muscles, left anterior and posterior papillary muscles, septomarginal trabecula (moderator band); plane of section shown.

## Chapter 8: CARDIAC ANATOMY USING CT</summary>

### Technical Considerations in Cardiac CT
* **Imaging volume:** Restricted to heart only (typically begins just below carina); full thorax not included.
* **Contrast protocol:** Intravenous contrast bolus followed by saline chaser (“wash-out”) → bright enhancement of left-sided structures (aorta, pulmonary veins) while right heart and pulmonary arteries are poorly enhanced → optimal visualization of right coronary artery.
* **Acquisition timing:** At lower heart rates performed in diastole (minimal cardiac motion) → nearly motion-free images; mitral valve open confirms diastolic phase.
* **Reformatted planes:** Standard body axes replaced by cardiac-axis planes (2-chamber/vertical long axis, 3-chamber, 4-chamber/horizontal long axis, short axis) for precise assessment of ventricular size, wall motion, valves, ejection fraction, and left atrial appendage.

### Normal Anatomy – Axial Series
* **Pericardial recesses and sinuses:** Pericardium proper (postcaval, right/left pulmonary vein recesses) + transverse sinus (superior/inferior aortic recesses, right/left pulmonic recesses) + oblique sinus (posterior pericardial recess); small physiologic fluid commonly visible.
* **Pulmonary arteries:** Right interlobar → right middle lobe (medial/lateral segments) and right lower lobe; variants include direct origin of medial and lateral segments from interlobar artery.
* **Atrial structures:** Crista terminalis (vertical fibromuscular ridge separating smooth and pectinate portions of right atrium); prominent crista may mimic right atrial mass; septum spurium (most prominent anterior pectinate muscle arising from crista terminalis).
* **Coronary arteries:** First septal perforator (from proximal LAD) supplies interventricular septum and conduction system; sinoatrial nodal artery (usually from RCA, occasionally LCx); atrioventricular nodal artery (from RCA in 90 %); right-dominant circulation in ~85 % (PDA and AV nodal artery from RCA).
* **Ventricular features:** Left ventricular outflow tract smooth and adjacent to anterior mitral leaflet; trabeculae carneae cover most of LV (spared only in LVOT); right ventricle inflow (tricuspid) and outflow (infundibulum/pulmonic) widely separated.

### Clinical Relevance
* **Risk areas:** Proximal LAD + first septal perforator occlusion → large anteroseptal MI + conduction disturbances → sudden cardiac death.
* **Diagnostic pitfalls:** Crista terminalis or septum spurium can be mistaken for mass; normal pericardial fluid must not be confused with effusion.
* **Reformatted views utility:** 2-chamber → LV anterior/inferior walls, mitral valve, LAA; 3-chamber → LVOT, mitral/aortic valves, anteroseptal/inferolateral walls; 4-chamber → mitral/tricuspid function, septal/lateral wall motion; short axis → ventricular volumes/EF and wall motion.

## Chapter 9: CARDIAC ANATOMY USING MR</summary>

### Technical Considerations in Cardiac MR
* **Imaging planes:** Cardiac-axis oriented (2-chamber/vertical long axis, 3-chamber, 4-chamber/horizontal long axis, short axis) rather than standard body axes; used for precise ventricular size, wall motion, valve function, and ejection fraction.
* **Blood signal techniques:** “Black blood” sequences suppress flowing blood signal (vessels/chambers appear black); “white blood” sequences produce bright blood signal without intravenous contrast and are preferred for functional analysis.
* **Pericardium:** Normal pericardial sac (fibrous outer + serous inner with visceral epicardium and parietal layer); up to 25 mL physiologic fluid; normal thickness ≤ 2 mm on MR/CT; fluid often visible around ascending aorta.
* **Advantages over CT:** Excellent demonstration of right ventricular anatomy (trabeculae carneae, papillary muscles, moderator band, tricuspid valve) without contrast or saline chaser; no need for iodinated contrast or radiation.

### Normal Anatomy – Axial Series
* **Vascular variants:** Aberrant right subclavian artery (common congenital anomaly) arises from distal aortic arch/proximal descending aorta and courses posterior to esophagus.
* **Pericardial structures:** Pericardial fluid, recesses, and sinuses; normal small amounts visible.
* **Chamber and vessel details:** Superior vena cava, pulmonary arteries/veins, ascending/descending aorta, right/left atrial appendages, coronary arteries (left main, anterior descending, circumflex), great cardiac vein.
* **Right ventricle specifics:** Trabeculae carneae, papillary muscles, moderator band clearly seen on MR (often obscured on contrast-enhanced CT).

### Clinical Relevance
* **Diagnostic pitfalls:** Aberrant right subclavian artery must not be mistaken for mediastinal mass; normal pericardial fluid distinguished from effusion.
* **Reformatted views utility:** 2-chamber for LV cavity, anterior/inferior walls, mitral valve, left atrial appendage; 3-chamber for LVOT, mitral/aortic valves, anteroseptal/inferolateral walls; 4-chamber for mitral/tricuspid function and septal/lateral motion; short axis for ventricular volumes/EF and wall motion.
