#### Key Algorithm Principles

1. **Preoperative Risk Stratification & Desensitization:**
   - Preformed anti-HLA antibodies identified by solid-phase single-antigen bead assays (Luminex SPA) to calculate panel reactive antibodies (%cPRA).
   - Mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) &gt; 1000 is considered positive, whereas composite or single MFI &gt; 4000 predicts a positive flow crossmatch and heightened rejection risk.
   - Virtual crossmatching (VXM) allows immediate electronic matching of donor HLA typing against recipient antibody profiles.
   - Candidates with cPRA &gt; 50% undergo desensitization (plasmapheresis + IVIG ± rituximab or bortezomib).

2. **Induction & Triple Maintenance Immunosuppression:**
   - **Induction:** Non-depleting IL-2 receptor antagonist (basiliximab 20 mg on Day 0 and Day 4) for standard-risk patients; cytolytic rabbit antithymocyte globulin (rATG/thymoglobulin 1.5 mg/kg/day for 3–5 days) for highly sensitized, retransplant, or high-risk patients.
   - **Triple Maintenance Regimen:**
     - Calcineurin inhibitor (CNI): **tacrolimus** is preferred (trough 8–12 ng/mL for first 6 months, then 5–10 ng/mL); demonstrates superior rejection freedom, improved lipid profile, and absence of hirsutism/gingival hyperplasia compared to cyclosporine.
     - Antiproliferative agent: **mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)** 1000–1500 mg BID, inhibiting inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPHD); randomized trials demonstrated a 45% reduction in 1-year mortality and attenuated CAV compared to azathioprine.
     - Corticosteroids: intraoperative high-dose methylprednisolone followed by oral prednisone taper with planned withdrawal at 6–12 months in stable, low-risk recipients.

3. **Hyperacute Rejection (HAR):**
   - Occurs within minutes to hours of aortic reperfusion due to preformed donor-specific antibodies (anti-ABO or anti-HLA class I).
   - Characterized by complement fixation, widespread microvascular thrombosis, myocardial necrosis, and an ischemic, cyanotic graft.
   - Management in OR: immediate mechanical circulatory support (VA-ECMO/BiVAD), mega-dose methylprednisolone (1000 mg IV), plasmapheresis, IVIG, eculizumab (anti-C5), rituximab, systemic heparinization; emergent retransplantation avoiding offending epitopes.

4. **Acute Cellular Rejection (ACR):**
   - T-cell mediated (CD4+ Th1 and cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells). ISHLT 2004 grading: 0R (none), 1R (mild), 2R (moderate with &ge; 2 foci of myocyte damage), 3R (severe with diffuse multifocal damage).
   - Asymptomatic 1R requires no pulse treatment; optimize maintenance CNI trough levels.
   - Asymptomatic 2R is treated with an oral prednisone taper or IV pulse steroids.
   - 3R or symptomatic ACR (hemodynamic compromise, LVEF &lt; 45%): 1st-line therapy is IV methylprednisolone 1000 mg/day for 3 days (~ 85% resolution).
   - Shock or steroid-resistance (&gt; 12–24 hours): add cytolytic rATG (thymoglobulin 1.5 mg/kg/day for 7–14 days) under antimicrobial prophylaxis.
   - Recurrent/resistant ACR: extracorporeal photopheresis (ECP), total lymphoid irradiation (TLI), conversion from CsA to tacrolimus, rule out concomitant AMR.

5. **Antibody-Mediated Rejection (AMR):**
   - Driven by donor-specific antibodies (DSAs) binding donor capillary endothelium, complement C4d deposition, and macrophage (CD68+) infiltration; presents with microthrombosis, edema, and biventricular systolic graft dysfunction.
   - Multimodal 4-pronged therapy:
     1. DSA clearance: plasmapheresis (5–6 sessions every other day).
     2. Neutralization: high-dose IVIG (1–2 g/kg total).
     3. DSA suppression: **rituximab (anti-CD20, 375 mg/m²)** depleting precursor B-cells; 26S proteasome inhibitor **bortezomib (1.3 mg/m²)** inducing mature plasma cell apoptosis.
     4. Graft protection &amp; support: IV methylprednisolone 1000 mg/day for 3 days, eculizumab (anti-C5) for refractory shock, heparin, inotropes / VA-ECMO, maintenance conversion to TAC + MMF.

6. **Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy (CAV / Chronic Rejection):**
   - Concentric fibrointimal hyperplasia of coronary arteries resulting from repetitive immune (ACR, AMR, DSA) and non-immune (CMV, dyslipidemia, HTN) insults.
   - Prevention: routine statins (pravastatin/atorvastatin) started at 1–2 weeks post-op in all recipients (Class I; improves long-term survival and retards CAV).
   - Progression or nephropathy: substitute CNI or MMF with an mTOR inhibitor (**sirolimus** or **everolimus**) for direct vascular antiproliferative effects.
   - Annual surveillance angiography/IVUS; PCI with DES for discrete stenoses; retransplantation for terminal CAV with heart failure.

#### Key Algorithm Principles

1. **Circle of Willis Anatomy & Collateral Pathways:**
   - **Anterior Circulation:** Internal carotid artery (ICA) divides into middle cerebral artery (MCA) and anterior cerebral artery (ACA). The anterior communicating artery (ACoA) links both ACAs, forming the anterior collateral pathway.
   - **Posterior Circulation:** Paired vertebral arteries merge into the basilar artery, branching into posterior cerebral arteries (PCAs). Bilateral posterior communicating arteries (PCoAs) connect the ICAs with the PCAs.
   - **Functional Reserve:** Cross-hemispheric blood flow during unilateral selective antegrade cerebral perfusion (uSACP) via the right axillary or innominate artery is entirely dependent upon patency and caliber of the ACoA and PCoA.

2. **Cerebral Hemodynamics & NIRS Thresholds:**
   - Normal cerebral blood flow (CBF) is approximately 50 mL/100g/min with intact autoregulation between MAP 50–150 mmHg.
   - Cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen (CMRO2) drops by approximately 50% for every 10°C decrease in brain temperature. Moderate hypothermia (25–28°C) significantly lowers metabolic demands.
   - Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) provides continuous real-time regional cerebral venous oxygen saturation (rSO2). Normal baseline is 60–80%.
   - **Critical Thresholds:** A relative decrease of > 20% from baseline or an absolute rSO2 < 50% indicates significant cerebral hypoperfusion and focal cerebral ischemia that demands immediate correction.

3. **Anatomical Variations & uSACP Vulnerability:**
   - A complete, symmetric classic Circle of Willis is present in only **20–30%** of the general population. Up to 50% have hypoplastic or aplastic segments.
   - **Absent or hypoplastic ACoA:** Directly blocks cross-hemispheric blood delivery from the perfused right carotid territory to the contralateral left anterior and middle cerebral circulations.
   - **A1 segment hypoplasia/aplasia (10%):** Forces anterior circulation to depend entirely on contralateral cross-flow.
   - **Absent/hypoplastic PCoA:** Prevents posterior-anterior collateral compensation.
   - In patients with an incomplete Circle of Willis, right-sided uSACP leaves the left cerebral hemisphere functionally isolated and ischemic.

4. **Clinical Scenario & Pathophysiology (The 48% vs 68% Dilemma):**
   - In a 65-year-old female undergoing aortic arch replacement with right axillary uSACP (flow 850 mL/min, right radial MAP 80 mmHg), a drop in left rSO2 from 76% to 48% with preserved right rSO2 (74% to 68%) indicates **focal left hemispheric ischemia due to Circle of Willis discontinuity**.
   - Because right radial MAP is already 80 mmHg (at the upper limit of the target autoregulatory perfusion pressure, 60–80 mmHg), right-sided perfusion is optimal.

5. **Stepwise Management & The Definitive Gold Standard:**
   - **Step 1:** Rapidly confirm NIRS signal integrity (optode contact, cable connection) within 30 seconds.
   - **Step 2 (Exam Trap Avoidance):** **Do NOT increase pump flow rate (e.g. to 1000 mL/min)**. With right MAP at 80 mmHg, increasing flow will escalate right carotid perfusion pressure above 100 mmHg, causing blood-brain barrier disruption, hyperemia, petechial hemorrhages, and severe right hemispheric cerebral edema, while delivering zero additional flow through the absent communicating artery to the left hemisphere.
   - **Step 3 (Definitive Treatment):** **Immediately convert uSACP to Bilateral Selective Antegrade Cerebral Perfusion (bSACP)** by placing an additional cannula into the **Left Common Carotid Artery (LCCA)** (12–15 Fr cannula or Pruitt-Inahara balloon catheter). Deliver dedicated flow of 250–350 mL/min at a target perfusion pressure of 50–70 mmHg. This immediately bypasses the anatomical defect and normalizes left rSO2 above 65%.
   - **Step 4:** Systemic optimization: maintain PaCO2 35–45 mmHg (prevent hypocapnic vasoconstriction), keep hematocrit > 25%, and maintain moderate hypothermia (25–28°C).
   - **Step 5 (Fallback / Bailout):** If the left carotid cannot be safely cannulated (severe atheroma, dissection, or anatomical friability), deepen hypothermia to deep hypothermic circulatory arrest (DHCA, < 20°C) or initiate retrograde cerebral perfusion (RCP) via the superior vena cava.
