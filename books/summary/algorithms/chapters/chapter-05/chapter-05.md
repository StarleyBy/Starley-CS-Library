# 05. Critical Care & Perioperative Hemodynamics

## Section 1. Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS) & Acute RV Failure

### Acute Right Ventricular Failure & LVAD Suction Events: Hemodynamic Pathophysiology and Management

Implantation of a continuous-flow left ventricular assist device (LVAD, e.g., HeartMate 3) fundamentally alters biventricular hemodynamics. Unloading the left ventricle (LV) dramatically elevates systemic cardiac output and systemic venous return to the right atrium and right ventricle (RV).

In patients with pre-existing pulmonary hypertension or subclinical RV dysfunction (hallmarks of end-stage non-ischemic or ischemic cardiomyopathy), the vulnerable RV fails to overcome the high pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR). The clinical hallmark is acute right ventricular failure (RVF) characterized by severe venous congestion and diminished transpulmonary transit to the left heart.

#### Pathophysiology of Suction Events (LV Cavity Collapse)

1. **Transpulmonary Bottleneck:** The failing RV cannot pump sufficient blood volume across high-resistance pulmonary capillary beds into the left atrium and LV.
2. **Critical LV Underfilling:** The LV cavity becomes acutely depleted ("empty ventricle").
3. **Negative Cavity Pressure:** As the continuous-flow rotary pump maintains its speed, severe subatmospheric vacuum develops inside the collapsed LV.
4. **Septal Suck-Down ("Suction Event"):** The compliant interventricular septum (IVS) or free wall is drawn mechanically into the LVAD inflow cannula. This triggers instantaneous flow drop alarms, severe ventricular arrhythmias, and profound systemic hypotension.

#### Differential Management Matrix

| Intervention | Status | Mechanism & Clinical Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO)** | **First-line (Class I)** | Selectively vasodilates pulmonary vasculature exclusively in ventilated lung segments. Unloads the RV, restores transpulmonary flow into the LV, and abolishes the vacuum without causing systemic vasodilation or hypotension. |
| **Inhaled Epoprostenol / PGI₂** | **Alternative** | Potent inhaled selective pulmonary vasodilator alternative when iNO is unavailable. |
| **Increasing LVAD Speed** | **Strictly Contraindicated** | Disastrously amplifies suction force inside the empty LV, draws the septum further leftward, mechanically distorts RV geometry, and triggers irreversible circulatory collapse. |
| **Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide)** | **Contraindicated in Shock** | Depletes intravascular volume, deepens LV underfilling, and produces intractable suction events. Elevated CVP reflects RV forward failure, not hypervolemia. |
| **Escalating Epinephrine** | **High Risk / Limited** | Increases myocardial oxygen consumption, induces refractory tachyarrhythmias, and high-dose alpha stimulation causes pulmonary vasoconstriction. |

#### Key Examination and Clinical Pearls

1. During acute post-LVAD right ventricular failure with suction events, elevated CVP, and systemic hypotension, the single most lethal reflex action is raising the pump speed (Option C). Always unload the RV first using selective pulmonary vasodilators (iNO, Option D).
2. Unlike intravenous vasodilators such as milrinone or nitroprusside, inhaled nitric oxide is inactivated immediately upon binding intravascular hemoglobin, ensuring targeted pulmonary vasodilation without triggering systemic vasodilation or worsening arterial hypotension.

## Section 2. Intra-Aortic Balloon Counterpulsation (IABP)

### Algorithm for Arterial Waveform Analysis & Timing Error Management

Intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation (IABP) remains the most widely deployed mechanical circulatory support modality for cardiogenic shock, acute myocardial ischemia, and post-cardiotomy low cardiac output syndrome.

Its physiological mechanism rests upon diastolic counterpulsation:
1. **Diastolic Augmentation (Inflation):** The balloon inflates with helium in the descending thoracic aorta immediately following aortic valve closure, displacing blood retrogradely into the coronary ostia and supra-aortic branches to markedly elevate coronary and cerebral perfusion.
2. **Systolic Unloading (Deflation):** Rapid active vacuum deflation just prior to left ventricular isovolumetric contraction creates a localized low-pressure sink (Venturi effect), dropping presystolic aortic end-diastolic pressure (afterload) and myocardial workload.

#### Waveform Analysis in 1:2 Assist Mode

Optimal calibration and verification of counterpulsation timing requires temporary placement into **1:2 assist mode (every other beat assisted)**:
* **Unassisted Beat:** Serves as the patient's intrinsic physiological benchmark. It defines native peak systolic pressure (PSP) and the true dicrotic notch, marking physiological aortic valve closure.
* **Assisted Beat:** Balloon inflation must initiate precisely at the dicrotic notch level, creating a crisp V-shaped take-off toward the peak diastolic augmentation (PDP). Balloon aortic end-diastolic pressure (BAEDP) must be 10-15 mmHg below native end-diastolic pressure (UAEDP), and assisted peak systolic pressure (APSP) must be lower than native PSP.

#### Differential Diagnosis of Four Classic Timing Errors

| Timing Error | Arterial Pressure Waveform Defect | Pathophysiological Consequence | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early Inflation** | Inflation notch appears **before** the dicrotic notch (during late systole) at an abnormally high pressure level. Produces a bifid/biphasic ('M'-shaped) systolic contour. | Paradoxical afterload increase instead of unloading. The LV ejects against the occluding balloon. Precipitous rise in MVO2, drop in stroke volume, acute LV strain, premature aortic valve closure. | **Immediately delay inflation timing** to match the true dicrotic notch. |
| **Late Inflation** | Sharp V-notch is replaced by a widened, blunted U-shaped notch. Peak diastolic augmentation is blunted and delayed. | Suboptimal diastolic coronary perfusion augmentation due to diastolic arterial pressure decay prior to balloon expansion. | Advance inflation timing earlier. |
| **Early Deflation** | Premature pressure decay mid-diastole with a retrograde drop back to baseline well before the subsequent ventricular systole. | Loss of late diastolic coronary perfusion and complete forfeiture of presystolic afterload reduction (vacuum effect). | Delay deflation timing closer to the R-wave. |
| **Late Deflation** | Balloon aortic end-diastolic pressure equals or exceeds native end-diastolic pressure (BAEDP >= UAEDP). Prolonged, slurred systolic upstroke. | LV contracts against an inflated balloon during early ejection. Severe increase in afterload, myocardial workload, and end-systolic wall stress. | Advance deflation timing earlier. |

#### Action Algorithm for Abnormal Waveforms

1. Switch IABP frequency to 1:2 assist ratio for precise visual identification of unassisted baseline landmarks.
2. Identify the true dicrotic notch on the unassisted complex (aortic valve closure reference).
3. Correlate inflation take-off with the dicrotic notch:
   * If inflation take-off begins on the systolic upstroke or above dicrotic pressure—**early inflation** is present. Delay inflation trigger.
   * If a blunted U-notch or gap exists between the dicrotic notch and pressure rise—**late inflation** is present. Advance inflation trigger.
4. Evaluate presystolic diastolic runoff:
   * If pressure collapses mid-diastole back to baseline—**early deflation** is present. Delay deflation.
   * If end-diastolic pressure is elevated with a sluggish systolic upstroke—**late deflation** is present. Advance deflation.
5. In the presence of significant tachyarrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, frequent PVCs), switch trigger mode from auto-timing to dedicated arrhythmia or R-wave tracking mode.
