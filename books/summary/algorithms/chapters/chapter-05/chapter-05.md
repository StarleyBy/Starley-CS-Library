# 05. Critical Care & Perioperative Hemodynamics

## Section 1. Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS) & Acute RV Failure

### Acute Right Ventricular Failure & LVAD Suction Events: Hemodynamic Pathophysiology and Management

Implantation of a continuous-flow left ventricular assist device (LVAD, e.g., HeartMate 3) fundamentally alters biventricular hemodynamics. Unloading the left ventricle (LV) dramatically elevates systemic cardiac output and systemic venous return to the right atrium and right ventricle (RV).

In patients with pre-existing pulmonary hypertension or subclinical RV dysfunction (hallmarks of end-stage non-ischemic or ischemic cardiomyopathy), the vulnerable RV fails to overcome the high pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR). The clinical hallmark is acute right ventricular failure (RVF) characterized by severe venous congestion and diminished transpulmonary transit to the left heart.

#### Pathophysiology of Suction Events (LV Cavity Collapse)

1. **Transpulmonary Bottleneck:** The failing RV cannot pump sufficient blood volume across high-resistance pulmonary capillary beds into the left atrium and LV.
2. **Critical LV Underfilling:** The LV cavity becomes acutely depleted ("empty ventricle").
3. **Negative Cavity Pressure:** As the continuous-flow rotary pump maintains its speed, severe subatmospheric vacuum develops inside the collapsed LV.
4. **Septal Suck-Down ("Suction Event"):** The compliant interventricular septum (IVS) or free wall is drawn mechanically into the LVAD inflow cannula. This triggers instantaneous flow drop alarms, severe ventricular arrhythmias, and profound systemic hypotension.

#### Differential Management Matrix

| Intervention | Status | Mechanism & Clinical Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO)** | **First-line (Class I)** | Selectively vasodilates pulmonary vasculature exclusively in ventilated lung segments. Unloads the RV, restores transpulmonary flow into the LV, and abolishes the vacuum without causing systemic vasodilation or hypotension. |
| **Inhaled Epoprostenol / PGI₂** | **Alternative** | Potent inhaled selective pulmonary vasodilator alternative when iNO is unavailable. |
| **Increasing LVAD Speed** | **Strictly Contraindicated** | Disastrously amplifies suction force inside the empty LV, draws the septum further leftward, mechanically distorts RV geometry, and triggers irreversible circulatory collapse. |
| **Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide)** | **Contraindicated in Shock** | Depletes intravascular volume, deepens LV underfilling, and produces intractable suction events. Elevated CVP reflects RV forward failure, not hypervolemia. |
| **Escalating Epinephrine** | **High Risk / Limited** | Increases myocardial oxygen consumption, induces refractory tachyarrhythmias, and high-dose alpha stimulation causes pulmonary vasoconstriction. |

#### Key Examination and Clinical Pearls

1. During acute post-LVAD right ventricular failure with suction events, elevated CVP, and systemic hypotension, the single most lethal reflex action is raising the pump speed (Option C). Always unload the RV first using selective pulmonary vasodilators (iNO, Option D).
2. Unlike intravenous vasodilators such as milrinone or nitroprusside, inhaled nitric oxide is inactivated immediately upon binding intravascular hemoglobin, ensuring targeted pulmonary vasodilation without triggering systemic vasodilation or worsening arterial hypotension.
