## Section 1. Obstructive Aortic Lesions: Coarctation of the Aorta (CoA)

### Hemodynamics of Aortic Clamping and Spinal Cord Protection in Coarctation Repair

Open surgical repair of coarctation of the aorta via left posterolateral thoracotomy necessitates cross-clamping of the transverse aortic arch/isthmus and distal descending aorta, completely interrupting antegrade systemic flow.

#### Physiology of Spinal Cord Perfusion and Collateral Networks

1. **Collateral Anatomy:** In patients beyond infancy with chronic coarctation, extensive arterial collateral networks (internal thoracic, scapular, and intercostal arteries) bypass the aortic coarctation shelf.
2. **Perfusion Driving Gradient:** Spinal cord perfusion downstream of the clamp (critically the anterior spinal artery via the artery of Adamkiewicz at Th9–Th12) depends directly on proximal hydrostatic head pressure. Permissive proximal hypertension drives vital collateral flow.
3. **Safety Threshold:** A distal femoral mean arterial pressure ≥ 40–45 mmHg (or > 50% of baseline pre-clamp mean pressure) confirms adequate collateral perfusion to prevent ischemic spinal cord injury during cross-clamping (up to 20–30 minutes).

#### Differential Surgical Management Matrix

| Distal Hemodynamic Status | Femoral Arterial Pressure | Action & Clinical Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Adequate Collateral Perfusion** | **≥ 40–45 mmHg** | **Proceed expeditiously with complete repair** (extended end-to-end anastomosis). Permissive hypertension is maintained. Shunts or bypass are unnecessary. |
| **Critical Collateral Deficit** | **&lt; 30–40 mmHg** | High risk of spinal cord infarction. Indication for mechanical distal perfusion: temporary Gott shunt or left heart / femoral-femoral partial bypass. |
| **Pharmacologic Afterload Reduction** | **Radial BP &lt; 120 mmHg** | **Strictly Contraindicated!** Vasodilators eliminate the driving gradient, crashing collateral flow and inducing irreversible paraplegia. |

#### Key Examination & Clinical Pearls

1. During aortic cross-clamping for coarctation repair, a femoral pressure ≥ 40–45 mmHg confirms adequate collateral flow: proceed with complete surgical repair without extracorporeal shunts.
2. Pharmacologic reduction of proximal hypertension with vasodilators (e.g., nitroprusside) eliminates the essential collateral driving gradient, causing distal perfusion to collapse and inducing postoperative paraplegia.
