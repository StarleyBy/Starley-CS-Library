#### Key Principles of the Algorithm

1. **Emergency Anti-Impulse Therapy (Target: HR < 60 bpm, SBP 100–120 mmHg):**
   - 1st line: IV beta-blockers (esmolol or labetalol) to reduce left ventricular dP/dt and aortic wall shear stress.
   - 2nd line: vasodilators (nicardipine or sodium nitroprusside) strictly AFTER beta-blockade.
   - Analgesia with IV opioids (morphine / fentanyl).
2. **Emergency Imaging:**
   - Stable: ECG-gated CTA of chest, abdomen, and pelvis (sensitivity & specificity > 99%).
   - Unstable: Bedside TEE in OR / resuscitation unit.
3. **Stanford Type A (Ascending Aorta):**
   - Emergent open surgical repair (median sternotomy, right axillary CPB cannulation, hypothermic arrest with antegrade SACP, entry tear excision, ascending aorta ± hemiarch / total arch replacement with FET, valve resuspension or Bentall / David procedure).
4. **Stanford Type B (Descending Aorta):**
   - **Uncomplicated:** Optimal medical therapy (OMT) in ICU + lifelong strict BP control < 120/80 mmHg. Consider elective subacute TEVAR (2–12 weeks) for high-risk anatomy (INSTEAD-XL).
   - **Complicated (malperfusion, refractory pain/HTN, rapid aortic expansion, rupture):** Urgent TEVAR (covered stent-graft across entry tear expanding true lumen; mortality < 10% vs. 30% for open repair).
