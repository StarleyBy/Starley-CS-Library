# Selected Reference Ranges

> *Accurate interpretation of physiological parameters, organ system tolerances, and diagnostic scores requires rapid bedside access to standardized reference ranges and anthropometric nomograms.*

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

### Core Reference Standards in Critical Care</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Diagnostic Radiation Safety:** Common diagnostic radiological studies deliver effective organ doses ranging from $0.02\text{ mSv}$ for single-view chest radiography up to $16\text{ mSv}$ for invasive coronary angiography. The annual occupational and clinical radiation exposure limit recommended by radiation protection guidelines is $50\text{ mSv}$.
- **Body Mass Index and Desirable Body Weight:** Patient nutritional status, mechanical ventilator tidal volume targeting, and hydrophilic drug dosing require precise distinction between actual body weight, ideal/desirable body weight, and body mass index (BMI).
- **Peak Expiratory Flow Normal Values:** Bedside spirometry evaluates airway resistance and bronchospastic airflow obstruction; predicted peak flow depends fundamentally on patient age, sex, and height.
- **Estimated Circulating Blood Volumes:** Normovolemic total blood volume approximates $66\text{ mL/kg}$ in adult males ($40\text{ mL/kg}$ plasma, $26\text{ mL/kg}$ erythrocytes) and $60\text{ mL/kg}$ in adult females ($36\text{ mL/kg}$ plasma, $24\text{ mL/kg}$ erythrocytes).
- **Delirium Screening with CAM-ICU:** The Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU) detects acute encephalopathy and delirium with high sensitivity and specificity based on acute onset/fluctuation, inattention, disorganized thinking, and altered level of consciousness.

</div></details>
</div></details>

## Radiation Exposure and Anthropometric Nomograms</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

### Diagnostic Radiation Dosimetry</summary><div class="details-content">

Critical care patients undergo frequent imaging surveillance. The biological impact of ionizing radiation is quantified by effective dose (in millisieverts, $\text{mSv}$), reflecting whole-body stochastic risk.

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

#### Principles of Cumulative Dosimetric Exposure</summary><div class="details-content">

Serial computed tomography and angiography rapidly contribute to cumulative patient radiation burden. Judicious imaging stewardship minimizes lifetime oncogenic risks.

</div></details>

### Body Mass Index and Desirable Weights</summary><div class="details-content">

Body mass index (BMI) correlates weight with stature, delineating nutritional risk categories from cachexia to severe morbid obesity.

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

#### Body Mass Index Matrix Nomogram</summary><div class="details-content">

Clinical categorizations define underweight as $\text{BMI} < 18.5\text{ kg/m}^2$, healthy weight as $18.5\text{–}24.9\text{ kg/m}^2$, overweight as $25.0\text{–}29.9\text{ kg/m}^2$, obese as $30.0\text{–}39.9\text{ kg/m}^2$, and extremely obese as $\text{BMI} \ge 40.0\text{ kg/m}^2$.

## Physiological Norms and Neurological Scoring</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

### Pulmonary Mechanics: Peak Expiratory Flow</summary><div class="details-content">

Peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) reflects large airway caliber and dynamic flow limitation during forced expiration.

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

#### Regression Modeling of Peak Airflow</summary><div class="details-content">

Predicted normal PEFR declines progressively with advancing age and varies directly with standing height, as modeled by validated regression equations.

</div></details>

### Hemodynamic Volumes and Delirium Screening</summary><div class="details-content">

Accurate fluid resuscitation and transfusion planning rely on weight-adjusted normovolemic blood volume estimations.

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

#### Blood Volume Compartmentalization</summary><div class="details-content">

Plasma volume accounts for roughly 60% of total circulating volume, while erythrocytes occupy the remaining 40%, differing slightly by sex.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

|:---|:---|:---|

| **Erythrocyte (Red Cell) Volume** | 26 | 24 |

*Adapted from American Association of Blood Banks (AABB) Technical Manual, 10th ed. Arlington, VA: 1990.*

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

#### <span id="tbl-app2-7"></span>TABLE App2.7 The Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU)</summary><div class="details-content">

| Clinical Feature | Assessment Protocol | Evaluation Criteria |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Feature I: Acute Onset or Fluctuating Course** | Is there evidence of an acute mental status change from baseline? OR Did the abnormal behavior fluctuate during the past 24 hours? | Positive if YES |
| **Feature II: Inattention** | Did the patient have difficulty focusing attention? (Tested by presenting 10 letters [e.g., S-A-V-E-A-H-A-A-R-T] or asking to recall 5 simple objects in succession). | Positive if $> 2$ errors |
| **Feature III: Disorganized Thinking** | Is there evidence of disorganized or incoherent thinking? Assessed by questions: (1) Will a stone float on water? (2) Are there fish in the sea? (3) Does 1 pound weigh more than 2 pounds? (4) Can you use a hammer to pound a nail? Followed by command: "Hold up this many fingers" and "Do the same with other hand". | Positive if $> 1$ error |
| **Feature IV: Altered Level of Consciousness** | Current level of consciousness on Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS) anything other than alert (RASS 0), such as drowsy (–1), light sedation (–2), or restless/agitated (+1 to +4). | Positive if RASS $\ne 0$ |

*Diagnostic Criterion:* Delirium is confirmed when **Feature I** AND **Feature II** are positive, accompanied by EITHER **Feature III** OR **Feature IV** (i.e., [I + II] + [III or IV] = Delirium). Adapted from Ely EW, et al. JAMA 2001; 286:2703.
