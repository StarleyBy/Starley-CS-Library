2. To evaluate the effects of disease or injury on heart function  
3. To evaluate pacemaker function  
4. To evaluate the response to medications (e.g., antiarrhythmics)  
5. To obtain a baseline recording before, during, and after a medical procedure  
6. To evaluate for signs of myocardial ischemia, injury, and infarction  
(Any three of the above)  
**OBJ:** Explain the purpose of ECG monitoring.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**57.** Describe the appearance of a pathologic Q wave.

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** A pathologic Q wave is more than 0.04 second in duration or more than one third the height of the following R wave in that lead.  
**OBJ:** Define and describe the significance of the Q wave.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**58.** What is the important difference between a sinus tachycardia and a sinus rhythm?

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** A sinus rhythm has a rate of 60 to 100 beats/min. A sinus tachycardia has a rate of 101 to 180 beats/min (or up to 220 minus age).  
**OBJ:** Describe the ECG characteristics of sinus tachycardia.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**59.** List two possible causes of artifact on an ECG tracing.

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** Any two of: loose electrodes, broken wires or ECG cables, muscle tremor, patient movement, external chest compressions, 60‑cycle interference.  
**OBJ:** Define the term artifact and explain methods used to minimize its occurrence.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**60.** How do coarse and fine ventricular fibrillation differ?

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** Coarse VF has waves that are 3 mm or more in amplitude. Fine VF has waves less than 3 mm in amplitude.  
**OBJ:** Describe the ECG characteristics of VF.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**61.** List four reasons why the AV junction may assume responsibility for pacing the heart.

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:**  
1. The SA node fails to discharge (e.g., sinus arrest).  
2. An impulse from the SA node is generated but blocked as it exits the SA node (e.g., SA block).  
3. The rate of discharge of the SA node is slower than that of the AV junction (e.g., sinus bradycardia or slower phase of sinus arrhythmia).  
4. An impulse from the SA node is generated and conducted through the atria but is not conducted to the ventricles (e.g., AV block).  
**OBJ:** Describe the location and function of the AV junction.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**62.** Explain why patients who experience AFib are at increased risk of having a stroke.

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** Because the atria do not contract effectively and expel all of the blood within them, blood may pool within them and form clots. A clot may dislodge on its own or because of conversion to a sinus rhythm. A stroke can result if a clot moves from the atria and lodges in an artery in the brain.  
**OBJ:** Describe the ECG characteristics and complications of AFib.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

**63.** On the ECG, what do the ST segment and T wave represent?

</summary><div class="details-content">

**Answer:** The ST segment represents the early part of ventricular repolarization. The T wave represents ventricular repolarization.  
**OBJ:** Define and describe the significance of the ST segment and T wave.

</div></details>
