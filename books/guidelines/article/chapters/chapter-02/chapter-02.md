## Five-Year Outcomes in Patients With Left Main Disease Treated With Either Percutaneous Coronary Intervention or Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting in the Synergy Between Percutaneous Coronary Intervention With Taxus and Cardiac Surgery Trial

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Background
Current guidelines recommend coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG) for significant de novo left main coronary artery (LM) stenosis; percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has a class IIa indication for unprotected LM disease in selected patients. This analysis compares 5‑year clinical outcomes in PCI‑ and CABG‑treated LM patients from the SYNTAX trial, the largest randomized comparison in this population to date.

### Methods and Results
The SYNTAX trial randomly assigned 1800 patients with LM or 3‑vessel disease to PCI (TAXUS Express paclitaxel‑eluting stents) or CABG. The prespecified LM cohort (N=705) was powered for the primary endpoint. At 5 years, major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular event (MACCE) rates were 36.9% (PCI) vs. 31.0% (CABG) (HR 1.23, 95% CI 0.95–1.59, *p*=0.12). Mortality was 12.8% vs. 14.6% (HR 0.88, *p*=0.53). Stroke was significantly higher with CABG (4.3% vs. 1.5%, HR 0.33, *p*=0.03), while repeat revascularization was higher with PCI (26.7% vs. 15.5%, HR 1.82, *p*<0.001). MACCE was similar between arms in patients with low/intermediate SYNTAX scores but significantly increased in PCI patients with high scores (≥33).

### Conclusions
At 5 years, no overall difference in MACCE was found between PCI and CABG for LM disease. PCI was associated with lower stroke but higher repeat revascularization. Both treatments are valid options; disease extent (SYNTAX score) should guide decision‑making, with high‑score patients deriving greater benefit from surgery.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

The optimal revascularization strategy (CABG or PCI) for patients with complex coronary artery disease is debated. Patients with unprotected LM lesions are at high risk for adverse events. Several large studies and meta‑analyses have compared CABG and PCI with stenting; most have shown similar intermediate‑ and long‑term safety (mortality, MI) and lower stroke but higher repeat revascularization with PCI. This analysis evaluates the 5‑year outcomes of the predefined LM subgroup from the SYNTAX trial.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Subject Selection, Procedure, and Follow‑Up</summary><div class="details-content">

Patients with LM or 3‑vessel disease, no prior CABG or PCI, were assessed by a heart team (interventional cardiologist and cardiac surgeon). If revascularization was judged equivalent by either technique, patients were randomized to CABG or PCI with TAXUS Express stents, stratified by LM disease and diabetes. Patients ineligible for one technique entered nested registries. Primary endpoint was noninferiority of 1‑year MACCE for PCI vs. CABG. All randomized patients had clinical follow‑up yearly through 5 years.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Definitions</summary><div class="details-content">

MACCE was defined as all‑cause death, MI, stroke, and repeat revascularization (PCI or CABG). Symptomatic graft occlusion (GO) and stent thrombosis (ST) were defined as clinical presentation of an acute coronary syndrome with documentation of flow‑limiting thrombus/occlusion within a bypass graft or stent. All events were adjudicated by an independent clinical events committee.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Statistical Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

The LM subgroup (N=700) was powered to detect a difference in 12‑month MACCE. However, because the primary endpoint was not met, results are hypothesis‑generating. Continuous variables were compared with t‑test; binary variables with χ² (or Fisher exact). Cumulative event rates were estimated by Kaplan‑Meier; log‑rank tests assessed differences. Hazard ratios and 95% CIs were from Cox proportional hazards. Subgroup analyses by baseline SYNTAX score tertile were performed post hoc.

</div></details>

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

The SYNTAX trial is the largest randomized comparison of PCI vs. CABG for LM disease. No significant differences in 5‑year MACCE were found, confirming and extending the 1‑year results. Outcomes in LM patients are related to downstream disease complexity rather than LM presence alone. Mortality was similar between groups, consistent with other randomized trials and registries. Stroke was significantly higher with CABG, mainly perioperative, while repeat revascularization was higher with PCI. The trade‑off between stroke and repeat revascularization is important in shared decision‑making.

In diabetic patients (small subgroup, n=67), MACCE seemed comparable between treatments, but revascularization was higher with PCI; excess mortality observed in other diabetic studies was not seen here.

MACCE was similar between treatments in low/intermediate SYNTAX scores (0–32), but PCI patients with high scores (≥33) had significantly worse outcomes, supporting current guidelines that recommend PCI for low/intermediate complexity LM disease and CABG for high complexity.

Since the SYNTAX trial (designed 2004), newer stents and surgical techniques have evolved; ongoing trials (e.g., EXCEL) will provide contemporary data. The heart team approach (class I indication) should guide treatment decisions to optimize patient outcomes.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Limitations</summary><div class="details-content">

The LM subgroup analysis was prespecified and powered, but because the primary endpoint of the overall trial was not met, the results are hypothesis‑generating. The LM subgroup was heterogeneous (including 0‑, 1‑, 2‑, and 3‑vessel disease). Additional differences may emerge with longer follow‑up.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

PCI can provide equivalent long‑term (to 5 years) death/stroke/MI outcomes compared with CABG in LM patients, particularly those with SYNTAX scores <33. CABG remains the gold standard for high‑complexity LM disease. Both revascularization strategies are valid options when selected appropriately.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Clinical Perspective</summary><div class="details-content">

SYNTAX is a landmark trial that has informed guidelines for LM revascularization. The 5‑year results of the LM subgroup support the current Class IIa indication for PCI in selected patients with unprotected LM disease. No overall difference in MACCE was observed; stroke was lower with PCI and revascularization was higher. The SYNTAX score stratifies patients: those with low/intermediate scores have similar outcomes with PCI, while those with high scores (>33) benefit more from CABG. These findings emphasize the need for a heart team approach and individualized decision‑making.
