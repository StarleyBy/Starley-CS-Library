## Coronary Artery Bypass Graft Surgery versus Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in Patients with Three-Vessel Disease and Left Main Coronary Disease: 5‑Year Follow‑Up of the Randomised, Clinical SYNTAX Trial

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Background
The SYNTAX trial compared coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG) with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for patients with left main or three‑vessel disease. This report presents the final 5‑year results to confirm earlier findings.

### Methods
Patients with de‑novo three‑vessel or left main disease were assessed by a heart team. Those suitable for both treatments were randomly assigned (1:1) to PCI with first‑generation paclitaxel‑eluting stents or to CABG. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) at 5 years, analysed by Kaplan‑Meier on an intention‑to‑treat basis.

### Findings
1800 patients were randomly assigned (CABG n=897; PCI n=903). At 5 years, MACCE was significantly lower with CABG (26.9% vs. 37.3%; p<0.0001). Myocardial infarction (3.8% vs. 9.7%; p<0.0001) and repeat revascularisation (13.7% vs. 25.9%; p<0.0001) were significantly higher with PCI. All‑cause death (11.4% vs. 13.9%; p=0.10) and stroke (3.7% vs. 2.4%; p=0.09) were not significantly different. In patients with low SYNTAX scores (≤22), MACCE did not differ significantly between groups (CABG 28.6% vs. PCI 32.1%; p=0.43). For intermediate (23–32) and high (≥33) scores, MACCE was significantly higher with PCI (intermediate: 25.8% vs. 36.0%, p=0.008; high: 26.8% vs. 44.0%, p<0.0001). In the left main subgroup, MACCE was not significantly different (31.0% vs. 36.9%; p=0.12).

### Interpretation
CABG remains the standard of care for patients with complex (intermediate or high SYNTAX score) lesions. PCI is an acceptable alternative for less complex disease (low SYNTAX scores) or left main disease with low/intermediate scores. All complex patients should be reviewed by a heart team.

## Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

CABG has been the standard of care for revascularisation of complex coronary artery disease since 1968. PCI, introduced in 1977, has expanded to treat increasingly complex disease, including multivessel and left main disease. The optimum method for revascularisation has been debated, with many trials limited by non‑randomised selection, inclusion of less complex disease, or insufficient power. The SYNTAX trial was designed to assess the optimum revascularisation treatment for patients with de‑novo left main or three‑vessel disease by randomly assigning patients to PCI with a first‑generation paclitaxel‑eluting stent or CABG. At 1 year, PCI did not meet non‑inferiority for MACCE due to higher repeat revascularisation. At 3 years, MACCE, myocardial infarction, and repeat revascularisation remained significantly higher with PCI, while the composite safety endpoint of death/stroke/MI and stroke alone were not significantly different. This report presents the final 5‑year results.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Design and Patients</summary><div class="details-content">

SYNTAX was a randomised clinical trial with nested registries conducted in 85 centres in the USA and Europe. A heart team (cardiac surgeon and interventional cardiologist) screened consecutive patients with de‑novo three‑vessel or left main disease. Patients suitable for both treatments were randomly assigned (1:1) to PCI or CABG. Patients suitable for only one treatment were entered into the respective registry. Exclusion criteria included previous PCI or CABG, persisting acute myocardial infarction, or need for concomitant cardiac surgery. All patients provided written informed consent.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Randomisation</summary><div class="details-content">

Eligible patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to PCI with the TAXUS Express paclitaxel‑eluting stent or to CABG. Randomisation was performed by an interactive voice response system, stratified by left main disease and diabetes mellitus. Patients and physicians were aware of treatment assignment.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Procedures</summary><div class="details-content">

Procedures were performed according to local practice with the goal of complete revascularisation. PCI was performed via femoral, brachial, or radial approach; staged procedures were permitted within 72 hours during the same hospital stay. Thienopyridine was given for at least 6 months, and aspirin indefinitely. CABG could be performed with or without cardiopulmonary bypass; use of arterial conduits was encouraged. Optimal medical therapy was recommended for all patients.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Definitions and Endpoints</summary><div class="details-content">

The primary endpoint was the composite rate of MACCE at 1 year, defined as all‑cause mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, and repeat revascularisation. Secondary endpoints included MACCE at 5 years, individual components, and stent thrombosis/graft occlusion. An independent clinical events committee adjudicated all events. Anatomic complexity was assessed using the SYNTAX score.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Statistical Analysis</summary><div class="details-content">

Five‑year outcomes were analysed using Kaplan‑Meier estimates with log‑rank p values. Hazard ratios and 95% CIs were from Cox regression. Sensitivity analysis for patient withdrawal was performed. Binary variables were compared with χ² or Fisher’s exact test. All analyses were intention‑to‑treat in the randomised cohort. Predefined subgroups (left main disease, three‑vessel disease, diabetes) were analysed only if the primary endpoint met non‑inferiority; since it did not, subgroup results are hypothesis‑generating.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Results</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Patients</summary><div class="details-content">

A total of 1800 patients were randomly assigned (897 to CABG, 903 to PCI). More CABG patients withdrew consent than PCI patients (50 vs. 11). At 5 years, follow‑up was available in 95.8% of the overall cohort. Baseline characteristics were well balanced ([Fig. 1](#fig-1)).

### MACCE Outcomes at 5 Years</summary><div class="details-content">

At 5 years, MACCE was significantly lower with CABG than with PCI (26.9% vs. 37.3%; HR 1.54 [95% CI 1.30–1.83]; p<0.0001; [Fig. 2F](#fig-2)). Myocardial infarction (3.8% vs. 9.7%; HR 2.57 [1.84–3.59]; p<0.0001; [Fig. 2B](#fig-2)) and repeat revascularisation (13.7% vs. 25.9%; HR 1.99 [1.62–2.45]; p<0.0001; [Fig. 2E](#fig-2)) were significantly higher with PCI. All‑cause death (11.4% vs. 13.9%; HR 1.22 [0.96–1.55]; p=0.10; [Fig. 2A](#fig-2)) and stroke (3.7% vs. 2.4%; HR 0.65 [0.39–1.07]; p=0.09; [Fig. 2C](#fig-2)) did not differ significantly. The composite of death/stroke/MI was similar between groups (20.8% vs. 24.3%; HR 1.22 [0.99–1.50]; p=0.06; [Fig. 2D](#fig-2)). Stent thrombosis/graft occlusion rates were 4.4% in CABG and 5.1% in PCI (p=0.70).

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

The SYNTAX trial is the largest randomised comparison of CABG vs. PCI for complex coronary artery disease. At 5 years, CABG significantly reduced MACCE compared with PCI, driven by lower myocardial infarction and repeat revascularisation rates. Mortality and stroke were not significantly different, although stroke trended higher with CABG. These results confirm that CABG should remain the standard of care for patients with complex (intermediate or high SYNTAX score) lesions. For patients with less complex disease (low SYNTAX scores) or left main disease with low/intermediate scores, PCI is a reasonable alternative. The heart team approach is essential to individualise treatment decisions.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Limitations</summary><div class="details-content">

Subgroup analyses are hypothesis‑generating because the primary endpoint of non‑inferiority was not met. More CABG patients withdrew consent than PCI patients, although sensitivity analysis suggested this did not affect the primary results. The SYNTAX score was based on angiographic complexity, not clinical risk. Newer stent generations and improved surgical techniques may alter outcomes.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

CABG remains the standard of care for patients with complex multivessel disease (intermediate or high SYNTAX score). PCI is an acceptable alternative for less complex disease (low SYNTAX score) or left main disease with low/intermediate SYNTAX score. All patients with complex coronary artery disease should be reviewed by a heart team.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Clinical Perspective</summary><div class="details-content">

The SYNTAX trial has provided robust evidence that CABG is superior to PCI with first‑generation DES in reducing MACCE at 5 years for patients with complex coronary artery disease. However, the trade‑offs of stroke (higher with CABG) and repeat revascularisation (higher with PCI) must be considered. The SYNTAX score helps stratify patients: those with low scores may do equally well with PCI, while those with higher scores clearly benefit from surgery. The heart team model, established by SYNTAX, is now a class I recommendation for decision‑making in complex coronary disease. Ongoing trials with newer stents (e.g., EXCEL) will further define the role of PCI in left main disease.

</div></details>
