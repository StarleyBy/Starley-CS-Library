## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Primary Composite Outcome at 5 Years
* **Equivalence in Primary Endpoint:** At 5-year follow-up, the primary composite endpoint of death from any cause, stroke, or myocardial infarction occurred in 22.0% (203 / 948) of patients in the PCI group versus 19.2% (176 / 957) of patients in the CABG group (absolute risk difference: 2.8 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.9 to 6.5; Odds Ratio [OR]: 1.19; 95% CI: 0.95–1.50; p = 0.13).
* **Statistical Conclusion:** The differences between percutaneous coronary intervention with contemporary thin-strut everolimus-eluting stents and surgical coronary revascularization for left main coronary disease of low or intermediate anatomical complexity did not reach statistical significance at 5 years.

### Secondary Composite Outcome and Repeat Revascularization
* **Four-Component Secondary Composite:** The composite of death, stroke, myocardial infarction, or ischemia-driven revascularization occurred in 31.3% (290 / 948) of PCI patients versus 24.9% (228 / 957) of CABG patients (absolute difference: 6.5 percentage points; 95% CI: 2.4 to 10.6; OR: 1.39; 95% CI: 1.13–1.71; p = 0.002).
* **Ischemia-Driven Revascularization (IDR):** Repeated revascularization was significantly higher following PCI (16.9% vs 10.0%; difference: 6.9 percentage points; 95% CI: 3.7 to 10.0; OR: 1.84; 95% CI: 1.39–2.44; p < 0.001).
* **Repeat Intervention Modalities:** In the PCI arm, repeat revascularization was performed via repeat PCI in 14.1% and conversion to CABG in 4.3%. In the CABG arm, repeat PCI was performed in 9.1% and re-CABG in only 0.9%.

### Individual Component Outcomes
* **All-Cause Mortality:** Total death occurred in 13.0% (119 / 948) of PCI patients compared with 9.9% (89 / 957) of CABG patients (absolute difference: 3.1 percentage points; 95% CI: 0.2 to 6.1; OR: 1.38; 95% CI: 1.03–1.85).
* **Cardiovascular vs Noncardiovascular Death:**
* *Definite Cardiovascular Death:* No significant difference (5.0% PCI vs 4.5% CABG; difference: 0.5 percentage points; 95% CI: -1.4 to 2.5; OR: 1.13; 95% CI: 0.73–1.74).
* *All Cardiovascular Death (including undetermined):* 6.8% vs 5.5% (difference: 1.3 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.9 to 3.6; OR: 1.26; 95% CI: 0.85–1.85).
* *Noncardiovascular Death:* 6.6% vs 4.6% (difference: 2.0 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.2 to 4.2; OR: 1.47; 95% CI: 0.97–2.23). The excess late deaths in the PCI cohort were predominantly driven by late noncardiovascular etiologies (malignancy, infection, chronic pulmonary disease).
* **Stroke:** 5-year stroke rates were low and similar (2.9% PCI vs 3.7% CABG; difference: -0.8 percentage points; 95% CI: -2.4 to 0.9; OR: 0.78; 95% CI: 0.46–1.31).
* **Myocardial Infarction:** Total MI rates through 5 years were comparable (10.6% vs 9.1%; OR: 1.14; 95% CI: 0.84–1.55).
* *Periprocedural MI (≤ 30 days):* Lower with PCI (3.9% vs 6.1%; OR: 0.63; 95% CI: 0.41–0.96).
* *Nonperiprocedural / Spontaneous MI (> 30 days):* Higher with PCI (6.8% vs 3.5%; OR: 1.96; 95% CI: 1.25–3.06).

### Piecewise Landmark Dynamics Across Three Chronological Intervals
* **0 to 30 Days:** PCI superior to CABG (Hazard Ratio [HR]: 0.61; 95% CI: 0.42–0.88) due to reduced surgical trauma, lower periprocedural MI, fewer transfusions, and zero sternal complications.
* **30 Days to 1 Year:** Equal hazard rates between modalities (HR: 1.07; 95% CI: 0.68–1.70).
* **1 Year to 5 Years:** CABG superior to PCI (HR: 1.61; 95% CI: 1.23–2.12), driven by surgical protection against spontaneous myocardial infarction and lower rates of repeat revascularization.

### Subgroup Consistency and Anatomical Boundaries
* **SYNTAX Score Terciles:** Primary outcome rates were consistent across low (≤ 22) and intermediate (23–32) anatomical complexity groups. In site-reported SYNTAX ≤ 22, primary event rate was 21.9% PCI vs 18.7% CABG (OR: 1.21); in SYNTAX 23–32, event rate was 22.2% PCI vs 20.0% CABG (OR: 1.16).
* **Diabetes Status:** Treatment effects were homogeneous in medically treated diabetic patients (OR: 1.24; 95% CI: 0.83–1.86) and non-diabetic patients (OR: 1.17; 95% CI: 0.89–1.55).
* **Bifurcation Lesions:** No interaction observed between bifurcation/trifurcation involvement (80.5% of trial cohort) versus ostial/shaft lesions.

## Introduction and Background</summary><div class="details-content">

Significant stenosis of the left main coronary artery (LMCA) represents the highest-risk anatomical subset of coronary artery disease, placing more than 75% of the left ventricular myocardial mass in jeopardy. Left untreated, hemodynamically significant unprotected left main stenosis carries a grave prognosis, with historical medical therapy cohorts demonstrating 3-year mortality rates approaching 50%.

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) has historically been established as the gold standard of care for unprotected left main disease, based on randomized clinical trials from the 1970s and 1980s demonstrating substantial survival prolongation with surgical bypass compared with medical management alone. By providing downstream surgical bypass conduits (predominantly the left internal mammary artery [LIMA] to the left anterior descending [LAD] artery) that bypass not only the left main bifurcation but also any proximal vessel disease, CABG confers robust protection against future proximal atherosclerotic plaque rupture.

With the advent of catheter-based revascularization and continuous technological refinements—including thin-strut metallic alloys, biocompatible fluoropolymers, antiproliferative limus-eluting agents, routine intravascular imaging (intravascular ultrasound [IVUS] and optical coherence tomography [OCT]), and advanced bifurcation stenting techniques—percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has emerged as an appealing, less invasive alternative.

Earlier randomized trials comparing first-generation drug-eluting stents (DES) with CABG, such as the SYNTAX (Synergy between PCI with Taxus and Cardiac Surgery) and PRECOMBAT trials, suggested comparable midterm death and stroke rates between PCI and CABG in low-to-intermediate anatomical complexity subsets, but were hindered by higher rates of repeat revascularization and early-generation stent thrombosis.

The EXCEL (Evaluation of XIENCE versus Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery for Effectiveness of Left Main Revascularization) trial was designed as an international, prospective, multicenter randomized trial to compare PCI utilizing modern thin-strut cobalt–chromium everolimus-eluting stents (CoCr-EES) versus contemporary CABG in patients with left main coronary disease exhibiting low or intermediate anatomical complexity (SYNTAX score ≤ 32).

At the initial primary endpoint analysis at a median follow-up of 3 years, the EXCEL trial met its prespecified noninferiority criteria: the composite rate of death, stroke, or myocardial infarction occurred in 15.4% of the PCI group and 14.7% of the CABG group (p = 0.02 for noninferiority). However, temporal analyses indicated that the early benefits of PCI within the initial 30 days were countered by fewer adverse events in the CABG arm between 30 days and 3 years. To definitively establish the long-term clinical trajectory, comparative safety, and durable efficacy of PCI versus CABG in left main coronary disease, the investigators conducted the prespecified final 5-year follow-up of the complete trial cohort.

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

### Trial Design and Oversight
The EXCEL trial was an international, open-label, multicenter, randomized clinical trial conducted across 126 clinical sites in 17 countries. The trial organization and execution were guided by an executive committee consisting of interventional cardiologists and cardiothoracic surgeons represented in equal numbers.

The institutional review board or medical ethics committee at each participating center reviewed and approved the clinical protocol. All enrolled patients provided written informed consent before randomization. An independent data and safety monitoring board monitored patient safety throughout the trial. An independent angiographic core laboratory (Cardiovascular Research Foundation, New York) performed blinded quantitative coronary angiography and adjudicated anatomical SYNTAX scores. All primary and secondary clinical events were adjudicated by an independent clinical events committee whose members were blinded to treatment group assignments.

### Patient Selection and Randomization
Patients were eligible for inclusion if they presented with:
1. Significant left main coronary artery disease, defined as stenosis ≥ 70% by visual angiographic estimation, or stenosis of 50% to < 70% if verified as hemodynamically significant by physiological assessment (fractional flow reserve ≤ 0.80) or intravascular imaging (IVUS minimal lumen area ≤ 6.0 mm²).
2. Clinical consensus by the local multidisciplinary Heart Team (comprising an interventional cardiologist and a cardiac surgeon) that clinical and anatomical equipoise was present, such that equivalent revascularization could be achieved with either PCI or CABG.
3. Low or intermediate anatomical coronary complexity, defined as a site-assessed SYNTAX score ≤ 32.

Patients were excluded if they possessed high anatomical complexity (site-reported SYNTAX score ≥ 33), required concomitant non-coronary cardiac surgical procedures (such as valve repair or replacement, or aortic repair), or had undergone prior coronary revascularization within 1 year.

Enrolled patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to undergo either PCI or CABG using an interactive voice-response or web-based system. Randomization was stratified by site, presence or absence of medically treated diabetes mellitus, and site-reported SYNTAX score (≤ 22 vs 23–32).

### Revascularization Procedures
* **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention:** PCI was performed exclusively utilizing fluoropolymer-based thin-strut (81 µm) cobalt–chromium everolimus-eluting stents (XIENCE, Abbott Vascular). Operators were instructed to treat all coronary artery segments with ≥ 50% diameter stenosis in vessels with reference diameter ≥ 2.25 mm. Intravascular ultrasound was mandatory pre- and post-stenting for left main lesions to optimize stent expansion and apposition. Post-procedural dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT; aspirin plus clopidogrel, prasugrel, or ticagrelor) was mandated for at least 1 year.
* **Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting:** CABG was executed by experienced cardiothoracic surgical teams. Arterial revascularization of the left anterior descending artery using the left internal mammary artery (LIMA) was strongly recommended unless contraindicated. Complete surgical revascularization was targeted for all diseased territories supplying viable myocardium using arterial conduits or saphenous vein grafts. Procedures were performed with cardiopulmonary bypass (on-pump) or off-pump according to institutional surgical discretion.

### Endpoints and Clinical Definitions
* **Primary Outcome:** Composite of death from any cause, stroke, or myocardial infarction (MI) evaluated through 5-year follow-up.
* **Major Secondary Outcomes:**
* Four-component composite of death from any cause, stroke, myocardial infarction, or ischemia-driven revascularization (IDR) through 5 years.
* Individual components: death (all-cause, definite cardiovascular, undetermined, noncardiovascular), stroke, and myocardial infarction.
* Ischemia-driven revascularization (any repeat PCI or CABG driven by recurrent symptoms or objective ischemia).
* Therapy failure: composite of definite stent thrombosis (Academic Research Consortium criteria) or symptomatic coronary graft stenosis/occlusion.
* **Protocol-Specific Myocardial Infarction Definition:**
* *Periprocedural MI (≤ 72 hours after revascularization):* Defined by a peak elevation of creatine kinase-myocardial band (CK-MB) to > 10 times the local upper limit of normal (ULN), or > 5 times the ULN with new pathological Q waves in ≥ 2 contiguous leads, new persistent left bundle branch block, angiographically documented acute graft or native vessel occlusion, or new significant loss of viable myocardium on imaging.
* *Spontaneous MI (> 72 hours):* Defined in accordance with the Universal Definition as a typical rise and fall of cardiac biomarkers (troponin or CK-MB) accompanied by symptoms of myocardial ischemia, new ischemic ECG changes, pathological Q waves, or imaging evidence of new regional wall motion abnormalities.

### Statistical Analysis
The primary study hypothesis was formulated to test the noninferiority of PCI to CABG with respect to the primary composite outcome at 3 years. The 5-year clinical outcomes were prespecified secondary endpoints designed to examine longitudinal durability.

Analyses were performed in the intention-to-treat population, comprising all randomized patients according to their allocated treatment group. Event rates were calculated using Kaplan–Meier cumulative incidence estimates.

Because the underlying assumption of proportional hazards across the full 5-year follow-up was violated (p < 0.001 for treatment-by-time interaction), principal between-treatment comparisons were performed using multivariable logistic regression with log-transformed follow-up time incorporated as an offset variable. Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals were estimated.

To characterize the evolving clinical trajectory, post hoc piecewise hazard models were evaluated across three clinically distinct landmark intervals during which proportional hazards were valid:
1. **0 to 30 Days:** The immediate periprocedural period.
2. **30 Days to 1 Year:** The early post-procedural period of potential restenosis.
3. **1 Year to 5 Years:** The long-term follow-up window.

Milestone analyses and restricted mean survival time (RMST) through 5 years were calculated to quantify net treatment differences. Sensitivity analyses were conducted in per-protocol and as-treated cohorts, and multiple imputation was utilized for missing follow-up data.

## Results</summary><div class="details-content">

### Patients, Procedures, and Follow-Up
Between September 29, 2010, and March 6, 2014, a total of 1905 patients with unprotected left main coronary artery disease were randomized: 948 patients to the PCI group and 957 patients to the CABG group.

Baseline clinical characteristics were well-balanced between the two cohorts:
* Mean patient age was 66.0 ± 9.6 years.
* 76.9% of patients were male.
* 29.1% had medically treated diabetes mellitus.
* Mean left ventricular ejection fraction was 57.0%.
* Baseline clinical presentation was stable angina or silent ischemia in 61.5%, and acute coronary syndrome (unstable angina or NSTE-ACS) in 38.5%.
* Left main anatomical bifurcation or trifurcation stenosis was present in 80.5% of patients.
* The mean site-reported SYNTAX score was 20.6 ± 6.2; by core laboratory assessment, the mean SYNTAX score was 26.5 ± 9.3, with 60.5% having low (≤ 22), 29.8% intermediate (23–32), and 9.7% high (≥ 33) scores.

In the PCI group, complete revascularization was achieved in 68.8% of patients; the mean number of stents deployed was 2.4 ± 1.5, with a total stent length of 49.1 ± 35.8 mm. IVUS was utilized in 86.4% of PCI procedures. In the CABG group, complete surgical revascularization was achieved in 69.7%; an internal mammary artery graft was utilized in 98.8% of patients, and 29.3% underwent off-pump CABG.

Complete 5-year clinical follow-up for the primary outcome was achieved in 93.2% of randomized patients (884 in the PCI group and 891 in the CABG group).

---

### Primary Composite Outcome at 5 Years
At 5 years, the primary composite outcome of death from any cause, stroke, or myocardial infarction had occurred in 22.0% (203 / 948) of patients in the PCI group and 19.2% (176 / 957) of patients in the CABG group:
* **Absolute Event Rate Difference:** 2.8 percentage points (95% CI: -0.9 to 6.5).
* **Odds Ratio:** 1.19 (95% CI: 0.95–1.50; p = 0.13).

The Kaplan–Meier curves for the primary composite outcome demonstrated an early divergence favoring PCI during the first 30 days, crossed at approximately 2 to 3 years, and showed a non-significant divergence favoring CABG between 3 and 5 years.

### Secondary Composite and Individual Clinical Endpoints

#### Four-Component Secondary Composite Outcome
The secondary composite outcome (death from any cause, stroke, myocardial infarction, or ischemia-driven revascularization) occurred in:
* **PCI Group:** 31.3% (290 / 948)
* **CABG Group:** 24.9% (228 / 957)
* **Difference:** 6.5 percentage points (95% CI: 2.4 to 10.6; OR: 1.39; 95% CI: 1.13–1.71; p = 0.002).

This statistically significant difference was entirely driven by higher rates of repeat revascularization in the PCI arm.

#### Analysis of Mortality
* **All-Cause Mortality:** Total death through 5 years was documented in 13.0% (119 / 948) of patients randomized to PCI versus 9.9% (89 / 957) randomized to CABG (difference: 3.1 percentage points; 95% CI: 0.2 to 6.1; OR: 1.38; 95% CI: 1.03–1.85).
* **Cause-Specific Mortality Breakdown:**
* *Definite Cardiovascular Death:* 5.0% in PCI vs 4.5% in CABG (difference: 0.5 percentage points; 95% CI: -1.4 to 2.5; OR: 1.13; 95% CI: 0.73–1.74).
* *Undetermined Death:* 1.9% in PCI vs 1.1% in CABG (difference: 0.9 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.3 to 2.0; OR: 1.78; 95% CI: 0.78–4.06).
* *All Cardiovascular Death (definite + undetermined):* 6.8% in PCI vs 5.5% in CABG (difference: 1.3 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.9 to 3.6; OR: 1.26; 95% CI: 0.85–1.85).
* *Noncardiovascular Death:* 6.6% in PCI vs 4.6% in CABG (difference: 2.0 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.2 to 4.2; OR: 1.47; 95% CI: 0.97–2.23). The late difference in mortality was primarily attributed to noncardiovascular conditions, including malignancies, infectious complications, and end-stage respiratory failure.

#### Stroke, Myocardial Infarction, and Revascularization
* **Stroke:** 5-year stroke occurred in 2.9% (26 / 948) of PCI patients versus 3.7% (33 / 957) of CABG patients (difference: -0.8 percentage points; 95% CI: -2.4 to 0.9; OR: 0.78; 95% CI: 0.46–1.31).
* **Myocardial Infarction:** Total MI occurred in 10.6% (95 / 948) of PCI patients versus 9.1% (84 / 957) of CABG patients (difference: 1.4 percentage points; 95% CI: -1.3 to 4.2; OR: 1.14; 95% CI: 0.84–1.55).
* *Periprocedural MI (≤ 30 days):* Significantly lower with PCI (3.9% vs 6.1%; difference: -2.1 percentage points; OR: 0.63; 95% CI: 0.41–0.96).
* *Nonperiprocedural / Spontaneous MI (> 30 days):* Significantly higher with PCI (6.8% vs 3.5%; difference: 3.2 percentage points; OR: 1.96; 95% CI: 1.25–3.06).
* **Ischemia-Driven Revascularization:** Revascularization through 5 years was required in 16.9% (150 / 948) of PCI patients versus 10.0% (88 / 957) of CABG patients (difference: 6.9 percentage points; 95% CI: 3.7 to 10.0; OR: 1.84; 95% CI: 1.39–2.44; p < 0.001).
* **Therapy Failure:** Definite stent thrombosis through 5 years occurred in 1.1% (10 / 948) and probable stent thrombosis in 0.7% (6 / 948) of PCI patients. In the CABG cohort, symptomatic coronary graft stenosis or occlusion occurred in 6.5% (58 / 957) of patients.

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

### Interpretation of Five-Year Equivalence and Temporal Trade-Offs
In the EXCEL trial, among patients with unprotected left main coronary artery disease of low or intermediate anatomical complexity (SYNTAX score ≤ 32), there was no statistically significant difference between PCI with everolimus-eluting stents and CABG in the primary composite endpoint of death, stroke, or myocardial infarction at 5 years (22.0% vs 19.2%; OR: 1.19; 95% CI: 0.95–1.50; p = 0.13).

However, characterizing these 5-year findings as simply demonstrating identical outcomes obscures complex, clinically vital temporal dynamics. The piecewise landmark analysis demonstrates three distinct chronological phases:
1. **Early Surgical Hazard (0 to 30 Days):** During the initial month, PCI yielded a 39% relative risk reduction in the primary composite outcome (HR: 0.61; p = 0.008), driven by fewer large periprocedural myocardial infarctions (3.9% vs 6.3%), lower surgical bleeding, absence of sternal wound infections, and faster functional convalescence.
2. **Equipotential Phase (30 Days to 1 Year):** Event rates between treatments were parallel (HR: 1.07; p = 0.77).
3. **Late Bypass Protection (1 Year to 5 Years):** Between 1 and 5 years, the risk curve reversed, favoring CABG with a 38% relative reduction in primary events (HR: 1.61; p < 0.001). This late surgical benefit was fueled by lower rates of spontaneous myocardial infarction and fewer repeat revascularizations.

Restricted mean survival time analysis indicated that event-free survival through 5 years was virtually identical between therapies (5.2 days longer with PCI; 95% CI: -46.1 to 56.5 days).

### Analysis of the All-Cause Mortality Difference
A major topic of clinical debate following the presentation of the 5-year EXCEL results has centered on the nominal difference in all-cause mortality (13.0% in the PCI group vs 9.9% in the CABG group; difference: 3.1 percentage points; OR: 1.38; 95% CI: 1.03–1.85).

Thorough examination of adjudicated cause-specific mortality reveals several critical nuances:
* **Cardiovascular Death Is Balanced:** Definite cardiovascular death was nearly identical between cohorts (5.0% PCI vs 4.5% CABG; difference: 0.5 percentage points; OR: 1.13; 95% CI: 0.73–1.74). Even when undetermined deaths were conservatively categorized as cardiovascular, the difference remained non-significant (6.8% vs 5.5%; difference: 1.3 percentage points; OR: 1.26; 95% CI: 0.85–1.85).
* **Predominance of Noncardiovascular Deaths:** The absolute difference in total mortality was largely accounted for by noncardiovascular deaths (6.6% PCI vs 4.6% CABG; difference: 2.0 percentage points; OR: 1.47). The most frequent causes were metastatic malignancies, sepsis, and end-stage chronic lung disease. Biological plausibility linking everolimus-eluting stent deployment to late cancer mortality is lacking.
* **Underpowering for Isolated Endpoints:** The EXCEL trial was explicitly powered for the 3-year primary composite endpoint, not for isolated 5-year mortality. When secondary endpoints are not adjusted for multiplicity, isolated nominal p-values must be interpreted with caution.

### Periprocedural vs Spontaneous Myocardial Infarction
Myocardial infarction exhibited a profound biological divergence based on timing:
* **Periprocedural Infarction (≤ 30 days):** Was significantly lower with PCI (3.9% vs 6.1%; OR: 0.63). The EXCEL trial employed a protocol biomarker definition based on CK-MB > 10 imesULN (or > 5 imesULN with new Q waves or angiographic occlusion). Extensively validated in prior studies, large CK-MB elevations after either PCI or CABG are independently associated with subsequent cardiovascular mortality.
* **Spontaneous Myocardial Infarction (> 30 days):** Was significantly higher with PCI (6.8% vs 3.5%; OR: 1.96; p = 0.003). This reflects the fundamental mechanistic difference between focal stenting and surgical bypass. Stents treat only the target focal stenosis, leaving the remainder of the coronary tree susceptible to neoatherosclerosis and de novo plaque rupture. In contrast, CABG conduits (particularly arterial LIMA-LAD grafts) bypass the entire proximal and mid-coronary vascular tree, shielding downstream myocardium from proximal plaque rupture.

### Repeat Revascularization and Conduit Failures
Ischemia-driven repeat revascularization remained significantly higher after PCI throughout 5 years (16.9% vs 10.0%; OR: 1.84; p < 0.001).
* In the PCI cohort, late repeat interventions were driven by in-stent restenosis (1.8% stent thrombosis), stent underexpansion, or disease progression in non-target segments.
* In the CABG cohort, surgical re-intervention was rare (0.9% re-CABG; 9.1% repeat PCI), despite a documented symptomatic graft failure rate of 6.5%. Physicians are historically more reluctant to perform repeat revascularization after prior sternotomy than after prior PCI.

### Concordance and Discordance with the NOBLE and SYNTAX Trials
The 5-year EXCEL results must be contextualized alongside the contemporary NOBLE trial and the historical SYNTAX trial:
* **The NOBLE Trial (Lancet 2016/2020):** Enrolled 1201 patients with left main disease and reported superiority of CABG over PCI at 5 years for major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE: 28% PCI vs 19% CABG; HR: 1.58; p = 0.0002). Several key differences explain the discordance:
1. *Primary Endpoint:* NOBLE included repeat revascularization in its primary composite endpoint; EXCEL excluded it from the primary composite, focusing strictly on death, stroke, and MI.
2. *Biomarker Definitions:* NOBLE did not collect routine periprocedural cardiac biomarkers and therefore omitted periprocedural enzymatic MI, capturing only spontaneous infarcts.
3. *Stent Technology:* NOBLE utilized first- and second-generation biolimus- and sirolimus-eluting stents (only 11% everolimus-eluting stents), with lower routine IVUS guidance.
* **The SYNTAX Left Main Subgroup (Lancet 2013):** In patients with low (≤ 22) and intermediate (23–32) SYNTAX scores, SYNTAX demonstrated comparable 5-year death, stroke, and MI rates between PCI and CABG, consistent with EXCEL.

### Study Limitations
1. **Selection Bias for Equipoise:** By design, patients were enrolled only if the multidisciplinary Heart Team confirmed anatomical and surgical equipoise. Patients with highly complex coronary disease (SYNTAX ≥ 33) were excluded.
2. **Core Laboratory SYNTAX Discordance:** While all enrolled patients had site-reported SYNTAX scores ≤ 32, retrospective core laboratory analysis revealed that 9.7% possessed scores ≥ 33, reflecting real-world variability in visual lesion grading.
3. **Open-Label Design:** Knowledge of assigned therapy may have influenced decisions regarding repeat revascularization during follow-up.
4. **Statistical Power for Mortality:** The trial lacked statistical power to detect modest differences in isolated death or stroke at 5 years.

## Conclusions and Clinical Perspectives</summary><div class="details-content">

### Conclusions
In patients with unprotected left main coronary artery disease of low or intermediate anatomical complexity (SYNTAX score ≤ 32), percutaneous coronary intervention with thin-strut everolimus-eluting stents resulted in rates of the primary composite outcome of death from any cause, stroke, or myocardial infarction at 5 years that were not significantly different from those observed with coronary artery bypass grafting (22.0% vs 19.2%; OR: 1.19; 95% CI: 0.95–1.50; p = 0.13).

PCI was associated with superior outcomes during the initial 30 days due to lower surgical morbidity and fewer periprocedural infarctions. Conversely, CABG provided superior protection between 1 and 5 years, characterized by fewer spontaneous myocardial infarctions and a lower need for repeat revascularization. All-cause mortality was nominally higher after PCI, predominantly driven by late noncardiovascular deaths.

### Heart Team Decision-Making in Left Main Disease
* **Patient-Centered Multidisciplinary Collaboration:** Optimal selection between PCI and CABG for left main coronary disease requires balanced, transparent deliberation by the Heart Team, actively engaging the informed patient.
* **Individualized Anatomical and Clinical Profiling:**
* *Features Favoring PCI:* Low anatomical complexity (SYNTAX ≤ 22), isolated ostial or shaft left main lesions, advanced age, high surgical risk (severe pulmonary disease, frailty, porcelain aorta), or patient preference for rapid recovery and avoidance of sternotomy.
* *Features Favoring CABG:* Intermediate-to-high anatomical complexity (SYNTAX ≥ 23), diffuse multi-vessel disease, bifurcation lesions requiring two-stent techniques, medically treated diabetes mellitus, impaired renal function, or high contraindication to prolonged dual antiplatelet therapy.

### Long-Term Lifetime Strategy
Left main revascularization must be viewed as a lifetime continuum rather than an isolated procedural episode. When PCI is selected, mandatory intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT), high-pressure post-dilatation, optimization of bifurcations, and rigorous secondary medical prevention (high-intensity statins, tight blood pressure control, and optimized antiplatelet therapy) are essential to prevent late neoatherosclerosis and spontaneous ischemic events.
