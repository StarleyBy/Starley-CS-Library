## Percutaneous Coronary Angioplasty versus Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting in the Treatment of Unprotected Left Main Stenosis: Updated 5‑Year Outcomes from the Randomised, Non‑Inferiority NOBLE Trial

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Background
Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is increasingly used for left main coronary artery disease instead of coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). The NOBLE trial previously reported outcomes at a median of 3.1 years. This report presents updated 5‑year outcomes after reaching the predefined number of events.

### Methods
Prospective, randomised, open‑label, non‑inferiority trial at 36 hospitals in 9 northern European countries. Patients with left main disease requiring revascularisation were randomly assigned (1:1) to PCI or CABG. Primary endpoint was major adverse cardiac or cerebrovascular events (MACCE) – composite of all‑cause mortality, non‑procedural myocardial infarction, repeat revascularisation, and stroke. Non‑inferiority limit: upper 95% CI of HR ≤1.35 after 275 events.

### Findings
1201 patients enrolled (PCI n=598, CABG n=603); 592 per group analysed. Median follow‑up 4.9 years. 5‑year MACCE: PCI 28% (165 events) vs. CABG 19% (110 events) – HR 1.58 (95% CI 1.24–2.01), exceeding non‑inferiority limit. CABG superior to PCI (p=0.0002). All‑cause mortality: PCI 9% vs. CABG 9% (HR 1.08, p=0.68). Non‑procedural MI: PCI 8% vs. CABG 3% (HR 2.99, p=0.0002). Repeat revascularisation: PCI 17% vs. CABG 10% (HR 1.73, p=0.0009). Stroke: PCI 4% vs. CABG 2% (HR 1.75, p=0.11).

### Interpretation
In left main revascularisation, PCI was inferior to CABG at 5 years. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural myocardial infarction and repeat revascularisation. These results do not support current guideline recommendations for PCI in low SYNTAX score patients.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

PCI has been compared with CABG for left main revascularisation. PRECOMBAT, SYNTAX left main subgroup, and EXCEL showed similar mortality with PCI and CABG; EXCEL reported higher mortality with PCI at 5 years. Repeat revascularisation was higher and stroke lower with PCI. The NOBLE trial was designed to assess non‑inferiority of PCI for the primary composite endpoint of MACCE. The initial report at 3.1 years indicated inferiority of PCI. This report presents updated 5‑year follow‑up after reaching the predefined number of events.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Design</summary><div class="details-content">

Prospective, randomised, open‑label, non‑inferiority trial at 36 hospitals in Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Latvia, Lithuania, Norway, Sweden, and the UK. Approved by national ethics committees.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Participants</summary><div class="details-content">

Inclusion: stable or unstable angina, acute coronary syndrome; significant left main stenosis (>50% or FFR ≤0.80); ≤3 additional non‑complex lesions. Exclusion: STEMI within 24 h; too high risk for either procedure; expected survival <1 year.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Randomisation and Masking</summary><div class="details-content">

1:1 randomisation via web‑based system, stratified by sex, distal LM bifurcation lesion, diabetes. Not blinded.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Procedures</summary><div class="details-content">

Complete revascularisation goal. PCI: mandatory drug‑eluting stents (mostly newer‑generation umirolimus‑eluting Biomatrix Flex). High‑pressure post‑dilatation recommended; IVUS guidance recommended. CABG: current best practice; LIMA to LAD recommended.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Outcomes</summary><div class="details-content">

Primary endpoint: MACCE at 5 years – all‑cause mortality, non‑procedural myocardial infarction, repeat revascularisation, stroke. Secondary endpoints: all‑cause mortality, non‑procedural MI, repeat revascularisation, stent thrombosis/graft occlusion, stroke. Independent clinical events committee adjudicated all events.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Statistical Analysis</summary><div class="details-content">

Non‑inferiority limit: upper 95% CI of HR ≤1.35 after 275 events. Kaplan‑Meier estimates, Cox regression adjusted for stratification variables. Superiority tested conditionally if non‑inferiority not met. Subgroup analyses by SYNTAX score tertile. Two‑sided p<0.05 significant.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Results</summary><div class="details-content">

1201 patients enrolled (PCI n=598, CABG n=603). 17 lost to follow‑up; 592 per group analysed. Baseline characteristics: median age 66.2 years; 20–24% women; 15–16% diabetes; 82–83% stable angina. SYNTAX score 22 (SD 8) PCI vs. 22 (SD 7) CABG. Follow‑up complete: 98% at 4 years, 98–99% at 5 years ([Fig. 1](#fig-1)).

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

NOBLE reached the predefined number of events for conclusive assessment. PCI did not meet non‑inferiority; CABG was superior for MACCE. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural MI and repeat revascularisation. Stroke rates did not differ. The subgroup of patients with low SYNTAX score (≤22) had almost double the MACCE rate with PCI vs. CABG (27% vs. 14%), contradicting current guidelines that recommend PCI (class IA) for SYNTAX <23. This finding suggests that the presence of multivessel disease was less relevant; even low‑score patients benefited from CABG.

Mortality after CABG in NOBLE (9%) was similar to EXCEL (9.9%), but PCI mortality in NOBLE was numerically lower (9% vs. 13% in EXCEL), possibly due to differences in diabetes prevalence and endpoint definitions. Repeat revascularisation and non‑procedural MI were consistent across trials. The higher rate of non‑procedural MI after PCI is concerning, as MI carries significant morbidity. Repeat revascularisation after PCI often required CABG (a quarter of cases), which should not be underrated.

The trial has limitations: participating centres were selected for PCI interest; enrolment was long; unstable patients excluded; small proportion treated with first‑generation DES; IVUS use heterogeneous; no correction for multiplicity.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

PCI was associated with inferior 5‑year clinical outcome compared with CABG for left main revascularisation. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural MI and repeat revascularisation. These findings do not support current guideline recommendations for PCI in low SYNTAX score patients. Heart team evaluation and individualised patient discussion remain essential.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Clinical Perspective</summary><div class="details-content">

The NOBLE trial provides robust evidence that CABG is superior to PCI for the composite endpoint of MACCE at 5 years in left main disease. The trade‑offs are important: PCI offers similar mortality, shorter hospital stay, less bleeding, and fewer sternal infections, but higher non‑procedural MI and repeat revascularisation. CABG offers lower MI and repeat revascularisation but higher periprocedural morbidity. The SYNTAX score did not identify a subgroup where PCI was clearly advantageous; even low‑score patients had worse outcomes with PCI. This challenges the current European guidelines (class IA for PCI in SYNTAX <23). A heart team approach and shared decision‑making are essential.
