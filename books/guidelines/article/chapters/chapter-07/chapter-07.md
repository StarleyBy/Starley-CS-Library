## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Primary Survival Advantage
* **All-Cause Mortality Reduction:** Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) is associated with a statistically significant 39% relative reduction in 1-year all-cause mortality compared with surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) (2.1% vs 3.5%; Risk Ratio [RR]: 0.61; 95% CI: 0.39–0.96; p = 0.03; I² = 0%).
* **Cardiovascular Mortality Reduction:** TAVR demonstrates a 45% relative reduction in 1-year cardiovascular death (1.6% vs 2.9%; RR: 0.55; 95% CI: 0.33–0.90; p = 0.02; I² = 0%).
* **Homogeneity Across Trials:** Both mortality outcomes demonstrate absolute consistency across all included low-risk trials (I² = 0%), confirming robust benefit regardless of specific prosthetic design.

### Secondary Clinical Endpoints
* **Stroke Rates:** No statistically significant difference in 1-year stroke between TAVR and SAVR (3.0% vs 4.2%; RR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.43–1.07; p = 0.10; I² = 17%).
* **Myocardial Infarction:** Similar rates observed between cohorts (1.7% vs 2.1%; RR: 0.78; 95% CI: 0.46–1.34; p = 0.37; I² = 0%).
* **Rehospitalization:** Valve- or heart failure-related rehospitalization rates at 1 year did not differ significantly in the primary pooled random-effects analysis (5.2% vs 7.9%; RR: 0.72; 95% CI: 0.42–1.23; p = 0.23; I² = 62%). However, when excluding the intermediate/low-risk post hoc SURTAVI cohort, TAVR demonstrated a marked reduction in rehospitalizations (RR: 0.59; 95% CI: 0.43–0.81; p = 0.001; I² = 0%).

### Procedural Safety and Complication Profile
* **Complications Favoring TAVR:**
* *New-Onset / Worsening Atrial Fibrillation:* Substantially lower with TAVR (10.0% vs 39.4%; RR: 0.27; 95% CI: 0.20–0.32; p < 0.001).
* *Life-Threatening or Disabling Bleeding:* Reduced by 63% with TAVR (3.9% vs 11.2%; RR: 0.37; 95% CI: 0.24–0.55; p < 0.001).
* *Acute Kidney Injury (Stage 2/3):* Reduced by 74% with TAVR (0.7% vs 2.9%; RR: 0.26; 95% CI: 0.13–0.52; p < 0.001).
* **Complications Favoring SAVR:**
* *Permanent Pacemaker Implantation (PPM):* Higher following TAVR (17.4% vs 5.5%; RR: 3.85; 95% CI: 1.73–8.58; p = 0.001; I² = 85%). This difference was predominantly driven by first-generation self-expanding prostheses (NOTION: 38.0%). In contrast, balloon-expandable valves demonstrated comparable PPM rates (7.3% vs 5.4% in PARTNER 3).
* *Moderate or Severe Paravalvular Leak (PVL):* Higher with TAVR (3.6% vs 1.7%; RR: 2.16; 95% CI: 1.03–4.54; p = 0.04; I² = 18%).
* **Equivalent Outcomes:** Major vascular complications (3.6% vs 2.4%; p = 0.11), infective endocarditis (0.4% vs 0.6%; p = 0.58), aortic valve reintervention (1.1% vs 0.6%; p = 0.25), and NYHA functional class ≥ II (21.2% vs 17.6%; p = 0.11) showed no statistically significant differences.

### Critical Patient Selection Criteria and Anatomic Boundaries
* **Eligible Population:** Older patients (mean age 75.4 years, STS-PROM score < 4%) with severe tricuspid aortic stenosis suitable for transfemoral access and bioprosthetic replacement.
* **Exclusion Criteria (Results Do Not Apply To):**
* Congenital unicuspid or bicuspid aortic valves.
* Non-calcified aortic valves.
* Severe concomitant multi-valvular disease (severe AR, MR, or MS).
* Complex multi-vessel coronary disease with SYNTAX score > 22 or unprotected left main disease.
* Young patients (< 65 years) who are candidates for mechanical prostheses.

## Introduction and Background</summary><div class="details-content">

Approximately 12% of individuals over 75 years of age suffer from aortic stenosis (AS), and 3.4% have severe AS. As global demographics shift and populations continue to age, the prevalence of calcific AS is anticipated to expand substantially. Over the past decade, transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has evolved from a rescue procedure reserved for inoperable individuals into an established, evidence-based therapeutic standard for patients with severe symptomatic AS at prohibitive, high, and intermediate surgical risk. Currently, tens of thousands of TAVR procedures are performed annually across specialized heart centers globally.

Despite these advances, surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) has historically remained the standard of care in low-operative-risk patients (defined by a Society of Thoracic Surgeons Predicted Risk of Mortality [STS-PROM] score < 4%). Observational comparisons and early single-center registries yielded conflicting outcomes, complicated by confounding and selection biases.

The Nordic Aortic Valve Intervention (NOTION) trial was the first randomized controlled trial (RCT) to evaluate an all-comers cohort aged ≥ 70 years, of whom over 80% possessed an STS-PROM < 4%. At 1-year and subsequent 5-year follow-up, NOTION demonstrated no significant difference between TAVR and SAVR for the primary composite endpoint of death, stroke, or myocardial infarction. Subsequently, the LRT (Low Risk TAVR) feasibility study confirmed safety in carefully selected low-risk patients at 1 year.

More recently, two landmark randomized trials—the PARTNER 3 trial (evaluating the third-generation balloon-expandable Sapien 3 valve) and the Evolut Low Risk trial (evaluating self-expanding CoreValve, Evolut R, and Evolut PRO prostheses)—reported clinical outcomes in low-risk surgical candidates. PARTNER 3 demonstrated statistical superiority of TAVR over SAVR for its primary composite endpoint of death, stroke, or rehospitalization at 1 year. The Evolut Low Risk trial established noninferiority of TAVR compared with SAVR regarding death or disabling stroke at 24 months.

Nonetheless, each individual trial utilized distinct composite primary endpoints and lacked adequate statistical power to evaluate differences across individual clinical outcomes, notably all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, stroke, and rehospitalization. Therefore, the present systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials was designed to evaluate the pooled comparative efficacy and safety of TAVR versus SAVR focusing specifically on isolated 1-year clinical endpoints in patients with severe AS at low surgical risk.

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

### Data Sources and Search Strategy
Electronic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), were queried from database inception through March 20, 2019, without language restrictions. The search utilized combinations of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and text keywords, including:
* `"transcatheter aortic valve replacement"` [MeSH]
* `"transcatheter aortic valve implantation"`
* `"TAVR"`
* `"TAVI"`
* `"low risk"`

In addition, reference lists of relevant review articles, meta-analyses, and identified clinical trials were cross-referenced to ensure complete capture of eligible studies.

### Study Selection and Eligibility Criteria
Studies were included in the meta-analysis if they satisfied all of the following explicit criteria:
1. Randomized controlled trial design (or formal post hoc subanalysis of an RCT).
2. Direct head-to-head comparison of TAVR versus SAVR.
3. Enrollment of patients classified as low surgical risk, defined by an STS-PROM score < 4% (or explicitly designated low-risk subset).
4. Reporting of clinical endpoints at 1-year follow-up.

Observational studies, non-randomized registries, duplicate publications, and trials enrolling intermediate- or high-risk patients without disaggregated low-risk subsets were excluded. The study adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.

### Data Extraction and Quality Assessment
Two physician reviewers independently evaluated eligibility, appraised methodological quality, and extracted data using standardized data extraction templates. Any discrepancies were resolved by consensus discussion. Data elements extracted included:
* Study design, publication year, sample size, and follow-up duration.
* Baseline patient demographics, functional status, and mean STS-PROM score.
* Transcatheter and surgical valve types and procedural approaches.
* Primary composite endpoints and disaggregated 1-year clinical event counts.

Study methodological quality and risk of bias were evaluated using version 2 of the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2), assessing randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result.

### Clinical Outcomes and Definitions
* **Primary Outcome:** All-cause death at 1 year.
* **Secondary Outcomes:**
* Cardiovascular death
* Stroke (all stroke and disabling stroke)
* Myocardial infarction (MI)
* Valve- or heart failure-related rehospitalization
* New-onset or worsening atrial fibrillation
* Permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation
* Major vascular complications
* Life-threatening or disabling bleeding
* Acute kidney injury (AKI) stage 2 or 3
* Infective endocarditis
* Aortic valve reintervention
* Moderate or severe paravalvular leak (PVL)
* New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class ≥ II

### Statistical Analysis
Pooled risk ratios (RR) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) for dichotomous clinical outcomes were calculated using the random-effects models of DerSimonian and Laird. Between-study statistical heterogeneity was quantified using the Higgins I² statistic:
* I² < 25% indicated low heterogeneity.
* I² = 25%–75% indicated moderate heterogeneity.
* I² > 75% indicated substantial heterogeneity.

Publication bias was assessed qualitatively through visual inspection of funnel plot asymmetry and formally quantified using Egger's linear regression test. Sensitivity analyses were performed using fixed-effect models (Mantel-Haenszel method). An additional pre-specified sensitivity analysis was conducted by excluding the post hoc low-risk subgroup of the SURTAVI trial (STS-PROM < 3%) to evaluate the consistency of results across purely de novo low-risk randomized trials.

All statistical tests were two-tailed, with a p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Analyses were conducted using Review Manager (RevMan) Version 5.3 (The Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, Denmark) and Meta-Essentials Version 1.4.

## Results</summary><div class="details-content">

### Search Results and Study Characteristics
The initial database search yielded 578 citations. After removing 90 duplicate entries, 488 records were screened at the title and abstract level, resulting in the exclusion of 479 records (observational registries, non-comparative series, review articles, and trials without low-risk cohorts). Nine full-text articles were retrieved for detailed evaluation, of which 4 randomized studies fulfilled all inclusion criteria:
1. **NOTION (2015):** All-comers randomized trial (N = 280; 145 TAVR vs 135 SAVR).
2. **SURTAVI Low-Risk Post Hoc Subgroup (2018):** Subgroup of patients with STS-PROM < 3% (N = 254; 131 TAVR vs 123 SAVR).
3. **PARTNER 3 (2019):** Low-risk randomized trial (N = 950 analyzed at 1 year; 496 TAVR vs 454 SAVR).
4. **Evolut Low Risk (2019):** Low-risk randomized trial (N = 1403 evaluated at 1 year; 725 TAVR vs 678 SAVR).

In total, the meta-analysis included 2887 randomized patients (1497 assigned to TAVR and 1390 assigned to SAVR).

### Primary and Secondary Clinical Outcomes at 1 Year

#### All-Cause and Cardiovascular Mortality
At 1-year follow-up, TAVR was associated with a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality compared with SAVR:
* **All-Cause Death:** 2.1% (31 / 1497) in TAVR vs 3.5% (48 / 1390) in SAVR (pooled RR: 0.61; 95% CI: 0.39–0.96; p = 0.03; I² = 0%).
* **Cardiovascular Death:** 1.6% (24 / 1497) in TAVR vs 2.9% (41 / 1390) in SAVR (pooled RR: 0.55; 95% CI: 0.33–0.90; p = 0.02; I² = 0%).

Both mortality endpoints exhibited complete homogeneity across studies (I² = 0%, τ² = 0.00), confirming that the survival benefit of TAVR was consistent across trials and valve platforms.

### Subgroup and Sensitivity Analyses
* **Fixed-Effect Model Analysis:** Yielded results virtually identical to the primary random-effects analysis across all primary and secondary clinical endpoints.
* **Exclusion of SURTAVI Post Hoc Subgroup:**
* When excluding the SURTAVI low-risk cohort (N = 254), all-cause mortality remained numerically lower with TAVR (2.0% vs 3.1%; RR: 0.66; 95% CI: 0.41–1.06; p = 0.09; I² = 0%). While the point estimate remained largely unchanged (0.66 vs 0.61), statistical significance was attenuated due to reduced statistical power.
* Rehospitalization for valve or heart failure became statistically significantly lower with TAVR versus SAVR (RR: 0.59; 95% CI: 0.43–0.81; p = 0.001; I² = 0%), resolving the moderate heterogeneity observed in the primary model.

### Publication Bias and Quality Appraisal
Methodological assessment using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool established a low risk of overall bias across 3 trials (NOTION, PARTNER 3, and Evolut Low Risk) and some concerns for 1 trial (the post hoc subgroup analysis of SURTAVI).

Visual inspection of funnel plots revealed minor asymmetry across selected procedural outcomes. However, formal mathematical assessment using Egger's linear regression test confirmed no evidence of publication bias across clinical outcomes, with the single exception of major vascular complications (p = 0.025).

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

### Interpretation of Mortality and Morbidity Benefits
In this comprehensive meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing TAVR versus SAVR in low-surgical-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis, TAVR was associated with a statistically significant 39% relative reduction in all-cause mortality (2.1% vs 3.5%) and a 45% relative reduction in cardiovascular mortality (1.6% vs 2.9%) at 1 year. To our knowledge, this represents the first systematic meta-analysis demonstrating an isolated survival advantage for TAVR in this patient population.

The observed 1-year survival advantage is biologically and clinically plausible, driven by several interrelated mechanisms:
1. **Less Invasive Nature and Physiological Sparing:** Transfemoral TAVR avoids median sternotomy, cardiopulmonary bypass, cardioplegic arrest, and surgical root dissection. This eliminates systemic inflammatory activation and ischemia-reperfusion injury.
2. **Accelerated Recovery and Mobilization:** Patients undergoing TAVR experience shorter intensive care unit stays, faster post-procedural mobilization, and expedited return to independent baseline activity, which prevents deconditioning in elderly populations.
3. **Drastic Reductions in Perioperative Insults:**
* *Atrial Fibrillation:* SAVR was associated with an absolute incidence of nearly 40% (39.4%) of new-onset or worsening atrial fibrillation, compared with only 10.0% with TAVR. New-onset post-cardiotomy atrial fibrillation is known to trigger acute hemodynamic decompensation, prolong hospitalization, and increase late thromboembolic and stroke risks.
* *Major Bleeding:* Life-threatening or disabling bleeding was reduced threefold (3.9% vs 11.2%) with TAVR, avoiding extensive blood product transfusions that are recognized independent predictors of late mortality.
* *Acute Kidney Injury:* Severe AKI (stage 2/3) was reduced by 74% (0.7% vs 2.9%) with TAVR. Cardiopulmonary bypass-induced hypoperfusion and hemolysis during SAVR predispose to acute tubular necrosis, carrying lasting prognostic consequences.

### Pacemaker Implantation and Paravalvular Leak Considerations
Despite the clear survival advantage, TAVR was associated with significantly higher rates of permanent pacemaker implantation (17.4% vs 5.5%) and moderate or severe paravalvular regurgitation (3.6% vs 1.7%) at 1 year.

The elevated pacemaker risk reflects mechanical compression of the conduction system within the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT), where the atrioventricular conduction axis passes along the membranous septum close to the aortic annular plane. Conduction injury varies substantially by valve type:
* **Self-Expanding vs Balloon-Expandable Systems:** Three of the four included trials utilized self-expanding prostheses. In NOTION, which used the first-generation CoreValve, the 1-year PPM rate was 38.0%. In contrast, in PARTNER 3, which utilized the balloon-expandable Sapien 3 valve, PPM rates were low and non-significantly different from surgery (7.3% vs 5.4%).
* **Paravalvular Leak:** Residual PVL was also predominantly driven by older-generation self-expanding devices lacking dedicated outer sealing skirts. In PARTNER 3, moderate or severe PVL occurred in only 0.6% with TAVR versus 0.5% with SAVR.
* **Prognostic Impact:** While mild paravalvular regurgitation is well-tolerated, moderate-to-severe PVL promotes left ventricular volume overload, eccentric hypertrophy, and excess late mortality. Modern transcatheter valve iterations with adaptive polyethylene terephthalate (PET) or pericardial sealing cuffs and pre-procedural multidetector CT annular sizing have markedly curtailed PVL in contemporary practice.

### Anatomic Restrictions and Patient Selection
A critical finding of this meta-analysis is that trial outcomes cannot be indiscriminately generalized to all low-risk individuals with aortic stenosis. The landmark trials enforced rigorous anatomic exclusion criteria:
* **Congenital Bicuspid or Unicuspid Anatomy:** Bicuspid aortic valves (BAV) represent over 50% of surgical AVR cases in patients younger than 65 years. BAV was excluded from PARTNER 3 and Evolut Low Risk due to asymmetric annular geometry, heavy raphe calcification, risk of elliptical valve expansion, higher paravalvular leak, and potential aortic root trauma.
* **Concomitant Valve Disease:** Patients with severe aortic, mitral, or tricuspid regurgitation, or significant mitral stenosis were systematically excluded. In patients with multi-valvular disease, SAVR with concomitant surgical valve repair/replacement remains standard.
* **Coronary Artery Disease:** Patients with extensive coronary artery disease (SYNTAX score > 22 or unprotected left main bifurcation stenosis) were excluded. In such cases, SAVR with concomitant CABG offers superior revascularization completeness.
* **Age Demographics:** Although strict chronologic age limits were not applied, patients were required to be candidates for bioprosthetic replacement. Consequently, the mean cohort age across trials was 75.4 years. In younger individuals (< 65 years), mechanical valve prostheses or the Ross procedure remain vital considerations to avert repeated reinterventions.

### Bioprosthetic Durability and Structural Valve Deterioration (SVD)
The long-term durability of transcatheter heart valves remains the principal unanswered question in lower-risk cohorts who possess longer life expectancies. Several registry and randomized datasets provide reassuring midterm data:
* **UK TAVI Registry:** Among 241 patients followed between 5 and 10 years (median 5.8 years), moderate and severe structural valve deterioration occurred in 8.7% and 0.4%, respectively.
* **FRANCE-2 Registry:** In 4,201 patients evaluated through 5 years, moderate/severe SVD was observed in 13.3% and severe SVD in 2.5%.
* **NOTION Trial (6-Year Follow-up):** The NOTION trial reported lower rates of moderate/severe structural valve deterioration with TAVR than with SAVR (4.8% vs 24.0%; p < 0.001), primarily attributable to larger effective orifice areas and lower transvalvular mean gradients with transcatheter supra-annular designs.

Both the PARTNER 3 and Evolut Low Risk trials incorporate mandated 10-year clinical and echocardiographic follow-up protocols, which will deliver definitive comparative evidence regarding long-term structural durability.

### Study Limitations
1. **Study-Level Data:** This meta-analysis was conducted using aggregated study-level data rather than individual patient-level data, precluding multivariable patient-level time-to-event and competing risk modeling.
2. **Follow-Up Duration:** Analysis was restricted to 1-year clinical endpoints to harmonize across all trials. Long-term comparative outcomes beyond 5 to 10 years remain pending for low-risk cohorts.
3. **Bayesian Event Derivation in Evolut Low Risk:** Event counts in the Evolut Low Risk trial were derived from estimated incidences calculated through Bayesian Markov models rather than purely observed crude counts.
4. **Limited Number of Studies:** Because only 4 trials met inclusion criteria, meta-regression analyses to explore the independent effect of prosthesis type (self-expanding vs balloon-expandable) or chronologic age could not be performed.

## Conclusions and Clinical Perspectives</summary><div class="details-content">

### Conclusions
In this systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing TAVR versus SAVR in low-surgical-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis, TAVR was associated with a statistically significant 39% relative reduction in all-cause mortality and a 45% relative reduction in cardiovascular mortality at 1 year. Furthermore, TAVR resulted in substantial reductions in new-onset atrial fibrillation, life-threatening or disabling bleeding, and stage 2/3 acute kidney injury. Conversely, TAVR carried higher rates of permanent pacemaker implantation and moderate or severe paravalvular leak.

These findings suggest that transfemoral TAVR should be regarded as a preferred therapeutic modality over SAVR in patients aged ≥ 70–75 years with severe calcific aortic stenosis who are at low operative risk, possess suitable transfemoral vascular anatomy, and meet the anatomical criteria established in pivotal randomized trials.

### Competency in Patient Care and Procedural Skills
* **Multidisciplinary Heart Team Evaluation:** Clinical decision-making for low-risk patients with severe AS must involve a collaborative Heart Team (interventional cardiologists, cardiothoracic surgeons, advanced imaging specialists, and anesthesiologists).
* **Tailored Risk-Benefit Profiling:** The survival advantage, accelerated recovery, and lower procedural morbidity of TAVR must be weighed against patient-specific factors, including annular calcium distribution, risk of conduction disturbance, life expectancy, and lifetime management requirements (coronary access, future valve-in-valve reinterventions).
* **Pre-Procedural Imaging Quality:** Dedicated high-resolution ECG-gated multidetector cardiac CT is mandatory to confirm absence of bicuspid anatomy, evaluate coronary ostial heights, quantify annular and LVOT dimensions, and assess iliofemoral vascular caliber and tortuosity.

### Translational Outlook and Future Directions
* **Long-Term Durability Surveillance:** Complete 10-year follow-up of PARTNER 3 and Evolut Low Risk cohorts will determine whether bioprosthetic hemodynamic advantages translate into equal or superior long-term freedom from structural valve deterioration.
* **Expansion to Anatomically Complex Subsets:** Dedicated prospective trials are actively investigating TAVR in bicuspid aortic valve morphology, asymptomatic severe AS with preserved ventricular function (e.g., the EARLY TAVR trial), and moderate AS complicated by left ventricular systolic dysfunction.
* **Coronary Re-Access and TAV-in-TAV Strategies:** As TAVR is deployed in younger and lower-risk patients, developing standardized techniques for commissural alignment during initial valve deployment and establishing lifetime strategies for subsequent repeat transcatheter valve-in-valve procedures represent paramount priorities.
