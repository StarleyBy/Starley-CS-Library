## Percutaneous Coronary Angioplasty versus Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting in Treatment of Unprotected Left Main Stenosis (NOBLE): A Prospective, Randomised, Open‑Label, Non‑Inferiority Trial

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Background
Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is the standard treatment for left main coronary artery disease, but percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is increasingly used. The NOBLE trial compared PCI and CABG in patients with left main stenosis.

### Methods
Prospective, randomised, open‑label, non‑inferiority trial at 36 centres in northern Europe. Patients with left main disease (stable angina, unstable angina, or NSTEMI) were randomised 1:1 to PCI or CABG. Primary endpoint: major adverse cardiac or cerebrovascular events (MACCE) – composite of all‑cause mortality, non‑procedural myocardial infarction, repeat revascularisation, and stroke. Non‑inferiority limit: HR 1.35 (upper bound of 95% CI).

### Findings
1201 patients randomised (PCI n=598, CABG n=603); 592 per group analysed. 5‑year MACCE: PCI 28% (121 events) vs. CABG 18% (80 events); HR 1.51 (95% CI 1.13–2.00) – exceeded non‑inferiority limit; CABG superior (p=0.0044). All‑cause mortality: PCI 11% vs. CABG 9% (HR 1.08, p=0.84). Non‑procedural MI: PCI 6% vs. CABG 2% (HR 2.87, p=0.0040). Repeat revascularisation: PCI 15% vs. CABG 10% (HR 1.50, p=0.0304). Stroke: PCI 5% vs. CABG 2% (HR 2.20, p=0.08).

### Interpretation
CABG was superior to PCI for the composite MACCE endpoint. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural myocardial infarction and repeat revascularisation. These findings suggest CABG may be the better treatment for left main coronary artery disease.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

Treatment of unprotected left main coronary artery disease with PCI has increased rapidly following favourable results from randomised trials and registries. Current guidelines recommend PCI in patients with left main disease and favourable coronary anatomy, based largely on the SYNTAX trial and smaller trials (LE MANS, PRECOMBAT, Boudriot). However, these trials had wide non‑inferiority margins and limited power. The NOBLE trial was designed to compare PCI with drug‑eluting stents versus CABG in a larger, adequately powered randomised trial.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Methods</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Design</summary><div class="details-content">

Prospective, randomised, open‑label, non‑inferiority trial conducted at 36 centres in Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Latvia, Lithuania, Norway, Sweden, and the UK. The study was approved by national ethics committees and all patients provided written informed consent.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Participants</summary><div class="details-content">

Inclusion: stable angina, unstable angina, or NSTEMI; significant left main stenosis (>50% or FFR ≤0.80); ≤3 additional non‑complex lesions. Exclusion: STEMI within 24 h; too high risk for either procedure; expected survival <1 year.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Randomisation and Masking</summary><div class="details-content">

Patients suitable for either treatment were randomised 1:1 via web‑based system, stratified by sex, distal LM bifurcation, and diabetes. The study was not blinded; treating physicians could over‑rule assignment if ineligible or patient refusal.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Procedures</summary><div class="details-content">

Complete revascularisation was the goal. PCI: mandatory drug‑eluting stents (first 73 patients received first‑generation DES; subsequently the biolimus‑eluting Biomatrix Flex became the study stent). High‑pressure post‑dilatation recommended; IVUS strongly recommended. Distal bifurcation lesions could be treated with two‑stent techniques (culotte preferred). CABG: left internal mammary artery to LAD recommended; other grafts as per local practice.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Outcomes</summary><div class="details-content">

Primary endpoint: MACCE at up to 5 years (all‑cause mortality, non‑procedural myocardial infarction, repeat revascularisation, stroke). Secondary endpoints: individual components, stent thrombosis, graft occlusion. Independent clinical events committee adjudicated all events. SYNTAX score assessed by core laboratory.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Statistical Analysis</summary><div class="details-content">

Sample size: 275 events required for non‑inferiority (HR limit 1.35). Primary endpoint assessed when events reached (originally planned at 2 years, later changed to median 3 years due to low event rates, and finally at 5 years). Kaplan‑Meier estimates, Cox regression, intention‑to‑treat. Subgroup analyses by SYNTAX score tertile.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Results</summary><div class="details-content">

### Patients</summary><div class="details-content">

1201 patients enrolled (PCI n=598, CABG n=603). 14 withdrew consent, 3 lost to follow‑up; 592 per group analysed ([Fig. 1](#fig-1)). Baseline characteristics: mean age 66.2 years; 20–24% women; 15% diabetes; 82–83% stable angina. SYNTAX score 22.4 vs. 22.3. Median follow‑up 3.1 years (IQR 2.0–5.0); 69% of total planned follow‑up completed.

## Discussion</summary><div class="details-content">

The NOBLE trial is one of the largest randomised comparisons of PCI vs. CABG for left main disease. CABG was superior to PCI for the composite MACCE endpoint. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural MI and repeat revascularisation. Stroke rates were not significantly different (PCI numerically higher at 5 years). The SYNTAX score did not identify a subgroup where PCI was clearly advantageous; even low‑score patients had worse outcomes with PCI. This contrasts with the SYNTAX trial, where low‑score patients had similar outcomes.

The increased non‑procedural MI after PCI may reflect target‑lesion and de‑novo lesion events, while CABG provides protection by bypassing long segments. Repeat revascularisation was more than 50% higher with PCI, mainly driven by de‑novo lesions. Stroke rates were low; early stroke was higher with CABG, but late strokes accumulated in the PCI group after discontinuation of dual antiplatelet therapy, though this may be chance.

The 30‑day outcomes highlight the trade‑offs: PCI offers shorter hospital stay, less bleeding, and fewer reoperations, but CABG provides superior long‑term event reduction. These findings support individualised decision‑making by a heart team.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Study Limitations</summary><div class="details-content">

- Primary endpoint assessment changed from 2‑year to 5‑year due to low event rates.
- Open‑label design may introduce bias.
- SYNTAX score subgroups were small and not powered for definitive conclusions.
- A small proportion of patients received first‑generation DES; newer stents may improve PCI outcomes.
- IVUS use was heterogeneous.
- The study excluded clinically unstable patients.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

CABG provided better 5‑year clinical outcomes than PCI for left main coronary artery disease. Mortality was similar, but PCI had higher non‑procedural myocardial infarction and repeat revascularisation. These findings suggest that CABG may be the preferred treatment for most patients with left main disease, with PCI reserved for those with limited life expectancy or high surgical risk.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Clinical Perspective</summary><div class="details-content">

The NOBLE trial provides robust evidence that CABG is superior to PCI for the composite endpoint of MACCE at 5 years in left main disease. The trade‑offs are clear: PCI offers similar mortality, shorter hospital stay, less bleeding, and fewer sternal infections, but higher non‑procedural MI and repeat revascularisation. CABG offers lower MI and revascularisation but higher periprocedural morbidity. The SYNTAX score did not identify a subgroup where PCI was clearly advantageous; even low‑score patients had worse outcomes with PCI. This challenges the current European guideline recommendation (class IA for PCI in SYNTAX <23). A heart team approach and shared decision‑making are essential.

</div></details>
