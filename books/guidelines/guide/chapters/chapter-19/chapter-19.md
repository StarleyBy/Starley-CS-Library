## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Principles of Surgery-Based Intraoperative Transesophageal Echocardiography

Intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) is an indispensable diagnostic and monitoring modality in modern cardiovascular surgery. It transforms perioperative care from passive observational hemodynamic monitoring into active, real-time surgical guidance across the full continuum of open, minimally invasive, robotic, and hybrid cardiovascular procedures:

1. **The Dual Phases of Intraoperative TEE:**
   - **Pre-Cardiopulmonary Bypass (Pre-CPB) Evaluation:** Establishes baseline cardiac anatomy and hemodynamics under general anesthesia, confirms or refines the preoperative diagnosis, identifies unexpected secondary or incidental pathology (e.g., patent foramen ovale, ascending aortic atheroma, unsuspected valve regurgitation), determines surgical feasibility (e.g., valve repairability vs replacement), and guides vascular cannulation strategies.
   - **Post-Cardiopulmonary Bypass (Post-CPB) Evaluation:** Assesses immediate surgical adequacy (residual regurgitation or stenosis, prosthetic valve seating, ventricular assist device cannula position, de-airing thoroughness), monitors regional and global biventricular performance during weaning from extracorporeal circulation, detects iatrogenic surgical complications (circumflex coronary artery distortion, aortic dissection, paravalvular leak, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction), and directs post-bypass pharmacotherapy and mechanical support.

2. **Surgery-Specific Focus:**
   - Unlike outpatient diagnostic imaging, perioperative TEE is strictly goal-directed and procedure-specific. Echocardiographers must thoroughly master each technical surgical step, cannulation route, clamping sequence, and potential mechanical failure mode.
   - Rapid and unambiguous communication between the echocardiographer, cardiac surgeon, and perfusionist is mandatory to ensure patient safety.

3. **Comprehensive Coverage Across 11 Surgical Domains:**
   - This guideline provides structured protocols for: (1) Mitral Valve Surgery, (2) Aortic Valve Surgery, (3) Tricuspid Valve Surgery, (4) Pulmonary Valve Surgery, (5) Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG), (6) Thoracic Aortic Surgery, (7) Mechanical Circulatory Support (LVAD, RVAD, BiVAD, TAH, ECMO, IABP), (8) Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (Septal Myectomy), (9) Orthotopic Heart Transplantation, (10) Lung Transplantation, and (11) Pericardial Diseases and Intracardiac Masses.

## 1. Background and Rationale</summary><div class="details-content">

Intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) has evolved from an investigational tool into an essential clinical component of modern cardiac surgery. Multiple societal practice guidelines (ASE, SCA, ASA, ACC, AHA, STS) assign Class I recommendations for routine TEE in all adult open cardiac and thoracic aortic procedures, and in catheter-based structural heart interventions.

The operating room environment presents unique diagnostic conditions that distinguish intraoperative TEE from outpatient examinations:
- General anesthesia, mechanical positive-pressure ventilation, neuromuscular blockade, and altered loading conditions (hypovolemia, vasodilation, myocardial stunning) profoundly impact cardiac chamber dimensions and Doppler hemodynamics.
- The echocardiographer must reconcile preoperative outpatient findings with the immediate intraoperative state, identifying dynamic shifts in valvular regurgitation, loading-dependent outflow gradients, and right ventricular functional reserve.
- Surgical intervention alters cardiac geometry instantaneously. Post-CPB evaluation requires targeted interrogation for specific iatrogenic risks, paravalvular leaks, residual defects, myocardial ischemia, and adequate de-airing before chest closure.

This document provides a comprehensive, surgery-based roadmap detailing standardized preprocedure and postprocedure imaging strategies for each cardiovascular operative category.

## 2. General Principles & Right Ventricular Function Assessment</summary><div class="details-content">

### Baseline Standard Examination
A comprehensive baseline TEE examination should precede cardiopulmonary bypass in every patient, systematically evaluating the left ventricle, right ventricle, left atrium, left atrial appendage, interatrial septum, all four cardiac valves, thoracic aorta, and pulmonary veins, unless contraindicated by severe esophageal pathology.

### Assessment of Right Ventricular Function by TEE
Right ventricular (RV) failure is a catastrophic cause of post-bypass morbidity and mortality across cardiac surgery, particularly in mitral valve surgery, heart transplantation, and mechanical circulatory support. Because the complex crescentic geometry of the RV precludes single-plane volumetric formulas, a multi-parametric quantitative assessment is mandatory.

## 3. Valve Surgery</summary><div class="details-content">

### 3.1. Mitral Valve: Preprocedure Assessment & SAM Risk</summary><div class="details-content">

Pre-CPB evaluation of the mitral valve (MV) establishes the etiology (degenerative, functional, ischemic, rheumatic, endocarditis), mechanism according to Carpentier's classification (Type I: normal leaflet motion; Type II: leaflet prolapse/flail; Type IIIa: restricted in diastole/systole; Type IIIb: restricted in systole), and repairability.
- **3D TEE "Surgical View":** Real-time 3D volume rendering from the left atrial perspective ("surgeon's view" with aorta at 12 o'clock, left atrial appendage at 9 o'clock) maps individual scallop pathology (A1, A2, A3; P1, P2, P3; anterolateral and posteromedial commissures).

*Figure 1: 3D surgical en face view of the mitral valve from the left atrial perspective. (A) Standard orientation with the aortic valve at 12 o'clock, left atrial appendage at 9 o'clock, showing anterior leaflet scallops (A1, A2, A3) and posterior leaflet scallops (P1, P2, P3). (B) Color flow Doppler mapping demonstrating eccentric regurgitant jet trajectory corresponding to P2 prolapse.*

#### Prediction of Post-Repair Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM)
Systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the anterior mitral leaflet produces acute dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction and severe recurrent MR after ring annuloplasty. Pre-CPB geometric risk factors must be measured in the ME LVOT / LAX view:
1. **Ratio of Anterior to Posterior Leaflet Length ($AL/PL$):** Ratio $< 1.3$ indicates an elongated posterior leaflet that pushes the coaptation line anteriorly into the LVOT.
2. **Coaptation-to-Septum Distance ($C\text{--}Sept$):** Distance $< 25\text{ mm}$ strongly predisposes to SAM.
3. **Basal Septal Thickness:** Asymmetric septal bulge ($> 15\text{ mm}$) narrows the LVOT entry angle.
4. **Aorto-Mitral Angle:** Acute angle ($< 120^\circ$) increases drag forces on the anterior leaflet.

*Figure 2: Echocardiographic measurements for predicting the risk of systolic anterior motion (SAM) after mitral valve repair. ME long-axis view showing measurement of coaptation-to-septum distance (C-Sept), anterior leaflet length (AL), posterior leaflet length (PL), and basal interventricular septal thickness.*

### 3.1. Mitral Valve: Post-Surgical Assessment & Minimally Invasive</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Minimally Invasive and Robotic Mitral Surgery
Minimally invasive and robotic approaches require unique TEE guidance:
- **Venous Cannulation:** Transesophageal guidance of bicaval or femoral venous cannulas into the SVC-RA and IVC-RA junctions.
- **Endoaortic Balloon (Endoclamp):** Real-time tracking of the endoaortic balloon catheter advanced from the femoral artery into the ascending aorta ($2\text{--}3\text{ cm}$ above the sinotubular junction and proximal to the brachiocephalic takeoff). Color Doppler verifies complete aortic occlusion and absence of balloon migration or dissection.

#### Table 3: Key Points for Post-Surgical Assessment in Mitral Valve Surgery

| Category | Specific TEE Checklist Items |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adequacy of Repair** | • Confirm coaptation length $\ge 5\text{--}8\text{ mm}$ in ME 4Ch and LAX views.<br>• Exclude residual MR: any residual MR greater than mild ($> 1+$) warrants immediate surgeon notification.<br>• Rule out iatrogenic mitral stenosis: transmitral mean gradient must be $< 5\text{ mmHg}$ at physiological heart rates; effective orifice area $> 1.5\text{--}1.8\text{ cm}^2$. |

| **Prosthetic Valve Seating** | • In MV replacement: verify normal leaflet opening/closing arcs; exclude leaflet impingement by preserved subvalvular apparatus; quantify transvalvular vs paravalvular leak (PVL). |

### 3.2. Aortic Valve: Pre-Surgical Root Sizing & Mechanism</summary><div class="details-content">

Aortic valve evaluation requires precise anatomical measurements in the ME aortic valve LAX view ($110^\circ\text{--}130^\circ$) at end-diastole (or mid-systole for annulus):
1. **Aortic Annulus (hinge-to-hinge diameter in mid-systole)**
2. **Sinuses of Valsalva diameter**
3. **Sinotubular Junction (STJ) diameter**
4. **Ascending Aorta diameter ($2\text{--}3\text{ cm}$ above STJ)**
5. **Distance from Annulus to Coronary Ostia (left and right coronary arteries)**

## 6. Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS)</summary><div class="details-content">
<details class="med-details"><summary>
### 6.1. Left Ventricular Assist Device (LVAD): Pre- and Post-Implantation</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Pre-LVAD Echocardiographic Strategy
Pre-implantation TEE identifies pathological conditions that cause catastrophic LVAD malfunction:
1. **Aortic Regurgitation (AR):** Even mild pre-existing AR deteriorates significantly under continuous continuous-flow LVAD unloading. Left ventricular decompression combined with elevated aortic root pressure causes continuous recirculating regurgitant volume loop, rendering the LVAD ineffective. *Moderate or severe AR mandates concomitant aortic valve closure or replacement.*
2. **Right Ventricular Dysfunction:** Post-LVAD RV failure occurs in $20\text{--}30\%$ of cases due to sudden increase in venous return and leftward septal shift. Measure RV fractional area change (FAC $< 20\%$ indicates severe dysfunction), TAPSE, and tricuspid annular diameter.
3. **Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) / ASD:** Unloading the LV reduces LA pressure below RA pressure. A patent PFO causes immediate severe right-to-left shunting and refractory systemic hypoxemia upon LVAD activation. *Mandatory pre-CPB agitated saline bubble study; any PFO must be surgically closed.*
4. **Aortic Valve Opening:** Assess baseline AV leaflet excursion.

#### Post-LVAD Echocardiographic Strategy
Immediately following separation from CPB, TEE guides speed titration and position verification:
1. **Inflow Cannula Alignment:** In the deep transgastric or ME 4Ch view, verify that the LV apex inflow cannula is directed strictly parallel to the interventricular septum toward the mitral orifice. Angle deviations cause cannula suction against the septal or lateral wall ("suction events").
2. **Septal Position:** Interventricular septum must remain in a neutral or slightly left-convex position throughout the cardiac cycle. Extreme leftward septal shift indicates LV over-pumping, leading to RV geometric distortion and acute RV failure.
3. **Outflow Graft Patency:** In the ME ascending aorta view, interrogate the end-to-side outflow graft anastomosis with color and pulsed-wave Doppler (normal peak velocity $1.0\text{--}2.0\text{ m/s}$). Exclude graft kinking, thrombus, or anastomotic stenosis.

| Phase | Critical Echocardiographic Checkpoints |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-LVAD** | • Exclude PFO/ASD (mandatory bubble study).<br>• Quantify AR (any $\ge$ moderate AR requires surgical intervention).<br>• Assess RV size and function (FAC, TAPSE, free wall strain) to predict post-LVAD right heart failure.<br>• Exclude LV apical thrombus (dislodgement risk upon coring). |
| **Post-LVAD** | • Inflow cannula alignment towards MV center without septal/wall suction.<br>• Septal position in midline (neutral contour).<br>• Verify outflow graft Doppler flow profile and exclude kinking.<br>• Monitor RV contractility, TR severity, and de-airing. |

<details class="med-details"><summary>
### 6.3. Total Artificial Heart (TAH) & Biventricular Assist Devices (BiVAD)</summary><div class="details-content">

Total artificial heart (TAH) and biventricular assist device (BiVAD) support represent extreme salvage strategies for end-stage biventricular failure:
- **Exclusion of Remnant Shunts:** Both atrial walls are retained. TEE must verify complete absence of residual interatrial defects across the remnant atrial cuffs to prevent massive right-to-left or left-to-right shunts.
- **Atrial Inflow Anastomoses:** Interrogate systemic venous and pulmonary venous returns. Verify that right atrial cannula/cuff does not obstruct the IVC or SVC, and that left atrial anastomotic cuff does not compress pulmonary venous ostia (normal laminar low-velocity inflow $< 1.0\text{ m/s}$).
- **Outflow Grafts:** Interrogate the ascending aortic and main pulmonary artery prosthetic conduits with color Doppler to rule out anastomotic stenosis, graft torsion, or perigraft hematoma compressing adjacent structures.

#### Table 14: Key Points for Imaging in Total Artificial Heart (TAH) Placement

| Component | Specific TEE Checklist Items |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Atrial Remnants** | • Exclude residual interatrial communication across the atrial septum remnant.<br>• Confirm unrestricted pulmonary venous drainage into the left atrial remnant (PWD peak velocity $< 1.0\text{ m/s}$).<br>• Verify unobstructed SVC and IVC drainage into the right atrial remnant. |
| **Outflow Grafts** | • Check aortic and pulmonary artery graft anastomoses for kinking, thrombus, or high-velocity turbulent jets ($> 2.5\text{ m/s}$). |
| **Chamber De-airing** | • Exhaustive de-airing of both artificial ventricles and atrial cuffs prior to cross-clamp removal and full pumping. |

## 12. Summary and Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

Transesophageal echocardiography has become an indispensable extension of the cardiovascular surgical team. Adopting a structured, surgery-based protocol guarantees standardized, reproducible, and rigorous assessment across all phases of the operative procedure.

By executing a systematic pre-CPB scan, communicating dynamic findings to surgeons, optimizing cardiopulmonary bypass weaning, immediately verifying repair adequacy, and proactively intercepting iatrogenic mechanical complications, perioperative echocardiographers directly safeguard patient outcomes across the entire spectrum of modern cardiovascular surgery.

</div></details>
