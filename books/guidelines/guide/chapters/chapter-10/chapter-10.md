## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Central Paradigm and Scope
* **Multidisciplinary cardio-oncology integration:** Cancer treatments have significantly improved survival across all malignancies, but cancer therapy-related cardiovascular toxicity (CTR-CVT) represents a major cause of morbidity and mortality. Cardio-oncology aims to facilitate optimal cancer therapy without unnecessary interruption while proactively identifying, preventing, and managing cardiovascular (CV) complications throughout the patient journey: baseline, active treatment, post-treatment, and survivorship.
* **Proactive Baseline Risk Stratification:** Baseline risk assessment using the Heart Failure Association–International Cardio-Oncology Society (HFA-ICOS) risk stratification tool categorizes patients scheduled for potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapy into low, moderate, high, and very high risk before initiating treatment.
* **Low-Risk Pathway:** Patients at low baseline risk should proceed to cancer therapy immediately without delay and undergo standard routine surveillance.
* **High- and Very High-Risk Pathway:** High- and very high-risk patients require prompt cardiology/cardio-oncology referral for specialized evaluation, cardioprotective therapy optimization, and multidisciplinary risk/benefit assessment to avoid therapy cancellation or unnecessary postponement.

### Uniform Definitions of Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiovascular Toxicity (CTR-CVT)
* **Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiac Dysfunction (CTRCD):**
  * **Symptomatic CTRCD:** Categorized as very severe (requiring inotropic/mechanical circulatory support or transplant evaluation), severe (requiring HF hospitalization), moderate (requiring outpatient intensification of diuretic/HF therapy), or mild (mild symptoms without intensification of therapy).
  * **Asymptomatic CTRCD:** Categorized into severe (new LVEF reduction to $< 40\%$), moderate (new LVEF reduction by $\ge 10$ percentage points to LVEF $40	ext{--}49\%$, OR new LVEF drop $< 10$ points to $40	ext{--}49\%$ with either new relative GLS decline $> 15\%$ or new rise in cardiac biomarkers), and mild (LVEF remains $\ge 50\%$ with new relative GLS decline $> 15\%$ from baseline and/or new elevation in cardiac troponin or natriuretic peptides above upper limit of normal [ULN]).
* **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) Myocarditis:** Defined pathohistologically (endomyocardial biopsy showing multifocal lymphocytic infiltrates with overt cardiomyocyte loss) or clinically by elevated cardiac troponin with 1 major criterion (CMR diagnostic for acute myocarditis by modified Lake Louise criteria) or 2 minor criteria (clinical syndrome, ventricular arrhythmia/conduction delay, LV systolic dysfunction, other immune-related adverse events such as myositis/myasthenia gravis, or suggestive CMR), after excluding acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and infectious myocarditis. Classified as fulminant, non-fulminant, or steroid-refractory.
* **Vascular Toxicity:** Spans arterial hypertension, acute coronary syndromes (ACS), chronic coronary syndromes (CCS), stroke/TIA, peripheral artery disease (PAD), vasospasm/Raynaud phenomenon, venous thromboembolism (DVT and pulmonary embolism), and arterial thrombosis.
* **Arrhythmias and Repolarization:** Corrected QT (QTcF) prolongation (marked if $> 500\ 	ext{ms}$ or prolongation by $> 60\ 	ext{ms}$), atrial fibrillation/flutter (AF), ventricular arrhythmias, and conduction system disease.

### Baseline Diagnostic Modalities and Thresholds
* **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Recommended in all cancer patients prior to starting therapy. QTcF (Fridericia correction: $QTcF = QT / \sqrt[3]{RR}$) is the gold standard; baseline $QTcF > 450\ 	ext{ms}$ in men or $> 460\ 	ext{ms}$ in women warrants investigation of electrolyte and drug interactions.
* **Serum Biomarkers:** Cardiac troponin (cTnI or cTnT, preferably high-sensitivity assays) and natriuretic peptides (BNP or NT-proBNP) provide key prognostic baseline values. Baseline elevations identify subclinical myocardial injury or hemodynamic stress, mandating closer surveillance.
* **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line imaging modality. Three-dimensional (3D) TTE is preferred for LVEF and volume quantification due to lower intra- and inter-observer variability. When 3D is unavailable, biplane Simpson method is recommended. Global longitudinal strain (GLS) using speckle tracking should be measured at baseline; a relative decline of $> 15\%$ from baseline signifies subclinical CTRCD even with preserved LVEF.
* **Cardiac Magnetic Resonance (CMR):** Gold standard for tissue characterization (T1/T2 mapping, edema, extracellular volume, and late gadolinium enhancement [LGE]) and reference volumetric assessment when echocardiography is non-diagnostic or suboptimal.
* **Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing (CPET):** Measures cardiorespiratory fitness ($VO_{2	ext{peak}}$), providing integrative assessment of cardiopulmonary reserves and independent prognostic stratification before high-risk cancer surgery and cardiotoxic therapies.

## 1. Preamble and 2. Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) and cancer are the two leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Over the past three decades, significant advances in early cancer detection and targeted antineoplastic therapies have dramatically improved cancer-specific survival. However, many cancer treatments—including classical cytotoxic chemotherapy, targeted small-molecule inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies, immunotherapies, and radiation therapy—can induce acute, subacute, or chronic adverse cardiovascular effects.

Cancer therapy-related cardiovascular toxicity (CTR-CVT) can compromise quality of life, limit the deliverability of curative-intent cancer regimens, and cause premature cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Cardio-oncology is an established, specialized discipline dedicated to providing comprehensive, multidisciplinary cardiovascular care to oncology and hematology patients across the entire cancer care continuum.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 2.1 Cancer and Cardiovascular Needs of Patients with Cancer</summary><div class="details-content">

Patients with cancer and CVD frequently share common demographic, lifestyle, and biological risk factors, including advanced age, tobacco smoking, obesity, diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension, sedentary lifestyle, and chronic systemic inflammation. In addition, the direct effects of antineoplastic agents and radiation, compounded by physiological stress, can destabilize pre-existing cardiovascular conditions or induce de novo cardiotoxicity.

Cardiovascular complications during cancer treatment not only lead to direct cardiac morbidity, but often necessitate unplanned dose reductions, treatment interruptions, or permanent discontinuation of life-saving anticancer drugs. This balance between oncologic efficacy and cardiovascular safety requires structured coordination between oncologists, hematologists, cardiologists, and allied health professionals.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 2.2 Role of Cardio-Oncology Services</summary><div class="details-content">

The overarching mission of cardio-oncology services is to facilitate uninterrupted, optimal cancer therapy while actively preventing, detecting, and treating cardiovascular complications. Specialized cardio-oncology pathways ensure that patients with suspected or proven cardiovascular disease receive timely evaluation, evidence-based cardioprotective therapies, and individualized risk assessment.

Key operational objectives of cardio-oncology programs include:
* Pre-treatment cardiovascular toxicity risk stratification.
* Primary prevention and optimization of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors.
* Structured surveillance protocols tailored to the cardiotoxic profile of specific antineoplastic regimens.
* Early diagnosis and treatment of acute CTR-CVT without unnecessary cancellation of oncologic therapies.
* Long-term cardiovascular survivorship follow-up.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 2.3 General Principles and Dynamics of Cardio-Oncology Care</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiovascular toxicity risk in patients with cancer is a dynamic entity that fluctuates throughout the patient journey ([Fig. 1](#fig-01)). Baseline risk is determined by host factors, pre-existing CVD, cardiovascular risk factors, and the intrinsic cardiotoxic potential of the planned regimen. During treatment, direct pharmacological toxicities, cumulative drug doses, systemic illnesses, and drug-drug interactions can elevate this risk. Following treatment completion, accelerated atherosclerosis, late myocardial fibrosis, and radiation-induced vasculopathy may manifest decades later.

## 3. Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiovascular Toxicity Definitions</summary><div class="details-content">

Standardized definitions of CTR-CVT are essential to harmonize clinical decision-making, clinical trial endpoints, and epidemiological reporting. The 2022 ESC Guidelines adopt international consensus definitions formulated by the Heart Failure Association (HFA) and the International Cardio-Oncology Society (IC-OS).

Its primary goals are to establish baseline cardiopulmonary function, identify occult or symptomatic CVD, optimize cardiovascular risk factors, and determine the frequency and intensity of subsequent monitoring without causing unjustified delays in antineoplastic therapy.

#### Scoring and Risk Stratification Algorithm
Based on the cumulative point total of risk factors in [Table 4](#table-04), patients are categorized into four distinct risk strata:
* **Low Risk:** No risk factors, OR only one Moderate 1 (M1 = 1 point) risk factor.
* **Moderate Risk (M):** Moderate risk factors totaling **2 to 4 points** ($	ext{M1} = 1\ 	ext{point}$; $	ext{M2} = 2\ 	ext{points}$).
* **High Risk (H):** Any single High-risk (**H**) factor, OR moderate risk factors totaling **$\ge 5$ points**.
* **Very High Risk (VH):** Any single Very High-risk (**VH**) factor.

For patients receiving anthracyclines, calculation of cumulative anthracycline dose equivalence is essential ([Table 5](#table-05)).

### 4.2 History and Clinical Examination</summary><div class="details-content">

A thorough clinical history and physical examination establish baseline functional status and distinguish between primary prevention (patients without pre-existing CVD or previous CTR-CVT) and secondary prevention (patients with established CVD or previous cardiotoxicity).

Key components of baseline evaluation include:
* **Review of Modifiable Cardiovascular Risk Factors:** Identification and treatment efficacy assessment of arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, and smoking. Calculation of 10-year cardiovascular risk using SCORE2 / SCORE2-OP is recommended for cancer patients $> 40$ years old without established CVD or diabetes.
* **Oncologic History:** Prior malignancies, prior exposure to anthracyclines (including cumulative doses, [Table 5](#table-05)), prior chest/mediastinal radiation therapy, and prior targeted therapies.
* **Symptom Review:** Screening for typical cardiovascular symptoms, such as exertional chest discomfort, dyspnea (NYHA functional class), orthopnea, palpitations, presyncope, syncope, and lower extremity edema.
* **Physical Examination:** Documentation of resting blood pressure, heart rate, cardiac murmurs, elevated jugular venous pressure, pulmonary rales, and peripheral edema.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 4.3 Electrocardiogram (ECG)</summary><div class="details-content">

A standard 12-lead ECG is an accessible, mandatory baseline test that identifies pre-existing conduction delays, chamber enlargement, prior myocardial infarction, silent ischemia, arrhythmias, and repolarization abnormalities.

* **Repolarization and QTc Interval:** Baseline corrected QT (QTc) measurement is mandatory prior to initiating therapies known to prolong ventricular repolarization (e.g. arsenic trioxide, histone deacetylase inhibitors, specific TKIs, and CDK4/6 inhibitors).
* **Fridericia Correction (QTcF):** The Fridericia formula ($QTcF = QT / \sqrt[3]{RR}$) is recommended over Bazett formula, because Bazett overcorrects at elevated heart rates and undercorrects at low heart rates, leading to substantial misclassification in oncologic patients.
* **Normal Thresholds:** Baseline $QTcF > 450\ 	ext{ms}$ in men and $> 460\ 	ext{ms}$ in women is abnormal; values $\ge 480\ 	ext{ms}$ or $\ge 500\ 	ext{ms}$ confer high risk of ventricular arrhythmias (*Torsade de Pointes*) and require correction of reversible factors (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, interacting medications) prior to starting antineoplastic therapy.
* **Atrial Enlargement and Conduction Abnormalities:** Left atrial enlargement, AV block, and frequent premature atrial complexes on baseline ECG are established predictors of atrial fibrillation during ibrutinib therapy and autologous stem cell transplantation.

### 4.4 Cardiac Serum Biomarkers</summary><div class="details-content">

Measurement of baseline cardiac biomarkers—cardiac troponins (cTnI or cTnT, preferably high-sensitivity assays) and natriuretic peptides (BNP or NT-proBNP)—serves two vital clinical purposes:
1. Identifying pre-existing subclinical myocardial injury or hemodynamic wall stress before starting therapy.
2. Providing a baseline reference value to allow accurate detection of therapy-induced dynamic biomarker changes during surveillance.

* **Cardiac Troponin (cTn):** Baseline elevation above the 99th percentile upper reference limit (ULN) independently predicts subsequent CTRCD, persistent LV systolic dysfunction, and major adverse cardiac events in patients receiving anthracyclines, HER2 inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors.
* **Natriuretic Peptides (NP):** Elevated baseline BNP ($> 100\ 	ext{pg/mL}$) or NT-proBNP ($> 125\ 	ext{pg/mL}$) is a powerful predictor of cardiotoxicity, heart failure hospitalization, and adverse cardiovascular events, particularly in patients with multiple myeloma scheduled for proteasome inhibitors (carfilzomib, bortezomib) with an odds ratio of 10.8 for subsequent CV events.

### 4.5 Cardiovascular Imaging Modalities</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiovascular imaging provides non-invasive, quantitative structural and functional assessment of cardiac chambers, valves, pericardium, and hemodynamics.

* **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line imaging modality. Three-dimensional (3D) echocardiography is the preferred method for measuring LVEF and ventricular volumes due to superior reproducibility and lower intra-observer variability. If 3D is unavailable or image tracking is suboptimal, the 2D biplane Simpson method is recommended.
* **Contrast-Enhanced Echocardiography:** Ultrasound-enhancing contrast agents should be administered whenever $\ge 2$ contiguous LV segments are inadequately visualized on standard unenhanced imaging.
* **Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS):** Speckle-tracking GLS is recommended in all cancer patients undergoing echocardiography. GLS detects early subclinical myocardial contractile impairment prior to overt LVEF decline. A relative percentage reduction in GLS $> 15\%$ from baseline is the standard diagnostic threshold indicating subclinical CTRCD ([Table 3](#table-03)). Serial GLS measurements must be performed using the same vendor software to eliminate inter-vendor variability.
* **Cardiac Magnetic Resonance (CMR):** The reference standard for cardiac chamber volumetry, ejection fraction, and multi-parametric myocardial tissue characterization. Indicated when echocardiography is inconclusive, technically suboptimal, or when complex myocardial pathology (myocarditis, cardiac amyloidosis, myocardial metastases) is suspected. Multiparametric CMR protocols include T2-weighted STIR and T2 mapping for edema, Native T1 mapping and extracellular volume (ECV) for diffuse fibrosis, and Late Gadolinium Enhancement (LGE) for focal replacement fibrosis and scar.
* **Multigated Acquisition (MUGA) Radionuclide Ventriculography:** Considered only as a third-line modality when TTE and CMR are both unavailable or non-diagnostic. MUGA is discouraged due to cumulative ionizing radiation exposure and inability to assess valvular pathology, pericardial disease, RV function, or GLS.

### 4.6 Cardiopulmonary Fitness Assessment</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) provides an objective, integrative evaluation of the cardiovascular, respiratory, and skeletal muscle systems under metabolic stress. The primary metric derived from CPET is peak oxygen uptake ($VO_{2	ext{peak}}$), representing cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF).

* **Oncologic Prognostic Value:** Markedly reduced baseline CRF ($VO_{2	ext{peak}} < 16\ 	ext{mL/kg/min}$) is prevalent in oncology patients prior to therapy and is an independent predictor of treatment intolerance, non-cardiovascular and cardiovascular mortality, and adverse surgical outcomes.
* **Presurgical Risk Evaluation:** CPET provides superior risk stratification for patients undergoing major thoracic, abdominal, or pelvic cancer resections, guiding perioperative triage and prehabilitation programs.
* **Exercise Prescription:** Baseline CPET enables tailored aerobic and resistance exercise prescription to preserve cardiovascular fitness and mitigate functional decline during cardiotoxic chemotherapy.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 4.7 Cardiovascular Risk Evaluation Before Cancer Surgery</summary><div class="details-content">

Major surgical oncology procedures entail significant perioperative hemodynamic stress, systemic inflammatory activation, and thrombotic risks. Preoperative cardiovascular assessment should follow the ESC Guidelines on non-cardiac surgery:
* **Risk Stratification:** Stratification incorporates intrinsic surgical risk (low, intermediate, high surgical risk based on procedural invasiveness and anticipated fluid shifts) and patient-specific cardiovascular risk.
* **Elective Optimization:** Optimization of blood pressure, glycemic control, myocardial ischemia, and heart failure prior to elective surgery.
* **Biomarker Screening:** Preoperative measurement of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin and natriuretic peptides in patients with known CVD or aged $\ge 65$ years undergoing intermediate- or high-risk cancer surgery.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 4.8 Genetic Testing</summary><div class="details-content">

Emerging genomic studies indicate that inherited genetic variants can substantially influence individual susceptibility to CTR-CVT:
* **Anthracycline Cardiotoxicity:** Genetic variants in titin (*TTN* truncating variants) and cardiac sarcomere genes, as well as polymorphisms in ATP-binding cassette transporter genes (*ABCC1*, *ABCC2*, *ABCBP*), topoisomerase II beta (*TOP2B*), and carbonyl reductases (*CBR1*, *CBR3*), correlate with increased propensity to anthracycline-induced cardiomyopathy.
* **Genetic Arrhythmia Syndromes:** Congenital long QT syndrome (LQTS) mutations (*KCNQ1*, *KCNH2*, *SCN5A*) predispose to catastrophic drug-induced *Torsade de Pointes* when exposed to QTc-prolonging antineoplastic agents.
* **Clinical Indications:** Routine unselected genetic screening is not currently recommended; however, genetic counseling and targeted testing should be considered in patients with strong family histories of premature cardiomyopathy or severe, unexplained cardiotoxicity occurring at low cumulative drug doses.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 5. Prevention and Monitoring of Cardiovascular Complications During Cancer Therapy</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardioprotection strategies during cancer therapy comprise primary prevention (interventions initiated in patients without pre-existing CVD or prior CTR-CVT) and secondary prevention (interventions in patients with active or pre-existing CVD, including prior CTRCD). Routine surveillance protocols aim to identify subclinical cardiovascular toxicity at an early, reversible stage before clinical heart failure or irreversible vascular occlusion ensues ([Fig. 9](#fig-09)).

<span id="fig-09"></span>![](fig09_cardioprotection_strategies.jpg)

**FIGURE 9.** Primary and secondary cancer therapy-related cardiovascular toxicity prevention and monitoring pathways across the cancer care continuum.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5.1 General Principles of Cardioprotection</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiovascular toxicity prevention requires an aggressive, multi-targeted approach:
* **Optimization of Cardiovascular Risk Factors:** Aggressive control of arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, and smoking cessation according to ESC general prevention guidelines.
* **Cardioprotective Pharmacotherapy:** Neurohormonal antagonists (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors [ACEI] or angiotensin receptor blockers [ARB], and beta-blockers) and statins have demonstrated cardioprotective efficacy in reducing the incidence of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) decline during anthracycline and HER2-targeted therapies.
* **Oncologic Drug Selection and Formulation Modification:** Utilizing liposomal anthracycline formulations or dexrazoxane in patients at high and very high risk of cardiotoxicity.
* **Radiotherapy Optimization:** Employing advanced radiation planning techniques (intensity-modulated radiotherapy [IMRT], deep inspiration breath-hold [DIBH], proton beam therapy) to minimize mean heart dose (MHD).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5.2 Primary Prevention Strategies</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 5.2.1 Primary Prevention During Anthracycline Chemotherapy
Primary prevention is specifically recommended for patients categorized as high and very high cardiovascular toxicity risk prior to anthracycline therapy:
* **Dexrazoxane:** An intracellular iron-chelating agent and topoisomerase II beta inhibitor that significantly reduces free radical generation and anthracycline-induced double-strand DNA breaks. Dexrazoxane is recommended in high- and very high-risk patients, or when high cumulative doses of doxorubicin ($\ge 250\ 	ext{mg/m}^2$) or epirubicin ($\ge 600\ 	ext{mg/m}^2$) are planned.
* **Liposomal Anthracyclines:** Non-pegylated or pegylated liposomal doxorubicin alters tissue biodistribution, dramatically reducing myocardial drug accumulation while preserving antineoplastic efficacy. Recommended in patients at high or very high cardiotoxicity risk.
* **Neurohormonal Therapy and Statins:** Prophylactic administration of ACEI/ARB (enalapril, perindopril, candesartan) and beta-blockers (carvedilol, nebivolol) should be considered in high- and very high-risk patients. Statins (atorvastatin) reduce reactive oxygen species and preserve LVEF during anthracycline treatment.

#### 5.2.2 Primary Prevention of Radiation-Induced Cardiovascular Toxicity
Radiation-induced heart disease (RIHD) encompasses accelerated coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, pericardial disease, conduction abnormalities, and myocardial fibrosis:
* **Dose Constraints:** Minimizing the mean heart dose (MHD) is the primary determinant of long-term risk. Every $1\ 	ext{Gy}$ increase in MHD increases the relative rate of major adverse coronary events by $7.4\%$ linearly without a safe lower threshold.
* **Modern Radiotherapy Techniques:** Deep inspiration breath-hold (DIBH) displaces the heart caudally and dorsally away from anterior chest wall radiation fields in left-sided breast cancer, reducing cardiac exposure by up to $50	ext{--}70\%$. IMRT and proton therapy enable highly conformal dose distributions avoiding cardiac substructures (left anterior descending coronary artery [LAD], cardiac valves).

### 5.3 Secondary Prevention Strategies</summary><div class="details-content">

Secondary prevention applies to cancer patients with established pre-existing CVD or prior CTR-CVT:
* **Continuation of Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT):** In patients with pre-existing heart failure, CAD, or hypertension, baseline cardioprotective medications (ACEI/ARNi, beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists [MRA], SGLT2 inhibitors, statins, antiplatelet/anticoagulant drugs) should be continued and uptitrated to target doses unless clinically contraindicated.
* **Multidisciplinary Cardio-Oncology Review:** Any dose adjustment, temporary pause, or drug change must be discussed within the cardio-oncology team to ensure uninterrupted cancer therapy while mitigating cardiovascular decompensation.

### 5.4 Cardiovascular Surveillance During Cancer Therapies</summary><div class="details-content">

Serial cardiovascular surveillance combines clinical evaluations, serial 12-lead ECGs, serum biomarker tracking (cardiac troponins and natriuretic peptides), and transthoracic echocardiography (TTE with 3D-LVEF and GLS).

#### 5.4.1 Cardiac Serum Biomarkers
* **Serial Troponin Testing:** Troponin release reflects acute or ongoing cardiomyocyte injury. Measurements should be obtained at baseline, prior to cycles 2, 4, and 6 of anthracycline chemotherapy, and periodically during targeted therapies in high-risk patients. A new troponin elevation above the laboratory 99th percentile ULN indicates subclinical cardiac injury, triggering cardiology review and consideration of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy.
* **Natriuretic Peptides (NP):** BNP and NT-proBNP reflect ventricular wall stress and volume overload. Serial measurements identify hemodynamic compromise during proteasome inhibitor and targeted therapy regimens.

#### 5.4.2 Cardiac Imaging Modalities
* **TTE Monitoring Protocol:** Echocardiography is the primary imaging modality. Transthoracic echocardiography should be scheduled at baseline, at mid-treatment, at completion of anthracycline therapy, and every 3 months during HER2-targeted therapy.
* **GLS Monitoring:** A relative percentage reduction in GLS $> 15\%$ from baseline indicates subclinical CTRCD even if LVEF remains $\ge 50\%$.

</div></details>

## 6. Diagnosis and Management of Acute and Subacute Cardiovascular Toxicity in Patients Receiving Anticancer Treatment</summary><div class="details-content">

Acute and subacute cancer therapy-related cardiovascular toxicity (CTR-CVT) encompasses a diverse spectrum of myocardial, vascular, arrhythmic, and pericardial complications that manifest during or immediately after active cancer treatment. The cornerstone of acute management is rapid diagnostic verification, prompt initiation of guideline-directed cardioprotective therapy, and coordinated multidisciplinary evaluation to determine whether cancer treatment can be safely continued, temporarily interrupted, or permanently discontinued.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 6.1 Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiac Dysfunction (CTRCD)</summary><div class="details-content">

CTRCD is broadly categorized into anthracycline chemotherapy-induced cardiac dysfunction, HER2-targeted therapy-related cardiac dysfunction, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-associated myocarditis, and non-inflammatory heart failure.

#### 6.1.1 Anthracycline Chemotherapy-Related Cardiac Dysfunction
Cardiotoxicity from anthracyclines represents dose-dependent, cumulative cardiomyocyte injury. When CTRCD develops, management is guided by clinical symptom severity and LVEF ([Fig. 25](#fig-25)):
* **Symptomatic CTRCD:** Anthracycline chemotherapy should be temporarily interrupted in patients who develop symptomatic heart failure. Immediate initiation of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT: ACEI/ARB/ARNi, evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor) and loop diuretics for volume overload is recommended. Re-challenge with anthracyclines requires multidisciplinary consensus and should be considered only when no alternative effective oncologic regimens exist and after full recovery of cardiac function.
* **Asymptomatic Severe CTRCD ($LVEF < 40\%$):** Anthracycline therapy should be temporarily suspended, GDMT initiated, and LVEF reassessed in 2–3 weeks.
* **Asymptomatic Moderate CTRCD ($LVEF\ 40	ext{--}49\%$):** Continuation of anthracycline chemotherapy is reasonable in selected patients under close cardio-oncology surveillance and concurrent GDMT initiation.
* **Asymptomatic Mild CTRCD ($LVEF \ge 50\%$ with relative $GLS\ 	ext{drop} > 15\%$ or new biomarker rise):** Anthracyclines should be continued without interruption; initiation of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy (ACEI/ARB and/or beta-blocker) should be considered.

#### 6.1.3 Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Associated Myocarditis
ICI-associated myocarditis is an uncommon ($0.5	ext{--}1.5\%$) but highly lethal adverse event (case fatality $25	ext{--}50\%$), often accompanied by severe concurrent autoimmune myositis, myopathy, and myasthenia gravis ([Fig. 27](#fig-27)).

Clinical management requires immediate hospitalization and a structured, urgent diagnostic and therapeutic pathway ([Fig. 28](#fig-28)):
* **Immediate ICI Cessation:** Immediate, permanent discontinuation of ICI therapy is recommended for all patients with suspected or confirmed ICI myocarditis.
* **First-Line Immunosuppression:** Immediate administration of high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($500	ext{--}1000\ 	ext{mg/day}$ i.v. bolus for 3 consecutive days) is recommended without waiting for histological or advanced imaging confirmation. Following acute bolus therapy, transition to oral prednisone ($1\ 	ext{mg/kg/day}$) with slow, structured tapering over at least 4–6 weeks while monitoring serial troponin and clinical parameters.
* **Second-Line Immunosuppression for Steroid-Refractory Myocarditis:** If troponin fails to decline by $> 50\%$ within 24–48 hours, or in fulminant cases (cardiogenic shock, complete AV block, refractory arrhythmias), prompt escalation to second-line immunosuppressive agents is recommended:
  * **Abatacept** (CTLA-4 agonist fusion protein, $10\ 	ext{mg/kg}$ on days 1, 14, 28).
  * **Infliximab** (TNF-alpha inhibitor, $5\ 	ext{mg/kg}$; caution in moderate-to-severe heart failure).
  * **Mycophenolate Mofetil** ($1000\ 	ext{mg}$ b.i.d.).
  * **Anti-Thymocyte Globulin (ATG)** or **Plasmapheresis**.

### 6.2 Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 6.2.1 Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS)
Cancer patients presenting with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) or non-ST-segment elevation ACS (NSTEMI) should be managed with an invasive strategy according to standard ESC ACS guidelines:
* **Invasive Coronary Angiography and PCI:** Primary PCI is recommended for STEMI. Radial access is strongly preferred over femoral access to minimize bleeding complications. New-generation drug-eluting stents (DES) are recommended over bare-metal stents even when shortened dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) is anticipated.
* **Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) Selection:** Aspirin and clopidogrel represent the default antiplatelet combination. Potent P2Y12 inhibitors (prasugrel, ticagrelor) should only be used in patients with normal platelet counts ($> 50 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$) and low bleeding risk. Shortened DAPT durations (1–3 months) should be favored in patients undergoing active chemotherapy or with high bleeding risk.
* **Management of Thrombocytopenia During ACS:** Platelet thresholds guide antiplatelet therapy:
  * Platelets $> 50 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$: Full-dose DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel).
  * Platelets $30	ext{--}50 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$: Aspirin and clopidogrel may be continued with caution, or clopidogrel monotherapy utilized; prasugrel and ticagrelor contraindicated.
  * Platelets $10	ext{--}30 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$: Aspirin monotherapy only.
  * Platelets $< 10 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$: Withhold all antiplatelet therapy. Platelet transfusions indicated prior to urgent PCI if platelets $< 30	ext{--}50 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$.

### 6.3 Valvular Heart Disease (VHD)</summary><div class="details-content">

VHD in oncology patients includes pre-existing degenerative valve disease, radiation-induced valvular heart disease (aortic stenosis, mitral regurgitation developing 5–20 years post-mediastinal radiation), infective endocarditis (catheter-related, immunosuppression), and non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE / marantic endocarditis):

* **Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation (TAVI):** TAVI is preferred over surgical valve replacement in cancer patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis who have an estimated oncologic life expectancy $> 1\ 	ext{year}$ and high surgical operative risk.

#### 6.4.1 Atrial Fibrillation (AF)

### 6.5 Arterial Hypertension</summary><div class="details-content">

### 6.7 Bleeding Complications and 6.8 Peripheral Artery Disease</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 6.7 Bleeding Complications and Antiplatelet Management
Cancer patients often have concurrent coagulopathies, thrombocytopenia, and tumor neovascularization:
* **High Bleeding Risk (HBR):** GI/GU tumors, intracranial metastases, hematologic malignancies, and severe thrombocytopenia.
* **Bleeding Management:** In acute major bleeding, immediate interruption of anticoagulation/antiplatelet therapy, local mechanical hemostasis, reversal agents (idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet alfa for apixaban/rivaroxaban, protamine for LMWH/UFH), and platelet/blood transfusions are indicated.

Second- and third-generation BCR-ABL TKIs (nilotinib, ponatinib) and VEGFi can trigger severe, rapidly progressive limb ischemia:
* **Surveillance:** Regular assessment of peripheral pulses, ankle-brachial index (ABI), and arterial duplex ultrasound.
* **Management:** Aggressive risk factor modification, statins, antiplatelet therapy, and timely referral for endovascular or surgical revascularization.

### 6.9 Pulmonary Hypertension and 6.10 Pericardial Diseases</summary><div class="details-content">

#### 6.9 Pulmonary Hypertension (PH)
All five clinical groups of pulmonary hypertension can occur in patients with cancer:
* **Group 1 (Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension [PAH]):** Associated with targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (especially **dasatinib**, bosutinib, ponatinib), proteasome inhibitors (carfilzomib), interferon-alpha, and alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide, mitomycin C, which frequently induce pulmonary veno-occlusive disease [PVOD]). In the DASISION trial, $5\%$ of patients on dasatinib developed PH compared with $0.4\%$ on imatinib.
* **Group 2 (PH Due to Left Heart Disease):** Secondary to cancer therapy-induced heart failure with reduced or preserved ejection fraction (anthracyclines, HER2-targeted agents, radiation).
* **Group 3 (PH Due to Lung Diseases and/or Hypoxia):** Secondary to antineoplastic drug-induced interstitial lung disease (bleomycin, checkpoint inhibitors) or thoracic radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis.
* **Group 4 (Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension [CTEPH]):** Occurs as a chronic sequel of cancer-associated venous thromboembolism or from recurrent thrombotic occlusions around long-term indwelling central venous access catheters. Malignant obstruction of pulmonary arteries can also arise from angiosarcoma, renal cell carcinoma, or testicular germ cell metastases.
* **Group 5 (PH with Unclear or Multifactorial Mechanisms):** Includes tumoral pulmonary microembolism, pulmonary tumour thrombotic microangiopathy (PTTM), and myeloproliferative neoplasms (myelofibrosis with extramedullary hematopoiesis).

##### Diagnostic and Hemodynamic Stratification
Transthoracic echocardiography is the frontline screening tool:
* **Low PH Probability:** Peak tricuspid regurgitation velocity ($TRV$) $\le 2.8\ 	ext{m/s}$ (estimated systolic PAP $\le 35\ 	ext{mmHg}$) without secondary signs of right ventricular pressure overload.
* **Intermediate PH Probability:** Peak $TRV\ 2.9	ext{--}3.4\ 	ext{m/s}$. In patients receiving dasatinib, dose reduction and monthly echocardiographic surveillance are indicated.
* **High PH Probability:** Peak $TRV > 3.4\ 	ext{m/s}$ (estimated systolic PAP $> 50\ 	ext{mmHg}$) or significant right ventricular dysfunction. Immediate discontinuation of dasatinib and prompt right-heart catheterization (RHC) are mandatory to confirm precapillary hemodynamics (mean PAP $> 20\ 	ext{mmHg}$, PAWP $\le 15\ 	ext{mmHg}$, and pulmonary vascular resistance $> 2\ 	ext{Wood Units}$).

#### 6.10 Pericardial Diseases
Pericarditis and pericardial effusion can stem from thoracic radiation, cytotoxic agents (anthracyclines, bleomycin, high-dose cyclophosphamide, cytarabine), molecular targeted inhibitors (dasatinib, ATRA, arsenic trioxide), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).

##### 6.10.1 Acute Pericarditis
* **Presentation and Diagnosis:** Often atypical in cancer patients; diagnosed by classic criteria (pericarditic chest pain, friction rub, widespread ST-segment elevation or PR depression, and new pericardial effusion).
* **Treatment:** First-line therapy consists of NSAIDs (e.g. ibuprofen $600\ 	ext{mg}$ t.i.d.) plus colchicine ($0.5\ 	ext{mg}$ b.i.d. or $0.5\ 	ext{mg}$ daily if $< 70\ 	ext{kg}$) for 3 months to prevent recurrences. Low-to-moderate doses of systemic corticosteroids are reserved for NSAID-refractory cases or severe thrombocytopenia.
* **ICI-Associated Pericarditis:** Carries high mortality and frequently coexists with myocarditis. Requires immediate interruption of ICI therapy, hospitalization, telemetry, and high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($1	ext{--}2\ 	ext{mg/kg/day}$) combined with colchicine.

##### 6.10.2 Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade
* **Etiology:** Malignant effusions (direct invasion by lung, breast, esophageal cancers; or metastasis from melanoma and lymphomas) represent $> 30\%$ of tamponade cases and carry a grave prognosis.
* **Monitoring:** Small-to-moderate asymptomatic effusions ($< 20\ 	ext{mm}$) can be monitored with serial echocardiograms every 1–2 weeks initially, then every 4–6 weeks.
* **Intervention:** In hemodynamically compromising effusions or overt tamponade, urgent percutaneous echocardiography-guided pericardiocentesis with prolonged catheter drainage is the gold standard. Intrapericardial instillation of cytostatic or sclerosing agents (bleomycin, cisplatin, thiotepa) or creation of a surgical/balloon pericardial window is recommended for recurrent malignant effusions.

### 7.2 Surveillance Protocols and Weaning of Cardioprotective Medications

* **Asymptomatic Low-Risk Patients:** Re-evaluation within 12 months may be considered.

### 7.3 Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing (CPET) and Cardiac Rehabilitation

* **CPET:** Indicated in survivors experiencing persistent exertional intolerance, fatigue, or dyspnea to differentiate cardiac, pulmonary, musculoskeletal, and deconditioning components.
* **Cardiac Rehabilitation:** Supervised individualized aerobic and resistance training, including high-intensity interval training (HIIT), is safe, improves CRF, restores endothelial function, reduces metabolic risk factors, and decreases long-term cardiovascular mortality.

Cancer survivors represent an expanding population with accelerated vascular aging and disproportionate cardiovascular morbidity. Long-term surveillance strategies differ markedly between childhood/adolescent survivors and adult survivors.

### 8.3 Management of Late Cardiovascular Complications in Cancer Survivors</summary><div class="details-content">

Late sequelae of antineoplastic treatment present decades following remission, mandating disease-specific diagnostic and interventional thresholds:

#### 8.3.1 Heart Failure and Late CTRCD
Cardiomyopathy after anthracyclines or radiation presents as HF with reduced, mildly reduced, or preserved ejection fraction. Treatment follows standard HF guidelines: GDMT (ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) and device therapy (ICD/CRT) where indicated.

#### 8.3.2 Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
Mediastinal radiation induces ostial and proximal epicardial coronary stenoses, predominantly involving the left main and LAD coronary arteries. 
* **Surveillance:** In asymptomatic adult cancer survivors who received thoracic radiation exposing the heart, non-invasive stress testing (stress echocardiography, CMR perfusion, SPECT/PET) or coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) should be considered every 5–10 years.
* **Revascularization:** Revascularization decisions require MDT consensus. Pre-operative assessment must evaluate internal mammary artery patency, extensive chest wall adhesions, and the presence of a "porcelain" calcified ascending aorta, which can make cross-clamping impossible and favor percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) over coronary artery bypass graft (CABG).

#### 8.3.3 Valvular Heart Disease (VHD)
Radiation-associated valvular disease manifests after a latent period of 10–20 years as diffuse fibrous thickening and dense calcification of the aortic valve cusps, aortic root, mitral annulus, and aortomitral curtain. Pure regurgitation is typical early; severe calcific stenosis dominates late.
* **Surgical vs Transcatheter Valve Intervention:** Mediastinal radiation markedly elevates operative mortality during conventional sternotomy. Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) should be considered for patients with radiation-induced severe symptomatic aortic stenosis who are at high or prohibitive surgical risk, or who have porcelain aorta or severe chest wall radiation deformity.

Survivors exposed to neck radiation (head and neck cancers, Hodgkin lymphoma) carry high risks of carotid artery stenosis and ischemic stroke. Annual carotid auscultation and screening carotid duplex ultrasound every 5–8 years post-RT are recommended.

#### 8.3.5 Pericardial Complications
Chronic constrictive pericarditis develops years after mediastinal RT. CMR and cardiac CT are critical for identifying pericardial thickening ($> 4\ 	ext{mm}$), calcification, and interventricular interdependence. Total pericardiectomy carries substantial operative mortality due to concomitant radiation cardiomyopathy.

### 9.1 Cardiac Tumours
Cardiac masses encompass non-neoplastic pseudotumours (thrombi, vegetations, annular calcification), primary benign neoplasms ($75\%$), primary malignant neoplasms ($10	ext{--}25\%$), and secondary metastatic tumours ($20	ext{--}30$ times more common than primary neoplasms) ([Fig. 39](#fig-39)).

<span id="fig-39"></span>![](fig39_cardiac_tumour_locations.jpg)

**FIGURE 39.** Anatomical predilection and frequency of primary and secondary cardiac tumours across cardiac chambers.

#### Diagnostic Algorithm
Comprehensive non-invasive multimodality imaging is the diagnostic cornerstone ([Fig. 40](#fig-40)):
1. **Transthoracic and Transesophageal Echocardiography:** Rapidly identifies mobility, stalk attachment, hemodynamic obstruction, and vascularity with myocardial contrast agents.
2. **Cardiac Magnetic Resonance (CMR):** Provides definitive tissue characterization based on $T_1$, $T_2$, fat suppression, perfusion, and early/late gadolinium enhancement ($LGE$) to distinguish avascular thrombi from vascularized neoplasms.
3. **Computed Tomography (CT) and PET:** Delineates calcification, extracardiac staging, and FDG-avidity for metabolic differentiation between benign and aggressive malignant neoplasms.

### 9.2 Pregnant Patients with Cancer
Cancer diagnosed during pregnancy poses profound clinical challenges, requiring careful balance between maternal survival and fetal teratogenicity ([Fig. 41](#fig-41)).

<span id="fig-41"></span>![](fig41_pregnant_cancer_monitoring_protocol.jpg)

**FIGURE 41.** Cardiovascular monitoring and therapeutic protocol for pregnant women receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy.

#### Principles of Therapy in Pregnancy
* **Anthracycline Chemotherapy:** Can be administered during the second and third trimesters ($> 14$ weeks' gestation) with close fetal and maternal monitoring (TTE every 2 cycles). Anthracyclines are contraindicated during the first trimester.
* **Trastuzumab and Targeted HER2 Agents:** Strictly contraindicated throughout pregnancy due to risk of severe oligohydramnios and fetal renal agenesis.
* **Heart Failure GDMT:** ACEI, ARB, ARNi, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitors are strictly contraindicated during pregnancy due to fetotoxicity. Beta-blockers (metoprolol succinate, carvedilol) and hydralazine/nitrates are the agents of choice.
* **Anticoagulation:** DOACs and warfarin (in the first trimester) are contraindicated. Therapeutic low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) with anti-Xa monitoring is the standard of care for acute VTE in pregnancy.

### 9.3 Carcinoid Valvular Heart Disease
Carcinoid heart disease develops in up to $50\%$ of patients with carcinoid syndrome, typically when neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) metastasize to the liver, releasing high circulating levels of serotonin ($5	ext{-HT}$), bradykinin, and tachykinins into the systemic circulation ([Fig. 42](#fig-42)).

<span id="fig-42"></span>![](fig42_carcinoid_heart_disease_workup.jpg)

**FIGURE 42.** Diagnostic and clinical assessment algorithm for carcinoid valvular heart disease: biomarker triggers, echocardiographic features, and surgical intervention criteria.

#### Clinical and Diagnostic Characteristics
* **Pathophysiology:** Serotonin stimulates 5-$	ext{HT}_{2	ext{B}}$ receptors on subendocardial interstitial cells, inducing plaque-like fibrous deposits that fuse and retract the tricuspid and pulmonary valves, causing severe tricuspid regurgitation and pulmonary stenosis/regurgitation. Left-sided valve involvement occurs in $< 10\%$ (patent foramen ovale or bronchial NETs).
* **Biomarkers:** Elevated **urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA)** excretion ($> 300\ \mu	ext{mol}/24\ 	ext{h}$) and elevated NT-proBNP ($> 260\ 	ext{pg/mL}$).
* **Echocardiography:** Thickened, shortened, rigid tricuspid leaflets with failure of coaptation (free-floating regurgitation) and restricted pulmonary valve leaflets.
* **Surgical Management:** Valve replacement (bioprosthetic valves preferred) is indicated in patients with severe right-sided carcinoid valve disease and refractory right heart failure, prior to or concurrently with liver metastases debulking or PRRT. Perioperative octreotide infusion is mandatory to prevent fatal carcinoid crisis.

### 9.4 Amyloid Light-Chain (AL) Cardiac Amyloidosis
AL cardiac amyloidosis arises from plasma cell dyscrasias that produce misfolded monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains that deposit as amyloid fibrils in the myocardial interstitium ([Fig. 43](#fig-43)).

<span id="fig-43"></span>![](fig43_al_cardiac_amyloidosis_diagnosis.jpg)

**FIGURE 43.** Non-invasive and invasive diagnostic pathway for amyloid light-chain (AL) cardiac amyloidosis: monoclonal screening, multimodality imaging, and biomarker staging.

#### Diagnostic Pathway and Staging
1. **Monoclonal Protein Screening:** Mandatory trifecta: serum free light chains (sFLC, kappa/lambda ratio), serum immunofixation electrophoresis (IFE), and urine IFE.
2. **Echocardiography and CMR:** Concentric biventricular thickening, granular myocardial sparkling, biatrial enlargement, restrictive filling, apical sparing strain pattern, and diffuse transmural late gadolinium enhancement with elevated extracellular volume ($ECV > 40\%$).
3. **Biopsy:** Abdominal fat pad or bone marrow biopsy with Congo red staining; endomyocardial biopsy if non-invasive testing or extracardiac biopsy is non-diagnostic.
4. **Mayo Biomarker Staging:** Based on troponin T/I, NT-proBNP, and difference between involved and uninvolved free light chains ($	ext{dFLC}$). Stage IV patients carry severe median survival ($4	ext{--}6$ months) without urgent therapy.
5. **Therapy:** Frontline quadruplet therapy with **daratumumab, bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone (Dara-CyBorD)** rapidly clears circulating amyloidogenic light chains.

### 9.5 Cardiac Implantable Electronic Devices (CIED) During Radiotherapy
Patients with permanent pacemakers (PPM), implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICD), or cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) undergoing radiation therapy are at risk of device malfunction, software reset, circuit corruption, or lead damage ([Fig. 44](#fig-44)).

<span id="fig-44"></span>![](fig44_cied_radiotherapy_risk_stratification.jpg)

**FIGURE 44.** Risk stratification for patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIED) undergoing radiotherapy: beam energy, dose exposure, and pacing dependence.

#### Mechanisms and Risk Factors
* **Direct Ionizing Radiation:** Cumulative dose absorption damages complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) components, causing battery depletion, charge loss, and irreversible hardware reset.
* **Neutron Beam Scatter:** High-energy photon beams ($> 6\ 	ext{MV}$ or $> 10\ 	ext{MV}$) produce photoneutron scatter that induces single-event upsets (SEU), RAM corruption, and software reset.
* **Electromagnetic Interference (EMI):** May trigger inappropriate inhibition of pacing in pacemaker-dependent patients or inappropriate defibrillation shocks in ICD carriers.

#### Risk Categories and Management Protocols ([Fig. 45](#fig-45), [Fig. 46](#fig-46))
* **Low Risk:** Cumulative device dose $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$, photon energy $\le 6\ 	ext{MV}$, non-pacemaker-dependent. Interrogation pre- and post-RT regimen.
* **Intermediate Risk:** Cumulative dose $2	ext{--}10\ 	ext{Gy}$ or pacing-dependent (dose $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$). Continuous visual/audio monitoring and weekly interrogation.
* **High Risk:** Cumulative dose $> 10\ 	ext{Gy}$ (pacemaker), cumulative dose $> 1\ 	ext{Gy}$ (ICD), pacing-dependent with dose $2	ext{--}10\ 	ext{Gy}$, or use of neutron-producing beams ($> 6\ 	ext{MV}$). Continuous ECG/telemetry during fractions, weekly interrogation, and surgical relocation of the CIED prior to RT if the generator lies directly within the primary radiation beam.

## 12. Key Messages</summary><div class="details-content">

1. **Cardiovascular Risk Assessment:** Baseline risk stratification using HFA-ICOS tools is recommended before starting potentially cardiotoxic anticancer treatment.
2. **Primary and Secondary Prevention:** Aggressive management of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors and continuation/optimization of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy reduces CTRCD.
3. **Cardioprotective Therapy:** Statins, ACEI/ARBs, and beta-blockers prevent or mitigate CTRCD in high- and very high-risk patients receiving anthracyclines or targeted therapies.
4. **Multidisciplinary Decision-Making:** Cardiotoxicity management requires close collaboration between cardiologists, oncologists, and hematologists to avoid unnecessary cancer treatment interruptions.
5. **Surveillance During Therapy:** Serial echocardiography (3D LVEF, GLS) and cardiac serum biomarkers (cTn, NP) detect subclinical myocardial damage early.
6. **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Myocarditis:** Rare but highly lethal; requires immediate permanent ICI discontinuation and immediate high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($500	ext{--}1000\ 	ext{mg/day}$).
7. **Anticoagulation in Active Cancer:** DOACs are preferred for both atrial fibrillation and VTE in cancer patients without gastrointestinal/genitourinary bleeding risks or drug-drug interactions.
8. **End-of-Therapy Assessment:** Evaluation within 12 months post-treatment identifies survivors at high future risk who require lifelong cardiology follow-up.
9. **Long-Term Survivorship:** High-risk survivors of childhood, adolescent, and adult cancers require lifelong surveillance every 2 to 5 years for late cardiomyopathy, premature CAD, and valvular heart disease.
10. **Patient-Centered Education:** Empowering patients through shared decision-making, red flag symptom awareness, and exercise rehabilitation significantly improves long-term outcomes.

</div></details>

## 13. Gaps in Evidence</summary><div class="details-content">

Despite major advances, critical knowledge gaps remain across cardio-oncology:
* **Precision Risk Models:** Lack of prospectively validated multi-omic and artificial intelligence risk prediction algorithms integrating genomic susceptibility and clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP).
* **Cardioprotection Efficacy:** Optimal duration of cardioprotective pharmacotherapy in asymptomatic CTRCD and long-term benefit of primary prevention in moderate-risk cohorts.
* **ICI Myocarditis Therapies:** Comparative efficacy and sequencing of second-line immunosuppressive agents (abatacept, infliximab, alemtuzumab, tofacitinib) in steroid-refractory cases.
* **CAR-T Cell Toxicity Mechanisms:** Incomplete understanding of early cytokine-mediated myocardial injury vs late immune-mediated vascular remodelling.
* **Survivorship Outcomes:** Scarcity of prospective randomized trials evaluating the optimal frequency of imaging surveillance and preventive pharmacotherapy in cancer survivors 10–30 years post-cure.

## 14. "What to Do" and "What Not to Do" Messages from the Guidelines</summary><div class="details-content">

The following compilation summarizes the strong Class I (recommended/indicated) and Class III (not recommended/contraindicated) directives across the 2022 ESC Guidelines on cardio-oncology:

### What to Do (Class I Recommendations)
* **Baseline Assessment:** Perform baseline clinical risk assessment, ECG, and cardiotoxicity risk stratification using HFA-ICOS tools in all patients scheduled for potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapy.
* **Imaging Modality:** Use 3D echocardiography as the preferred first-line modality for LVEF assessment, and global longitudinal strain (GLS) for subclinical CTRCD detection.
* **High-Dose Anthracycline Protection:** Use dexrazoxane and/or liposomal anthracyclines in patients with high baseline risk or high cumulative doses.
* **Symptomatic CTRCD:** Initiate guideline-directed heart failure medical therapy (ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) in all patients who develop symptomatic CTRCD.
* **ICI Myocarditis:** Immediately hospitalize, initiate continuous telemetry, discontinue ICI permanently, and administer immediate high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone ($500	ext{--}1000\ 	ext{mg/day}$ for 3 days).
* **Anticoagulation in Cancer:** Use DOACs as first-line therapy for non-valvular AF and acute VTE in cancer patients without gastrointestinal/mucosal lesions, severe thrombocytopenia ($< 50	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$), or drug interactions.
* **CIED Protection During RT:** Limit cumulative absorbed dose to CIED generators $< 2\ 	ext{Gy}$, avoid photon energies $> 6\ 	ext{MV}$, and monitor pacing-dependent patients continuously.
* **Long-Term Survivorship:** Re-stratify cardiovascular risk 5 years after treatment completion and conduct regular echocardiographic surveillance in high-risk adult and childhood cancer survivors.

### What Not to Do (Class III Directives)
* **Unnecessary Interruption:** Do not permanently discontinue life-saving cancer therapy for mild asymptomatic CTRCD ($LVEF \ge 50\%$, $\Delta GLS > 15\%$, or biomarker rise).
* **Avoid Obsolete Thresholds:** Do not withhold ACEI/ARB or beta-blocker therapy in asymptomatic patients who develop significant $LVEF$ drops below $50\%$.
* **Avoid DOACs in High Bleeding Risk:** Do not administer DOACs in patients with active gastrointestinal bleeding, severe thrombocytopenia ($< 25	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$), or unoperated luminal GI/GU tumours.
* **Avoid Low-Risk Over-Surveillance:** Routine periodic echocardiography surveillance is not recommended in asymptomatic low-risk cancer survivors.
* **Contraindicated Medications in Pregnancy:** Do not prescribe ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNi, MRAs, or DOACs during pregnancy.
* **HER2 Targeted Agents in Pregnancy:** Do not administer trastuzumab or other HER2-targeted agents during pregnancy due to high risk of fetal oligohydramnios and death.

## 15. Quality Indicators for Cardio-Oncology</summary><div class="details-content">

To facilitate clinical governance, continuous audit, and benchmark quality of care across healthcare institutions, the European Society of Cardiology established five key quality indicator domains:

### Domain 1: Structural and Multidisciplinary Organization
* **QI 1.1:** Availability of a dedicated, specialized cardio-oncology multidisciplinary team and structured weekly MDT consultation pathways.
* **QI 1.2:** Written institutional clinical protocols for cardiotoxicity risk assessment, monitoring, and expedited cardiology referral within 48–72 hours for acute CTR-CVT.

### Domain 2: Baseline Cardiovascular Risk Assessment
* **QI 2.1:** Proportion of patients receiving potentially cardiotoxic therapies who undergo documented baseline HFA-ICOS cardiovascular risk assessment before treatment initiation.
* **QI 2.2:** Proportion of patients undergoing baseline ECG, transthoracic echocardiography, and cardiac serum biomarker measurement according to protocol.

### Domain 3: Monitoring During Cancer Treatment
* **QI 3.1:** Adherence rate to guideline-recommended surveillance imaging and biomarker intervals during active cancer therapy.
* **QI 3.2:** Proportion of patients who undergo echocardiographic GLS evaluation in addition to LVEF.

### Domain 4: Management of Acute and Subacute Toxicities
* **QI 4.1:** Proportion of patients diagnosed with symptomatic CTRCD who are prescribed guideline-directed heart failure medical therapy (ACEI/ARB/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i).
* **QI 4.2:** Time to initiation of high-dose corticosteroids in patients with suspected or confirmed ICI-associated myocarditis (target: $< 24\ 	ext{hours}$ from clinical presentation).
* **QI 4.3:** Proportion of cancer patients with acute VTE or AF who receive appropriate guideline-concordant anticoagulation.

### Domain 5: End-of-Therapy and Long-Term Survivorship Care
* **QI 5.1:** Proportion of high- and moderate-risk cancer patients who receive a documented end-of-cancer therapy cardiovascular risk evaluation within 12 months of completing therapy.
* **QI 5.2:** Implementation of structured long-term survivorship surveillance plans for high-risk childhood and adult cancer survivors.
