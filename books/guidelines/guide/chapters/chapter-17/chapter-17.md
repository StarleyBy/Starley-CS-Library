## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Key Principles of the Comprehensive Transthoracic Echocardiographic Examination

The 2018 American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) Comprehensive Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE) Guideline establishes a standardized, reproducible, and evidence-based protocol for adult echocardiography. It transitions practice from ad-hoc imaging to a rigorous, sequential, multi-window diagnostic standard designed to eliminate diagnostic omissions, optimize acoustic instrumentation, and integrate advanced modalities into routine cardiovascular assessment:

1. **Standardized Complete Examination Sequence:**
   - A complete comprehensive adult TTE comprises a minimum sequence of standardized digital recordings (video loops and calibrated still frames) obtained across five primary acoustic windows: Parasternal (Long- and Short-Axis), Apical (4-, 5-, 2-, and 3-Chamber), Subcostal, Suprasternal Notch, and Right Parasternal.
   - Standard examinations require recording at least one to two complete cardiac cycles for patients in regular sinus rhythm, and three to five consecutive cycles for patients with arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation) to capture hemodynamic beat-to-beat variability.

2. **Digital Workflow and Clip Architecture:**
   - Image acquisition is structured around DICOM-compliant digital cine loops and calibrated still frames rather than static captures.
   - Measurements must be performed on recorded digital frames with full cardiac-cycle timing (using integrated simultaneous ECG tracing) rather than live "on-the-fly" frozen displays, ensuring verifiable quality assurance and peer review.

3. **Rigid Measurement Standards (Inner Edge–to–Inner Edge):**
   - In alignment with ASE/EACVI chamber quantification guidelines, all two-dimensional (2D) linear dimensions (LV, RV, atria, and great vessels) must be measured using the **inner edge–to–inner edge** convention at strictly defined physiological timepoints (end-diastole defined at the onset of the QRS complex or mitral valve closure; end-systole defined at aortic valve closure or maximum inward excursion).
   - Trabeculations, papillary muscles, and false tendons must be strictly excluded from the LV cavity volume and included in myocardial mass calculations.

4. **Instrumentation and Image Optimization:**
   - Fundamental transducer maneuvers are explicitly classified into six physical movements: **tilt, sweep, rotate, slide, rock, and angle**.
   - Harmonic imaging, optimal dynamic range (typically $50	ext{--}70\text{ dB}$), single transmit focal zone placement at the region of interest, and acoustic zoom (maintaining high frame rate $> 30\text{ Hz}$) are required to maximize spatial and temporal resolution while avoiding clutter and side-lobe artifacts.

5. **Multiparametric Spectral and Color Doppler Hemodynamics:**
   - Doppler evaluation must adhere to strict beam-to-flow alignment (angle of incidence $\le 20^\circ$ to avoid velocity underestimation).
   - Default sweep speed for time-interval measurements (e.g., deceleration time, acceleration time, systolic ejection period) must be set to **$100\text{ mm/s}$**, reserving slower sweep speeds ($25	ext{--}50\text{ mm/s}$) for assessing respiratory variation in tamponade or constrictive pericarditis.
   - Color Doppler Nyquist limits must be configured between **$50\text{ and }70\text{ cm/s}$** for high-velocity intracardiac flows, preventing false-positive jet broadening or aliasing ambiguity.

6. **Routine Integration of Myocardial Mechanics (Strain / GLS):**
   - 2D speckle-tracking Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS) is recommended for routine quantitative assessment of LV systolic function, offering superior sensitivity over LVEF in detecting early subclinical myocardial dysfunction (cardio-oncology, amyloidosis, valvular disease). Frame rates must be optimized between **$50\text{ and }80\text{ frames/s}$**.

7. **Standardized Criteria for Ultrasound Enhancing Agents (UEAs / Contrast):**
   - Routine administration of UEAs is indicated whenever two or more contiguous LV myocardial segments are inadequately visualized on non-contrast fundamental or harmonic imaging at rest.
   - Low mechanical index (MI $0.15	ext{--}0.30$) imaging is mandatory to prevent microbubble destruction while maintaining complete LV cavity opacification and apical endocardial border delineation.

8. **Focused / Limited Echocardiography Frameworks:**
   - Structured sub-protocols are codified for emergent or targeted clinical questions (pericardial effusion/tamponade, acute LV systolic failure/cardiogenic shock, and acute RV strain/pulmonary hypertension), preventing incomplete or unsafe focused studies.

## I. Introduction</summary><div class="details-content">

Since the publication of the original American Society of Echocardiography guidelines for the standard echocardiographic examination, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) has evolved from a predominantly qualitative diagnostic tool into an indispensable, highly quantitative, multiparametric cardiovascular imaging modality. Advances in ultrasound physics, digital array transducer engineering, tissue harmonic processing, digital beamforming, and DICOM-compliant picture archiving and communication systems (PACS) have dramatically expanded both the diagnostic capabilities and clinical responsibilities of echocardiography laboratories.

Today, clinical decision-making—ranging from the timing of transcatheter or surgical valve interventions to guideline-directed medical therapy titration, mechanical circulatory support candidate selection, and cardio-oncology surveillance—rests directly on standardized, reproducible quantitative measurements. Variable imaging planes, inconsistent transducer maneuvers, haphazard instrumentation adjustments, and arbitrary caliper placements directly produce diagnostic errors, inter-observer discordance, and inappropriate clinical care.

The purpose of this guideline is to establish a rigorous, standardized adult comprehensive TTE protocol. This document provides:
1. Standardized nomenclature for acoustic windows, imaging planes, and transducer scanning maneuvers.
2. Principles of ultrasound instrumentation optimization across 2D, M-mode, color flow, and spectral Doppler modalities.
3. A systematic, view-by-view acquisition protocol detailing anatomical landmarks, transducer manipulation, and structures demonstrated.
4. Methodological rules for 2D, M-mode, and Doppler measurements according to consensus chamber quantification standards.
5. Standard operating procedures for advanced techniques, including agitated-saline contrast, ultrasound enhancing agents (UEAs), myocardial strain, and three-dimensional (3D) volume acquisition.
6. Structured frameworks for focused/limited examinations in acute clinical emergencies.
7. An atlas of alternative and nonstandard acoustic views for challenging clinical scenarios.

## II. Nomenclature</summary><div class="details-content">

Standardized terminology is foundational to consistent image acquisition and communication. Echocardiographic nomenclature defines the acoustic windows, imaging planes, scanning maneuvers, and measurement conventions utilized throughout the cardiovascular examination.

### A. Image Acquisition Windows and Planes
The spatial orientation of the heart within the thoracic cavity is described relative to orthogonal anatomical axes: long-axis, short-axis, and four-chamber planes ([Fig. 1](#fig-01)). Because the cardiac axes do not align with standard sagittal, coronal, and transverse body planes, echocardiographic planes are defined entirely by internal cardiac geometry:
* **Long-Axis Plane:** Oriented parallel to the major anatomical axis of the left ventricle, running from the mid-point of the mitral annulus through the LV apex. This corresponds to parasternal and apical long-axis imaging views.
* **Short-Axis Plane:** Oriented perpendicular to the long-axis plane, slicing the heart transversely from the base (great arteries) through the mitral valve, papillary muscles, and apex.
* **Four-Chamber Plane:** Oriented orthogonally to both the long- and short-axis planes, transecting all four cardiac chambers simultaneously from base to apex.

Transthoracic images are obtained from five standard acoustic windows on the thoracic wall where bone and lung tissue do not obstruct ultrasound penetration ([Fig. 2](#fig-02)):
1. **Parasternal Window:** Located over the third, fourth, or fifth left intercostal spaces immediately adjacent to the sternum. The patient is placed in the steep left lateral decubitus position ($45^\circ	ext{--}90^\circ$) to bring the heart anteriorly against the chest wall and displace lung tissue.
2. **Apical Window:** Located at the point of maximal impulse (PMI), typically at the fifth or sixth intercostal space along the anterior or mid-axillary line. Placing the patient in the left lateral decubitus position with the left arm elevated overhead widens intercostal spaces.
3. **Subcostal (Subxiphoid) Window:** Located immediately beneath the xiphoid process in the epigastrium. The patient is placed in the supine position with the knees slightly bent to relax the abdominal musculature.
4. **Suprasternal Notch Window:** Located in the jugular fossa superior to the manubrium sterni. The patient is positioned supine with the neck hyperextended and the head turned slightly toward the left shoulder.
5. **Right Parasternal Window:** Located in the second or third right intercostal space adjacent to the sternum. Used with dedicated non-imaging continuous-wave (Pedoff) or imaging probes to detect peak aortic stenosis velocities in tortuous or vertically oriented ascending aortas.

### B. Transducer Scanning Maneuvers
Precise manipulation of the ultrasound probe is essential to navigate between acoustic windows and align standard imaging planes. Every transducer possesses an orientation index marker (ridge, groove, or illuminated dot) corresponding to a display marker on the video screen. The writing group classifies all probe manipulations into six discrete physical maneuvers:

1. **Tilt:** A movement in which the transducer face remains fixed at a specific surface point on the chest wall while the probe body/cable is angled along the imaging plane ([Fig. 3](#fig-03)). Tilting changes the sector direction within the same plane (e.g., tilting superiorly or inferiorly).

2. **Sweep:** A continuous tilting motion that moves the imaging plane sequentially through consecutive parallel or diverging planes of the heart (e.g., sweeping from the aortic valve to the LV apex in the parasternal short axis).

### C. Measurement Conventions and Boundaries
Accurate quantification depends on standardized border identification:
* **Compacted vs. Non-Compacted Myocardium:** All linear dimensions and volumetric cavity tracings must be measured at the interface between the fully compacted myocardial wall and the blood pool. Prominent endocardial trabeculations, papillary muscle heads, and false tendons must be excluded from cavity dimensions and treated as part of the ventricular mass ([Fig. 8](#fig-08)).
* **Foreshortening Prevention:** In apical views, placing the probe too anteriorly or superiorly cuts the LV obliquely, creating a pseudo-apex and significantly underestimating LV long-axis length, end-diastolic volume, and ejection fraction ([Fig. 8](#fig-08)). The sonographer must slide the transducer to the true apex, ensuring the longest achievable LV cavity length with a circular cross-section at the base.

## III. Instrumentation and Machine Optimization</summary><div class="details-content">

Optimal diagnostic image quality requires active, deliberate adjustment of ultrasound instrumentation parameters rather than passive reliance on default presets. The sonographer must dynamically balance spatial resolution, temporal resolution, and contrast resolution throughout each examination phase.

### A. Two-Dimensional Imaging Parameters
1. **Grayscale Maps:** Curve transfer functions convert raw echo amplitudes into display luminance. Linear or slightly S-shaped grayscale maps should be selected to optimize endocardial-to-blood contrast without oversaturating myocardial parenchyma ([Table 1](#table-01)).
2. **B-Mode Colorization:** Chromatic tinting (sepia, amber, or blue tint) can enhance dynamic range perception in low-contrast acoustic windows by matching human retinal sensitivity.
3. **Dynamic Range:** Controls the ratio between the strongest and weakest displayed echo signals (expressed in decibels). A wide dynamic range ($60	ext{--}75\text{ dB}$) provides soft, graded tissue imaging, whereas a narrower dynamic range ($45	ext{--}55\text{ dB}$) enhances boundary contrast between the myocardium and dark blood pool, facilitating endocardial border detection.
4. **Transmit Frequency:** Higher transmit frequencies ($3.5	ext{--}5.0\text{ MHz}$) yield superior axial and lateral resolution but suffer rapid acoustic attenuation. Lower frequencies ($1.5	ext{--}2.5\text{ MHz}$) provide necessary acoustic penetration in obese patients or those with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
5. **Tissue Harmonic Imaging (THI):** Transmitting at fundamental frequency $f_0$ and receiving at harmonic frequency $2f_0$ substantially reduces near-field clutter, reverberation, and side-lobe artifacts. THI is the default standard for adult 2D imaging, though fundamental imaging should be retained when evaluating thin, delicate structures (e.g., endocarditis vegetations, leaflet perforations).
6. **Sector Size and Depth:** Excessive sector depth or width introduces redundant acoustic scan lines and round-trip travel times, sharply reducing the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) and frame rate. Depth must be set just deep enough to visualize posterior pericardial boundaries, and sector width narrowed to encompass the region of interest.
7. **Transducer Beam Focus:** The electronic focal zone must be positioned at or immediately below the anatomical structure of interest (e.g., aortic valve or LV apex) to maximize lateral resolution at that exact depth.
8. **Overall Gain and Time-Gain Compensation (TGC):** Overall gain amplifies all returning signals equally. TGC sliders compensate for depth-dependent tissue attenuation, ensuring uniform myocardial brightness from the near-field chest wall to the far-field posterior wall. Excessive gain introduces artificial speckle noise; insufficient gain drops true endocardial borders.
9. **Zoom / Magnification:** Acoustic (Res-zoom) concentrates ultrasound scan lines exclusively across a localized region of interest, preserving or increasing pixel density and frame rate. Optical (display zoom) merely magnifies pre-existing pixels without enhancing acoustic resolution. Acoustic zoom is mandatory when measuring aortic annulus, LVOT, or valve leaflets.
10. **Frame Rate:** Temporal resolution is governed by frame rate. Diagnostic 2D imaging requires frame rates $\ge 30\text{ frames/s}$ (Hz) in resting sinus rhythm, and $\ge 50	ext{--}80\text{ Hz}$ for speckle-tracking myocardial strain.

### B. Spectral Doppler Optimization
1. **Velocity Scale / PRF:** The velocity scale must be adjusted so that the spectral envelope fills approximately two-thirds to three-quarters of the vertical display without aliasing.
2. **Sweep Speed:** In standardized protocols, all measurements of velocity and time intervals (e.g., deceleration time, acceleration time, ejection time) must be recorded at a default sweep speed of **$100\text{ mm/s}$**. A slow sweep speed ($25	ext{--}50\text{ mm/s}$) is reserved for documenting respiratory variation across multiple cardiac cycles in suspected constrictive pericarditis or cardiac tamponade.
3. **Sample Volume Size:** In pulsed-wave (PW) Doppler, sample volume length is typically set to **$1.5	ext{--}3.0\text{ mm}$** for localized blood flow interrogation (e.g., LVOT, mitral tips). In Doppler tissue imaging (TDI), sample volume is widened to **$5.0	ext{--}10.0\text{ mm}$** to encompass the excursion of the fibrous annulus.
4. **Wall Filters and Gain:** Wall filters reject high-amplitude, low-frequency Doppler shifts produced by myocardial and valvular motion. For blood flow PW/CW, filters should be set low ($50	ext{--}100\text{ Hz}$) to preserve low-velocity diastolic signals without clipping the spectral baseline. Doppler gain should be adjusted to display a clean spectral envelope with an audible tonal signal, eliminating "fuzziness" and artificial blooming.
5. **PW vs. HPRF vs. CW Doppler:**
   - **Pulsed-Wave (PW) Doppler:** Employs a single crystal alternating transmit and receive cycles, providing precise depth resolution ($D = c \cdot t / 2$). Its maximum detectable velocity is strictly governed by the Nyquist limit ($V_{\max} = c \cdot \text{PRF} / (4f_0 \cos \theta)$). Velocities exceeding the Nyquist limit alias.
   - **High-PRF (HPRF) Doppler:** Utilizes multiple sample volumes along the beam line to measure intermediate velocities beyond the conventional Nyquist limit, accepting range ambiguity.
   - **Continuous-Wave (CW) Doppler:** Employs dual dedicated crystals continuously transmitting and receiving ultrasound simultaneously. It possesses no Nyquist limit, allowing measurement of ultra-high velocities (e.g., severe aortic stenosis, tricuspid regurgitation), but lacks depth resolution, sampling all velocities along its path.
6. **Doppler Tissue Imaging (DTI / TDI):** Reconfigures high-pass wall filters into low-pass filters and decreases gain, selectively detecting high-amplitude, low-velocity signals ($< 20\text{ cm/s}$) generated by myocardial tissue excursion.

### C. Color Doppler Imaging (CDI)
1. **Region of Interest (ROI) and Sector Size:** Color Doppler demands immense processing overhead because multiple acoustic pulses (packet size / ensemble length 8–16 pulses per line) are required for autocorrelation. The color box must be tightly sized around the specific valve or orifice of interest to maintain frame rates $> 15	ext{--}20\text{ Hz}$.
2. **Color Gain:** Set color gain by increasing until random background "snow" or speckle appears across non-flow tissue, then decreasing just below the noise threshold. Suboptimal color gain grossly underestimates regurgitant jet area.
3. **Color Velocity Scale:** Baseline color scale must be set between **$50\text{ and }70\text{ cm/s}$** for intracardiac flow. Lower velocity scales ($20	ext{--}40\text{ cm/s}$) are selected when searching for low-velocity pulmonary venous flow, atrial septal defect shunting, or apical thrombi.

### D. M Mode
1. **Color M Mode:** Combines the high temporal resolution of M mode ($> 1000\text{ lines/s}$) with color Doppler flow mapping, providing precise spatiotemporal timing of flow propagation (e.g., mitral flow propagation velocity $V_p$, aortic regurgitation onset).
2. **Anatomical / Steerable M Mode:** Permits digital orientation of the M-mode cursor at arbitrary angles across stored 2D cine loops, ensuring strict perpendicularity to walls (e.g., IVC, tricuspid annulus) even in non-coaxial windows.

### E. Electrocardiographic Setup
A stable, artifact-free 3-lead surface ECG is mandatory for every examination. The R-wave trigger synchronizes digital clip capture, timing calipers, and Doppler measurements. End-diastole is defined at the peak of the R wave (or frame immediately prior to mitral valve closure); end-systole is defined at the end of the T wave (or frame immediately following aortic valve closure).

| **1.16. CW Doppler Alignment** | Continuous dual-crystal line-of-sight velocity detection. | Align beam strictly parallel to high-velocity jets ($\le 20^\circ$ angle). Use non-imaging Pedoff probe when needed. |
| **1.17. Doppler Tissue Imaging (TDI)** | Bandpass filtering tuned to high-amplitude, low-velocity tissue signals. | Set velocity scale $\pm 15	ext{--}20\text{ cm/s}$, sweep speed $100\text{ mm/s}$; measure peak annular $s'$, $e'$, $a'$. |

| **1.19. Color Gain** | Preamplifier gain applied to color Doppler autocorrelation. | Increase until random background noise appears, then reduce just below speckle threshold. |
| **1.20. Color Velocity Scale** | Nyquist velocity limit governing color shift threshold. | Set between $50	ext{ and }70\text{ cm/s}$ for routine valves; lower to $20	ext{--}40\text{ cm/s}$ for ASD shunts or venous flow. |

| **1.22. Color M Mode** | Spatiotemporal color Doppler velocity registration. | Align cursor across inflow or regurgitant jet; evaluate flow propagation velocity $V_p$ or valve timing. |
| **1.23. Anatomical M Mode** | Free-angle digital M-mode reslicing from 2D cine loops. | Ensures perpendicular diameter transection through IVC, RV, or LV even in oblique transducer positions. |
| **1.24. ECG R-Wave Triggering** | Cardiac cycle synchronization based on surface ECG. | Essential for prospective/retrospective gating, end-diastolic caliper timing, and multibeat looping. |

## IV. Two-Dimensional Imaging Protocol</summary><div class="details-content">

A comprehensive TTE requires systematic interrogation of the heart from multiple windows to assess anatomy, chamber dimensions, global and regional systolic function, and hemodynamics. Images must be acquired in a logical anatomical sequence to ensure complete coverage without clinical omission.

### A. Parasternal Long-Axis (PLAX) Views
The transducer is positioned at the left third or fourth intercostal space adjacent to the sternum, with the index marker oriented toward the patient's right shoulder (approximately 10:00 to 11:00 clock position).
1. **PLAX Left Ventricle (Increased Depth):** The initial recording must be performed with increased sector depth to evaluate the posterior retrocardiac space, distinguishing pericardial effusion (located anterior to the descending thoracic aorta) from pleural effusion (located posterior to the descending aorta).
2. **PLAX Left Ventricle (Optimized):** Depth is reduced to maximize the left ventricle within the sector. The imaging plane must bisect the true LV apex, perpendicular to the interventricular septum and posterior wall at the mitral leaflet tips. Visualizes the right ventricle anteriorly, interventricular septum, LV cavity, posterior LV wall, mitral valve, aortic valve, LVOT, and left atrium.
3. **PLAX Zoomed Aortic Valve and LVOT:** Acoustic zoom is focused on the aortic root, aortic valve leaflets, and proximal tubular ascending aorta. Evaluates cusp excursion, calcification, leaflet coaptation, and provides the reference plane for mid-systolic LVOT diameter measurement.
4. **PLAX Zoomed Mitral Valve:** Acoustic zoom is focused on the anterior and posterior mitral leaflets, annulus, and chordae tendineae. Evaluates leaflet thickness, prolapse, flail segments, calcification, and systolic anterior motion (SAM).
5. **PLAX Right Ventricular Outflow Tract (RVOT) View:** The probe is tilted and angled superiorly and toward the left shoulder. Demonstrates the RVOT, pulmonic valve leaflets, and proximal main pulmonary artery.
6. **PLAX Right Ventricular Inflow View:** The probe is tilted and angled inferiorly and toward the right hip, with slight clockwise rotation. Visualizes the right atrium, coronary sinus entrance, anterior and posterior/septal tricuspid leaflets, and right ventricular inflow cavity.

### B. Parasternal Short-Axis (PSAX) Views
From the PLAX position, the transducer is rotated $90^\circ$ clockwise, orienting the index marker toward the patient's left shoulder (approximately 1:00 to 2:00 clock position). Tilting the beam from superior (base) to inferior (apex) produces four standard short-axis levels:
1. **PSAX Aortic Valve Level:** Tilted superiorly toward the base. Demonstrates the aortic valve ("Mercedes-Benz" configuration with right, left, and non-coronary cusps), interatrial septum, right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricular outflow tract, pulmonic valve, and main pulmonary artery bifurcation. Origins of the left main and right coronary arteries can frequently be visualized.
2. **PSAX Mitral Valve Level:** Tilted slightly inferiorly. Demonstrates the circular cross-section of the LV with the anterior and posterior mitral valve leaflets opening in a characteristic "fish-mouth" pattern. Interrogates basal regional wall motion (basal anteroseptal, anterior, anterolateral, inferolateral, inferior, and inferoseptal segments).
3. **PSAX Mid-Papillary Muscle Level:** Tilted to the mid-ventricle. Demonstrates the circular LV cavity with the discrete anterolateral and posteromedial papillary muscles projecting inward. Crucial reference plane for evaluating mid-ventricular regional wall motion and cross-sectional chamber geometry.
4. **PSAX LV Apex Level:** Tilted further inferiorly and angled toward the true apex. Demonstrates the small circular apical cavity without papillary muscles. Evaluates apical regional wall motion, apical thrombi, or apical hypertrophic remodeling.

### C. Apical Views
The patient remains in the steep left lateral decubitus position. The transducer is placed over the cardiac impulse at the point of maximal impulse (PMI), with the beam directed toward the right shoulder.
1. **Apical Four-Chamber (A4C) View:** The index marker is oriented toward the left axilla / examination bed (approximately 9:00 position). Demonstrates the LV lateral wall, apex, interventricular septum, right ventricle, tricuspid valve, and mitral valve. The sonographer must optimize transducer position to ensure the true LV apex is visualized without foreshortening ([Fig. 8](#fig-08)).
2. **Right Ventricle–Focused View:** From the A4C view, the probe is adjusted slightly medially or rotated to center the right ventricle within the ultrasound sector, maximizing RV basal and mid-cavity dimensions and the tricuspid annulus for quantitative evaluation (RVD1, RVD2, RVD3, TAPSE, and RVFAC).
3. **Apical Five-Chamber (A5C) View:** From the A4C view, the transducer is tilted anteriorly (rocked upward). Brings the LVOT, aortic valve, and proximal ascending aorta into view, providing the primary acoustic window for continuous-wave Doppler alignment with aortic stenosis and regurgitation jets.
4. **Coronary Sinus (CS) View:** From the A4C view, the transducer is tilted posteriorly. Demonstrates the elongated tubular cross-section of the coronary sinus traversing the posterior atrioventricular groove before entering the right atrium.
5. **Apical Two-Chamber (A2C) View:** From the A4C view, the transducer is rotated approximately $60^\circ$ counterclockwise with the index marker oriented toward the left shoulder (approximately 12:00 to 1:00 position). Visualizes the anterior wall, apex, inferior wall, mitral valve, and left atrium.
6. **Apical Long-Axis / Three-Chamber (A3C / ALAX) View:** The transducer is rotated another $30^\circ	ext{--}60^\circ$ counterclockwise with the index marker oriented toward the patient's right shoulder (approximately 10:00 to 11:00 position). Replicates the anatomical plane of the PLAX view, demonstrating the anteroseptal wall, apex, inferolateral (posterior) wall, mitral valve, aortic valve, and LVOT.
7. **Apical Focused Atrial and Pulmonary Vein Views:** Dedicated high-resolution sweeps of the atria from A4C and A2C views to assess atrial appendage geometry, atrial septal integrity, and right/left superior pulmonary venous flow profiles.

### D. Subcostal Window Views
The patient is placed in the supine position with knees flexed to relax abdominal wall tension. The transducer is placed in the epigastric angle immediately beneath the xiphoid process.
1. **Subcostal Four-Chamber View:** Transducer index marker oriented toward the left shoulder (approximately 3:00 position), aiming the beam toward the left thorax. Provides an excellent global view of all four chambers, particularly valuable in patients with hyperinflated lungs or barrel chests where parasternal and apical windows are obscured. The interatrial septum is oriented strictly perpendicular to the ultrasound beam, making this the reference window for identifying patent foramen ovale (PFO) and atrial septal defects (ASD) without dropout artifacts.
2. **Subcostal Short-Axis View:** From the subcostal four-chamber view, the transducer is rotated $90^\circ$ counterclockwise with the marker pointing toward the head. Sweeps from the great arteries through the LV apex provide alternative short-axis views.
3. **Subcostal Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) View:** The transducer is rotated so the marker points superiorly toward the patient's head (12:00 position), angling slightly to the right of the spine. Visualizes the longitudinal course of the IVC entering the right atrium, the hepatic vein convergence, and dynamic respiratory diameter changes.
4. **Subcostal Abdominal Aorta View:** The transducer is moved slightly to the left of the midline in the sagittal plane to image the abdominal aorta and assess pulsatility, caliber, and flow reversal.

### E. Suprasternal Notch (SSN) Long-Axis View
The patient is positioned supine with a pillow under the shoulders to hyperextend the neck, with the head turned slightly to the left. The transducer is seated in the jugular notch with the index marker oriented toward the left ear / left shoulder (approximately 1:00 position).
* Demonstrates the transverse aortic arch, origin of the innominate (brachiocephalic) artery, left common carotid artery, left subclavian artery, proximal descending thoracic aorta, and the right pulmonary artery in cross-section beneath the arch.
* Mandatory view for evaluating aortic coarctation, patent ductus arteriosus (PDA), aortic dissection flaps, and arch aneurysms.

## V. Two-Dimensional Linear and Volumetric Measurements</summary><div class="details-content">

Accurate quantification requires standardized anatomical landmark recognition, correct physiological timing in the cardiac cycle, and consistent caliper application conforming to ASE/EACVI chamber quantification guidelines. All 2D linear measurements must be obtained using the **inner edge–to–inner edge** convention.

### A. Parasternal Long-Axis View Measurements
1. **Left Ventricle Internal Dimensions and Wall Thickness:** Measured at the level of the mitral valve leaflet tips in the minor axis perpendicular to the long axis of the ventricle.
   - **Timing:** End-diastole is measured at the onset of the QRS complex (or frame immediately prior to mitral valve closure). End-systole is measured at the frame of maximum inward systolic excursion of the posterior wall (or frame immediately prior to aortic valve opening).
   - **Parameters:** Interventricular septal thickness in diastole (IVSd), LV internal diameter in diastole (LVEDD), LV posterior wall thickness in diastole (LVPWd), and LV internal diameter in systole (LVESD).
2. **Proximal RVOT Diameter:** Measured in end-diastole from the inner edge of the anterior RV wall to the inner edge of the interventricular septum immediately superior to the aortic valve.
3. **Anteroposterior Left Atrial Dimension:** Measured in end-systole (frame of maximum atrial expansion immediately prior to mitral valve opening) along a line perpendicular to the aortic root from the posterior aortic wall inner edge to the posterior LA wall inner edge.
4. **LVOT and Aortic Annulus Diameters:** Measured in mid-systole (at the time of maximal aortic cusp separation).
   - **Aortic Annulus:** Measured from the insertion point of the right coronary cusp to the non-coronary cusp (inner edge–to–inner edge). Critical parameter for aortic valve area continuity calculations and transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) sizing.
   - **LVOT Diameter:** Measured $3	ext{--}10\text{ mm}$ apical to the aortic annular plane, parallel to the aortic valve plane.
5. **Ascending Aorta Dimensions:** Measured in end-diastole perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of the vessel at four specific anatomical landmarks: (1) aortic annulus, (2) maximal diameter at the sinuses of Valsalva, (3) sinotubular junction, and (4) proximal tubular ascending aorta (measured at least $2\text{ cm}$ distal to the sinotubular junction).

### B. Parasternal Short-Axis View Measurements
1. **Distal RVOT Diameter:** Measured in end-diastole immediately proximal to the pulmonic valve annulus in the PSAX aortic valve view.
2. **Pulmonary Artery Main Stem:** Measured at mid-systole midway between the pulmonic valve annulus and the bifurcation into left and right branches.

### C. Apical View Measurements
1. **Left Ventricular Volumes and Ejection Fraction (Biplane Simpson's Method of Disks):**
   - The method of disks divides the LV cavity into 20 equal transverse cylindrical slices along the long axis.
   - Endocardial borders are traced in both A4C and A2C views at end-diastole and end-systole.
   - The basal contour is closed by connecting the medial and lateral mitral annular hinge points with a straight line. Trabeculations and papillary muscles are excluded from the cavity and treated as part of the myocardium.
   - Calculates Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Volume (LVEDV), End-Systolic Volume (LVESV), Stroke Volume ($SV = EDV - ESV$), and Ejection Fraction ($LVEF = [EDV - ESV] / EDV \cdot 100\%$). Normal reference threshold: LVEF $\ge 52\%$ in males, $\ge 54\%$ in females.
2. **Left Atrial Volume:**
   - Traced at end-systole (frame immediately before mitral opening) in both A4C and A2C views.
   - Confluence of the pulmonary veins and the left atrial appendage must be excluded from the tracing. The inferior border is defined by a straight line connecting the mitral annular hinge points.
   - Calculated via biplane disk summation or biplane area-length formula ($V = 0.85 \cdot A_1 \cdot A_2 / L$). Indexed to body surface area (BSA). Normal threshold: $\le 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
3. **Right Ventricular Linear Dimensions:** Measured in the RV-focused A4C view at end-diastole:
   - **RVD1 (Basal Diameter):** Maximal transverse diameter in the basal third of the RV cavity (normal $< 41\text{ mm}$).
   - **RVD2 (Mid-Cavity Diameter):** Transverse diameter at the level of the LV papillary muscles (normal $< 35\text{ mm}$).
   - **RVD3 (Longitudinal Length):** Distance from the tricuspid annular plane to the RV apex (normal $< 83\text{ mm}$).
4. **Right Ventricular Fractional Area Change (RVFAC):**
   - Manual tracing of the RV endocardial border in the RV-focused A4C view at end-diastole and end-systole.
   - Tracing extends along the free wall, apex, and septum, closed by a straight line across the tricuspid annulus.
   - Formula: $RVFAC = (Area_{diastole} - Area_{systole}) / Area_{diastole} \cdot 100\%$. Normal reference threshold: $\ge 35\%$.
5. **Right Atrial Volume:** Traced in the A4C view at end-systole, excluding IVC and SVC entrances. Calculated using single-plane disk summation. Normal threshold: $\le 25\text{ mL/m}^2$ (females), $\le 30\text{ mL/m}^2$ (males).

### D. Subcostal View Measurements
1. **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Diameter and Collapsibility:**
   - Measured in the longitudinal subcostal view $1.0	ext{--}2.0\text{ cm}$ caudal to the junction with the hepatic veins.
   - Measured at end-expiration at rest, and evaluated during a quick inspiratory "sniff" maneuver.
   - **Right Atrial Pressure (RAP) Estimation Criteria:**
     - IVC diameter $\le 2.1\text{ cm}$ collapsing $> 50\%$ with sniff $
ightarrow$ Normal RAP: **$3\text{ mmHg}$** (range $0	ext{--}5\text{ mmHg}$).
     - IVC diameter $> 2.1\text{ cm}$ collapsing $< 50\%$ with sniff $
ightarrow$ High RAP: **$15\text{ mmHg}$** (range $10	ext{--}20\text{ mmHg}$).
     - Intermediate scenarios (diameter $> 2.1\text{ cm}$ with $> 50\%$ collapse, or diameter $\le 2.1\text{ cm}$ with $< 50\%$ collapse) $
ightarrow$ Intermediate RAP: **$8\text{ mmHg}$** (range $5	ext{--}10\text{ mmHg}$).

## VI. M-Mode Measurements</summary><div class="details-content">

M-mode echocardiography provides ultra-high temporal resolution ($> 1000\text{ lines/s}$), making it invaluable for assessing rapid physiological events, valve leaflet motion, and longitudinal annular displacement.

### A. Tricuspid Annular Plane Systolic Excursion (TAPSE)
* **Technique:** In the RV-focused or RV-modified A4C view, the M-mode cursor is aligned parallel to the longitudinal excursion of the lateral tricuspid annulus.
* **Measurement:** Total vertical displacement from the baseline at end-diastole to the maximum systolic peak.
* **Clinical Significance:** Validated parameter of RV longitudinal systolic function. A TAPSE **$< 17\text{ mm}$** indicates right ventricular systolic dysfunction.

### B. Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Dynamics
* **Technique:** In the subcostal longitudinal view, the M-mode cursor is directed perpendicularly across the IVC $1.0	ext{--}2.0\text{ cm}$ caudal to the hepatic vein junction.
* **Measurement:** Continuous recording during quiet respiration and during a brief, vigorous inspiratory "sniff" maneuver. Captures dynamic minimum inspiratory and maximum expiratory calibers.

### C. Aortic Valve M-Mode
* **Technique:** In the PLAX view, the M-mode cursor is placed through the aortic valve leaflet tips.
* **Clinical Utility:** Evaluates timing of aortic valve opening, premature mid-systolic closure (characteristic of dynamic subaortic obstruction in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy), and opening duration in patients with continuous-flow left ventricular assist devices (LVADs).

## VII. Color Doppler Imaging Protocol</summary><div class="details-content">

Color Doppler imaging (CDI) visualizes the spatial distribution and direction of blood flow velocity within selected two-dimensional regions of interest. CDI must be recorded immediately following 2D anatomical clips in all standard views.

### Systematic Flow Interrogation
1. **RVOT, Pulmonic Valve, and Main Pulmonary Artery:** Interrogated in PLAX RVOT and PSAX views. Detects pulmonic regurgitation (PR) jets and RVOT subvalvular acceleration.
2. **RV Inflow and Tricuspid Valve:** Evaluated in PLAX RV inflow, PSAX AV level, and apical 4-chamber views. Identifies tricuspid regurgitation (TR) jet origin, direction (eccentric vs. central), vena contracta width, and provides spatial guidance for continuous-wave Doppler cursor alignment.
3. **LV Inflow and Mitral Valve:** Evaluated in PLAX, A4C, A2C, and A3C views. Color Doppler identifies physiological diastolic filling, mitral regurgitation (MR) jets, vena contracta, and proximal isovelocity surface area (PISA) shells. Regurgitant jets must be interrogated in multiple planes to distinguish functional/secondary MR from primary organic prolapse/flail jets.
4. **LVOT and Aortic Valve:** Interrogated in PLAX, A5C, and A3C views. Evaluates systolic forward flow for dynamic subaortic obstruction, flow acceleration in aortic stenosis, and diastolic aortic regurgitation (AR) jets (evaluating jet height-to-LVOT height ratio and vena contracta).
5. **Aortic Arch and Descending Thoracic Aorta:** Interrogated in the suprasternal notch window. Identifies turbulent post-stenotic jets in coarctation of the aorta and holodiastolic retrograde flow in severe aortic regurgitation.
6. **Pulmonary Veins:** Evaluated in A4C with the color scale lowered ($20	ext{--}40\text{ cm/s}$) to locate right and left superior pulmonary venous inflow entering the posterolateral LA.
7. **Hepatic Veins and IVC:** Interrogated in the subcostal window. Evaluates holosystolic flow reversal indicative of severe tricuspid regurgitation.
8. **Interatrial Septum:** Interrogated in subcostal 4-chamber and parasternal short-axis views with lowered color scales ($20	ext{--}40\text{ cm/s}$) and Nyquist baseline shift to detect low-velocity left-to-right shunting across patent foramen ovale (PFO) or atrial septal defects (ASD).

## VIII. Spectral Doppler Imaging Measurements</summary><div class="details-content">

Spectral Doppler echocardiography provides quantitative hemodynamic velocities and pressure gradients. Doppler shifts are directionally limited: the maximum Doppler shift is detected when the ultrasound beam is parallel to blood flow ($	heta = 0^\circ, \cos 0^\circ = 1.0$). If the ultrasound beam is angled away from the flow vector, velocity is underestimated by a factor of $\cos \theta$. Interrogation angle must remain $\le 20^\circ$ ($\le 6\%$ error); mathematical angle correction is discouraged in cardiac ultrasound due to complex, three-dimensional intracardiac flow vectors.

All spectral Doppler recordings must be obtained at a sweep speed of **$100\text{ mm/s}$** for velocity and time-interval measurements, and **$25	ext{--}50\text{ mm/s}$** for assessing respiratory variations.

### Systematic Doppler Protocol by Valve
1. **RVOT and Pulmonic Valve:**
   - **PW Doppler:** Sample volume ($2	ext{--}3\text{ mm}$) placed immediately proximal to the pulmonic valve leaflets in the PSAX or PLAX RVOT view. Measures peak forward systolic velocity and acceleration time (short acceleration time $< 100\text{ ms}$ indicates elevated pulmonary vascular resistance).
   - **CW Doppler:** Positioned across the pulmonic valve. Measures peak pulmonic velocity and peak end-diastolic pulmonic regurgitation velocity (PR end-diastolic gradient $= 4v^2 + \text{RAP} = \text{pulmonary artery diastolic pressure}$).
2. **Tricuspid Valve:**
   - **CW Doppler:** Positioned across the tricuspid valve guided by color Doppler to align with the highest-velocity TR jet.
   - **PASP Calculation:** The modified Bernoulli equation calculates the peak systolic pressure difference between the right ventricle and right atrium: $\Delta P = 4 \cdot (V_{\text{TR}})^2$. Pulmonary artery systolic pressure is derived as:
     $$\text{PASP} = 4(V_{\text{TR}})^2 + \text{RAP}$$
     where RAP is estimated from IVC caliber and collapsibility ($3\text{ mmHg}$, $8\text{ mmHg}$, or $15\text{ mmHg}$).
3. **Mitral Valve (Inflow and Regurgitation):**
   - **PW Inflow:** Sample volume ($1	ext{--}2\text{ mm}$) placed precisely at the tips of the mitral leaflets in A4C during diastole. Measures peak early filling velocity ($E$), peak atrial contraction velocity ($A$), $E/A$ ratio, and deceleration time ($DT$).
   - **CW Doppler:** Aligned through the center of the mitral regurgitant jet. Measures peak MR velocity and density of the spectral profile; enables calculation of LV rate of pressure rise ($dP/dt$) during isovolumic contraction.
4. **LVOT and Aortic Valve:**
   - **PW LVOT Velocity:** Sample volume ($2	ext{--}3\text{ mm}$) placed in the LVOT immediately apical ($3	ext{--}5\text{ mm}$) to the aortic valve leaflets in A5C or A3C. A clean, smooth laminar spectral curve with a distinct closing click must be recorded to measure the LVOT Velocity-Time Integral ($VTI_{\text{LVOT}}$) and peak velocity.
   - **CW Aortic Valve Velocity:** Continuous-wave cursor directed through the aortic valve from A5C, A3C, suprasternal notch, or right parasternal window (using dedicated non-imaging Pedoff probe). Measures peak aortic velocity ($V_{\max}$), mean gradient (by automated trace of the outer VTI envelope), and peak gradient ($4V_{\max}^2$).
   - **Continuity Equation for Aortic Valve Area (AVA):**
     $$AVA = \frac{CSA_{\text{LVOT}} \cdot VTI_{\text{LVOT}}}{VTI_{\text{AV}}} = \frac{\pi \cdot (D_{\text{LVOT}} / 2)^2 \cdot VTI_{\text{LVOT}}}{VTI_{\text{AV}}}$$
   - **Doppler Velocity Index (DVI):** Dimensionless ratio independent of LVOT diameter: $DVI = VTI_{\text{LVOT}} / VTI_{\text{AV}}$ (severe aortic stenosis threshold: $DVI < 0.25$).
5. **Aortic Arch and Descending Aorta:**
   - Evaluated in the SSN window using PW and CW Doppler. CW identifies high-velocity systolic flow across coarctation shelves; PW in the descending aorta detects holodiastolic flow reversal indicative of severe aortic regurgitation.
6. **Pulmonary Veins:**
   - PW Doppler sample volume ($2	ext{--}3\text{ mm}$) placed $1\text{--}2\text{ cm}$ into the right superior pulmonary vein in A4C. Records peak systolic velocity ($S$), peak diastolic velocity ($D$), $S/D$ ratio, and atrial reversal wave velocity ($A_r$) and duration ($A_{r\text{ dur}}$). A prolonged $A_{r\text{ dur}} > DT_{\text{mitral A}} + 30\text{ ms}$ indicates elevated LV end-diastolic filling pressure.
7. **Hepatic Veins:**
   - PW Doppler in the subcostal window with sample volume in the middle or right hepatic vein. Evaluates forward systolic ($S$) and diastolic ($D$) flow, and detects prominent systolic flow reversal pathognomonic of severe tricuspid regurgitation.
8. **Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI) of Annular Motion:**
   - PW Doppler sample volume ($5	ext{--}10\text{ mm}$) placed on the septal and lateral mitral annulus in A4C.
   - Measures peak systolic myocardial velocity ($s'$), early diastolic velocity ($e'$), and late diastolic velocity ($a'$).
   - **Average $E/e'$ Ratio:** Calculated as $E_{\text{mitral}} / \text{average } e'_{(\text{septal} + \text{lateral})}$. Ratio $> 14$ indicates elevated left ventricular filling pressures ($LAP > 12	ext{--}15\text{ mmHg}$).
   - Lateral tricuspid annular $s'$ velocity is measured to assess RV systolic function (normal $s' \ge 9.5\text{ cm/s}$).

## IX. Additional Advanced Echocardiographic Techniques</summary><div class="details-content">

Advanced echocardiographic modalities expand diagnostic sensitivity when fundamental 2D and Doppler techniques are inconclusive or limited by acoustic barriers.

### A. Agitated-Saline Contrast Imaging (Bubble Study)
Agitated saline creates microbubbles ($> 10\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) that opacify the right-sided cardiac chambers but cannot pass through the normal pulmonary capillary bed ($7	ext{--}10\text{ }\mu\text{m}$).
* **Preparation and Administration:** Two $10\text{ mL}$ syringes connected via a heavy-duty three-way stopcock. Standard recipe: $9\text{ mL}$ bacteriostatic normal saline mixed with $1\text{ mL}$ room air, or $8\text{ mL}$ saline $+ 1\text{ mL}$ blood $+ 1\text{ mL}$ air (blood enhances microbubble persistence and acoustic reflectivity). The mixture is agitated vigorously between syringes at least 10–20 times until a dense, opaque white microcavitation suspension forms, then injected as a rapid intravenous bolus through an 18- or 20-gauge catheter in the right antecubital vein.
* **Recording Protocol:** An extended digital clip of at least 15–20 consecutive cardiac cycles is initiated in A4C or subcostal 4-chamber view *prior* to injection.
* **Diagnostic Criteria for Shunts:**
  - **Intracardiac Shunt (PFO / ASD):** Microbubbles appear in the left atrium and left ventricle within **3 to 6 cardiac cycles** following complete opacification of the right atrium.
  - **Extracardiac / Intrapulmonary Shunt (Pulmonary Arteriovenous Malformation - AVM):** Microbubbles appear in the left atrium after **$\ge 5	ext{--}10$ cardiac cycles**, often clearing from pulmonary veins.
* **Provocative Maneuvers:** If the resting bubble study is negative, provocative maneuvers (sustained Valsalva strain for 10 seconds followed by abrupt release as bubbles enter the RA, forceful cough, or transient abdominal compression) must be performed. Release of Valsalva transiently elevates right atrial pressure above left atrial pressure, forcing open a compliant patent foramen ovale flap.

### B. Ultrasound Enhancing Agents (UEA / Contrast Echocardiography)
UEAs consist of biocompatible lipid or albumin shells encapsulating high-molecular-weight fluorocarbon gas (perflutren, sulfur hexafluoride). Microbubble diameter ($1	ext{--}5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) allows free transpulmonary capillary transit, producing dense opacification of the left ventricular cavity and enhancing myocardial perfusion.
1. **Clinical Indications:**
   - Suboptimal endocardial visualization where **$\ge 2$ contiguous LV segments** cannot be adequately evaluated in any standard apical view on non-contrast imaging.
   - Evaluation of suspected LV apical thrombus, apical hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, non-compaction cardiomyopathy, myocardial rupture/pseudoaneurysm, and stress echocardiography wall-motion assessment.
2. **Instrumentation and Physics:**
   - **Mechanical Index (MI):** Conventional diagnostic ultrasound ($MI > 1.0$) causes instantaneous microbubble destruction. Contrast imaging requires specialized low-MI non-linear pulsing software ($MI \text{ set between } 0.15 \text{ and } 0.30$).
   - **Focus:** Focal zone must be positioned at the level of the mitral valve or posterior wall (never at the apex) to avoid acoustic destruction of apical contrast.
   - **Gain and Artifacts:** Avoid high bolus rates to prevent near-field acoustic shadowing. Swirling artifacts occur when contrast is under-dosed or destroyed by excessive acoustic power.
3. **Image Acquisition Sequence:** Standardized low-MI loops are recorded across A4C, A2C, A3C, PLAX, and PSAX views.

### C. Myocardial Strain Imaging (Speckle-Tracking Echocardiography)
Two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography tracks acoustic "fingerprints" (natural acoustic interference speckles within the myocardium) frame-by-frame independent of ultrasound beam insonation angles.
* **Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS):** Represents the percentage systolic shortening of the left ventricular myocardium along its long axis relative to end-diastole:
  $$GLS = \frac{L_{\text{systole}} - L_{\text{diastole}}}{L_{\text{diastole}}} \times 100\%$$
* **Technical Protocol:**
  - Recorded from three apical views: A4C, A2C, and A3C.
  - Frame rate must be strictly optimized between **$50\text{ and }80\text{ frames/s}$**.
  - Endocardial borders are traced; the software automatically generates an epicardial boundary and segments the myocardium into a 17- or 18-segment polar "bull's-eye" display. Tracking must be visually validated; poorly tracked segments should be readjusted or excluded.
* **Diagnostic Reference Limits:** Normal adult GLS is typically more negative than **$-20\%$** (e.g., $-21\%$ is normal; $-14\%$ represents severe systolic impairment). A relative reduction of $> 15\%$ from baseline during chemotherapy is diagnostic of subclinical cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD).

### D. Three-Dimensional (3D) Echocardiography
3D matrix-array transducers acquire pyramidal volume datasets of the heart.
* **Advantages:** Eliminates geometric shape assumptions and foreshortening errors inherent in 2D biplane Simpson's estimations. Validated against cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) as the most accurate echocardiographic method for LV volumes, RV volumes, and ejection fraction.
* **Acquisition Modes:** Multi-beat stitched acquisition (4–6 cardiac cycles during breath-hold) provides high temporal resolution ($> 40	ext{--}50\text{ volumes/s}$), but is susceptible to stitch artifacts if arrhythmias or patient respiration occur. Single-beat real-time full-volume acquisition eliminates stitch artifacts at the expense of lower temporal resolution.

## X. The Integrated Complete Transthoracic Examination</summary><div class="details-content">

A comprehensive adult transthoracic echocardiogram is an integrated medical procedure requiring smooth, ergonomic, and sequential progression across all acoustic windows. The American Society of Echocardiography recommends a standardized sequence of approximately 70 discrete digital recordings (cine loops and calibrated still frames) to maximize diagnostic yield and laboratory workflow efficiency.

Laboratories must establish written quality assurance policies, sonographer scanning protocols, and peer-review processes to ensure that full-protocol examinations are uniformly performed and documented.

### Complete Recommended Examination Sequence
The standardized protocol begins in the left lateral decubitus position with the parasternal long-axis window, transitions through short-axis levels to the apical window, proceeds to the supine position for subcostal imaging, and concludes in the suprasternal notch.

## XI. The Limited / Focused Transthoracic Examination</summary><div class="details-content">

The Intersocietal Accreditation Commission (IAC) and ASE define a limited echocardiographic examination as a targeted, problem-focused evaluation performed to answer a single clinical question in a patient who has recently undergone a comprehensive baseline examination, or in an unstable patient in the emergency department, intensive care unit, or perioperative suite.

A limited examination is never a substitute for a comprehensive evaluation, but must adhere to strict standardized sub-protocols to ensure safety and diagnostic accuracy.

### Standardized Focused Clinical Protocols
1. **Targeted Examination for Pericardial Effusion and Tamponade:**
   - Evaluates size and distribution of effusion, distinguishing pericardial fluid from pleural fluid in PLAX.
   - Assesses hemodynamic compromise: right ventricular early diastolic free-wall collapse (highly specific), right atrial late diastolic collapse (highly sensitive if lasting $> 1/3$ of the cardiac cycle), IVC plethora (dilated IVC without respiratory collapse), and exaggerated respiratory variation in transmitral ($> 25	ext{--}30\%$) and transtricuspid ($> 40	ext{--}50\%$) pulsed-wave Doppler inflow velocities at slow sweep speeds ($25	ext{ mm/s}$).
2. **Targeted Examination for Left Ventricular Systolic Function and Shock:**
   - Assesses global LV systolic performance, chamber dimensions, regional wall motion abnormalities, end-systolic cavity obliteration (hypovolemia/hyperdynamic shock), and gross valvular catastrophes (acute MR, chordal rupture, acute AR).
3. **Targeted Examination for Right Ventricular Failure and Suspected Pulmonary Embolism:**
   - Evaluates acute RV pressure/volume overload: RV dilatation ($RVD1 > LV$ diameter), paradoxical systolic septal flattening ("D-shaped" LV in PSAX), McConnell's sign (akinesia of the mid RV free wall with preserved or hyperdynamic apical contractility, highly suggestive of acute pulmonary embolism), tricuspid regurgitation peak velocity, and 60/60 sign (pulmonary acceleration time $< 60\text{ ms}$ with peak TR gradient $< 60\text{ mmHg}$).

## XII. Appendix: Nonstandard and Alternative Acoustic Views</summary><div class="details-content">

In patients with complex congenital heart disease, thoracic deformities, severe emphysema, surgical dressings, or chest tubes, standard acoustic windows may be severely compromised. The ASE protocol incorporates alternative acoustic windows to visualize specific structures and overcome acoustic barriers.

### Alternative Views Atlas
1. **PSAX Coronary Artery View:** High-resolution zoom of the aortic valve plane in PSAX. Visualizes the left main bifurcation into LAD and LCx, and the proximal right coronary artery to exclude anomalous origins or ostial aneurysms.
2. **Right Ventricle Two-Chamber View:** From the apical window, rotating slightly clockwise from A4C demonstrates the junction of the right atrium, SVC, anterior and inferior RV walls, and tricuspid valve apparatus.
3. **Subcostal Bicaval View (SVC/IVC):** Transducer oriented in the sagittal plane from the subxiphoid window, demonstrating simultaneous entry of the SVC and IVC into the right atrium. Essential for evaluating cavoatrial junctions, central venous catheters, pacemaker/ICD leads, and sinus venosus atrial septal defects.
4. **Subcostal Abdominal Aorta View:** Slices the abdominal aorta longitudinally and transversely, evaluating for aneurysms, dissections, and persistent diastolic forward/reverse flow.
5. **Right Lateral Transhepatic IVC View:** In patients with severe abdominal distension or bowel gas obscuring the subxiphoid window, placing the probe on the right mid-axillary line and imaging through the hepatic acoustic window allows clear visualization of the IVC.
6. **Transverse Subcostal IVC View:** Cross-sectional imaging of the IVC and abdominal aorta, ensuring true minor-axis diameter measurement without tangential volume averaging.
7. **Subcostal Focused Interatrial Septum:** Acoustic zoom on the fossa ovalis in the subcostal window, ideal for pulsed-wave and color Doppler evaluation of low-velocity shunts without dropout artifacts.
8. **Subcostal Short-Axis RVOT View:** Alternative view for imaging the RVOT, pulmonic valve, and main pulmonary artery bifurcation in patients with COPD.
9. **Subcostal Full Sweep from Great Arteries to Apex:** Complete short-axis sweep performed from the epigastrium, substituting for parasternal short-axis imaging in patients on mechanical ventilation.
10. **Right Parasternal Ascending Aorta View:** Transducer positioned in the second or third right intercostal space, with the patient in the right lateral decubitus position. Utilizing a dedicated small-footprint continuous-wave Doppler transducer (Pedoff probe) aligns the beam coaxially with the ascending aortic flow jet in elderly patients with horizontal or dilated aortic roots, frequently uncovering the true peak aortic stenosis velocity when apical windows underestimate it.
11. **SSN Innominate Veins View:** Demonstrates the left innominate vein crossing anterior to the aortic arch to join the right innominate vein and SVC. Identifies persistent left SVC, thrombosis, and arteriovenous malformations.
12. **SSN Short-Axis "Crab View" (LA and Pulmonary Veins):** Rotating the transducer $90^\circ$ from the SSN long axis demonstrates the left atrium superiorly and the simultaneous drainage of all four pulmonary veins (right and left superior and inferior veins), resembling the legs of a crab. Critical for excluding anomalous pulmonary venous return.
13. **Color M-Mode Flow Propagation Velocity ($V_p$):** In A4C, aligning the M-mode cursor through the mitral orifice into the LV apex with color flow Doppler maps the velocity at which the early diastolic inflow jet propagates toward the apex. A $V_p < 45\text{ cm/s}$ reflects delayed LV active relaxation.
