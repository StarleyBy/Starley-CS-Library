## Executive Summary: Top 10 Take-Home Messages</summary><div class="details-content">

1. **Four Pillars of Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF:** Pharmacotherapy for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) now rests upon four foundational medication classes: (1) SGLT2 inhibitors; (2) beta-blockers; (3) mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs); and (4) renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, with angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNI) recommended as the preferred first-line agent over ACE inhibitors or ARBs.
2. **SGLT2 Inhibitors Across the LVEF Spectrum:** SGLT2 inhibitors hold a Class 2a recommendation in heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF) and in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), significantly reducing cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalizations regardless of the presence of type 2 diabetes mellitus.
3. **Multifaceted Medical Therapy for HFpEF:** In patients with HFpEF, evidence-based management includes SGLT2 inhibitors (Class 2a), MRAs (Class 2b), and ARNI (Class 2b), alongside rigorous blood pressure control (Class 1), management of atrial fibrillation (Class 2a), and avoiding routine use of nitrates or phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (Class 3: No Benefit).
4. **Heart Failure With Improved Ejection Fraction (HFimpEF):** A dedicated category is established for patients with previous HFrEF who experience documented recovery of LVEF to $> 40\%$. GDMT must be maintained indefinitely in HFimpEF, as treatment withdrawal precipitates relapse of LV dysfunction and clinical decompensation.
5. **Formal Value and Cost-Effectiveness Statements:** High-value statements are integrated for interventions supported by published high-quality economic analyses, demonstrating that ARNI, SGLT2 inhibitors, and CRT provide high economic value and cost-effectiveness in clinical practice.
6. **Amyloid Cardiomyopathy Diagnostic and Treatment Pathway:** Comprehensive recommendations for cardiac amyloidosis include routine screening with serum and urine immunofixation electrophoresis and serum free light chains (to exclude AL amyloidosis), $^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc}$-PYP/DPD bone scintigraphy (to diagnose ATTR amyloidosis noninvasively), genetic testing (*TTR* sequencing), tetramer stabilizer therapy (tafamidis, Class 1), and universal anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation.
7. **Documenting Elevated Filling Pressures When LVEF $> 40\%$:** Objective confirmation of elevated left ventricular filling pressures—obtained noninvasively (elevated natriuretic peptides, Doppler diastolic parameters, left atrial enlargement) or invasively (hemodynamic catheterization at rest or exercise)—is required to confirm the diagnosis of HFmrEF or HFpEF.
8. **Timely Specialty Referral for Advanced HF (Stage D):** Patients with advanced heart failure who desire survival prolongation should be referred promptly to a specialized multidisciplinary heart failure team (using the "I-NEED-HELP" clinical triggers) to evaluate eligibility for mechanical circulatory support (durable LVAD), orthotopic heart transplantation, or palliative inotropes.
9. **Revised Staging System Emphasizing Prevention:** The ACC/AHA stages are updated with refined terminology: **Stage A: At Risk for HF** (hypertension, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, cardiotoxic exposure) and **Stage B: Pre-HF** (asymptomatic structural heart disease, elevated filling pressures, or abnormal natriuretic peptides/troponins), emphasizing early lifestyle modification and neurohormonal protection before symptom onset.
10. **Targeted Comorbidity Management:** Specific guideline-directed recommendations address prevalent comorbidities in heart failure, including intravenous iron repletion for iron deficiency, rhythm control and catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation, continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) for obstructive sleep apnea, and SGLT2 inhibitors for type 2 diabetes.

## 1. Introduction: Scope, Methodology, and Recommendation System</summary><div class="details-content">

### 1.1 Scope of the Guideline
Heart failure is a complex clinical syndrome resulting from structural or functional impairment of ventricular filling or ejection. This guideline provides contemporary, evidence-based recommendations for clinicians to prevent, diagnose, and manage heart failure across the full continuum of ejection fraction and disease stages.

### 1.2 Class of Recommendation and Level of Evidence

#### Table 2: Applying ACC/AHA Class of Recommendation and Level of Evidence
* **Class 1 (Strong):** Benefit >>> Risk. The treatment/intervention SHOULD be performed.
* **Class 2a (Moderate):** Benefit >> Risk. The treatment/intervention is REASONABLE.
* **Class 2b (Weak):** Benefit $\ge$ Risk. The treatment/intervention MAY BE CONSIDERED.
* **Class 3: No Benefit (Moderate):** Benefit = Risk. The treatment/intervention is NOT recommended (no clinical efficacy).
* **Class 3: Harm (Strong):** Risk > Benefit. The treatment/intervention causes HARM and should NOT be performed.

**Levels of Evidence (LOE):**
* **Level A:** High-quality evidence from $> 1$ randomized controlled trial (RCT), or meta-analyses of high-quality RCTs.
* **Level B-R (Randomized):** Moderate-quality evidence from 1 or more RCTs.
* **Level B-NR (Nonrandomized):** Moderate-quality evidence from well-designed observational studies or registries.
* **Level C-LD (Limited Data):** Observational or registry studies with design limitations.
* **Level C-EO (Expert Opinion):** Consensus of expert opinion based on clinical experience.

#### Table 3: ACC/AHA Stages of Heart Failure

| Stage | Definition and Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Stage A: At Risk for HF** | At risk for HF but without current or previous symptoms/signs of HF, without structural heart disease, and without cardiac biomarkers of stretch or injury (e.g., patients with hypertension, atherosclerotic CVD, diabetes, metabolic syndrome and obesity, exposure to cardiotoxic agents, genetic variant for cardiomyopathy, or positive family history of cardiomyopathy). |
| **Stage B: Pre-HF** | No current or previous symptoms/signs of HF AND evidence of 1 of the following: (1) Structural heart disease (reduced LV or RV systolic function, LV hypertrophy, chamber enlargement, wall motion abnormalities, valvular heart disease); (2) Evidence of increased filling pressures (by invasive hemodynamic measurements or noninvasive imaging); (3) Patients with risk factors AND increased levels of BNP/NT-proBNP OR persistently elevated cardiac troponin in the absence of competing diagnoses. |
| **Stage C: Symptomatic HF** | Structural heart disease with current or previous symptoms/signs of HF. |
| **Stage D: Advanced HF** | Marked HF symptoms that interfere with daily life and with recurrent hospitalizations despite attempts to optimize guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT). |


### 2.2 Classification of Heart Failure by Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF)
Heart failure is phenotypically categorized according to resting left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), which dictates specific evidence-based therapeutic pathways:
* **HFrEF (Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction):** LVEF **$\le 40\%$**. Characterized by progressive chamber dilatation, eccentric remodeling, and primary systolic contractile failure.
* **HFmrEF (Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction):** LVEF **$41\%\text{ to }49\%$**. Represents a transitional phenotype with features overlapping HFrEF and HFpEF. Requires evidence of spontaneous or provoked elevated LV filling pressures.
* **HFpEF (Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction):** LVEF **$\ge 50\%$**. Driven primarily by diastolic dysfunction, concentric remodeling, impaired active relaxation, and systemic vascular inflammation. Requires objective proof of elevated LV filling pressures (natriuretic peptides, invasive hemodynamics, or stress echocardiography).
* **HFimpEF (Heart Failure with Improved Ejection Fraction):** Baseline LVEF $\le 40\%$ with a documented interval increase of $\ge 10$ points to a second measurement **$> 40\%$**. These patients remain biologically distinct from de novo HFpEF and require lifelong maintenance of GDMT.

## 3. Epidemiology and Causes of Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

### 3.1 Epidemiology of Heart Failure
* More than **6 million adults** in the United States currently live with heart failure, with prevalence projected to exceed 8 million by 2030 (a 46% increase).
* Lifetime risk of developing heart failure at age 40 exceeds **20% to 25%** for both men and women.
* Despite therapeutic advances, 5-year all-cause mortality following heart failure diagnosis remains approximately 50%, highlighting the paramount importance of early primary prevention.

### 3.2 Etiologies of Heart Failure
* **Ischemic Heart Disease:** Accounts for approximately 50% to 60% of all HFrEF cases, resulting from previous myocardial infarction, severe multivessel coronary artery disease, or chronic ischemic hibernation.
* **Nonischemic Cardiomyopathies:** Encompass diverse genetic, inflammatory, toxic, infiltrative, metabolic, and systemic disorders.

## 4. Initial and Serial Clinical Evaluation</summary><div class="details-content">

### 4.1 History, Physical Examination, Laboratory, and ECG Testing
* **Initial Evaluation:** Thorough assessment of cardiovascular risk factors, family history of cardiomyopathy or sudden death, alcohol/substance use, chemotherapeutic exposures, and functional limitation using the New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification.
* **Physical Examination:** Systematic evaluation of jugular venous distension, hepatojugular reflux, third heart sound ($S_3$ gallop), laterally displaced apical impulse, pulmonary crackles, and peripheral edema.
* **Laboratory Testing:** Complete blood count, serum electrolytes, blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, estimated GFR, liver function tests, fasting lipid profile, hemoglobin A1c, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and urinalysis.
* **12-Lead Electrocardiogram:** Evaluates cardiac rhythm, QRS complex duration, presence of bundle branch block (especially left bundle branch block [LBBB] $\ge 150	ext{ ms}$ for CRT candidacy), left ventricular hypertrophy, and prior pathological Q waves.

### 4.2 Use of Biomarkers: Natriuretic Peptides and Troponins
* **Diagnostic Biomarkers:** In patients presenting with acute dyspnea, measurement of **B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP)** or **N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP)** is recommended to support or exclude the diagnosis of decompensated heart failure (Class 1, Level of Evidence A).
  - Diagnostic exclusion cutoffs: BNP $< 100	ext{ pg/mL}$ or NT-proBNP $< 300	ext{ pg/mL}$ have high negative predictive value ($> 95\%$) to rule out acute HF.
  - Obesity lowers circulating natriuretic peptide levels by approximately 50%, whereas advanced age, female sex, renal dysfunction, and atrial fibrillation cause non-hemodynamic elevations.
  - **Interaction with ARNI:** Sacubitril inhibits neprilysin degradation of BNP, leading to elevated circulating BNP levels; NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate and reliably reflects clinical response to ARNI therapy.
* **Prognostic Stratification:** Baseline and predischarge natriuretic peptide levels strongly predict postdischarge rehospitalization and mortality (Class 1, Level A).
* **Cardiac Troponins:** Measurement of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I or T is recommended in patients presenting with acute heart failure to detect concurrent myocardial injury and establish prognosis (Class 1, Level A).

### 4.3 Genetic Evaluation and Testing
* **Indications:** In patients with nonischemic cardiomyopathy, genetic evaluation and testing with targeted next-generation sequencing cardiomyopathy panels should be considered when there is a family history of cardiomyopathy, early unexplained sudden cardiac death, conduction system disease, or specific phenotypic syndromic features (Class 2a, Level of Evidence B-NR).
* Cascade clinical and genetic screening should be offered to first-degree relatives of probands harboring pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants.

#### Table 7: Examples of Factors Implicating Possible Genetic Cardiomyopathy

* Concomitant skeletal myopathy, muscular dystrophy, or elevated creatine kinase
* Progressive conduction system disease (AV block) at young age (e.g., *LMNA, PRKAG2*)
* Recurrent sustained ventricular arrhythmias out of proportion to LV dysfunction
* Sensorineural hearing loss, pigmentary retinopathy, or lipodystrophy
* Facial dysmorphism, lentigines, or renal angiomyolipomas

### 4.4 Evaluation With Cardiac Imaging
* **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line imaging recommended for all patients with suspected HF (Class 1, Level of Evidence A) to quantify LVEF, chamber dimensions, regional wall motion, wall thickness, diastolic function, and valvular regurgitation/stenosis.
* **Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance (CMR):** Indicated when echocardiography is suboptimal or when tissue characterization is critical to establish the underlying etiology (e.g., myocarditis, sarcoidosis, amyloidosis, hemochromatosis, Fabry disease) and quantify late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) (Class 1, Level B-NR).
* **Cardiac CT:** Reasonable for evaluating coronary anatomy in patients with low-to-intermediate pretest probability of CAD who have contraindications to CMR.

### 4.5 Invasive Evaluation
* **Invasive Hemodynamic Assessment (Right Heart Catheterization):** Recommended in patients with persistent cardiogenic shock, respiratory failure, uncertain volume status, or when evaluating candidacy for mechanical circulatory support or heart transplantation (Class 1, Level B-NR).
* **Endomyocardial Biopsy (EMB):** Reasonable when a specific systemic or myocardial infiltrative/inflammatory etiology is suspected (e.g., giant cell myocarditis, eosinophilic myocarditis, cardiac amyloidosis) where histological verification directly alters management (Class 2a, Level B-NR).

### 4.6 Wearables and Remote Monitoring
* Remote pulmonary artery pressure monitoring (e.g., CardioMEMS) is reasonable in selected symptomatic patients (NYHA class III) with a prior HF hospitalization to guide diuretic titration and reduce recurrent hospitalizations (Class 2b, Level B-R; CHAMPION trial).

### 4.7 Exercise and Functional Capacity Testing
* Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) measuring peak oxygen consumption ($V\text{O}_2\text{ peak}$) is recommended when evaluating patients for advanced therapies (LVAD or heart transplantation) or differentiating cardiac from pulmonary causes of exercise intolerance (Class 1, Level B-NR). A peak $V\text{O}_2 \le 12\text{--}14\text{ mL/kg/min}$ identifies severe impairment and supports advanced listing.

### 4.8 Clinical Assessment: HF Risk Scoring
* Multivariable clinical risk scores (e.g., Seattle Heart Failure Model [SHFM], MAGGIC score) are reasonable to estimate mortality and guide therapeutic intensity and goals of care discussions (Class 2a, Level B-NR).

#### Table 8: Selected Multivariable Risk Scores to Predict Mortality in Heart Failure
* **Seattle Heart Failure Model (SHFM):** Incorporates clinical characteristics, laboratory values, medications, and device therapies to predict 1-, 2-, and 5-year survival.
* **MAGGIC Heart Failure Risk Score:** Validated across both HFrEF and HFpEF cohorts using 13 clinical variables to predict 1- and 3-year mortality.
* **Get With The Guidelines-Heart Failure (GWTG-HF) Score:** Predicts in-hospital mortality during acute heart failure hospitalization based on admission clinical parameters.

## 5. Stage A: Patients at Risk for Heart Failure (Primary Prevention)</summary><div class="details-content">

Stage A represents individuals at high risk for developing heart failure who have no symptoms, no structural heart disease, and no biomarker evidence of myocardial stretch or injury:
* **Blood Pressure Control:** In patients with hypertension, aggressive blood pressure lowering to achieve a systolic target $< 130\text{ mm Hg}$ is recommended to prevent the transition to symptomatic heart failure (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; SPRINT trial).
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors in Type 2 Diabetes:** In patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and established cardiovascular disease or high cardiovascular risk, treatment with an SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) is recommended to prevent heart failure hospitalizations (Class 1, Level A; EMPA-REG, CANVAS, DECLARE-TIMI 58).
* **Healthy Lifestyle:** Regular physical activity, maintaining healthy weight, smoking cessation, and avoidance of excessive alcohol consumption are recommended for all individuals at risk (Class 1, Level B-NR).
* **Natriuretic Peptide Screening:** In patients with cardiovascular risk factors, biomarker-based screening (measuring BNP/NT-proBNP) followed by team-based collaborative care is reasonable to prevent structural heart disease and clinical heart failure (Class 2a, Level B-R; STOP-HF trial).

## 6. Stage B: Patients With Pre-Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Stage B identifies asymptomatic individuals who have evidence of structural heart disease, increased filling pressures, or persistently elevated natriuretic peptides or cardiac troponins:
* **Asymptomatic Reduced LVEF ($\le 40\%$):**
  - **ACE Inhibitors or ARBs:** Recommended in patients with asymptomatic LVEF $\le 40\%$ to prevent symptomatic heart failure and reduce mortality (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; SOLVD Prevention trial).
  - **Beta-Blockers:** Evidence-based beta-blockers (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol) are recommended in patients with asymptomatic LVEF $\le 40\%$ to prevent progression to clinical heart failure (Class 1, Level A).
* **Post-Myocardial Infarction Management:**
  - **Statins:** Recommended in all patients with prior MI to prevent heart failure development (Class 1, Level A).
  - **Primary Prevention ICD:** In patients with prior MI and LVEF $\le 30\%$ on optimal medical therapy (at least 40 days post-MI), prophylactic ICD implantation is recommended to reduce sudden cardiac death (Class 1, Level A; MADIT-II).
* **Avoidance of Harm (Class 3: Harm):** Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) with negative inotropic effects should not be used in patients with asymptomatic reduced LVEF.

## 7. Stage C Heart Failure: Symptomatic Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Stage C designates patients with current or prior symptoms of heart failure caused by structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities. Management depends upon phenotypic classification according to LVEF.

### 7.1 Nonpharmacological Interventions

#### 7.1.1 Self-Care Support in Heart Failure
Self-care support is an essential component of comprehensive heart failure management:
* **Multidisciplinary Education:** Patients with heart failure should receive specific education and support on symptom monitoring (daily morning weights), flexible diuretic adjustments, low-sodium dietary habits, medication adherence, and early recognition of decompensation (Class 1, Level of Evidence A).

#### Table 11: Potential Barriers to Effective HF Self-Care and Example Interventions
* **Cognitive Impairment / Depression:** Structured pill organizers, caregiver involvement, simplified once-daily regimens, routine depression screening.
* **Low Health Literacy / Language Barriers:** Culturally tailored bilingual visual aids, "teach-back" educational methodology.
* **Financial and Transportation Hardships:** Social work consultation, patient assistance drug programs, telehealth monitoring.
* **Complex Polypharmacy:** Multidisciplinary pharmacist-led medication reconciliation and protocolized uptitration.

#### 7.1.2 Dietary Sodium Restriction
* In patients with Stage C heart failure, avoiding excessive sodium intake (generally targeting **$< 2{,}000\text{ to }3{,}000\text{ mg/day}$**) is reasonable to reduce congestive symptoms and prevent volume overload (Class 2a, Level of Evidence C-LD).
* Extremely strict sodium restriction ($< 1{,}500\text{ mg/day}$) is **not recommended** because it may activate neurohormonal cascades (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone and sympathetic nervous system) and impair nutritional intake without conferring clinical benefit.

#### 7.1.3 Physical Activity, Exercise Prescription, and Cardiac Rehabilitation
* In patients with stable Stage C heart failure who are able to participate, regular aerobic exercise training or formal enrollment in a supervised **cardiac rehabilitation program** is recommended to improve functional capacity, exercise duration, and health-related quality of life, and to reduce hospitalizations (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; HF-ACTION trial).

### 7.2 Diuretics and Decongestion Strategies in Patients With Heart Failure
* **Loop Diuretics for Congestion:** In patients with Stage C heart failure who have evidence of fluid retention, loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide) are recommended to achieve and maintain euvolemia (Class 1, Level of Evidence B-NR).
* **Diuretic Titration:** Diuretic dosing should be dynamically titrated to achieve decongestion with the lowest effective dose necessary to avoid neurohormonal activation, intravascular volume depletion, and worsening renal function.
* **Combination Diuretic Therapy for Resistance:** In patients with refractory congestion despite high doses of loop diuretics, the addition of a second diuretic with a complementary site of tubular action (e.g., metolazone, oral chlorothiazide, or IV chlorothiazide) is reasonable (Class 2a, Level B-NR).

#### Table 12: Commonly Used Oral Diuretics in the Treatment of Heart Failure
* **Furosemide:** Initial dose $20\text{--}40\text{ mg}$ once or twice daily; maximum daily dose $600\text{ mg}$; oral bioavailability $10\%\text{--}90\%$ (erratic/food-dependent); duration of action 6–8 hours.
* **Bumetanide:** Initial dose $0.5\text{--}1.0\text{ mg}$ once or twice daily; maximum daily dose $10\text{ mg}$; oral bioavailability $80\%\text{--}100\%$; duration of action 4–6 hours.
* **Torsemide:** Initial dose $10\text{--}20\text{ mg}$ once daily; maximum daily dose $200\text{ mg}$; oral bioavailability $80\%\text{--}100\%$ (consistent/predictable); duration of action 12–16 hours.
* **Metolazone:** Initial dose $2.5\text{ mg}$ once daily; maximum daily dose $20\text{ mg}$; acts on distal convoluted tubule; synergistic when co-administered with loop diuretics.
* **Spironolactone / Eplerenone (as diuretic adjunct):** Doses of $25\text{--}50\text{ mg}$ daily provide distal nephron sodium blockade and potassium sparing.

### 7.3 Pharmacological Treatment for HFrEF (The Four Pillars of GDMT)
Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) for patients with HFrEF ($\text{LVEF} \le 40\%$) is anchored on **four foundational medication classes** that independently reduce all-cause mortality and heart failure hospitalizations:

![Figure 6: Treatment of HFrEF Stages C and D](images/fig06_treatment_hfref_stages_c_d.jpg)

#### 7.3.1 Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibition: ARNI, ACEi, or ARB
1. **ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan) as Preferred First-Line (Class 1, Level of Evidence A):** In patients with chronic symptomatic HFrEF (NYHA class II–III), treatment with an ARNI is recommended to reduce cardiovascular mortality and heart failure hospitalizations.
   - In the PARADIGM-HF trial ($n=8{,}442$), sacubitril/valsartan reduced cardiovascular death by 20%, HF hospitalization by 21%, and all-cause mortality by 16% compared with enalapril ($10\text{ mg}$ twice daily).
   - In the PIONEER-HF and TRANSITION trials, in-hospital initiation of ARNI in stabilized acute decompensated HFrEF patients was safe, well-tolerated, and reduced postdischarge cardiovascular death and rehospitalizations.
   - **Switching from ACEi to ARNI (Class 3: Harm):** ARNI must not be administered within **36 hours** of the last dose of an ACE inhibitor to prevent severe angioedema. ARNI is contraindicated in patients with any history of hereditary or ACEi-associated angioedema.
2. **ACE Inhibitors (Class 1, Level A):** When ARNI is not feasible or affordable, an ACE inhibitor (enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril) is recommended to reduce morbidity and mortality (CONSENSUS, SOLVD Treatment).
3. **Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (Class 1, Level A):** Recommended in patients intolerant of ACE inhibitors due to intractable cough, or when ARNI is not feasible (CHARM-Alternative, Val-HeFT).

#### 7.3.2 Beta-Blockers
* In patients with HFrEF and current or previous symptoms, evidence-based beta-blockers—specifically **bisoprolol, carvedilol, or sustained-release metoprolol succinate**—are recommended to reduce mortality and hospitalizations (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; CIBIS-II, COPERNICUS, MERIT-HF).
* Beta-blockers should be initiated in clinically stable, euvolemic patients at very low starting doses and uptitrated every 2 to 4 weeks toward target trial doses.

#### 7.3.3 Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRAs)
* In patients with HFrEF (NYHA class II–IV, LVEF $\le 35\%$), treatment with an MRA (**spironolactone** or **eplerenone**) is recommended to reduce all-cause mortality, cardiovascular death, and hospitalizations (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; RALES, EMPHASIS-HF).
* **Monitoring Requirements:** Baseline serum potassium must be $< 5.0\text{ mEq/L}$ and estimated GFR $> 30\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$. Serum potassium and renal function must be monitored closely at 1, 4, 8, and 12 weeks following initiation or dose escalation, and every 6 months thereafter.

#### 7.3.4 Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) Inhibitors
* In patients with symptomatic chronic HFrEF, an SGLT2 inhibitor (**dapagliflozin $10\text{ mg}$ once daily** or **empagliflozin $10\text{ mg}$ once daily**) is recommended to reduce cardiovascular mortality and heart failure hospitalizations, **regardless of the presence of type 2 diabetes mellitus** (Class 1, Level of Evidence A).
  - In DAPA-HF ($n=4{,}744$), dapagliflozin reduced the primary composite endpoint of worsening HF or CV death by 26% (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.65–0.85; $P<0.001$) with identical benefits in diabetic and non-diabetic cohorts.
  - In EMPEROR-Reduced ($n=3{,}730$), empagliflozin reduced CV death or HF hospitalization by 25% (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.65–0.86; $P<0.001$) and slowed the rate of renal function decline.
  - SGLT2 inhibitors demonstrate immediate hemodynamic decongestion, do not require blood pressure or potassium titration, and can be initiated safely during hospital admission or early outpatient care.

#### 7.3.5 Hydralazine and Isosorbide Dinitrate
* In self-identified Black/African American patients with HFrEF (NYHA class III–IV) receiving optimal therapy with beta-blockers, MRAs, and RAS inhibitors, the addition of **hydralazine and isosorbide dinitrate** is recommended to reduce mortality and hospitalizations (Class 1, Level of Evidence A; A-HeFT trial).
* In patients with HFrEF unable to tolerate any RAS inhibitor or ARNI due to severe renal impairment, hyperkalemia, or bilateral renal artery stenosis, the combination of hydralazine and isosorbide dinitrate is reasonable (Class 2b, Level C-LD).

#### 7.3.6 Other Medical Therapies for HFrEF
* **Ivabradine (Class 2a, Level of Evidence B-R):** In symptomatic HFrEF patients with LVEF $\le 35\%$ in normal sinus rhythm with a resting heart rate **$\ge 70\text{ bpm}$** despite maximally tolerated beta-blocker therapy (or who have beta-blocker contraindications), ivabradine is reasonable to reduce HF hospitalizations and cardiovascular mortality (SHIFT trial).
* **Vericiguat (Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase Stimulator, Class 2b, Level B-R):** In high-risk patients with symptomatic HFrEF and recent heart failure hospitalization or elevated natriuretic peptides despite quadruple GDMT, vericiguat ($10\text{ mg}$ daily) may be considered to reduce HF hospitalizations and CV death (VICTORIA trial).
* **Digoxin (Class 2b, Level B-R):** In patients with persistent HFrEF symptoms despite GDMT, low-dose digoxin (targeting serum concentration $0.5\text{--}0.9\text{ ng/mL}$) may be considered to reduce HF hospitalizations (DIG trial).

### 7.4 Device and Interventional Therapies for HFrEF

#### 7.4.1 Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillators (ICD)
* **Secondary Prevention (Class 1, Level of Evidence A):** ICD is recommended for patients who have survived cardiac arrest due to VF or hemodynamically unstable VT.

* **Sinus Rhythm with LBBB $\ge 150\text{ ms}$ (Class 1, Level of Evidence A):** CRT (CRT-D or CRT-P) is recommended for patients with LVEF $\le 35\%$, sinus rhythm, left bundle branch block (LBBB) with QRS duration $\ge 150\text{ ms}$, and NYHA class II, III, or ambulatory class IV symptoms on GDMT (COMPANION, CARE-HF, MADIT-CRT, RAFT).
* **Sinus Rhythm with LBBB $120\text{--}149\text{ ms}$ (Class 2a, Level B-R):** CRT is reasonable for patients with LVEF $\le 35\%$, LBBB with QRS $120\text{--}149\text{ ms}$, and NYHA class II–IV.
* **Non-LBBB QRS $\ge 150\text{ ms}$ (Class 2a, Level B-R):** CRT is reasonable for patients with non-LBBB morphology and QRS $\ge 150\text{ ms}$.
* **High Expected Ventricular Pacing ($> 40\%$):** In patients with LVEF $\le 50\%$ and anticipated high burden of ventricular pacing for AV block, biventricular pacing or conduction system pacing is recommended over conventional RV apical pacing (Class 1, Level B-R; BLOCK-HF).

### 7.8 Cardiac Amyloidosis: Diagnosis and Management
Cardiac amyloidosis is an increasingly recognized cause of heart failure, primarily caused by transthyretin (ATTR) or immunoglobulin light-chain (AL) deposition:
* **Diagnostic Algorithm:**
  1. Concomitant screening for monoclonal light chains with **serum and urine protein electrophoresis with immunofixation** AND **serum free light chain assay** is mandatory to rule out AL amyloidosis (Class 1, Level of Evidence B-NR).
  2. If monoclonal testing is negative, **$^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc}$-PYP or DPD bone scintigraphy** is recommended; intense myocardial radiotracer uptake (Grade 2 or 3) provides definitive nonbiopsy diagnosis of ATTR cardiac amyloidosis (Class 1, Level B-NR).
  3. When ATTR amyloidosis is confirmed, ***TTR* genetic sequencing** is recommended to differentiate hereditary (variant) ATTR from wild-type ATTR (Class 1, Level B-NR).
* **Tafamidis for ATTR Amyloidosis (Class 1, Level B-R):** In patients with wild-type or variant ATTR cardiac amyloidosis and NYHA class I to III symptoms, the transthyretin tetramer stabilizer **tafamidis** ($61\text{ mg}$ daily) is recommended to reduce all-cause mortality, CV hospitalizations, and functional decline (ATTR-ACT trial).
* **Anticoagulation in Atrial Fibrillation (Class 1, Level B-NR):** Anticoagulation is recommended for all patients with cardiac amyloidosis who develop atrial fibrillation, regardless of the CHA2DS2-VASc score, due to high intracardiac thrombus burden.

## 8. Stage D: Advanced Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Stage D heart failure designates patients with refractory symptoms that severely interfere with daily life and who experience recurrent hospitalizations despite maximally tolerated guideline-directed medical therapy.

### 8.1 Specialty Referral for Advanced Heart Failure
* **Timely Specialty Referral (Class 1, Level of Evidence C-LD):** In patients with advanced heart failure who wish to prolong survival, referral to a specialized multidisciplinary advanced heart failure team is recommended to evaluate eligibility for mechanical circulatory support (MCS), heart transplantation, or palliative care.
* **The "I-NEED-HELP" Clinical Triggers:**
  - **I:** Intravenous inotropes (current or past requirement).
  - **N:** NYHA functional class IIIb/IV symptoms or persistently elevated natriuretic peptides.
  - **E:** End-organ dysfunction (worsening renal or liver function due to hypoperfusion/congestion).
  - **E:** Ejection fraction $\le 20\%$ or severe diastolic dysfunction.
  - **D:** Defibrillator shocks (recurrent appropriate ICD shocks).
  - **H:** Hospitalizations ($\ge 2$ unplanned HF admissions within the past 12 months).
  - **E:** Edema refractory to escalating diuretic regimens.
  - **L:** Low blood pressure (systolic $\text{BP} < 90\text{ mm Hg}$ or symptomatic hypotension).
  - **P:** Prognostic medication down-titration (inability to tolerate beta-blockers, ARNI/ACEi, or MRAs).

## 9. Patients Hospitalized With Acute Decompensated Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) is a frequent cause of emergency presentation and hospitalization, requiring urgent hemodynamic stabilization, decongestion, and structured transitional care.

### 9.1 Initial Assessment of Hospitalized Patients
* **Hemodynamic and Volume Profiling (Class 1, Level of Evidence C-EO):** In all patients admitted with ADHF, bedside evaluation must characterize the hemodynamic profile based on congestion ("wet" vs "dry") and peripheral perfusion ("warm" vs "cold"). The vast majority ($> 90\%$) present as "warm and wet".
* **Identification of Precipitating Factors:** Systematic search for acute triggers—including myocardial ischemia, acute arrhythmias (especially rapid atrial fibrillation), uncontrolled hypertension, medication non-adherence, dietary excess, NSAID use, infection, and worsening renal failure.

### 9.2 Maintenance or Optimization of GDMT During Hospitalization
* **Continuation of Chronic GDMT (Class 1, Level of Evidence B-NR):** In patients with HFrEF hospitalized for ADHF without hemodynamic instability, shock, or symptomatic hypotension, pre-existing GDMT (including beta-blockers, ARNI/ACEi/ARB, MRAs, and SGLT2 inhibitors) should be **continued and not routinely discontinued**.
* **In-Hospital Initiation / Titration (Class 1, Level B-NR):** Hospitalization represents a critical window of opportunity to initiate and titrate foundational GDMT, including ARNI and SGLT2 inhibitors, once patients are stabilized and decongestion is underway (PIONEER-HF, EMPULSE trials).
* **Transient Dose Reduction (Class 2a, Level B-NR):** If severe renal impairment or symptomatic hypotension occurs, temporary dose reduction of RAS inhibitors or beta-blockers may be considered, with prompt reinstatement prior to discharge.

### 9.3 Diuretics in Hospitalized Patients: Decongestion Strategy
* **Intravenous Loop Diuretics First-Line (Class 1, Level of Evidence B-NR):** Patients admitted with signs of fluid overload should receive prompt intravenous loop diuretics. For patients on chronic outpatient oral loop diuretics, the initial IV dose should be equal to or greater than their home oral daily dose (e.g., $1.0\text{ to }2.5\times$ oral dose in IV milligrams; DOSE-AHF trial).
* **Dynamic Monitoring of Diuretic Response:** Urine output, daily morning weight, clinical signs of congestion, and serum electrolytes must be monitored serial. If diuretic response is inadequate (e.g., spot urine sodium $< 50\text{--}70\text{ mEq/L}$ at 2 hours or urine output $< 100\text{--}150\text{ mL/hr}$), the IV loop diuretic dose should be doubled (Class 1, Level B-NR).
* **Combination Diuresis for Resistance:** Adding a thiazide-type diuretic (metolazone $2.5\text{--}5\text{ mg}$ or IV chlorothiazide $500\text{ mg}$) or initiating an SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended when loop diuresis alone fails to achieve decongestion (Class 2a, Level B-NR).

### 9.5 Evaluation and Management of Cardiogenic Shock
Cardiogenic shock is a life-threatening state of critical end-organ hypoperfusion caused by severe cardiac dysfunction:
* **Multidisciplinary Shock Team Care (Class 2a, Level of Evidence B-NR):** In patients presenting with cardiogenic shock, management by a dedicated multidisciplinary "Shock Team" and early transfer to a high-volume shock center improves survival.
* **Invasive Hemodynamic Monitoring (Class 2a, Level B-NR):** Pulmonary artery catheterization (PAC) is reasonable to guide inotropic, vasopressor, and mechanical circulatory support titration.
* **Pharmacological Support:** Inotropic agents (dobutamine, milrinone) to restore cardiac index and vasopressors (norepinephrine preferred over dopamine) to maintain mean arterial pressure $\ge 65\text{ mm Hg}$.
* **Temporary Mechanical Circulatory Support (Class 2a, Level B-NR):** In patients with refractory cardiogenic shock unresponsive to inotropes/vasopressors, early temporary MCS (Impella, VA-ECMO, TandemHeart) is reasonable to restore systemic perfusion and prevent multi-organ failure.

## 10. Comorbidities in Patients With Heart Failure</summary><div class="details-content">

Comorbid conditions frequently coexist with heart failure, aggravating symptoms, accelerating disease progression, and complicating pharmacotherapy.

### 10.1 Management of Comorbidities in Heart Failure
* **Iron Deficiency Anemia (Class 2a, Level of Evidence B-R):** In patients with HFrEF (NYHA class II–III) and iron deficiency (defined as serum ferritin $< 100\text{ ng/mL}$ or ferritin $100\text{--}299\text{ ng/mL}$ with transferrin saturation [TSAT] $< 20\%$), intravenous iron repletion with **ferric carboxymaltose or ferric derisomaltose** is reasonable to improve functional status, exercise capacity, and quality of life, and to reduce heart failure hospitalizations (CONFIRM-HF, AFFIRM-AHF).
  - Oral iron administration is ineffective in replaying myocardial iron stores due to hepcidin-mediated intestinal malabsorption.
* **Hypertension:** Target blood pressure should be $< 130/80\text{ mm Hg}$ using GDMT neurohormonal agents (Class 1, Level A).
* **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended as foundational therapy to reduce cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalizations (Class 1, Level A).
* **Sleep-Disordered Breathing:** In patients with HF and symptoms suggestive of sleep-disordered breathing, comprehensive polysomnography is recommended (Class 2a, Level C-LD).
  - Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) is reasonable for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).
  - **Adaptive Servo-Ventilation Contraindicated (Class 3: Harm):** In patients with HFrEF (NYHA class II–IV, LVEF $\le 45\%$) and predominant central sleep apnea, adaptive servo-ventilation (ASV) is harmful and contraindicated due to increased all-cause and cardiovascular mortality (SERVE-HF trial).

### 10.2 Management of Atrial Fibrillation in Heart Failure
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained arrhythmia in heart failure, exacerbating hemodynamic compromise and multiplying thromboembolic risk:
* **Anticoagulation First-Line (Class 1, Level of Evidence A):** Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs: apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, rivaroxaban) are recommended in preference to warfarin in all HF patients with AF and elevated stroke risk.
* **Catheter Ablation for Rhythm Control (Class 2a, Level B-R):** In patients with symptomatic HFrEF and paroxysmal or persistent AF, radiofrequency catheter ablation (pulmonary vein isolation) is reasonable to improve symptoms, improve LVEF, and reduce mortality and hospitalizations (CASTLE-AF trial).
* **AV Node Ablation With CRT ("Ablate and Pace") (Class 2a, Level B-NR):** In patients with HFrEF and rapid AF when rate control cannot be achieved with medical therapy or catheter ablation, AV nodal ablation combined with biventricular pacing (CRT) is reasonable.
* **Rate Control:** Beta-blockers are the preferred first-line rate control agents; digoxin may be added if rate control remains inadequate. Non-dihydropyridine CCBs are contraindicated in HFrEF.

### 11.2 Cardio-Oncology: Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiac Dysfunction
Cancer therapies—including anthracyclines (doxorubicin), HER2 inhibitors (trastuzumab), VEGF inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)—can induce serious cardiovascular toxicity:
* **Pretreatment and Serial Surveillance (Class 1, Level of Evidence B-NR):** In patients scheduled to receive cardiotoxic chemotherapies, baseline cardiovascular risk stratification, measurement of cardiac biomarkers (troponins and natriuretic peptides), and baseline echocardiography with global longitudinal strain (GLS) are recommended.
* **Primary Prevention in High-Risk Cancer Patients (Class 2a, Level B-R):** In cancer patients with high cardiotoxic risk, cardioprotective therapy with beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, or statins is reasonable during anthracycline treatment.
* **Management of Cancer Therapy-Related LV Dysfunction (Class 1, Level B-NR):** When chemotherapy-induced LV dysfunction (drop in LVEF $\ge 10$ percentage points to $< 50\%$) occurs, guideline-directed medical therapy for HFrEF should be promptly initiated in coordination with the multidisciplinary oncology team.

### 11.3 Heart Failure and Pregnancy (Peripartum Cardiomyopathy)
* **Preconceptional Counseling (Class 1, Level of Evidence C-LD):** Women with pre-existing cardiomyopathy or heart failure should receive specialized preconceptional counseling by a multidisciplinary Cardio-Obstetrics team.
* **Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM):** Characterized by acute heart failure developing during the final month of pregnancy or within 5 months postpartum in the absence of other identifiable causes.
* **Medication Safety Across Pregnancy:**
  - **Safe Therapies:** Beta-blockers (metoprolol succinate, carvedilol; avoid atenolol), hydralazine, oral nitrates, and loop diuretics (when clinically congested).
  - **Strictly Contraindicated (Class 3: Harm):** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists are teratogenic and cause severe fetal fetopathy, oligohydramnios, and neonatal renal failure. SGLT2 inhibitors and ivabradine must be avoided due to lack of pregnancy safety data.
* **Delivery Mode:** Vaginal delivery with epidural anesthesia is preferred, with cesarean section reserved for standard obstetric indications.

## 13. Goals of Care, Palliative Care, and End-of-Life</summary><div class="details-content">

### 13.1 Palliative and Supportive Care, Shared Decision-Making, and End-of-Life
Palliative care should be integrated early alongside curative and life-prolonging therapies across the heart failure trajectory:
* **Early Integration of Palliative Care (Class 1, Level of Evidence A):** Interdisciplinary palliative care consultations should be offered to patients with advanced heart failure to improve symptom burden, enhance quality of life, assist with complex decision-making, and support caregivers.
* **Advance Care Planning (Class 1, Level B-NR):** Clinicians should proactively engage patients and surrogate decision-makers in structured conversations regarding goals of care, preferences for mechanical support, resuscitation status, and designation of durable power of attorney for healthcare.
* **Elective ICD Deactivation (Class 1, Level C-LD):** In terminal Stage D patients or when comfort-focused care is adopted, proactive counseling regarding the deactivation of the shock functionality of an ICD should occur to prevent distressing and painful shocks during dying. Bradycardia pacing functionality may be preserved.

Research suggests that patients tend to overestimate their survival17 and overestimate the potential benefits of treatment.18 Objective risk models can calibrate expectations, but discussion of uncertainty should accompany prognostic conversations, often summarized as "hope for the best, plan for the worst." |
| Clarifying goals of care | Management of patients with HF as their disease becomes end-stage and death seems near includes decisions about when to discontinue treatments designed primarily to prolong life (eg, ICD, hospitalization, tube feeding), decisions on when to initiate treatments to reduce pain and suffering that may hasten death (eg, narcotics), and decisions about the location of death, home services, and hospice care. Exploring patients' expressed preferences, values, needs, concerns, means and desires through clinician-led discussion can clarify values-treatment concordance and improve medical decision-making.12 |
| Shared decision-making | Shared decision-making is a process by which patients and clinicians work together to make optimal health care decisions from medically reasonable options that align with what matters most to patients. Shared decision-making requires: unbiased medical evidence about the risks, benefits, and burdens of each alternative, including no intervention; clinician expertise in communication and tailoring that evidence for individual patients; and patient goals and informed preferences.19 |
| Symptom management | Dyspnea, fatigue, pain, nausea, depression, anxiety, and other symptoms of HF refractory to cardiovascular therapies can be partially remediated through palliative and supportive approaches in addition to GDMT.1 |
| Caregiver support | Care of the patient with heart failure should extend to their loved ones, including beyond their death, to offer support to families and help them cope with loss. |

### 14.1 Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs)
* **Standardized Health Status Instruments (Class 2a, Level of Evidence B-NR):** In patients with heart failure, routine measurement of patient-reported health status using validated, disease-specific instruments—such as the **Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ)** or the **Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ)**—is reasonable to quantify symptom burden, physical limitations, social functioning, and monitor response to therapeutic interventions.
