## 1. Introduction, History, and Clinical Scope</summary><div class="details-content">

### Clinical Scope and Evolution
This consensus statement by the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and the Society of Cardiovascular Anesthesiologists (SCA) delineates the dedicated role of a **basic perioperative transesophageal echocardiography (PTE)** examination. 

While comprehensive (advanced) intraoperative multiplane TEE encompasses full structural and hemodynamic quantification across 28 distinct cross-sectional planes—predominantly deployed in cardiac surgery and structural catheter-based interventions—basic PTE focuses directly on:
1. Rapid qualitative and semiquantitative evaluation of acute, unexplained perioperative hemodynamic instability;
2. Differentiating primary ventricular pump failure (LV and/or RV) from severe hypovolemia and distributive shock;
3. Focused monitoring of ventricular filling, wall motion, gross valvular pathology, and pericardial tamponade;
4. Real-time guidance of resuscitation therapies (inotropes, vasopressors, volume expansion) in non-cardiac and cardiac perioperative environments.

## 2. Training Pathways and Core Competencies</summary><div class="details-content">

### Training Framework (National Board of Echocardiography)
Basic perioperative echocardiography requires dedicated cognitive understanding of cardiovascular pathophysiology, ultrasound physics, artifact recognition, and technical hand-eye coordination for safe probe manipulation.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

| Training Pathway | Clinical Experience Requirements | Supervision Criteria | Continuing Medical Education (CME) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supervised Training Pathway** | ≥150 basic PTE examinations reviewed and interpreted under qualified supervision | ≥50 of the 150 examinations must be personally performed, acquired, and interpreted under direct bedside supervision | Integrated within ACGME-accredited residency/fellowship curriculum |
| **Practice Experience Pathway** *(historical)* | ≥150 basic intraoperative TEE examinations performed and interpreted within 4 years of application (≤25 in any single year) | Formal direct supervision not mandated during examination execution | ≥40 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits dedicated to perioperative echocardiography acquired within the same 4-year window |

## 3. The 11 Standardized Basic PTE Views Protocol</summary><div class="details-content">

The ASE/SCA Basic PTE examination consists of **11 core anatomical cross-sections** designed to achieve a complete qualitative assessment of all four cardiac chambers, valves, great vessels, and pericardium in the shortest acquisition time.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Overview and Anatomical Landmarks</summary><div class="details-content">

![Figure 1: Cross-sectional views of the 11 views of the ASE and SCA basic PTE examination](images/fig01_11_views_basic_protocol.jpg)
*Figure 1: Cross-sectional overview of the 11 standardized basic PTE imaging planes, depicting transducer multiplane rotation angle and anatomical orientation.*

![Figure 2: Lateral chest x-ray depicting relative positions of the heart, aorta, and esophagus](images/fig02_lateral_chest_xray_probe_positions.jpg)
*Figure 2: Lateral chest radiograph demonstrating relative positions of the heart (black outline), aorta (white line), and esophagus (yellow line). Arrows depict Upper Esophageal (UE), Mid-Esophageal (ME), and Transgastric (TG) probe depths.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 1: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Four-Chamber View (0°–20°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-esophagus, depth approximately 30–35 cm from incisors, posterior to left atrium.
- **Manipulation**: Multiplane angle rotated from 0° forward to approximately 10°–20° to eliminate the LVOT and maximize tricuspid annular diameter. Probe rotated counterclockwise or clockwise to center the mitral valve and LV cavity. Slight retroflexion may align the LV apex without foreshortening.
- **Structures Visualized**: Left atrium (LA), right atrium (RA), interatrial septum (IAS), mitral valve (MV, anterior and posterior leaflets), tricuspid valve (TV, septal and posterior leaflets), left ventricle (LV), right ventricle (RV), and interventricular septum (IVS).
- **Coronary Perfusion Territories**:
  - Basal and mid inferoseptum: Right Coronary Artery (RCA);
  - Apical septum and apical cap: Left Anterior Descending (LAD);
  - Basal, mid, and apical anterolateral wall: Circumflex (LCx) or LAD.
- **Doppler Interrogation**: Color flow Doppler (Nyquist 50–60 cm/s) across MV and TV for regurgitation/stenosis; color interrogation across IAS for interatrial shunting.

![Figure 3: ME four-chamber view](images/fig03_me_four_chamber_view.jpg)
*Figure 3: ME four-chamber view displaying LA, LV, RA, RV, MV, and TV.*

![Figure 4: Coronary artery perfusion territories mapped onto TEE views](images/fig04_coronary_artery_territories.jpg)
*Figure 4: Segmental coronary artery distribution corresponding to standardized multiplane TEE views (ME 4-chamber, ME 2-chamber, ME LAX, and TG midpapillary SAX).*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 2: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Two-Chamber View (80°–100°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Maintained at mid-esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From ME four-chamber view, rotate multiplane angle forward to 80°–100° until the right heart structures vanish and the left atrial appendage (LAA) is brought into view.
- **Structures Visualized**: Left atrium, mitral valve, LV anterior and inferior walls, left atrial appendage, and coronary sinus (in short axis, superior to the basal inferior LV wall).
- **Coronary Perfusion Territories**:
  - Basal and mid inferior segments: RCA;
  - Apical inferior, apical cap, apical anterior, mid anterior, and basal anterior segments: LAD.
- **Doppler Interrogation**: Color Doppler over MV to assess regurgitation jets along the anteroposterior coaptation line.

![Figure 5: ME two-chamber view](images/fig05_me_two_chamber_view.jpg)
*Figure 5: ME two-chamber view displaying LA, MV, and anterior and inferior LV walls.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 3: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Long-Axis (LAX) View (120°–160°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From ME two-chamber view, advance multiplane angle forward to 120°–160° until the LVOT, aortic valve (AV), and proximal ascending aorta appear.
- **Structures Visualized**: LA, MV, LV, LVOT, AV (right and noncoronary cusps), and proximal aortic root.
- **Coronary Perfusion Territories**:
  - Basal and mid inferolateral wall segments: LCx or RCA;
  - Apical lateral, apical cap, apical anterior, mid anteroseptum, and basal anteroseptum: LAD.
- **Doppler Interrogation**: Color Doppler across MV, LVOT, and AV. Essential for identifying aortic regurgitation, dynamic systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve, and LVOT subaortic obstruction.

![Figure 6: ME long-axis (LAX) view](images/fig06_me_lax_view.jpg)
*Figure 6: ME long-axis view showing LA, LV, LVOT, AV, and proximal ascending aorta.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 4: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Ascending Aortic LAX View (120°–140°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Upper-to-mid esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From ME LAX view, withdraw probe cephalad while maintaining 120°–140° multiplane angle until the tubular ascending aorta is visualized in longitudinal profile. Rotating counterclockwise aligns the main pulmonary artery and pulmonic valve.
- **Structures Visualized**: Tubular ascending aorta, right pulmonary artery (RPA, imaged in short axis behind the aorta), main PA, and pulmonic valve.
- **Clinical Utility**: Detection of acute Type A aortic dissection flaps, proximal thromboemboli in the main pulmonary artery, and Doppler interrogation of RVOT/PV.

![Figure 7: ME ascending aortic LAX view](images/fig07_me_ascending_aortic_lax_view.jpg)
*Figure 7: ME ascending aortic LAX view displaying the tubular ascending aorta and right pulmonary artery.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 5: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Ascending Aortic SAX View (20°–40°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Upper-to-mid esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From the ascending aortic LAX view, decrease multiplane angle to 20°–40°.
- **Structures Visualized**: Ascending aorta in cross-section (circular profile), main PA bifurcation into right and left branches, and superior vena cava (SVC) in cross-section.
- **Clinical Utility**: Evaluation of aortic caliber, acute hematomas, and direct visualization of saddle or main PA bifurcation pulmonary emboli.

![Figure 8: ME ascending aortic SAX view](images/fig08_me_ascending_aortic_sax_view.jpg)
*Figure 8: ME ascending aortic SAX view demonstrating cross-sections of the ascending aorta, SVC, and main PA bifurcation.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 6: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Aortic Valve (AV) SAX View (30°–60°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From the ascending aortic SAX view, advance the probe slightly deeper until the aortic valve trileaflet coaptation ("Mercedes-Benz" configuration) appears at 30°–60°.
- **Structures Visualized**: Left coronary cusp (posterior-right), right coronary cusp (anterior), noncoronary cusp (adjacent to interatrial septum), interatrial septum, right ventricular outflow tract, and right atrium.
- **Clinical Utility**: Evaluation of aortic valve leaflet morphology (tricuspid vs bicuspid), calcification, leaflet excursion, and aortic regurgitant orifice location by color Doppler.

![Figure 9: ME AV SAX view](images/fig09_me_av_sax_view.jpg)
*Figure 9: ME aortic valve short-axis view demonstrating trileaflet morphology.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 7: Mid-Esophageal (ME) RV Inflow-Outflow View (60°–90°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From ME AV SAX view, turn probe clockwise to center the tricuspid valve and rotate multiplane forward to 60°–90° until the entire RV wrap-around profile (inflow, body, RVOT, PV, and proximal PA) is visualized simultaneously.
- **Structures Visualized**: RA, TV, RV body and free wall, RVOT, PV, and proximal main PA.
- **Hemodynamic Application**: Alignment of continuous-wave Doppler with the tricuspid regurgitation (TR) jet allows calculation of estimated RV systolic pressure (RVSP) via the modified Bernoulli equation:
  $$\text{RVSP} = 4 \times (V_{\text{TR, peak}})^2 + \text{CVP}$$
  In the absence of pulmonary stenosis, $\text{RVSP} = \text{PASP}$.

![Figure 10: ME RV inflow-outflow view](images/fig10_me_rv_inflow_outflow_view.jpg)
*Figure 10: ME RV inflow-outflow view displaying RA, TV, RV, RVOT, and pulmonic valve.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 8: Mid-Esophageal (ME) Bicaval View (90°–110°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-esophagus.
- **Manipulation**: From ME RV inflow-outflow view, turn probe clockwise towards the patient's right side and adjust angle to 90°–110°.
- **Structures Visualized**: Right atrium, superior vena cava (entering superiorly/right), inferior vena cava with Eustachian valve (inferiorly/left), interatrial septum, left atrium, and right atrial appendage.
- **Clinical Utility**: 
  - Guidance of central venous cannulae, pulmonary artery catheters, and ECMO cannulae;
  - Detection of secundum atrial septal defects (ASD) and patent foramen ovale (PFO);
  - Agitated saline bubble study with Valsalva release to identify right-to-left shunts;
  - Monitoring for air emboli entering the right atrium during neurosurgical or orthopedic procedures.

![Figure 11: ME bicaval view](images/fig11_me_bicaval_view.jpg)
*Figure 11: ME bicaval view displaying SVC, IVC, RA, LA, and interatrial septum.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 9: Transgastric (TG) Midpapillary SAX View (0°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Transgastric, advanced into the stomach, depth ~40–45 cm.
- **Manipulation**: Transducer multiplane kept at 0°. Probe anteflexed against the gastric wall. Probe depth adjusted until both the posteromedial and anterolateral papillary muscles are clearly identified as distinct round muscular structures projecting into the LV cavity.
  - If chordae tendineae are seen, probe is too shallow (advance probe or decrease anteflexion);
  - If no papillary muscles and small cavity are seen, probe is too deep at apex (withdraw probe).
- **Structures Visualized**: True circular cross-section of the LV mid-ventricular cavity, anterolateral and posteromedial papillary muscles, and all 6 mid-wall segments.
- **Clinical Importance**:
  - The premier "gold standard" monitoring view for hemodynamic instability, acute hypovolemia, and global/regional ischemia;
  - Dynamic end-systolic cavity obliteration indicates profound hypovolemia;
  - Simultaneously interrogates all 3 major coronary artery territories (LAD, LCx, RCA).

![Figure 12: TG midpapillary SAX view](images/fig12_tg_midpapillary_sax_view.jpg)
*Figure 12: TG midpapillary SAX view showing anterolateral and posteromedial papillary muscles.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 10: Descending Aortic SAX View (0°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Mid-to-distal esophagus, rotated posterolaterally to the patient's left (180° rotation from cardiac views).
- **Manipulation**: Multiplane angle at 0°. The descending thoracic aorta appears as a circular pulsating vessel adjacent to the transducer.
- **Clinical Utility**: Rapid assessment of aortic caliber, severe atheroma, mural thrombi, intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) positioning, and detection of aortic dissection intimal flaps and false lumens.

![Figure 13: Descending aortic SAX view](images/fig13_descending_aortic_sax_view.jpg)
*Figure 13: Descending thoracic aortic short-axis view.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### View 11: Descending Aortic LAX View (90°–100°)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Transducer Position**: Same probe depth as descending SAX view.
- **Manipulation**: From descending SAX view, rotate multiplane angle forward to 90°–100° to transform the circular vessel into parallel linear tubular walls.
- **Clinical Utility**: Longitudinal interrogation of descending aortic dissection flaps, measurement of aortic aneurysm extension, and pulsed-wave Doppler assessment of diastolic flow reversal (indicative of significant aortic regurgitation).

![Figure 14: Descending aortic LAX view](images/fig14_descending_aortic_lax_view.jpg)
*Figure 14: Descending thoracic aortic long-axis view showing tubular parallel walls.*

</div></details>

## 4. Clinical Indications and Hemodynamic Assessment</summary><div class="details-content">

A basic PTE examination is indicated whenever unexplained, life-threatening circulatory instability persists despite initial corrective therapy in the operating room or intensive care unit.

```
                         [UNEXPLAINED HYPOTENSION / SHOCK]
                                         │
                    ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
                    ▼                                         ▼
         [Echocardiographic Cavity Size]            [Ventricular Contractility]
         TG Midpapillary SAX View (0°)             ME 4-Ch, ME 2-Ch, ME LAX
                    │                                         │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐                 ┌───────────┴───────────┐
        ▼                       ▼                 ▼                       ▼
[Small / Obliterated]   [Severely Dilated]   [Hyperdynamic LV]    [Global/Regional Hypokinesis]
  Profound Hypovolemia    Acute RV Strain      Vasoplegia / Sepsis    Cardiogenic Shock / Ischemia
  Fluid Resuscitation     PE / RV Infarct      Vasopressors Needed    Inotropes / Revascularization
```

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 1. Global and Regional LV Systolic Function</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Qualitative Visual Estimation**: The basic echocardiographer relies primarily on qualitative eyeballing to classify systolic function into normal, mildly depressed, moderately depressed, or severely depressed.
- **Regional Wall Motion Analysis**: 
  - Monitored dynamically across the TG midpapillary SAX view (all 6 segments);
  - Comprehensive regional assessment incorporates ME 4-chamber, ME 2-chamber, and ME LAX views to verify concordance across the 17-segment model.
  - New regional wall motion abnormalities (hypokinesis, akinesis, dyskinesis) warrant immediate evaluation for coronary hypoperfusion, kinking of grafts, or embolization.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 2. RV Systolic Function and Acute Cor Pulmonale</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Vulnerability**: The thin-walled right ventricle is exquisitely sensitive to acute increases in afterload (pulmonary hypertension, hypercarbia, hypoxemia, protamine reactions, acute pulmonary embolism).
- **Echocardiographic Signs**:
  - RV chamber dilatation (RV end-diastolic area > 2/3 of LV end-diastolic area in ME 4-chamber view);
  - Interventricular septal flattening ("D-shaped" LV in systole and/or diastole on TG midpapillary view);
  - Severe tricuspid regurgitation with elevated TR jet velocity.
- **High-Risk Populations**: Liver transplantation (risk of acute RV failure upon reperfusion), major orthopedic and trauma surgery.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 3. Acute Hypovolemia and Preload Monitoring</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Hallmark Features**:
  - Decreased LV end-diastolic area (LVEDA) and LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) on TG midpapillary SAX;
  - Dynamic end-systolic cavity obliteration ("kissing papillary muscles");
  - Rapid, dramatic improvement in cavity dimensions following intravenous fluid boluses or blood transfusion.
- **Superiority over Invasive Pressure Monitoring**: TEE provides an accurate real-time index of true preload, avoiding misleading filling pressures caused by altered ventricular compliance or positive-pressure mechanical ventilation.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 4. Basic Valvular Lesions Screening</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Regurgitation Screening**: Color Doppler applied over AV, MV, TV, and PV.
  - Qualitative visual grading: mild vs moderate vs severe based on regurgitant jet area within the receiving chamber and vena contracta width (>0.7 cm for severe MR/AR).
  - Identification of flail leaflets, ruptured chordae, or gross leaflet prolapse.
- **Consultation Trigger**: If a valvular lesion is severe, eccentric, or potentially requires immediate surgical intervention, formal consultation with an advanced PTE echocardiographer is mandatory.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5. Pulmonary Embolism (PE)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Direct Thrombus Visualization**: 
  - Main PA, right PA, and bifurcation directly visible in ME ascending aortic LAX and SAX views;
  - Highly specific when visualized in the proximal pulmonary arterial tree.
- **Indirect Signs (McConnell's Sign)**: Acute RV dilation, severe hypokinesis of the RV free wall with sparing of apical contraction, and acute tricuspid regurgitation.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 6. Neurosurgery and Venous Air Embolism (VAE)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Sensitivity**: TEE is the most sensitive clinical monitoring modality for venous air embolism, capable of detecting minute quantities of microbubbles (as little as 0.02 mL/kg).
- **Monitoring Plane**: The ME bicaval view is continuous-monitoring view of choice during sitting-craniotomy or posterior fossa neurosurgery.
- **Paradoxical Embolism**: Continuous surveillance of the interatrial septum for patent foramen ovale (PFO) to prevent fatal systemic arterial embolization.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 7. Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Differentiation**:
  - Pericardial fluid: Visualized anterior to the descending thoracic aorta;
  - Pleural fluid: Extends posterior to the descending thoracic aorta.
- **Tamponade Hemodynamics**:
  - Right atrial inversion/collapse in late diastole/early systole (>1/3 of cardiac cycle);
  - Right ventricular diastolic collapse;
  - Hyperdynamic, underfilled LV with marked respiratory variation across transvalvular inflow velocities.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 8. Adult Congenital Heart Disease (Simple Shunts)</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Secundum ASD & PFO**:
  - Best evaluated in the ME bicaval view using color flow Doppler (lowering Nyquist limit to 30–40 cm/s);
  - Agitated saline contrast injection with Valsalva release to identify right-to-left microbubble transit within 3 cardiac cycles.
- **Complex Congenital Lesions**: Ventricular septal defects (VSD), primum ASDs, and sinus venosus defects require advanced PTE consultation.

</div></details>

## 5. Quality Assurance, Documentation, and Maintenance of Competence</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Maintenance of Basic PTE Competence</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Minimum Procedural Volume**: Anesthesiologists holding basic PTE certification must perform and interpret a **minimum of 25 examinations per year** to preserve technical and interpretive competence.
- **Continuing Medical Education**: Regular participation in accredited perioperative echocardiography CME courses and quality improvement morbidity/mortality conferences is strongly recommended.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Structured Examination Documentation</summary><div class="details-content">

Every basic PTE examination must be documented and archived in accordance with hospital CQI policies. A permanent written or electronic report must be entered into the patient's medical record prior to operating room discharge, containing:
1. Date and exact time of the study;
2. Patient demographic identifiers (name, MRN, date of birth, age, gender);
3. Specific clinical indication for the examination;
4. Documentation of informed consent (or emergency exception);
5. Names of the performing and interpreting physicians;
6. Detailed structural and Doppler findings across visualized views;
7. Diagnostic impression and clinical recommendations;
8. Documentation of any procedure-related complications (or absence thereof);
9. Date and time of report signature;
10. Permanent archiving medium and digital image storage location.

</div></details>

</div></details>
