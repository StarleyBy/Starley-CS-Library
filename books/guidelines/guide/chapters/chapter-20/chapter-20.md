## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Principles of the Comprehensive Transesophageal Examination

Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) is an indispensable clinical modality that overcomes the acoustic window limitations of transthoracic imaging. The physical proximity of the esophagus and stomach to the posterior aspect of the heart permits the use of high-frequency transducers ($5	ext{--}7	ext{ MHz}$), yielding superior spatial and temporal resolution of intracardiac anatomy, valves, and great vessels:

1. **Standardized 28-View Protocol:**
   - A complete comprehensive examination comprises **28 standardized cross-sectional views** acquired across four acoustic levels: **Upper Esophageal (UE)**, **Mid-Esophageal (ME)**, **Transgastric (TG)**, and **Deep Transgastric (Deep TG)**.
   - Each standard view requires multiplane angle rotation ($0^\circ	ext{--}180^\circ$) and coordinated probe manipulation (advancement, withdrawal, right/left rotation, anteflexion, retroflexion, right/left lateral flexion).

2. **Integration of 3D Echocardiography:**
   - Real-time 3D matrix-array imaging (live narrow-angle, live wide-angle zoom, full-volume multi-beat, and 3D color Doppler) is integrated into the routine protocol.
   - 3D imaging is the diagnostic standard for mitral valve prolapse mapping, aortic annulus non-planar geometry, tricuspid valve morphology, and complex atrial septal defect sizing.

3. **Patient Safety, Sedation, and Contraindications:**
   - TEE is an semi-invasive procedure requiring rigorous pre-procedure screening for esophageal pathology, strict adherence to ASA physical status classification, standardized conscious sedation protocols, and post-procedure recovery monitoring (Modified Aldrete Score $\ge 9$ for discharge).
   - Absolute contraindications (perforated viscus, active upper GI bleeding, esophageal stricture/tumor) and relative contraindications (varices, Barrett's esophagus, severe cervical spine disease) must be systematically excluded.

## 1. Introduction, Indications, Patient Safety and Sedation</summary><div class="details-content">
<details class="med-details"><summary>
### 1.1. Clinical Indications for TEE</summary><div class="details-content">

TEE is indicated whenever transthoracic echocardiography is technically limited or non-diagnostic, or when high-resolution acoustic imaging fundamentally alters patient management:
- Unexplained cardiovascular collapse or persistent hypotension.
- Identification of cardiac sources of embolism (LAA thrombus, PFO/ASD, aortic arch atheroma).
- Infective endocarditis of native and prosthetic valves (vegetations, abscesses, pseudoaneurysms, perforations).
- Prosthetic valve dysfunction (paravalvular leak, dehiscence, pannus, thrombosis).
- Evaluation of mitral valve reparability and quantitative assessment of regurgitation mechanisms.
- Acute aortic syndromes: Type A dissection, intramural hematoma, penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer.
- Intraoperative monitoring in cardiac surgery and real-time guidance during structural heart interventional procedures.

<details class="med-details"><summary>
### 1.2. Patient Sedation, Monitoring and Recovery</summary><div class="details-content">

In awake adult patients, conscious sedation is titrated using short-acting intravenous agents (midazolam, fentanyl) combined with topical pharyngeal anesthesia (lidocaine spray). Baseline physical status is categorized according to the ASA classification (Table 8). Discharge from the recovery area requires a Modified Aldrete Score $\ge 9$ (Table 9).

## 2. Probe Manipulation, Controls and Multiplane Imaging Planes</summary><div class="details-content">

### Standardized Probe Maneuvers
Precise, reproducible TEE imaging requires coordinating six mechanical probe movements and electronic multiplane steering:
1. **Advance / Withdraw:** Linear movement of the probe deeper into the esophagus/stomach or higher toward the hypopharynx.
2. **Turn Right / Turn Left:** Axial rotation of the probe shaft clockwise (rightward) or counterclockwise (leftward).
3. **Anteflex / Retroflex:** Movement of the probe tip anteriorly (toward the anterior chest wall) or posteriorly using the large control wheel.
4. **Flex Left / Flex Right:** Lateral movement of the probe tip to the patient's left or right using the small control wheel.
5. **Multiplane Angle Rotation:** Electronic steering of the acoustic crystal array from $0^\circ$ (horizontal/transverse) through $90^\circ$ (longitudinal/vertical) to $180^\circ$ (mirror-image transverse).

## 4. Three-Dimensional (3D) TEE Examination Protocol</summary><div class="details-content">
<details class="med-details"><summary>
### 4.1. 3D Acquisition Modalities & Technical Optimization</summary><div class="details-content">

Real-time 3D matrix-array probes provide four core acquisition modes:
1. **Live 3D (Narrow-Angle):** Real-time pyramidal volume (up to $30^\circ \times 60^\circ$) rendered instantaneously at high temporal resolution without ECG gating. Ideal for dynamic valve leaflet motion.
2. **3D Zoom:** Focused high-resolution volume with user-defined region of interest (ROI) cropped tightly around a target structure (mitral valve, aortic valve, LAA).
3. **Full-Volume (Wide-Angle):** Stitched multi-beat volume ($2\text{--}7\text{ beats}$) providing wide field of view ($90^\circ \times 90^\circ$). Requires stable rhythm and breath-hold to prevent stitch artifacts.
4. **3D Color Flow Doppler:** Superimposes color Doppler velocity maps within a 3D pyramid, essential for mapping eccentric vena contracta area.

## 7. Conclusions</summary><div class="details-content">

The 2013 ASE/SCA Comprehensive Transesophageal Echocardiography Guideline establishes the universal gold standard for transesophageal diagnostic imaging. Adherence to the standardized 28-view protocol ensures diagnostic completeness, prevents omissions, integrates state-of-the-art 3D echocardiography, and guarantees maximal patient safety across cardiology, cardiac surgery, anesthesiology, and interventional structural heart programs.
