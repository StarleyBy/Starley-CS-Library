# Chapter 8. Cardiomyopathies

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">
Cardiomyopathies represent a diverse group of primary myocardial disorders categorized by structural and functional phenotypes (ESC/EACVI classification: Dilated, Hypertrophic, Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular, Restrictive, and Unclassified/Specific forms including Left Ventricular Non-Compaction, Takotsubo, and Myocarditis).

### Core Diagnostic Objectives
1. **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)**: Identification of left ventricular dilation ($\text{LVEDV} > 112\text{ mL/m}^2$, $\text{LVEDD} > 117\%$ of predicted) with global systolic depression ($\text{LVEF} < 45\%$, $\text{FS} < 25\%$) out of proportion to loading conditions or coronary anatomy. Assessment of sphericity, mechanical dyssynchrony (apical rocking, septal flash) for cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT), and secondary mitral/tricuspid regurgitation.
2. **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)**: Recognition of unexplained myocardial hypertrophy ($\text{wall thickness} \ge 15\text{ mm}$, or $\ge 13\text{ mm}$ in genotyped relatives). Differentiation of asymmetric septal (ASH), concentric, apical, and mid-ventricular variants. Accurate quantification of dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction ($\text{gradient} \ge 30\text{ mmHg}$ at rest or $\ge 50\text{ mmHg}$ with provocation) driven by systolic anterior motion (SAM) of elongated mitral leaflets. Multi-parametric distinction from athlete's heart, hypertensive remodeling, and cardiac amyloidosis.
3. **Arrhythmogenic RV Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)**: Application of the 2010 Revised Task Force criteria: regional RV akinesis/dyskinesis/aneurysm combined with RVOT enlargement ($\text{PLAX} \ge 32\text{ mm}$, $\text{PSAX} \ge 36\text{ mm}$) or reduced fractional area change ($\text{RV FAC} \le 33\%$).
4. **Left Ventricular Non-Compaction (LVNC)**: End-systolic non-compacted to compacted myocardial thickness ratio ($\text{NC/C} > 2.0$) in short-axis views with prominent trabeculations and deep intertrabecular recesses perfused by intraventricular blood on colour Doppler.
5. **Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy**: Transient neurogenic apical/mid-ventricular ballooning with basal hyperkinesis, often complicated by dynamic LVOT obstruction, acute MR, and apical thrombosis, resolving completely within weeks.
6. **Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)**: Severe diastolic filling restriction ($E/A > 2$, $\text{EDT} < 140\text{ ms}$) with marked biatrial enlargement in normal-sized ventricles. Clear distinction from constrictive pericarditis using tissue Doppler ($e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$ in RCM vs. preserved $e'$ in constriction) and absence of significant respiratory variation.
</div></details>

## 8.0 Introduction: Classification of Cardiomyopathies</summary><div class="details-content">
### Morphofunctional Classification
According to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) working group on myocardial and pericardial diseases, cardiomyopathies are defined as myocardial disorders in which the heart muscle is structurally and functionally abnormal in the absence of coronary artery disease, hypertension, valvular heart disease, or congenital heart disease sufficient to explain the observed myocardial abnormality.

| Phenotypic Class | Architectural Hallmark | Primary Functional Abnormality | Key Clinical Consequences |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dilated (DCM)** | Left (or biventricular) ventricular dilation with normal/thinned wall | Global systolic impairment ($\text{LVEF} < 45\%$) | Congestive heart failure, ventricular tachyarrhythmias, thromboembolism |
| **Hypertrophic (HCM)** | Inappropriate hypertrophy ($\ge 15\text{ mm}$) without afterload trigger | Diastolic dysfunction $\pm$ dynamic LVOT obstruction | Sudden cardiac death (SCD), exertional syncope, heart failure |
| **Arrhythmogenic (ARVC)** | Fibrofatty replacement of RV myocardium $\pm$ LV involvement | Regional/global RV systolic dysfunction and aneurysm | Malignant ventricular arrhythmias (LBBB morphology), sudden death |
| **Restrictive (RCM)** | Rigid, non-compliant walls with biatrial dilation; normal cavity size | Severe diastolic restriction with normal/near-normal LVEF | Low-output state, severe pulmonary and systemic venous congestion |
| **Unclassified: LVNC** | Prominent endocardial trabeculations with deep perfused recesses | Variable systolic/diastolic dysfunction | Heart failure, stroke/embolism, ventricular arrhythmias |
| **Stress (Takotsubo)** | Transient regional wall motion abnormalities extending beyond single coronary territory | Acute stunning with apical/mid-ventricular ballooning | Acute pulmonary edema, cardiogenic shock, dynamic LVOT obstruction |
</div></details>

## 8.1 Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Diagnostic Echocardiographic Criteria
- **Ventricular Dilation**:
  - Left ventricular end-diastolic volume index ($\text{LVEDVi} > 112\text{ mL/m}^2$).
  - Left ventricular end-diastolic dimension ($\text{LVEDD} > 117\%$ of predicted based on age and body surface area by Henry's formula).
- **Depressed Systolic Function**:
  - $\text{LVEF} < 45\%$ (measured via 2D biplane Simpson method or 3D echocardiography) and/or fractional shortening ($\text{FS} < 25\%$).
- **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Absence of severe coronary artery disease ($> 50\%$ stenosis of major epicardial coronary trunk), sustained arterial hypertension ($\text{BP} > 160/100\text{ mmHg}$), significant valvular disease, or congenital shunts.

### Architectural Remodelling & Spherical Deformation
- **Spherical Remodelling**: In DCM, transverse diameters dilate disproportionately compared to long-axis length, transforming the normal prolate ellipsoid ventricle into a sphere.
  $$\text{Sphericity Index} = \frac{\text{LV Long-Axis Length}}{\text{LV Short-Axis Diameter}}$$
  A sphericity index $< 1.5$ in end-diastole denotes advanced spherical remodeling and wall stress elevation.
- **Relative Wall Thickness (RWT)**:
  $$\text{RWT} = \frac{2 \times \text{PWT}_d}{\text{LVEDD}}$$
  $\text{RWT} < 0.32$ confirms eccentric ventricular remodeling.

### Ventricular Dyssynchrony & Selection for CRT
In patients with DCM, mechanical dyssynchrony exacerbates pump failure:
1. **Atrioventricular (AV) Dyssynchrony**: Shortened diastolic filling time ($< 40\%$ of the RR cycle), fusion of mitral $E$ and $A$ waves, diastolic mitral regurgitation.
2. **Interventricular Dyssynchrony**: Interventricular mechanical delay (IVMD: difference between aortic pre-ejection interval and pulmonary pre-ejection interval $> 40\text{ ms}$).
3. **Intraventricular Mechanical Dyssynchrony**:
   - **Apical Rocking**: Rocking motion of the LV apex during ejection, swinging toward the late-activated wall (typically posterolateral).
   - **Septal Flash**: Early rapid inward-outward contraction of the interventricular septum within the isovolumic contraction period.
   - Delay between anteroseptal and posterior wall motion in M-mode $> 130\text{ ms}$.

### Prognostic Predictors in DCM
- Coexistence of right ventricular dysfunction ($\text{TAPSE} < 14\text{ mm}$, $\text{RV FAC} < 32\%$).
- Presence of a restrictive transmitral Doppler filling pattern ($E/A \ge 2.0$, $\text{EDT} < 140\text{ ms}$), particularly when fixed/unresponsive to preload reduction.
- Severe functional mitral regurgitation ($\text{EROA} \ge 0.20\text{ cm}^2$).
- Detection of left ventricular apical mural thrombus.

## 8.2 Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Morphologic Definition & Hypertrophic Patterns
HCM is defined by an unexplained maximal left ventricular end-diastolic wall thickness $\ge 15\text{ mm}$ in any myocardial segment (or $\ge 13\text{ mm}$ in an adult first-degree relative of a patient with unequivocal disease):
- **Asymmetric Septal Hypertrophy (ASH)**: Most common pattern ($> 70\%$). Hypertrophy is concentrated in the basal and mid-anterior interventricular septum. Ratio of septal thickness to posterior wall thickness ($\text{IVS/PW} > 1.3$ in normotensive, $> 1.5$ in hypertensive individuals).
- **Concentric Hypertrophy**: Symmetric distribution throughout all segments ($~10\%$).
- **Apical HCM (Yamaguchi Variant)**: Hypertrophy predominantly localized to the LV apex ($> 15\text{ mm}$). Characterized by "ace-of-spades" systolic obliteration in apical views, giant negative T waves on ECG, and absence of LVOT obstruction.
- **Mid-Ventricular Obstruction**: Mid-cavity muscular hypertrophy with systolic hourglass apposition of the septum and lateral wall, creating an isolated apical aneurysm with high risk of thromboembolism.

### Mitral Valve Apparatus Abnormalities
In HCM, primary anatomical abnormalities of the mitral valve complex contribute directly to dynamic obstruction:
- Elongation of the anterior mitral leaflet ($> 30\text{ mm}$) and posterior leaflet.
- Anterior and inward displacement of the papillary muscles.
- Direct insertion of anomalous papillary muscle heads into the body or free margin of the anterior leaflet without intervening chordae tendineae.

### Dynamic LVOT Obstruction & Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM)
- **Pathophysiology**: During early-to-mid systole, flow acceleration across the narrowed outflow tract generates drag and hydrodynamic forces that pull the redundant anterior (or posterior) mitral leaflet anteriorly toward the hypertrophied septum (**Systolic Anterior Motion — SAM**).
- **M-Mode Features**: Direct leaflet-septal contact occurring in early systole ($< 30\%$ of systole indicates mild, $> 30\%$ indicates severe obstruction) accompanied by mid-systolic partial closure (notch) of the aortic valve cusps.
- **Doppler Spectral Profile**: Continuous-wave (CW) Doppler interrogation across the LVOT reveals a late-peaking, dagger-shaped, concave velocity envelope:
  - *Distinction from MR*: The MR jet envelope begins earlier (during isovolumic contraction), has higher peak velocity ($5\text{--}6\text{ m/s}$), is symmetric, and contains higher frequency signals.
- **Hemodynamic Grading**:
  - **Non-Obstructive HCM**: Gradient $< 30\text{ mmHg}$ at rest and with provocation.
  - **Latent (Provocable) Obstruction**: Resting gradient $< 30\text{ mmHg}$, but rises to $\ge 50\text{ mmHg}$ during provocation (Valsalva manoeuvre, standing, or exercise echocardiography).
  - **Obstructive HCM**: Resting gradient $\ge 30\text{ mmHg}$ (severe obstruction defined as $\ge 50\text{ mmHg}$).

### Differential Diagnosis of Left Ventricular Hypertrophy

### Table 8.2.1 EACVI Updated Maron Criteria: HCM vs. Athlete's Heart
| Parameter | Athlete's Heart (Physiological Remodeling) | Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maximal Wall Thickness** | Rarely exceeds $12\text{--}13\text{ mm}$ (up to $15\text{ mm}$ in elite rowers) | Frequently $\ge 15\text{ mm}$ (often $> 20\text{--}30\text{ mm}$) |
| **LV End-Diastolic Dimension** | Enlarged ($\text{LVEDD} \ge 55\text{ mm}$, often $> 60\text{ mm}$) | Small or normal ($\text{LVEDD} < 45\text{ mm}$) |
| **Diastolic Function (TDI $e'$)** | Normal or supranormal (septal $e' > 10\text{ cm/s}$, lateral $> 12$) | Impaired (septal $e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$, lateral $< 10$) |
| **Tissue Doppler $E/e'$ Ratio** | Normal ($E/e' < 8$) | Elevated ($E/e' > 14$) |
| **Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS)** | Normal or supernormal ($> -18\%$) | Reduced (particularly in hypertrophied segments, $< -15\%$) |
| **Left Atrial Volume Index** | Normal or mildly dilated in proportion to LV | Markedly enlarged ($\text{LAVI} \ge 34\text{ mL/m}^2$) |
| **Response to Deconditioning** | Regression of wall thickness by $2\text{--}5\text{ mm}$ in $3\text{ months}$ | No regression of hypertrophy upon cessation of training |

| Feature | Hypertensive Heart Disease (HHD) | Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pattern of Hypertrophy** | Concentric, symmetric, homogeneous | Asymmetric (septal, apical, focal) |
| **Maximal Wall Thickness** | Rarely $> 15\text{ mm}$ | Frequently $> 15\text{ mm}$ |
| **Interventricular / Posterior Ratio** | $\text{IVS/PW} < 1.3$ (or $< 1.5$) | $\text{IVS/PW} \ge 1.5$ |
| **Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM)** | Absent (rare, unless severe dehydration/inotropes) | Characteristic hallmark in obstructive phenotype |
| **Mitral Leaflet Dimensions** | Normal length | Elongated anterior leaflet ($> 30\text{ mm}$) |

### Table 8.2.3 Differential Diagnosis: HCM vs. Cardiac Amyloidosis
| Characteristic | Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy | Cardiac Amyloidosis (AL or ATTR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Myocardial Appearance** | Normal echogenicity, regional hypertrophy | Granular, sparkling ground-glass appearance |
| **Biventricular Involvement** | Isolated LV hypertrophy (rare RV free wall) | Biventricular wall thickening + thickened interatrial septum |
| **Valvular Leaflets** | Normal thickness (elongated leaflets) | Diffuse nodular thickening of AV and semilunar valves |
| **Pericardial Effusion** | Rare | Common (small-to-moderate circumferential effusion) |
| **Strain Pattern (Bull's Eye)** | Patchy or septal strain reduction | Pathognomonic **apical sparing ("cherry-on-top")** pattern |

### Risk Stratification for Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)
The ESC 5-year SCD risk calculation integrates:
- Age at presentation.
- Maximal wall thickness ($\ge 30\text{ mm}$ represents high risk).
- Left atrial diameter ($\ge 45\text{ mm}$).
- Maximal resting or provoked LVOT gradient ($\ge 30\text{ mmHg}$).
- Family history of sudden cardiac death in $\ge 1$ first-degree relative under age 40.
- Documented non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (NSVT) on 24–48h Holter monitoring.
- Unexplained syncope.

### Guidance for Septal Reduction Therapy
- **Alcohol Septal Ablation (ASA)**: Myocardial contrast echocardiography (intracoronary injection of sonicated albumin or agitated radiographic contrast into target septal perforator) is mandatory before ethanol injection to delineate the territory and avoid inadvertently infusing remote myocardium or papillary muscles.
- **Surgical Septal Myectomy (Morrow Procedure)**: Intraoperative transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) defines the resection boundary, guides concomitant mitral valve procedures (plication, chordal cutting), and evaluates post-bypass results (residual gradient $< 20\text{ mmHg}$, abolition of SAM, no iatrogenic VSD).

## 8.3 Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Pathophysiology & Task Force Criteria
ARVC is a genetic cardiomyopathy characterized by progressive fibrofatty infiltration of the right ventricular myocardium, predisposing to re-entrant ventricular tachycardias with LBBB morphology and sudden death.

### Table 8.3.1 2010 Revised Task Force Echocardiographic Criteria for ARVC
| Category | Echocardiographic Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Major Criteria** | **Regional RV akinesis, dyskinesis, or aneurysm** AND $\ge 1$ of the following (measured in end-diastole):<br>• Parasternal long-axis RVOT ($\text{PLAX RVOT}$) $\ge 32\text{ mm}$ (or $\ge 19\text{ mm/m}^2$ indexed)<br>• Parasternal short-axis RVOT ($\text{PSAX RVOT}$) $\ge 36\text{ mm}$ (or $\ge 21\text{ mm/m}^2$ indexed)<br>• RV Fractional Area Change ($\text{FAC}$) $\le 33\%$ |

## 8.4 Left Ventricular Non-Compaction (LVNC)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Pathomorphology & Diagnostic Criteria
LVNC is characterized by failure of intrauterine myocardial compaction, resulting in a distinctive two-layer myocardial structure:
1. A thin compacted epicardial layer.
2. An abnormally thickened, loosely organized non-compacted endocardial layer composed of prominent muscular trabeculae and deep intertrabecular recesses communicating directly with the left ventricular cavity.

### Established Echocardiographic Criteria
- **Jenni Criteria (End-Systolic Measurement)**:
  - Ratio of non-compacted to compacted myocardium ($\text{NC/C} > 2.0$) measured at end-systole in the parasternal short-axis view (at basal, mid, and apical levels).
  - Predominant involvement of the apex, inferior, and lateral walls.
  - Direct demonstration of intraventricular blood flowing through the intertrabecular recesses using low-velocity color Doppler.
- **Chin Criteria (End-Diastolic Measurement)**:
  - Ratio of the distance from the epicardium to the trough of the trabeculae ($X$) to the distance from the epicardium to the peak of the trabeculae ($Y$): $X/Y \le 0.5$ at end-diastole.
- **Stöllberger Criteria**:
  - Visualization of $> 3$ hypertrabecular structures projecting from the wall, apical to the papillary muscles.

## 8.5 Myocarditis</summary><div class="details-content">
### Clinical Spectrum & Findings
Acute myocarditis presents with clinical manifestations ranging from asymptomatic or mild chest discomfort to fulminant cardiogenic shock.
- **Ventricular Geometry**: Usually normal cavity dimensions in acute fulminant phase with transient, diffuse increase in myocardial wall thickness secondary to interstitial edema. In subacute/chronic phases, progressive eccentric dilation mimicking DCM.
- **Contractile Dysfunction**: Segmental wall motion abnormalities (often non-coronary distribution, frequently sparing basal septum and involving inferolateral walls) or global hypokinesis.
- **Myopericarditis**: Concomitant small-to-moderate pericardial effusion is detected in $30\text{--}50\%$ of cases.
- **Thromboembolism**: Increased incidence of ventricular mural thrombi during acute inflammatory states.

## 8.6 Takotsubo (Stress) Cardiomyopathy</summary><div class="details-content">
### Diagnostic Features
Takotsubo syndrome is characterized by transient regional systolic dysfunction, typically preceded by acute emotional or physical triggers, presenting with chest pain and ECG changes mimicking STEMI:
- **Classic Apical Ballooning ($> 80\%$ of cases)**: Akinesis or dyskinesis of the apical and mid-ventricular segments with compensatory hyperkinesis of the basal segments.
- **Atypical / Variant Forms**:
  - Inverted / Basal Takotsubo (basal akinesis with apical hyperkinesis).
  - Mid-ventricular variant (mid-wall akinesis with preserved basal and apical function).
  - Focal variants (isolated segmental stunning).
  - Right ventricular involvement ($~30\%$ of cases).
- **Transient Complications**:
  - Dynamic LVOT obstruction (due to basal hyperkinesis and SAM, seen in $15\text{--}25\%$).
  - Acute severe mitral regurgitation.
  - Apical mural thrombosis ($2\text{--}5\%$).
- **Evolution**: Complete functional recovery of ejection fraction and wall motion within days to weeks ($> 95\%$ of cases).

## 8.7 Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Pathophysiology & Hemodynamics
RCM is characterized by increased myocardial stiffness and impaired ventricular filling with normal or decreased diastolic volumes:
- **Hallmark Features**:
  - Biatrial enlargement (often massive), normal or small ventricular cavities, preserved or mildly depressed LVEF.
  - Mitral inflow: Restrictive filling pattern ($E/A \ge 2.0$, $\text{EDT} < 140\text{ ms}$, elevated $E/e' > 15$).
- **Differential Diagnosis: RCM vs. Constrictive Pericarditis (CP)**:

| Feature | Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM) | Constrictive Pericarditis (CP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mitral Inflow Respiratory Variation** | Absent or minimal ($< 10\%$) | Marked ($> 25\%$ decrease in $E$ velocity with inspiration) |
| **Tissue Doppler Mitral Septal $e'$** | Markedly depressed ($< 7\text{ cm/s}$) | Preserved or elevated ($> 8\text{--}10\text{ cm/s}$) |
| **Annulus Paradoxus** | Absent | Present (normal/high $e'$ despite elevated filling pressures) |
| **Annulus Reversus** | Absent (lateral $e' >$ septal $e'$) | Present (medial septal $e' \ge$ lateral $e'$) |
| **Hepatic Vein Doppler** | Prominent diastolic flow reversal on inspiration | Prominent diastolic flow reversal on **expiration** |
| **Ventricular Interdependence** | Absent | Marked (respiratory septal bounce / shift) |
