# Chapter 17. Systemic Disease and Other Conditions

<details class="med-details" open><summary>
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Athlete's Heart vs. Cardiomyopathies**: Physiological adaptation in athletes (Morganroth hypothesis) produces harmonic eccentric remodeling in endurance sports (increased cavity size, LVEDD up to 60–65 mm) or concentric remodeling in resistance/static sports (mild wall thickening $\le 12$ mm, rarely 13–15 mm "grey zone"). Differentiating features from Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) include: regression of hypertrophy after 3 months of deconditioning ($\ge 2$ mm), normal/supernormal tissue Doppler relaxation ($e' > 9–10$ cm/s, $E/e' < 6–8$), normal global longitudinal strain (GLS $-18\%$ to $-22\%$) without segmental heterogeneity, and lack of systolic anterior motion (SAM) or dynamic LVOT obstruction.
- **Cardiovascular Adaptations & Pathology in Pregnancy**: Marked maternal hemodynamic volume overload (blood volume $+50\%$, cardiac output $+50\%$, heart rate $+25–30\%$, systemic vascular resistance $-30–40\%$) results in mild 4-chamber eccentric remodeling, physiological transvalvular flow murmurs, and benign small pericardial effusions ($< 40\%$). Modified WHO Class IV conditions (pregnancy contraindicated due to maternal mortality up to $50\%$) include severe pulmonary arterial hypertension, severe systemic LV dysfunction ($\text{LVEF} < 30\%$, NYHA III–IV), prior peripartum cardiomyopathy with residual dysfunction, severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, and Marfan syndrome with aortic root $> 45$ mm.
- **Infiltrative & Storage Diseases**:
- *Cardiac Amyloidosis*: Concentric biventricular pseudohypertrophy, granular sparkling myocardial texture, biatrial enlargement, atrial septal thickening ($> 3$ mm), thickened valve leaflets, and pathognomonic **apical sparing** of longitudinal strain ("cherry on top" on polar plot; apical-to-basal strain ratio $> 1.0$).
- *Fabry Disease*: Concentric LVH mimicking HCM, "binary" appearance of the endocardial border, and characteristic early basal inferolateral hypokinesia/thinning.
- **Rheumatological & Connective Tissue Diseases**:
- *Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)*: Pericarditis / pericardial effusion ($20–40\%$) and **Libman-Sacks verrucous endocarditis** (non-bacterial broad-based sessile vegetations on mitral/aortic leaflets).
- *Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)*: Patchy myocardial fibrosis and secondary severe precapillary pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH).
- *Marfan & Loeys-Dietz*: Severe annuloaortic ectasia, aortic root/ascending aneurysm, Type A dissection, and mitral valve prolapse.
- *Takayasu Arteritis*: Circumferential mural thickening of the aorta and major branches ("macaroni sign").
- **Tropical & Specialized Myocardial Diseases**:
- *Endomyocardial Fibrosis (EMF) & Hypereosinophilic Syndrome (Loeffler)*: Apical obliteration of LV and/or RV by dense fibrous tissue and organized thrombus with severe restrictive filling.
- *Carcinoid Heart Disease*: Rigid, retracted, thickened, non-coapting tricuspid valve leaflets with free low-velocity regurgitation.
- *Chagas Disease*: Pathognomonic thin-walled, narrow-neck apical aneurysm with apical thrombus.
- **Cardio-Oncology (CTRCD)**: Surveillance criteria for cancer therapy-related cardiotoxicity: drop in LVEF $\ge 10$ percentage points to a value $< 50\%$, or relative reduction in Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS) by $> 15\%$ from baseline.

## 17.1 Athlete's Heart: Physiological Remodeling & Differential Diagnosis</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Physical athletic training induces profound, reversible structural, functional, and electrical cardiac remodeling collectively designated as the **athlete's heart**.

### Morganroth Hypothesis & Exercise Modalities
The Morganroth hypothesis classifies cardiac remodeling according to the predominant hemodynamic stress of the sporting discipline:
1. **Endurance / Dynamic Sports (Running, Cycling, Swimming, Rowing)**: Characterized by sustained volume overload (elevated cardiac output and stroke volume with low systemic vascular resistance). Induces **eccentric left ventricular hypertrophy** (proportional enlargement of LV internal diameter, cavity volume, and wall thickness, with a normal relative wall thickness $\text{RWT} \le 0.42$).
2. **Strength / Static / Resistance Sports (Weightlifting, Wrestling, Martial Arts)**: Characterized by transient marked pressure overload (acute elevations in systolic and diastolic arterial blood pressure during Valsalva maneuvers). Induces **concentric left ventricular remodeling / hypertrophy** (increased wall thickness with normal or slightly reduced cavity dimension, resulting in elevated $\text{RWT} > 0.42$).
3. **Mixed / Combined Sports (Soccer, Rugby, Basketball, Tennis)**: Combine both substantial dynamic and static components, inducing mixed eccentric-concentric remodeling.

### Echocardiographic Features of the Athlete's Heart
- **LV Cavity Dimensions**: End-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) commonly exceeds normal adult cut-offs ($> 55$ mm), frequently reaching $60–65$ mm in elite endurance athletes (rowers, cyclists).
- **LV Wall Thickness**: Maximal wall thickness is typically $\le 12$ mm. A small minority ($1.5–2\%$) of elite male athletes display wall thickness in the "grey zone" between $13$ and $15$ mm. In female athletes, wall thickness rarely exceeds $11$ mm.
- **Left Atrial Remodeling**: Atrial dilation is common (volume indexed up to $35–45\text{ mL/m}^2$), but atrial reservoir and conduit strain remain normal or supernormal.
- **Right Ventricle**: Harmonious enlargement of RV outflow and inflow dimensions, matching LV dilation, with preserved systolic mechanics (TAPSE, fractional area change).

### Differential Diagnosis: Athlete's Heart vs. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

| Echocardiographic Parameter | Athlete's Heart (Physiological) | Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maximal Wall Thickness** | Rarely $> 13$ mm; very rarely $13–15$ mm ("grey zone") | Usually $> 15$ mm (or $> 13$ mm with positive family history) |
| **Hypertrophy Symmetry** | Symmetric, homogeneous, harmonious distribution | Asymmetric septal, apical, or focal hypertrophy |
| **LV End-Diastolic Dimension** | Enlarged: $\text{LVEDD} > 55$ mm (often $55–65$ mm) | Small cavity: $\text{LVEDD} < 45$ mm (almost always $< 50$ mm) |
| **Left Atrial Geometry & Function** | Symmetrically enlarged; normal/enhanced reservoir strain | Disproportionately enlarged; impaired reservoir strain |
| **Mitral Valve Apparatus** | Normal leaflet length; no systolic anterior motion (SAM) | Elongated leaflets; SAM of anterior leaflet; dynamic LVOT obstruction ($> 30$ mmHg) |
| **Mitral Regurgitation** | Trivial or physiological central jet | Moderate-to-severe eccentric posteriorly directed jet (due to SAM) |
| **Global Diastolic Function** | Supernormal: $E/A > 1.5$, deceleration time normal | Impaired relaxation: $E/A < 1.0$ or restrictive filling |
| **Tissue Doppler Early Diastolic Velocity ($e'$)** | Septal $e' > 9–10$ cm/s; lateral $e' > 12$ cm/s; $E/e' < 8$ | Septal $e' < 7$ cm/s; lateral $e' < 8$ cm/s; $E/e' > 12–14$ |
| **2D Speckle-Tracking Global Longitudinal Strain** | Normal/supernormal: $\text{GLS} -18\%$ to $-22\%$ | Impaired: $\text{GLS} > -16\%$ with marked regional heterogeneity in hypertrophied segments |
| **LV Untwisting Rate** | Rapid, normal untwisting kinetics | Delayed untwisting extending beyond $25\%$ of diastole |
| **Response to Deconditioning (Detraining)** | **Regression of LV wall thickness by 2–5 mm** after 3 months of complete physical inactivity | **Persistence of hypertrophy** without wall thinning |

### Differential Diagnosis: Athlete's Heart vs. Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) & ARVC

| Parameter | Athlete's Heart | Idiopathic Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LV Cavity Dilation** | Enlarged ($\text{LVEDD} > 55–60$ mm), but maintains normal conical/ellipsoid geometry (normal sphericity index) | Globular, spherical dilation (increased sphericity index) |
| **Resting LVEF** | Normal ($55–65\%$) or borderline low-normal ($50–54\%$) due to high stroke volume | Frank systolic depression ($\text{LVEF} < 45–50\%$) |
| **Contractile Reserve on Exercise** | **Normal**: LVEF increases markedly ($\Delta\text{EF} > 11\%$) during exercise | **Impaired**: LVEF fails to increase or declines during exercise |
| **Diastolic Filling Pressures** | Completely normal: $E/e' < 8$ | Elevated: $E/e' > 14$, shortened deceleration time |

<figure class="med-figure" id="fig-17-07">
  <img src="images/fig-17-07.jpg" alt="RV enlargement in endurance athlete" class="med-image" loading="lazy" /
  <figcaption class="med-caption">Fig. 17.1.7: Physiological right ventricular enlargement in an endurance athlete. Apical 4-chamber view demonstrating enlarged RV basal diameter (44 mm) with preserved global and regional contractility, distinguishing it from ARVC.</figcaption>
</figure>

In Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC), RV enlargement is characterized by regional wall motion abnormalities (akinesia, dyskinesia, or localized bulging/aneurysms of the outflow tract or subtricuspid region) and impaired RV fractional area change ($\text{FAC} < 35\%$), whereas in endurance athletes, RV enlargement is symmetric, harmonious, and devoid of dyssynchrony or sacculations.

## 17.2 The Heart During Pregnancy: Hemodynamics & Risk Stratification</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Pregnancy imposes profound hemodynamic demands on the maternal cardiovascular system, peaking between the 28th and 34th gestational weeks and during labor and delivery.

### Maternal Hemodynamic & Echocardiographic Changes

| Hemodynamic Parameter | Gestational Change | Clinical & Echocardiographic Correlates |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intravascular Blood Volume** | **Increases by $30–50\%$** | Plasma volume increases proportionally more than erythrocyte mass (physiological hemodilution / pregnancy anemia) |
| **Cardiac Output (CO)** | **Increases by $30–50\%$** | Due to combined rise in stroke volume ($+20–30\%$) and resting heart rate ($+15–20\text{ bpm}$) |
| **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)** | **Decreases by $30–40\%$** | Low-resistance uteroplacental vascular shunt, progesterone-mediated vascular smooth muscle relaxation |
| **Arterial Blood Pressure** | **Decreases in 1st/2nd trimester** | Diastolic BP drops by $10–15$ mmHg (nadir at 20–24 weeks), gradually returning to pre-pregnancy baseline by term |
| **LV End-Diastolic Dimension** | **Mildly increased** | Eccentric remodeling with 4-chamber chamber dilation ($5–10\%$ increase in LVEDD and LV mass) |
| **Valvular Regurgitation** | **Physiological increase** | Mild functional mitral, tricuspid, and pulmonary regurgitation secondary to annular stretching |
| **Pericardial Fluid** | **Small physiological effusion** | Benign, asymptomatic trivial-to-small pericardial effusion present in up to $40\%$ of healthy women near term |

### Modified WHO Classification of Maternal Cardiovascular Risk
Echocardiographic assessment is central to preconception counseling and risk stratification based on the Modified World Health Organization (mWHO) criteria:

- **mWHO Class I (Very Low Risk, Maternal Mortality $< 0.1\%$):** Mild pulmonary valve stenosis, patent ductus arteriosus, repaired simple lesions (ASD, VSD).
- **mWHO Class II (Low-to-Moderate Risk, Mortality $0.1–1\%$):** Unoperated ASD/VSD, repaired tetralogy of Fallot, mild mitral stenosis, Turner syndrome without aortic root dilation.
- **mWHO Class III (High Risk, Maternal Mortality $1–5\%$):** Mechanical prosthetic heart valve, moderate mitral stenosis, systemic right ventricle (congenitally corrected TGA), Fontan circulation, Marfan syndrome with aortic diameter $40–45$ mm, BAV with aortic diameter $45–50$ mm. Requires intensive multidisciplinary Pregnancy Heart Team monitoring.
- **mWHO Class IV (Extremely High Risk, Maternal Mortality $20–50\%$ — PREGNANCY CONTRAINDICATED):**
  1. **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** of any etiology (idiopathic, Eisenmenger syndrome, connective tissue disease).
  2. **Severe Systemic Ventricular Dysfunction**: $\text{LVEF} < 30\%$ or NYHA functional class III–IV.
  3. **Previous Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM)** with any residual impairment of LV systolic function.
  4. **Severe Symptomatic Valvular Aortic Stenosis** or severe mitral stenosis ($\text{MVA} < 1.0\text{ cm}^2$).
  5. **Vascular Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome** (high risk of fatal spontaneous uterine and arterial rupture).
  6. **Aortic Aneurysm Exceeding Safe Limits**: Marfan syndrome with ascending aorta $> 45$ mm (or $> 40$ mm in Loeys-Dietz / family history of rupture), Bicuspid aortic valve with aorta $> 50$ mm.

### Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM)
- **Definition**: Idiopathic cardiomyopathy presenting with heart failure secondary to LV systolic dysfunction ($\text{LVEF} < 45\%$) towards the end of pregnancy or in the months following delivery, where no other identifiable cause is found.
- **Echo Findings**: Global LV hypokinesia, spherical LV remodeling, dynamic mitral regurgitation, LV apical mural thrombi, and impaired global longitudinal strain.
- **Prognosis & Subsequent Pregnancies**: Full recovery of LVEF occurs in $50–70\%$ of patients within 6 months. Subsequent pregnancy is strongly discouraged if LVEF remains $< 50\%$ (mWHO IV, high mortality). Even if LVEF normalizes, subsequent pregnancy carries a $20–30\%$ risk of disease recurrence.

## 17.3 Infiltrative & Storage Diseases: Amyloidosis & Fabry Disease</summary>
<div class="details-content">

### Cardiac Amyloidosis (AL & ATTR)
Cardiac amyloidosis results from extracellular deposition of insoluble fibrillar protein aggregates (light-chain AL amyloidosis or transthyretin ATTR amyloidosis) within the myocardial interstitium.

<figure class="med-figure" id="fig-17-08">
  <img src="images/fig-17-08.jpg" alt="Cardiac amyloidosis 4-chamber view" class="med-image" loading="lazy" /
  <figcaption class="med-caption">Fig. 17.3.1: Cardiac amyloidosis on apical 4-chamber view: Markedly thickened biventricular walls with "granular sparkling" ground-glass myocardial appearance, biatrial enlargement, and interatrial septal thickening.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **2D Echocardiographic Hallmarks**:
  - Concentric biventricular wall thickening (pseudohypertrophy; typically $> 12–15$ mm) in the absence of systemic hypertension.
  - Granular, "sparkling" or ground-glass myocardial acoustic texture.
  - Interatrial septal thickening ($> 3$ mm).
  - Thickened atrioventricular and semilunar valve leaflets without significant stenosis.
  - Biatrial enlargement out of proportion to ventricular cavity size.
  - Small, non-compressive pericardial and pleural effusions.
- **Doppler Hemodynamics**: Restrictive LV filling pattern: tall $E$-wave, marked decrease in deceleration time ($\text{DT} < 150$ ms), tiny or absent $A$-wave ($E/A > 2.0$), profoundly diminished tissue Doppler velocities (septal and lateral $e' < 5$ cm/s), and markedly elevated $E/e' > 15–20$.

<figure class="med-figure" id="fig-17-09">
  <img src="images/fig-17-09.jpg" alt="Restrictive filling in amyloidosis" class="med-image" loading="lazy" /
  <figcaption class="med-caption">Fig. 17.3.2: Severe restrictive LV filling pattern in cardiac amyloidosis: tall E-wave, shortened deceleration time (< 140 ms), and low-velocity A-wave reflecting stiff, non-compliant ventricles.</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure class="med-figure" id="fig-17-10">
  <img src="images/fig-17-10.jpg" alt="TTE 4CV of amyloidosis in diastole and systole" class="med-image" loading="lazy" /
  <figcaption class="med-caption">Fig. 17.3.3: Apical 4-chamber view in diastole (A) and systole (B) in advanced cardiac amyloidosis illustrating small ventricular cavities and preserved radial ejection fraction despite severely reduced longitudinal shortening.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Strain Imaging: Apical Sparing Pattern ("Cherry on Top")**:
  - Marked, severe depression of longitudinal strain in the basal and mid-ventricular segments, with complete preservation of strain at the apex.
  - Quantified by the **Apical Sparing Ratio**:
    $$\text{Apical Sparing Index} = \frac{\text{Average Apical Longitudinal Strain}}{\text{Average Basal Strain} + \text{Average Mid-Ventricular Strain}} > 1.0$$
  - Highly sensitive ($> 90\%$) and specific ($> 85\%$) for distinguishing cardiac amyloidosis from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and hypertensive heart disease.

### Fabry Disease (Anderson-Fabry)
- **Etiology**: X-linked lysosomal storage disorder caused by deficient $\alpha$-galactosidase A activity, leading to intracellular accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3).
- **Echocardiographic Hallmarks**:
  - Progressive concentric LV hypertrophy mimicking non-obstructive HCM.
  - "Binary" appearance of the endocardial border (hyperechoic endocardial layer overlying a hypoechoic subendocardial layer).
  - Prominent early hypokinesia, thinning, and fibrosis confined to the **basal inferolateral / posterior wall** (best visualized on tissue Doppler and strain).
  - Papillary muscle hypertrophy and conduction system disease (short PR interval progressing to AV block).

## 17.4 Rheumatological, Autoimmune & Vasculitic Diseases</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Autoimmune rheumatic diseases frequently involve the pericardium, myocardium, endocardium, and great vessels:

### 1. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) & Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)
- **Pericarditis**: The most common cardiovascular manifestation ($20–40\%$), presenting with pericardial thickening, chest pain, and small-to-moderate pericardial effusions.
- **Myocarditis**: Occurs in $5–10\%$, presenting as diffuse LV hypokinesia, cavity dilation, and depressed global longitudinal strain.
- **Libman-Sacks Verrucous Endocarditis**:
  - Non-bacterial, broad-based, sessile, verrucous vegetations ($2–10$ mm in diameter).
  - Located characteristically on the atrial surface of the posterior mitral leaflet or ventricular surface of aortic leaflets.
  - Does not destroy leaflet integrity (no perforation or rupture), but causes leaflet thickening, retraction, and regurgitation.
  - Strongly associated with positive antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin).

### 2. Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma) & MCTD
- **Myocardial Involvement**: Patchy microvascular ischemia and progressive interstitial fibrosis leading to diastolic dysfunction and ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)**: Develops in up to $15–20\%$ of systemic sclerosis patients (especially limited cutaneous / CREST syndrome). Echo screening is mandatory: peak tricuspid regurgitant velocity $V_{\text{TR}} > 2.8–3.4\text{ m/s}$ (systolic PAP $> 40–50\text{ mmHg}$), RV dilation, and right atrial enlargement.
- **Mixed Connective Tissue Disease (MCTD)**: High incidence of mitral valve prolapse (MVP) and pulmonary hypertension.

### 3. Heritable Thoracic Aortic Diseases (Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, Ehlers-Danlos)
- **Marfan Syndrome (*FBN1* mutation)**:
  - Severe **annuloaortic ectasia**: pear-shaped symmetrical dilation of the aortic root and sinuses of Valsalva with effacement of the sinotubular junction.
  - Progressive severe aortic regurgitation.
  - High risk of acute Type A aortic dissection.
  - Myxomatous mitral valve prolapse ($40–50\%$) with bileaflet redundancy.
- **Loeys-Dietz Syndrome (*TGFBR1/2* mutations)**:
  - Widespread, aggressive arterial aneurysms and tortuosity throughout the head, neck, and thorax.
  - Aortic dissection occurs at smaller diameters ($< 40–45$ mm) and earlier ages than in Marfan syndrome.
- **Vascular Ehlers-Danlos (Type IV, *COL3A1*)**:
  - Extreme vascular fragility with catastrophic spontaneous aortic and visceral arterial rupture.

### 4. Large Vessel Vasculitis (Takayasu Arteritis & Giant Cell Arteritis)
- **Takayasu Arteritis**: Chronic granulomatous panarteritis of the aorta and its primary branches in young females ($< 40$ years).
- **Echo Findings**:
  - Smooth, concentric, circumferential wall thickening of the ascending, arch, or descending thoracic aorta (known as the **"macaroni sign"**).
  - Ostial narrowing of the supra-aortic branches (brachiocephalic, carotid, subclavian) and coronary ostia.
  - Secondary severe aortic regurgitation due to ascending aortic root dilation.

## 17.5 Tropical, Endocrine & Other Secondary Cardiomyopathies</summary>
<div class="details-content">

### 1. Hypereosinophilic Syndrome (Loeffler) & Endomyocardial Fibrosis (EMF)
- **Pathophysiology**: Sustained marked hypereosinophilia ($> 1.5 \times 10^9/\text{L}$) causes eosinophilic degranulation, releasing toxic cationic proteins (major basic protein, eosinophil cationic protein) that damage the endomyocardium. Evolves through three distinct stages:
  1. *Necrotic / Acute Stage*: Endomyocarditis, subendocardial necrosis, microabscesses.
  2. *Thrombotic Stage*: Extensive mural thrombus formation along denuded subendocardium.
  3. *Fibrotic Stage*: Dense endomyocardial collagenous scarring and calcification.
- **Echo Findings**:
  - Obliteration of the LV and/or RV apex by dense echogenic fibrous tissue and organized thrombus, with normal basal cavity dimensions (apical blunting).
  - Entrapment and tethering of the posterior mitral leaflet or tricuspid leaflet in dense fibrous plaque, causing severe regurgitation.
  - Marked biatrial enlargement with a restrictive ventricular filling pattern.

### 2. Carcinoid Heart Disease
- **Etiology**: Metastatic neuroendocrine tumors (typically ileal or appendiceal carcinoid with liver metastases) releasing vasoactive humoral substances (serotonin, tachykinins, bradykinin) directly into the systemic venous circulation.
- **Echo Hallmarks**:
  - Exclusively or predominantly affects **right-sided cardiac structures** (serotonin is inactivated during passage through the pulmonary capillary bed, sparing left-sided valves unless an intracardiac right-to-left shunt or bronchial carcinoid exists).
  - Tricuspid valve leaflets and subvalvular cords become thickened, rigid, shortened, and retracted, failing to coapt in systole.
  - **Severe "Free" Tricuspid Regurgitation**: Wide systolic coaptation gap with a dagger-shaped, low-velocity continuous-wave Doppler spectrum.
  - Pulmonary valve cusps become thickened, retracted, and fixed, leading to pulmonary stenosis and regurgitation.

### 3. Chagas Disease (*Trypanosoma cruzi*)
- **Etiology**: Endemic protozoan infection in Latin America transmitted by triatomine bugs.
- **Echo Hallmarks**:
  - **Narrow-Neck Apical Aneurysm**: Pathognomonic thin-walled saccular outpouching of the LV apex with a narrow connecting orifice (present in $> 50\%$ of chronic Chagas cardiomyopathy).
  - High propensity for apical mural thrombus and systemic thromboembolism.
  - Segmental inferolateral and apical hypokinesia/dyskinesia with progressive global biventricular dilated cardiomyopathy.
  - Severe conduction system disease (right bundle branch block and left anterior fascicular block).

### 4. Cancer Therapy-Related Cardiac Dysfunction (CTRCD / Cardio-Oncology)
With the exponential rise in cancer survivor longevity, echocardiographic monitoring for cardiotoxicity is a frontline standard of care:
- **Cardiotoxic Classes**:
  - *Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin, Epirubicin)*: Dose-dependent, cumulative, irreversible cardiomyocyte necrosis and vacuolization (Type I cardiotoxicity).
  - *HER2 Inhibitors (Trastuzumab)*: Dose-independent, reversible myocardial stunning (Type II cardiotoxicity).
  - *Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (ICIs - Nivolumab, Pembrolizumab)*: Acute fulminant autoimmune lymphocytic myocarditis.
- **EACVI/ESC Diagnostic Criteria for CTRCD**:
  - **Definite CTRCD**: A decrease in LVEF of $\ge 10$ percentage points to a value $< 50\%$, confirmed on repeat echocardiography 2–3 weeks later.
  - **Subclinical Cardiotoxicity**: A relative reduction in **Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS) by $> 15\%$** from pre-treatment baseline, even if LVEF remains $\ge 50\%$. Indicates early subclinical myocardial injury, warranting early cardioprotective therapy ($\beta$-blockers, ACE inhibitors).
