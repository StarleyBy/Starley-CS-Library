# Chapter 6. Ischaemic Cardiac Disease (ICD)

<details class="med-details" open><summary>📌 Executive Summary: Echocardiography in Ischaemic Heart Disease</summary><div class="details-content">
Echocardiography represents the premier first-line diagnostic and risk-stratification modality in acute coronary syndromes (ACS), acute myocardial infarction (AMI), and chronic ischaemic heart disease.

### Key Clinical Objectives
1. **Detection and Quantification of Ischaemia / Infarction**: Immediate identification of regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA) using the standardized 16- or 17-segment model, calculation of the Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI), and quantification of global left ventricular systolic function via 2D biplane Simpson method, 3D echocardiography, and global longitudinal strain (GLS).
2. **Identification of Mechanical Complications**: Rapid bedside recognition of life-threatening post-infarction mechanical emergencies:
   - Free wall rupture and acute pseudoaneurysm formation (narrow neck ratio $< 0.5$, to-and-fro turbulent flow).
   - Ventricular septal rupture (VSR) with left-to-right shunt and thrill.
   - Acute ischaemic mitral regurgitation due to papillary muscle rupture or severe tethering.
   - True left ventricular aneurysm with persistent diastolic/systolic deformity and secondary mural thrombosis.
   - Right ventricular infarction (concomitant in up to 33% of inferior STEMI).
   - Pericardial effusion (present in ~30% of transmural infarctions; contraindication to thrombolysis).
3. **Prognostic Stratification in Chronic ICD**: Long-term risk profiling based on $\text{LVEF} < 25\%$, $\text{WMSI} \ge 1.7$, restrictive diastolic filling ($E/e' > 15$, $e' < 3\text{ cm/s}$), significant functional mitral regurgitation ($\text{EROA} \ge 0.20\text{ cm}^2$), RV dysfunction ($\text{TAPSE} < 14\text{ mm}$, $\text{FAC} < 32\%$), and assessment of myocardial viability vs. scar (wall thickness $< 5\text{ mm}$).
</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>6.0 Introduction: Role of Echocardiography in ICD</summary><div class="details-content">
### Diagnostic Value of Echocardiography
In patients presenting with acute chest pain, echocardiography provides immediate confirmation or exclusion of myocardial ischaemia, regional necrosis, or alternative catastrophic aetiologies (e.g. acute aortic dissection, acute massive pulmonary embolism, pericardial tamponade).

#### Definition of Segmental Wall Motion Abnormalities
Segmental wall motion and thickening are evaluated in multiple standard views (parasternal long- and short-axis, apical 4-, 2-, and 3-chamber views):

| Wall Motion Class | Systolic Wall Thickening (%) | Endocardial Excursion & Characteristics | Clinical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal / Hyperkinetic** | $> 40\%$ | Vigorous inward endocardial motion and systolic thickening | Preserved regional perfusion / hyperdynamic compensation |
| **Hypokinesis** | $< 40\%$ | Reduced inward excursion and impaired thickening | Myocardial ischaemia, stunning, or subendocardial infarction |
| **Akinesis** | $< 10\%$ | Negligible thickening and absent inward displacement | Transmural infarction, severe stunning, or hibernating myocardium |
| **Dyskinesis** | $< 0\%$ (Thinning) | Paradoxical outward systolic expansion / bulging with thinning | Established transmural scar / true aneurysm |

#### Diastolic Wall Thickness and Chronicity
- **Acute / Recent Infarction**: Myocardial diastolic wall thickness is generally conserved or transiently increased secondary to interstitial oedema and intramyocardial haemorrhage.
- **Chronic Scar**: Chronic myocardial necrosis results in progressive resorption of myocytes, fibrous replacement, and structural thinning (diastolic wall thickness $< 5\text{ mm}$, hyperechoic/bright scarred appearance).

#### Segmental Scoring and Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI)
Using the standardized 16- or 17-segment model of the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI), each segment is assigned a numerical score:
$$\text{Segment Score} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{Normal or hyperkinetic} \\ 2 & \text{Hypokinetic} \\ 3 & \text{Akinetic} \\ 4 & \text{Dyskinetic (or aneurysmal)} \end{cases}$$

The **Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI)** represents the extent of regional systolic dysfunction:
$$\text{WMSI} = \frac{\sum \text{Segment Scores}}{\text{Number of Visualized Segments}}$$
- Normal WMSI $= 1.0$.
- A $\text{WMSI} \ge 1.7$ indicates substantial regional impairment and corresponds to an adverse clinical prognosis and elevated risk of congestive heart failure.

#### Ruling Out Acute MI in Prolonged Chest Pain
- The presence of completely normal wall motion and systolic thickening in all myocardial segments during active or recent prolonged chest pain has a high negative predictive value ($> 95\%$) for excluding acute transmural myocardial infarction.
- Normal regional wall motion does not completely exclude non-ST-elevation ACS with transient ischaemia, tiny subendocardial necroses, or high-risk unstable angina.

<details class="med-details"><summary>6.1 Assessment of Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) & Risk Stratification</summary><div class="details-content">
### Echocardiographic Stratification of Post-MI Risk
Immediate bedside echocardiography identifies patients at high risk of in-hospital cardiogenic shock, malignant ventricular arrhythmias, and long-term heart failure.

#### Global Left Ventricular Systolic Function
- **Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF)**:
  - $\text{LVEF} < 40\%$ denotes high in-hospital and post-discharge morbidity and mortality.
  - Quantification should be performed using the 2D biplane Simpson method of discs (apical 4- and 2-chamber views). 3D echocardiography should be used whenever available to circumvent apical foreshortening and geometric assumptions.
- **Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS)**:
  - Assessed by 2D speckle-tracking echocardiography; highly sensitive for subclinical or residual systolic dysfunction even when visual LVEF appears preserved.
- **Left Ventricular $dP/dt$**:
  - Measured from the continuous-wave (CW) Doppler spectrum of mitral regurgitation ($dP/dt < 1000\text{ mmHg/s}$ indicates severe contractile impairment).

#### Cavity Dimensions and Post-Infarction Remodelling
- **Early Infarct Expansion**:
  - Defined as acute LV cavity enlargement ($\text{LVEDD} \ge 60\text{ mm}$ or $\ge 40\text{ mm/m}^2$) within the first hours to days following STEMI without additional myocardial necrosis, driven by wall stretching, sliding, and thinning of necrotic tissue.
- **Global Progressive Remodelling**:
  - Occurs over days to months, manifested by progressive cavity dilation, architectural spherical deformation, and neurohormonal activation.
  - **Sphericity Index**:
    $$\text{Sphericity Index} = \frac{\text{LV End-Diastolic Short-Axis Diameter}}{\text{LV End-Diastolic Long-Axis Length}}$$
    A sphericity index $> 0.25$ is an established predictor of progressive adverse LV remodelling.
- **Left Atrial Volume Index (LAVI)**:
  - $\text{LAVI} \ge 31\text{ mL/m}^2$ reflects chronically elevated LV filling pressures and is an independent predictor of death and readmission for heart failure.
- **Diastolic Filling Dynamics**:
  - A restrictive filling pattern ($E/A \ge 2.0$, deceleration time $< 140\text{ ms}$, elevated $E/e' > 15$), especially if persistent after preload reduction or during Valsalva manoeuvre, indicates markedly elevated LV end-diastolic pressure and portends a dismal prognosis.
</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>6.2 Mechanical and Hemodynamic Complications of AMI</summary><div class="details-content">
Mechanical complications of AMI typically occur within the first 2 to 7 days (or up to 2 weeks) following the index ischaemic event. They carry catastrophic mortality rates without emergent surgical or catheter-based intervention.

---

### 1. Left Ventricular True Aneurysm
- **Incidence**: 10–22% of first anterior transmural myocardial infarctions.
- **Timing**: May be detected as early as 5 days post-MI.
- **Pathology**: Localized thinned, scarred, fibrous myocardial segment involving the entire wall thickness.
- **Echo Characteristics**:
  - Persistent systolic **and** diastolic structural deformity with a wide neck (ratio of aneurysm neck to maximum internal diameter $> 0.5$).
  - Paradoxical systolic expansion (dyskinesis) with loss of normal architecture.
  - Frequent site of intra-aneurysmal blood stasis and mural thrombus formation.
- **Prognosis**: Associated with high 3-year mortality (~60%), recurrent heart failure, and refractory ventricular tachyarrhythmias originating from the peri-infarct border zone.

### 2. Left Ventricular Pseudoaneurysm (False Aneurysm)
- **Pathogenesis**: Subacute free wall rupture contained exclusively by the visceral and parietal pericardium, organized fibrinous thrombus, and epicardial adhesions.
- **Etiology**: AMI (accounting for ~3% of AMI cases; responsible for 5–10% of early post-infarction deaths), cardiac surgery, blunt thoracic trauma, or infective endocarditis. Predominantly associated with left circumflex artery or RCA occlusion (inferolateral and posterior walls).
- **Prognostic Impact**: Extremely high risk of catastrophic secondary rupture into the pericardial space causing fatal cardiac tamponade. Requires immediate emergency surgical repair.
- **Differential Diagnostic Echo Features (True vs. False Aneurysm)**:

| Feature | True LV Aneurysm | LV Pseudoaneurysm (PSA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wall Composition** | Full-thickness scarred myocardium, endocardium, and epicardium | Pericardium and organized thrombus; absent myocardial fibres |
| **Neck-to-Chamber Ratio** | Wide neck ($\text{Orifice} / \text{Diameter} > 0.5$) | Narrow neck ($\text{Orifice} / \text{Diameter} < 0.5$) |
| **Wall Transition** | Smooth transition from adjacent myocardium | Abrupt disruption and sharp edges of the myocardial wall |
| **Chamber Contour** | Broad, blunted, continuous with cavity | Globular, saccular false chamber |
| **Doppler Flow Profile** | Low-velocity swirling or forward flow | High-velocity turbulent to-and-fro flow through the narrow neck |
| **PW Doppler Spectrum** | Monophasic or blunted low velocities | Biphasic: systolic flow into the pouch and diastolic return flow |

### 3. Left Ventricular Mural Thrombus
- **Incidence & Timing**: Occurs in up to 40% of anterior STEMI with severe apical akinesis or dyskinesis. Can be detected within 48 hours (50% of cases) and in $> 95\%$ within the first 2 weeks.
- **Screening Schedule**: Formal echocardiographic assessment is recommended at 24–48 hours post-MI, at 10–15 days, and at 1–3 months.
- **Morphologic Types & Embolic Risk**:
  - **Mural / Flat**: Non-protruding, broad-based, conforms to the endocardial contour; generally older, organized, and lower embolic risk.
  - **Protruding / Pedunculated / Mobile**: Extends into the cavity, independent motility; represents fresh, highly unstable clot associated with high risk of systemic arterial embolization (cerebrovascular, peripheral, mesenteric).
- **Echo Diagnostics**:
  - Distinct echodense mass with margins clearly demarcated from adjacent myocardium, visualized in at least two orthogonal scanning planes (apical 4-, 2-, and 3-chamber).
  - Use of high-frequency transducers, focal depth adjustment, and low-scale color Doppler (to reveal a distinct filling defect).
  - Ultrasound contrast agents (LV opacification) dramatically enhance diagnostic certainty by delineating the endocardial boundary and confirming avascularity.

### 4. Acute Ischaemic Mitral Regurgitation (MR)
- **Mechanisms**:
  1. **Papillary Muscle Dysfunction & Asymmetric Tethering**: Posterolateral myocardial infarction (RCA or LCx) alters the alignment of the posteromedial papillary muscle, displacing it apically and posteriorly. This causes severe leaflet tethering (predominantly P2/P3), leading to incomplete coaptation and an eccentric posterior-directed regurgitant jet.
  2. **Papillary Muscle Rupture**: Catastrophic mechanical emergency. The posteromedial papillary muscle has a solitary blood supply (posterior descending artery, usually from the RCA) and ruptures 6 to 12 times more frequently than the dual-perfused anterolateral papillary muscle (supplied by LAD and diagonal/marginal branches). Partial head rupture causes severe eccentric MR; complete trunk rupture causes immediate flash pulmonary oedema, cardiogenic shock, and flail leaflet.
  3. **Annular Dilatation**: Secondary to progressive global left ventricular enlargement.
- **Echo Pitfalls**:
  - The severity of acute MR can be substantially **underestimated** by colour Doppler because:
    - Marked eccentric jet adherence to the left atrial wall (**Coanda effect**) diminishes the apparent colour jet area.
    - Massive acute MR causes rapid equalization of LV and LA pressures in a non-compliant atrium, blunting the peak systolic gradient and jet velocity.
  - Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) is indispensable when TTE image quality is suboptimal or when flail papillary muscle heads are suspected.

### 5. Myocardial Wall Rupture (Ventricular Septal & Free Wall Rupture)
- **Ventricular Septal Rupture (VSR)**:
  - **Incidence**: 1–2% of AMI cases; accounts for 1–5% of post-MI deaths.
  - **Clinical Presentation**: Sudden hemodynamic deterioration with a new, harsh, holosystolic murmur radiating across the precordium, accompanied by a palpable systolic thrill.
  - **Anatomy**:
    - Anterior MI: Usually simple, direct septal defects located at the apical septum.
    - Inferior MI: Frequently complex, serpentine, ragged tracts with intramural dissection located at the basal inferoseptum.
  - **Echocardiographic Features**:
    - Direct color Doppler visualization of high-velocity left-to-right trans-septal flow.
    - Continuous-wave (CW) Doppler interrogation demonstrates high-velocity systolic jet (yielding peak systolic gradient between LV and RV via the modified Bernoulli equation: $\Delta P = 4v^2$).
    - Assessment of pulmonary-to-systemic flow ratio ($Q_p/Q_s$) and secondary RV volume overload.
  - **Management**: Emergent surgical repair or transcatheter closure in unstable patients.
- **Free Wall Rupture (FWR)**:
  - Accounts for up to 10% of in-hospital AMI fatalities.
  - Acute catastrophic rupture presents as electromechanical dissociation (PEA) and fatal tamponade.
  - Subacute rupture presents with recurrent chest pain, transient hypotension, pericardial effusion, and pseudoaneurysm formation, allowing life-saving surgical intervention.

### 6. Right Ventricular Infarction
- **Epidemiology**: Occurs in approximately 30–50% of patients presenting with acute inferior STEMI (due to proximal RCA occlusion proximal to the acute marginal branches). Isolated RV infarction is rare (3–5%).
- **Key Echocardiographic Findings**:
  - Right ventricular enlargement ($\text{RV basal diameter} > 41\text{ mm}$, mid-cavity $> 35\text{ mm}$).
  - Segmental hypokinesis or akinesis of the RV inferior and free wall (often with sparing of the RV apex — *reverse McConnell's sign*).
  - Paradoxical interventricular septal motion (diastolic flattening of the septum producing a D-shaped LV cavity, reflecting RV volume and pressure overload).
  - Impairment of longitudinal RV systolic indices:
    - $\text{TAPSE} < 17\text{ mm}$ (values $< 14\text{ mm}$ indicate severe compromise).
    - Tissue Doppler imaging peak systolic velocity at the lateral tricuspid annulus ($s' < 9.5\text{ cm/s}$, values $< 11\text{ cm/s}$ in chronic ICD).
    - Fractional Area Change ($\text{RV FAC} < 35\%$, values $< 32\%$ in high-risk cohort).
  - Dilated, non-collapsible inferior vena cava (IVC diameter $> 21\text{ mm}$ with $< 50\%$ respiratory collapse, corresponding to elevated right atrial pressure $> 15\text{ mmHg}$).
  - Interatrial septum (IAS) bulging persistently into the left atrium throughout the cardiac cycle.
  - Tricuspid regurgitation with **low peak systolic velocity** ($v < 2.5\text{ m/s}$), reflecting RV systolic failure rather than severe pulmonary hypertension.
  - Pulmonary regurgitation with steep pressure half-time (PHT) on CW Doppler, indicative of rapid equilibration of PA diastolic pressure with elevated RV end-diastolic pressure.

### 7. Pericardial Effusion & Post-Cardiac Injury Syndrome
- **Incidence**: Detected in ~30% of patients with acute transmural myocardial infarction between days 2 and 4.
- **Clinical Significance**:
  - Reflects localized epistenocardiac pericarditis over the necrotic transmural territory.
  - Correlates with larger infarct size, anterior location, and higher incidence of congestive heart failure and atrial/ventricular tachyarrhythmias.
  - True cardiac tamponade is rare unless complicated by free wall rupture or pseudoaneurysm.
- **Therapeutic Implications**:
  - Represents a relative contraindication to intensive therapeutic anticoagulation.
  - Serves as an absolute contraindication to thrombolytic therapy due to the catastrophic risk of hemopericardium and tamponade.
- **Dressler's Syndrome (Post-Myocardial Infarction Syndrome)**:
  - Develops 1 to 12 weeks post-MI.
  - Autoimmune-mediated pleuropericarditis characterized by fever, pleuritic chest pain, polyserositis, and circumferential pericardial effusion.

<details class="med-details"><summary>6.3 Determinants of Prognosis in Chronic ICD</summary><div class="details-content">
### Echocardiographic Predictors of Adverse Long-Term Outcome
In patients with established chronic ischaemic heart disease and prior myocardial infarction, comprehensive echocardiography provides indispensable parameters for risk stratification, device therapy selection (ICD/CRT), and revascularization planning.

| Echocardiographic Parameter | High-Risk Cut-off Value | Pathophysiologic Mechanism / Clinical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LV Ejection Fraction (LVEF)** | $< 25\%$ (or $< 35\%$) | Marked depression of global pump performance; prime indication for primary prevention ICD; high all-cause and arrhythmic mortality |
| **PW TDI Septal Annular $s'$** | $< 3.0\text{ cm/s}$ | Severe depression of longitudinal myofibre shortening |
| **LV End-Diastolic Dimension** | $\text{LVEDD} \ge 65\text{ mm}$ | Advanced adverse eccentric remodelling with wall stress elevation |
| **Left Atrial Volume Index** | $\text{LAVI} \ge 31\text{ mL/m}^2$ | Chronic diastolic filling pressure elevation and atrial cardiomyopathy |
| **Diastolic Filling Pattern** | Restrictive ($E/A \ge 2$, $\text{EDT} < 140\text{ ms}$, non-reversible) | Extremely elevated left ventricular filling pressures; marker of terminal stage |
| **Tissue Doppler $E/e'$ Ratio** | $E/e' > 15$ | Robust non-invasive correlate of mean pulmonary capillary wedge pressure $> 18\text{ mmHg}$ |
| **Mitral Annular Septal $e'$** | $e' < 3.0\text{ cm/s}$ | Profoundly impaired active myocardial relaxation |
| **RV Fractional Area Change** | $\text{RV FAC} < 32\%$ | Biventricular heart failure and independent predictor of cardiovascular death |
| **Tricuspid Annular Excursion** | $\text{TAPSE} < 14\text{ mm}$ | Severe longitudinal RV systolic dysfunction |
| **PW DMI RV Free Wall $s'$** | $< 11.0\text{ cm/s}$ | Impaired basal RV contraction velocity |
| **Tricuspid Regurgitation Velocity** | $v_{\text{TR}} > 2.5\text{ m/s}$ (or $> 2.8\text{ m/s}$) | Secondary pulmonary venous hypertension (post-capillary pulmonary hypertension) |
| **Secondary Mitral Regurgitation** | $\text{EROA} \ge 0.20\text{ cm}^2$, $\text{RVol} \ge 30\text{ mL}$ | Ischaemic MR with volume overload accelerating adverse ventricular remodelling |
| **Myocardial Viability** | Wall thickness $< 5\text{ mm}$, lack of contractile reserve | Extensive transmural fibrous scar; lack of functional recovery following revascularization |

### Assessment of Myocardial Viability & Ischaemia
- **End-Diastolic Wall Thickness**: An end-diastolic myocardial wall thickness $< 5\text{ mm}$ (with increased echogenicity) strongly indicates transmural fibrous scar tissue with virtually zero likelihood of functional contractile recovery after surgical or percutaneous revascularization.
- **Low-Dose Dobutamine Echocardiography**:
  - Infusion of dobutamine ($5\text{ to }10\text{ }\mu\text{g/kg/min}$) to detect contractile reserve (improvement of wall thickening by $\ge 1$ grade in $\ge 2$ adjacent segments).
  - A **biphasic response** (contractile improvement at low dose followed by worsening/ischaemia at high dose of $20\text{ to }40\text{ }\mu\text{g/kg/min}$) is the most specific predictor of contractile recovery post-revascularization.
- **Contrast Echocardiography**: Perfusion defect assessment to confirm microvascular integrity and exclude no-reflow phenomenon.
