# Chapter 10. Pericardial Disease

<details class="med-details" open><summary>📌 Executive Summary: Echocardiographic Diagnosis of Pericardial Diseases</summary><div class="details-content">
Echocardiography represents the premier first-line imaging modality for the detection, hemodynamic assessment, and guided therapeutic intervention of pericardial diseases.

### Core Clinical Objectives
1. **Pericardial Effusion Quantification & Localization**: Delineation of echo-free space around the heart in end-diastole:
   - Minimal / Small ($< 10\text{ mm}$, $< 300\text{ mL}$).
   - Moderate ($10\text{--}20\text{ mm}$, $300\text{--}500\text{ mL}$).
   - Large ($> 20\text{ mm}$, $> 500\text{ mL}$).
   - Accurate differentiation from pleural effusion (descending thoracic aorta landmark in PLAX: pericardial effusion lies anterior, pleural effusion lies posterior) and epicardial adipose tissue (anterior, hyperechoic/isoechoic, moves concordantly with myocardium).
2. **Cardiac Tamponade (Life-Threatening Emergency)**:
   - Early, sensitive sign: Right atrial (RA) systolic inversion / collapse persisting $> 1/3$ of the cardiac cycle.
   - Specific sign: Right ventricular (RV) early-diastolic collapse / indentation.
   - Exaggerated ventricular interdependence (echocardiographic pulsus paradoxus):
     - Respiratory variation in mitral inflow $E$ velocity $> 25\%$ (decreases on inspiration, rises on expiration).
     - Respiratory variation in tricuspid inflow $E$ velocity $> 40\text{--}50\%$ (increases on inspiration, drops on expiration).
   - Inferior vena cava (IVC) plethora ($> 21\text{ mm}$ with $< 50\%$ inspiratory collapse).
3. **Constrictive Pericarditis (CP)**:
   - Chronic fibrotic, calcific pericardial thickening ($> 3\text{--}5\text{ mm}$) encasing the ventricles.
   - Dynamic septal bounce (shudder) during early diastole upon inspiration.
   - Respiratory variation in mitral inflow ($> 25\%$ increase during expiration).
   - Diastolic flow reversal in hepatic veins predominantly during expiration.
   - Annulus paradoxus ($e' > 8\text{--}10\text{ cm/s}$ despite elevated filling pressures) and Annulus reversus (medial $e' \ge$ lateral $e'$).
4. **Pericardiocentesis Guidance**: Real-time echo monitoring of needle insertion, bubble contrast confirmation (agitated saline), and post-drainage assessment.
</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>10.1 Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade</summary><div class="details-content">
### Detection and Semi-Quantitative Grading
Normal pericardial fluid volume is $10\text{ to }50\text{ mL}$, visible as a trivial echo-free separation during ventricular systole:
- **Small Effusion**: Echo-free space in diastole $< 10\text{ mm}$ (localized primarily posterobasal to the LV; volume $< 300\text{ mL}$).
- **Moderate Effusion**: Echo-free space $10\text{ to }20\text{ mm}$ (circumferential; volume $300\text{ to }500\text{ mL}$).
- **Large Effusion**: Echo-free space $> 20\text{ mm}$ (circumferential with "swinging heart" motion; volume $> 500\text{ mL}$).

### Differential Diagnosis: Effusion vs. Mimickers

| Finding | Pericardial Effusion | Pleural Effusion | Epicardial Fat Pad |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship to Descending Aorta** | Lies **anterior** to descending thoracic aorta in PLAX | Lies **posterior** to descending thoracic aorta in PLAX | Anterior only; does not extend to posterior AV groove |
| **Acoustic Characteristics** | Echo-free space; may contain fibrinous strands | Echo-free space extending into posterior/lateral thorax | Hyperechoic, speckled, or layered appearance |
| **Motion Dynamics** | Independent fluid swirling | Fluid shift with pleural movement | Moves synchronously and concordantly with myocardium |

### Cardiac Tamponade: Pathophysiology and Echo Criteria
Cardiac tamponade is a hemodynamic diagnosis occurring when intrapericardial pressure exceeds intracardiac pressures, causing progressive chamber collapse and systemic hypoperfusion.

#### Sequential Echocardiographic Signs:
1. **Right Atrial (RA) Systolic Inversion / Collapse**:
   - Inward indentation of the RA free wall during ventricular systole (when RA pressure is lowest).
   - Collapse lasting $> 1/3$ of the cardiac cycle has a sensitivity of $95\text{--}100\%$ for early tamponade.
2. **Right Ventricular (RV) Diastolic Inversion / Collapse**:
   - Inward bucking of the RV free wall during early diastole (immediately following opening of the tricuspid valve).
   - Highly specific ($> 90\%$) for hemodynamic compromise.
3. **Left-Sided Chamber Collapse**:
   - Left atrial (LA) collapse (seen in $25\%$) and left ventricular (LV) diastolic collapse (rare; seen in localized/post-surgical tamponade).
4. **Exaggerated Respiratory Variations (Ventricular Interdependence)**:
   - Mitral inflow $E$ velocity decreases by $> 25\%$ during inspiration (and rebounds on expiration).
   - Tricuspid inflow $E$ velocity increases by $> 40\text{--}50\%$ during inspiration.
   - Aortic ejection velocity decreases by $> 10\text{--}15\%$ on inspiration.
5. **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Plethora**:
   - Dilated IVC ($> 21\text{ mm}$) with complete absence of inspiratory collapse ($< 50\%$), reflecting elevated right atrial pressure $> 15\text{ mmHg}$.

<details class="med-details"><summary>10.2 Constrictive Pericarditis (CP)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Pathophysiology & Structural Features
Constrictive pericarditis develops when a scarred, fibrosed, inelastic, or calcified pericardium impairs diastolic filling of all four cardiac chambers:
- Thickened pericardium ($> 3\text{--}5\text{ mm}$, though normal thickness does not rule out constriction).
- Pericardial calcification (hyperechoic acoustic shadowing, best confirmed by chest CT).
- Biatrial dilation with normal or small ventricular dimensions.

### Hemodynamic Hallmarks: The Mayo Clinic Criteria
1. **Ventricular Interdependence & Septal Bounce**:
   - In early diastole during inspiration, acute reduction in LV filling accompanied by preferential RV filling causes the interventricular septum to shift abruptly toward the left ventricle (**respiratory septal bounce / shudder**).
2. **Respiratory Mitral Inflow Variation**:
   - Expiratory increase of mitral $E$ velocity by $\ge 25\%$ compared to inspiration.
3. **Hepatic Vein Expiratory Diastolic Flow Reversal**:
   - Forward diastolic flow in the hepatic veins decreases or reverses prominently during **expiration** (reflecting marked ventricular interdependence; in contrast to tricuspid regurgitation or restrictive cardiomyopathy, where reversal occurs on inspiration).
4. **Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI): Annulus Paradoxus & Annulus Reversus**:
   - **Annulus Paradoxus**: Medial mitral annular $e'$ velocity is preserved or elevated ($\ge 8\text{--}10\text{ cm/s}$) despite markedly elevated filling pressures (because longitudinal LV contraction is tethered only at the epicardium, and endocardial longitudinal function is unimpaired).
   - **Annulus Reversus**: Medial mitral annular $e'$ is higher than lateral annular $e'$ (medial $e' \ge$ lateral $e'$), reversing the normal relationship (lateral normally $>$ medial), caused by rigid lateral pericardial adherence.

### Differential Diagnosis: Constrictive Pericarditis vs. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy

### Table 10.2.1 Differential Diagnosis: Constrictive Pericarditis vs. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy
| Diagnostic Parameter | Constrictive Pericarditis (CP) | Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pericardial Thickness / Calcification** | Thickened ($> 3\text{ mm}$) $\pm$ calcification | Normal ($< 2\text{ mm}$) |
| **Respiratory Septal Bounce** | Present and prominent | Absent |
| **Mitral Inflow Respiratory Variation** | Markedly elevated ($\ge 25\%$) | Minimal or absent ($< 10\%$) |
| **Tissue Doppler Medial Annular $e'$** | Preserved or high ($\ge 8\text{ cm/s}$) | Markedly depressed ($< 6\text{--}7\text{ cm/s}$) |
| **Annulus Paradoxus & Reversus** | Present (medial $e' \ge$ lateral $e'$) | Absent (lateral $e' >$ medial $e'$) |
| **Hepatic Vein Diastolic Flow Reversal** | Predominantly during **expiration** | Predominantly during **inspiration** |
| **Pulmonary Artery Systolic Pressure** | Usually $< 50\text{ mmHg}$ | Frequently $> 50\text{ mmHg}$ |
| **Ratio of Mitral $E$ to Color M-mode $v_p$** | $E / v_p < 2.5$ | $E / v_p \ge 2.5$ |

<details class="med-details"><summary>10.3 Pericardial Cysts & Congenital Absence of the Pericardium</summary><div class="details-content">
### 1. Pericardial Cysts
- **Pathogenesis**: Congenital benign fluid-filled unilocular malformation caused by incomplete fusion of embryonic pericardial mesenchymal lacunae.
- **Location**: Most commonly at the right cardiophrenic angle ($70\%$), followed by the left cardiophrenic angle ($20\%$).
- **Echo Characteristics**:
  - Thin-walled, well-circumscribed, round or oval echo-free structure adjacent to the pericardium.
  - Complete absence of internal colour Doppler flow.
  - Differentiated from hydatid cyst, loculated effusion, prominent fat pad, or coronary artery aneurysm.

### 2. Congenital Absence of the Pericardium
- **Anatomy**: Total absence (rare) vs. partial absence (predominantly left-sided).
- **Echo Hallmarks**:
  - Marked levo-displacement and hypermobility of the cardiac apex.
  - Unusual acoustic windows (heart imaged far into the left axilla).
  - Paradoxical interventricular septal motion mimicking right ventricular volume overload.
  - Absence of true pulmonary hypertension or intrinsic RV dysfunction.
