# Chapter 3. The Standard Transoesophageal Examination

##### Comprehensive EACVI practical guide to standard transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE), multiplane probe manipulation, systematic 2D and 3D acquisitions, indication-specific diagnostic protocols, structural intervention guidance, and standardized digital reporting.

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Physical Principles & Procedural Indications
* **Acoustic Physics:** Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) utilizes high-frequency transducers ($5.0	ext{–}7.0	ext{ MHz}$) positioned in the oesophagus and stomach immediately posterior to the left atrium. The absence of intervening ribs, chest wall musculature, and pulmonary parenchyma yields superior signal-to-noise ratio and ultra-high spatial and axial resolution ($< 1	ext{ mm}$).
* **Primary Clinical Indications:** TOE is indicated when transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is inconclusive or technically inadequate, or when superior anatomical resolution is required:
  * Investigation of cardiac sources of embolism, cryptogenic stroke, or transient ischaemic attack (exclusion of left atrial appendage thrombus, spontaneous echo contrast, patent foramen ovale with atrial septal aneurysm, complex aortic atheroma).
  * Suspected native or prosthetic valve infective endocarditis (detection of vegetations $< 5	ext{ mm}$, perivalvular extension, abscesses, pseudoaneurysms, and fistulae).
  * Assessment of native valve disease mechanism and feasibility of surgical repair (particularly mitral valve prolapse/flail localization and aortic valve repairability).
  * Evaluation of prosthetic valve dysfunction (distinction between pathological transvalvular regurgitation, paravalvular leak, structural deterioration, pannus, and thrombosis).
  * Evaluation of acute aortic syndromes (type A and type B aortic dissection, intramural haematoma, penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer, thoracic aortic aneurysm).
  * Intra-operative monitoring during cardiac and thoracic aortic surgery (verification of repair, weaning from cardiopulmonary bypass, de-airing, monitoring of biventricular function).
  * Real-time guidance of transcatheter structural heart interventions (transcatheter aortic valve implantation [TAVI], transcatheter edge-to-edge repair [TEER/MitraClip], left atrial appendage [LAA] closure, ASD/PFO device occlusion, and paravalvular leak closure).

### Safety Checklist, Sedation & Contraindications
* **Absolute Contraindications:** Active or suspected oesophageal perforation, oesophageal stricture or stenosis, oesophageal tumor, Zenker's diverticulum, active upper gastrointestinal bleeding, oesophageal laceration, and unstable cervical spine fracture/atlantoaxial instability.
* **Relative Contraindications:** Oesophageal varices without recent haemorrhage, history of mediastinal radiation, severe coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia ($	ext{platelets} < 50{,}000/\mu	ext{L}$), hiatus hernia, active severe odynophagia or dysphagia, and uncooperative patient.
* **Pre-Procedural Safety Checklist:** Verification of clinical indication, written informed consent, strict fasting ($\ge 6	ext{ h}$ for solids, $\ge 2	ext{ h}$ for clear liquids), removal of dental prostheses and loose teeth check, insertion of bite guard, intravenous access, baseline vitals, and emergency resuscitation availability (suction, oxygen, bag-valve mask, defibrillator, reversal agents flumazenil and naloxone).
* **Sedation Protocol:** Topical pharyngeal anaesthesia using lidocaine spray ($10\%$). Conscious sedation with intravenous midazolam ($1	ext{–}5	ext{ mg}$) and/or fentanyl ($25	ext{–}50\ \mu	ext{g}$). Continuous multi-parameter monitoring: ECG, continuous pulse oximetry ($	ext{SpO}_2$), and automated non-invasive blood pressure every $3	ext{–}5	ext{ min}$.

### Systematic Multiplane 2D & 3D Anatomy
* **Probe Manipulation Degrees of Freedom:** Five coordinated movements: (1) advancement and withdrawal (depth in cm from incisors); (2) rotation along long axis (clockwise towards right-sided structures, counter-clockwise towards left-sided/posterior structures); (3) anteflexion and retroflexion (large wheel); (4) lateral flexion left/right (small wheel); and (5) electronic multiplane angle rotation from $0^\circ$ (transverse) to $180^\circ$ (inverted transverse).
* **Four Standard Anatomical Probe Levels:**
  * *Upper Oesophageal ($20	ext{–}25	ext{ cm}$):* Ascending aorta long- and short-axis, main pulmonary artery and bifurcation, superior vena cava.
  * *Mid-Oesophageal ($30	ext{–}35	ext{ cm}$):* 4-chamber view ($0^\circ$), mitral commissural view ($60^\circ$), 2-chamber view ($90^\circ$), long-axis view ($120^\circ	ext{–}140^\circ$), aortic valve short-axis ($30^\circ	ext{–}60^\circ$), LAA multiplane sweep ($0^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ, 135^\circ$), and bicaval view ($90^\circ	ext{–}110^\circ$).
  * *Transgastric ($40	ext{–}45	ext{ cm}$):* Basal short-axis (mitral valve, $0^\circ$), mid-papillary short-axis ($0^\circ$), two-chamber view ($90^\circ$), and long-axis view ($120^\circ$).
  * *Deep Transgastric ($45	ext{–}50	ext{ cm}$ with anteflexion):* Coaxial spectral Doppler alignment with left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) and aortic valve ($0^\circ	ext{–}20^\circ$).
* **Standardized 3D Matrix Display Standards:**
  * *Mitral Valve "Surgeon's View":* En-face view from left atrium looking down into left ventricle; aortic valve positioned anteriorly at **11–12 o'clock**, LAA positioned laterally at **9 o'clock**, interatrial septum medially at **3 o'clock**, and posterior leaflet scallops (P1, P2, P3) at **5–7 o'clock**.
  * *Aortic Annulus Multi-Planar Reconstruction (MPR):* Orthogonal longitudinal alignment yielding true cross-sectional planimetry of basal ring for TAVI: area, perimeter, perimeter-derived diameter, and vertical coronary ostial heights (LMCA and RCA).

## 3.1 Transoesophageal Echocardiography (TOE)</summary><div class="details-content">

Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) utilizes an ultrasound transducer incorporated into the tip of a modified gastrointestinal endoscope. Positioned in the retrocardiac oesophageal lumen and stomach, the transducer operates at higher frequencies ($5.0	ext{–}7.0	ext{ MHz}$) than standard transthoracic probes ($1.5	ext{–}4.0	ext{ MHz}$), avoiding chest wall acoustic impedance and providing unprecedented spatial resolution of posterior cardiac structures.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 3.1.1 Clinical Indications, Competency, and Safety Checklist</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Indications for TOE
TOE is indicated when transthoracic examination is unable or unlikely to answer the clinical question:
* **Embolic Source & Arrhythmias:**
  * Unexplained embolic stroke, transient ischaemic attack, or peripheral arterial embolism.
  * Evaluation of the left atrial appendage (including pulsed Doppler emptying velocity) and detection of spontaneous echo contrast or thrombus before electrical/pharmacological cardioversion or catheter ablation of atrial fibrillation/flutter.
  * Detection of interatrial communication (patent foramen ovale [PFO], atrial septal defect [ASD]) and atrial septal aneurysm (ASA) utilizing agitated saline contrast study during resting respiration and Valsalva maneuver.
* **Valvular Pathology:**
  * Suspected native or prosthetic valve infective endocarditis (sensitivity $> 95\%$ vs $60\%$ for TTE): detection of vegetations, perivalvular extension, annular abscesses, pseudoaneurysms, fistulae, leaflet perforations, and valve dehiscence.
  * Precise mechanism and anatomical distribution of mitral valve regurgitation (scallop prolapse, flail leaflet, chordal rupture, annular dilatation, cleft, tethering, and tenting geometry) to assess feasibility of surgical repair.
  * Assessment of prosthetic valve dysfunction (stenosis vs regurgitation, distinguishing transvalvular jets from paravalvular leaks, pannus vs thrombus, and suture dehiscence).
* **Aortic Pathology:**
  * Acute aortic syndromes: type A and type B aortic dissection, intramural haematoma (IMH), penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer (PAU), and traumatic aortic injury.
  * Precise localization of intimal dissection flap, primary entry and re-entry tears (colour Doppler), differentiation between true and false lumen, presence of false lumen thrombosis, involvement of coronary artery ostia, and mechanism of associated aortic regurgitation.
  * Thoracic aortic aneurysm and grading of complex, ulcerated, or mobile atherosclerotic plaques in the ascending aorta, aortic arch, and descending aorta.
* **Structural and Interventional Monitoring:**
  * Intra-operative monitoring during cardiac surgery: baseline evaluation, assessment of valve repair results before closing chest, monitoring of biventricular function during weaning from cardiopulmonary bypass, and verification of complete intracardiac de-airing.
  * Guidance of percutaneous structural heart interventions: transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER/MitraClip), transcatheter closure of ASD/PFO, left atrial appendage closure, and occlusion of paravalvular regurgitant leaks.
* **Critical Care:**
  * Critically ill ICU patients with unexplained hemodynamic instability, hypovolemia, cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest, or severe chest trauma where TTE acoustic windows are non-diagnostic.

#### Competency Requirements in TOE
Performing and interpreting TOE requires dedicated formal training beyond basic TTE certification:
* **Core Curriculum:** Competency requires mastery of probe insertion, multiplane manipulation, cross-sectional anatomy, advanced Doppler hemodynamics, 3D matrix-array imaging, and recognition of urgent life-threatening conditions (dissection, tamponade, acute valve disruption).
* **Supervised Training:** Under European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI) recommendations, competence requires performing at least 150 supervised complete diagnostic TOE examinations and interpreting an additional 50 examinations under direct expert mentorship.
* **Maintenance of Competence:** Regular ongoing performance of at least 50–75 examinations annually, coupled with continuous medical education (CME) and participation in departmental quality assurance and morbidity/mortality audits.

#### Pre-Procedural Checklist & Contraindications
Every TOE examination must be preceded by a formal structured pre-procedure checklist:
* **Informed Consent:** Detailed discussion of procedural risks (dental damage, pharyngeal trauma, laryngospasm, aspiration, transient hypoxia, vasovagal reaction, cardiac arrhythmias, and oesophageal perforation [incidence $0.01	ext{–}0.03\%$, mortality up to $10\%$ if perforation occurs]). Signed informed consent obtained.
* **Fasting Status:** Strict nil per os (NPO) for at least $6	ext{ h}$ for solid foods and $2	ext{ h}$ for clear liquids to prevent gastric content regurgitation and pulmonary aspiration.
* **Dentition Check:** Inspection of the oral cavity; removal of all removable dental prostheses, plates, or bridges. Identification of loose, capped, or vulnerable teeth. Insertion of a rigid protective bite guard is mandatory before probe introduction.
* **Gastrointestinal Screen:** Explicit enquiry regarding history of dysphagia, odynophagia, oesophageal surgery, gastric bypass, radiation therapy, prior gastrointestinal bleeding, or varices.
* **Resuscitation Equipment:** Verified immediate availability of functioning wall suction, supplemental oxygen, emergency bag-valve-mask system, intravenous access, emergency airway cart, cardiac defibrillator, and pharmacological reversal agents (flumazenil for benzodiazepines, naloxone for opioids).
* **Contraindications:**
  * *Absolute:* Known oesophageal stricture, oesophageal carcinoma, Zenker's diverticulum, active upper gastrointestinal bleeding, suspected or confirmed oesophageal perforation, recent upper GI surgery ($< 6	ext{ weeks}$), and unstable cervical spine fracture.
  * *Relative:* Known low-grade oesophageal varices without bleeding, large hiatal hernia, mediastinal irradiation, severe coagulopathy or profound thrombocytopenia ($	ext{platelets} < 50{,}000/\mu	ext{L}$), and uncooperative patient unable to follow instructions.

### 3.1.2 Probe Insertion Technique and Multiplane Manipulation</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Probe Insertion Technique
Proper patient positioning and gentle, non-traumatic probe introduction are vital to prevent patient distress and pharyngeal injury:
1. **Patient Preparation & Positioning:** The patient is positioned comfortably in the left lateral decubitus position with the neck slightly flexed forward. An absorbent pad is placed under the mouth.
2. **Topical Anaesthesia:** Pharyngeal mucosa is sprayed with lidocaine ($10\%$ topical spray, 2–4 actuations). The patient is instructed to gargle and swallow the medication to maximize posterior pharyngeal hypoaesthesia.
3. **Conscious Sedation:** Intravenous access is established. Mild conscious sedation is administered incrementally (midazolam $1.0	ext{–}3.0	ext{ mg}$ IV, optional fentanyl $25	ext{–}50\ \mu	ext{g}$ IV), titrated to achieve patient relaxation while preserving protective airway reflexes and cooperative swallowing.
4. **Bite Guard Insertion:** A disposable plastic bite block is inserted between the teeth and secured with an elastic head strap.
5. **Probe Insertion:**
   * Lubricate the distal $15	ext{ cm}$ of the multiplane probe with sterile acoustic water-soluble gel.
   * Verify that both deflection brake mechanisms (large wheel and small wheel) are completely unlocked.
   * Advance the probe tip through the central orifice of the bite guard along the midline of the tongue.
   * When the probe tip reaches the posterior pharyngeal wall, ask the patient to perform an active swallowing motion ([Fig. 3.1.1](#fig-03-01)).
   * The upper oesophageal sphincter (cricopharyngeus muscle) offers mild physiological resistance. Gently guide the probe past the sphincter into the cervical oesophagus synchronized with the swallow.
   * **Rule of Safety:** Never apply excessive mechanical force. If significant resistance is encountered, withdraw slightly, ensure probe is straight in the midline, encourage the patient to swallow, and re-advance gently.

#### Probe Manipulation: Degrees of Freedom
A modern multiplane TOE probe provides 5 distinct mechanical and electronic degrees of freedom:
1. **Advancement and Withdrawal:** Linear repositioning along the longitudinal axis of the gastrointestinal tract, calibrated in centimeters measured from the patient's incisors.
2. **Shaft Rotation:** Axial rotation of the entire probe handle and shaft: clockwise rotation directs the ultrasound beam towards right-sided anatomical structures; counter-clockwise rotation directs the beam towards left-sided and posterior structures.
3. **Anteflexion and Retroflexion:** Controlled by the large inner steering wheel on the handle. Anteflexion angles the transducer tip anteriorly (bringing it into contact with the anterior oesophageal wall or gastric fundus); retroflexion angles the tip posteriorly.
4. **Lateral Flexion (Left and Right Tilt):** Controlled by the small outer steering wheel on the handle. Tilts the probe tip laterally to the left or right, refining planar alignment and optimizing orthogonal beam perpendicularity.
5. **Electronic Multiplane Rotation:** Two push-buttons on the handle electronically rotate the piezoelectric array within the transducer casing through a continuous angle of $0^\circ$ (transverse horizontal plane) to $180^\circ$ (inverted transverse plane), passing through $90^\circ$ (vertical sagittal plane).

#### The Four Anatomical Probe Positions
* **Upper Oesophageal (UE, $20	ext{–}25	ext{ cm}$ from incisors):** Transducer positioned immediately posterior to the trachea and great vessels. Visualizes the ascending aorta, aortic arch, main pulmonary artery, right and left pulmonary arteries, and superior vena cava.
* **Mid-Oesophageal (ME, $30	ext{–}35	ext{ cm}$ from incisors):** Transducer positioned directly behind the left atrium. Provides optimal acoustic proximity to the mitral valve, aortic valve, interatrial septum, left atrial appendage, pulmonary veins, and right heart chambers.
* **Transgastric (TG, $40	ext{–}45	ext{ cm}$ from incisors):** Transducer advanced past the gastro-oesophageal junction into the stomach and anteflexed against the gastric fundus. Provides short-axis and long-axis orthogonal views of the left ventricle and subvalvular apparatus without apical foreshortening.
* **Deep Transgastric (DTG, $45	ext{–}50	ext{ cm}$ from incisors):** Transducer advanced into the gastric antrum with steep anteflexion pointing superiorly towards the base of the heart. Achieves true coaxial alignment with the LVOT and aortic valve for continuous-wave Doppler interrogation.

### 3.1.3 Standard 2D Views and Cross-Sectional Anatomy</summary><div class="details-content">

A systematic TOE examination follows a standardized multiplane interrogation sequence across all acoustic windows:

#### Lower-Mid-Oesophageal Position: Mitral and Left Ventricular Views
Positioned at $30	ext{–}35	ext{ cm}$ from the incisors directly posterior to the left atrium ([Fig. 3.1.2a](#fig-03-02a) and [Fig. 3.1.2b](#fig-03-02b)):
* **ME 4-Chamber View ($0^\circ	ext{–}20^\circ$):** Transverse cross-section displaying the left atrium (top), mitral valve, left ventricle (inferolateral and anteroseptal walls), interatrial septum, right atrium, and tricuspid valve. Leaflets visualized: anterior leaflet (A2/A1) and posterior leaflet (P2/P1).
* **ME Mitral Commissural View ($50^\circ	ext{–}70^\circ$):** Diagonal cross-section transecting the mitral valve through both commissures. Displays from left to right on screen: posterior scallop P3, anterior scallop A2, and posterior scallop P1. Both papillary muscle heads and chordal attachments are delineated. Essential for identifying commissural regurgitation and localized prolapse.
* **ME 2-Chamber View ($80^\circ	ext{–}100^\circ$):** Vertical sagittal cross-section displaying the left atrium, left atrial appendage, anterior mitral leaflet (A1/A2), posterior mitral leaflet (P3), left ventricular anterior wall (right of screen), and inferior wall (left of screen).
* **ME Long-Axis View ($120^\circ	ext{–}140^\circ$):** Displays the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT), aortic valve (right coronary cusp anteriorly, non-coronary cusp posteriorly), proximal ascending aorta, mitral valve (A2 anteriorly, P2 posteriorly), basal anteroseptal LV wall, and inferolateral LV wall. Essential for measuring LVOT diameter, aortic annular dimensions, and assessing aortic/mitral regurgitation mechanisms.

#### Upper Oesophageal Position: Aortic Valve, Pulmonary Artery, and Appendage
With the probe withdrawn to $20	ext{–}25	ext{ cm}$ from the incisors:
* **ME Aortic Valve Short-Axis View ($30^\circ	ext{–}60^\circ$, [Fig. 3.1.3](#fig-03-03)):** Slices through the aortic valve en-face, visualizing all three cusps: Right Coronary Cusp (RCC, anteriorly/bottom), Left Coronary Cusp (LCC, right of screen), and Non-Coronary Cusp (NCC, adjacent to interatrial septum). Evaluates cusp thickening, mobility, calcification, bicuspid morphology, and commissural fusion. Surrounding structures include the interatrial septum, right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT), and pulmonary valve.
* **ME Aortic Valve Long-Axis View ($120^\circ	ext{–}150^\circ$):** Orthogonal longitudinal cut displaying the LVOT, aortic valve cusps (RCC and NCC), sinuses of Valsalva, sinotubular junction, and proximal tubular ascending aorta.
* **Main Pulmonary Artery & Bifurcation ($0^\circ	ext{–}20^\circ / 35^\circ	ext{–}45^\circ$, [Fig. 3.1.5](#fig-03-05)):** High upper oesophageal position displaying the main pulmonary artery (MPA), bifurcation into right pulmonary artery (RPA, coursing under aortic arch) and left pulmonary artery (LPA), and the superior vena cava in short axis.

* **Left Atrial Appendage (LAA) Protocol ($0^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ, 135^\circ$, [Fig. 3.1.4](#fig-03-04)):**

#### Right Atrium, Interatrial Septum, and Bicaval View
Rotating the probe clockwise from the mid-oesophageal 4-chamber view at an electronic angle of $90^\circ	ext{–}110^\circ$ yields the **Bicaval View** ([Fig. 3.1.6](#fig-03-06)):
* **Anatomical Visualization:** Displays the right atrium, entering Superior Vena Cava (SVC, right of screen), entering Inferior Vena Cava (IVC, left of screen), Eustachian valve, Chiari network, crista terminalis, and the entire interatrial septum with the thin fossa ovalis.
* **Evaluation of Shunts:**
  * Colour Doppler interrogation with lowered Nyquist scale ($30	ext{–}40	ext{ cm/s}$) to detect left-to-right shunting across secundum ASD, sinus venosus defect, or patent foramen ovale (PFO).
  * **Agitated Saline Contrast Study:** $9	ext{ mL}$ isotonic saline mixed with $1	ext{ mL}$ room air agitated between two syringes and injected rapidly via an antecubital vein. Appearance of microbubbles in the left atrium within 3 cardiac cycles after complete right atrial opacification (particularly post-release of a standardized Valsalva maneuver) confirms intracardiac right-to-left shunting (PFO/ASD). Delayed microbubble transit ($> 4	ext{–}5$ cycles) suggests intrapulmonary arteriovenous shunting.

#### Transgastric Views: Left Ventricular Mechanics and Hemodynamics
Advancing the probe into the stomach ($40	ext{–}45	ext{ cm}$) and anteflexing against the gastric wall ([Fig. 3.1.7](#fig-03-07)):
* **TG Basal Short-Axis View ($0^\circ$):** En-face view of the mitral valve opening from below, displaying both commissures and all leaflet scallops.
* **TG Mid-Papillary Short-Axis View ($0^\circ$):** Circular cross-section of the left ventricle demonstrating both posteromedial and anterolateral papillary muscles. The primary reference plane for regional wall motion analysis across all three major coronary artery territories (LAD anterior/anteroseptal; LCx anterolateral/inferolateral; RCA inferior/inferoseptal).
* **TG 2-Chamber View ($90^\circ$):** Long-axis cut displaying the inferior wall, anterior wall, and the entire subvalvular mitral apparatus (papillary muscles, primary and secondary chordae tendineae).
* **Deep Transgastric View ($0^\circ	ext{–}20^\circ$ with steep anteflexion):** Transducer positioned at the gastric apex directed towards the cardiac base. Achieves parallel, coaxial alignment with the LVOT and aortic valve, enabling accurate continuous-wave Doppler measurement of peak transvalvular aortic velocity and mean pressure gradient when transthoracic apical windows are suboptimal.

#### Thoracic Aorta Interrogation
Rotating the probe counter-clockwise to point posteriorly displays the descending thoracic aorta ([Fig. 3.1.8](#fig-03-08)):
* **Screening Protocol:** Continuous slow withdrawal of the probe from the gastro-oesophageal junction up to the distal aortic arch in short-axis ($0^\circ$) and long-axis ($90^\circ$).
* **Atherosclerosis Grading System:**
  * *Grade 1:* Normal aorta or minimal intimal thickening ($< 2	ext{ mm}$).
  * *Grade 2:* Extensive intimal thickening ($2	ext{–}3	ext{ mm}$) without protruding atheroma.
  * *Grade 3:* Protruding atheroma $> 4	ext{ mm}$ without mobile elements.
  * *Grade 4:* Large atheroma $> 4	ext{ mm}$ with ulceration or mobile pedunculated debris (severe embolic hazard).

### 3.1.4 Essential Imaging for Specific Clinical Indications</summary><div class="details-content">

The EACVI mandates targeted, comprehensive imaging protocols for complex cardiovascular pathologies:

#### 1. Infective Endocarditis (IE)
* **Native Valves:**
  * Systematically examine the aortic, mitral, tricuspid, and pulmonary valves in multiple orthogonal planes.
  * *Vegetation Characterization:* Measure maximum dimensions in $\ge 2$ planes; determine site of attachment (typically upstream side: atrial aspect of AV valves, ventricular aspect of semilunar valves); assess mobility (sessile vs pedunculated/flailing).
  * *Perivalvular Complications:* Scrutinize the aortic annulus and intervalvular fibrosa for thickening, abscess formation (non-vascularized echolucent or echodense mass), pseudoaneurysm (pulsatile cavity with colour flow communicating with cardiovascular lumen), or fistula formation.
  * *Leaflet Destruction:* Check for leaflet perforation (eccentric trans-leaflet jet on colour Doppler) and chordal rupture.
* **Prosthetic Valves:**
  * High suspicion for endocarditis even with minor thickening due to acoustic shadowing.
  * Evaluate valve seating: rocking motion ($> 15^\circ$), paravalvular regurgitant leaks, suture dehiscence, and periprosthetic cuff abscesses.
* **Right-Sided Structures:**
  * Detailed inspection of pacemaker/ICD leads, central venous lines, tricuspid leaflets, and Eustachian valve.

#### 2. Acute Aortic Syndromes (Aortic Dissection, IMH, PAU)
* **Ascending Aorta & Arch:**
  * Visualize maximal luminal diameter, shape, and identify the intimal flap separating true and false lumens.
  * Distinguish true from false lumen: true lumen expands in systole, demonstrates rapid forward flow, and is smaller; false lumen is larger, exhibits systolic compression, sluggish flow, or thrombosis.
  * *Entry and Re-entry Tears:* Pinpoint primary entry tear location using colour Doppler; measure tear dimensions.
  * *Coronary Ostia Relationship:* Determine whether the flap extends into or compromises the left main or right coronary artery ostia.
  * *Aortic Regurgitation Mechanism:* Differentiate between functional annular dilatation, leaflet prolapse, or intimal flap prolapsing across the valve into the LVOT.
* **Complications:**
  * Assess pericardial effusion and cardiac tamponade; detect pleural effusion.
  * Follow dissection flap throughout the entire descending thoracic aorta and abdominal aortic branches.

#### 3. Mitral Regurgitation (MR) Mechanisms & Quantification
* **Mechanism & Morphology:**
  * Map pathology to specific scallops (A1–A3, P1–P3) according to Carpentier classification (Type I: normal leaflet motion; Type II: excessive motion [prolapse/flail]; Type IIIa: restricted motion in systole and diastole [rheumatic]; Type IIIb: restricted motion in systole [ischaemic/functional tethering]).
* **Regurgitant Jet Quantification:**
  * Multiplane colour Doppler mapping of jet origin (central vs commissural vs paravalvular).
  * Vena contracta width measurement in orthogonal planes ($VC \ge 7	ext{ mm}$ indicates severe MR).
  * PISA radius calculation at alias velocity $30	ext{–}40	ext{ cm/s}$ ($EROA \ge 40	ext{ mm}^2$, regurgitant volume $\ge 60	ext{ mL}$ indicates severe organic MR).
* **Pulmonary Venous Flow:**
  * Interrogate right upper and left upper pulmonary veins with pulsed-wave Doppler: systolic flow reversal confirms severe mitral regurgitation.

#### 4. Prosthetic Valve Dysfunction
* **Stenosis vs Regurgitation:**
  * Measure transvalvular peak velocity, peak and mean pressure gradients, and calculate effective orifice area (EOA) using the continuity equation.
  * Differentiate between normal physiological washing jets (short, narrow, symmetrical) and pathological paravalvular leaks (wide, turbulent, eccentric, originating outside sewing ring).
* **Pannus vs Thrombus:**
  * *Pannus:* Dense, immobile, highly reflective fibrous mass tightly adherent to the sewing ring, associated with chronic progressive gradient elevation.
  * *Thrombus:* Larger, soft, mobile, lower-density mass associated with acute onset of symptoms and inadequate anticoagulation (INR subtherapeutic).

#### 5. Intra-Operative Monitoring & Structural Interventions
* **Surgical Valve Repair Monitoring:**
  * Pre-bypass: confirm pathology and measure coaptation length, tenting height, and tenting area.
  * Post-bypass: evaluate residual regurgitation, coaptation length ($> 5	ext{ mm}$ indicates stable repair), check for systolic anterior motion (SAM) and dynamic LVOT obstruction, and verify localized myocardial wall motion.
* **Transcatheter Structural Guidance:**
  * *TAVI:* Monitor wire crossing, balloon valvuloplasty, prosthesis positioning, post-deployment paravalvular leak, and rule out annular rupture or tamponade.
  * *TEER (MitraClip):* Guide transseptal puncture (posterior and superior fossa ovalis, height $4.0	ext{–}4.5	ext{ cm}$ above mitral plane), guide clip delivery system into left atrium, verify clip arm perpendicularity to coaptation line, grasp leaflets, verify tissue bridge, and measure final transmitral gradient ($< 5	ext{ mmHg}$) and residual MR.
  * *LAA Occlusion:* Guide delivery sheath, confirm landing zone diameter and depth, confirm device stability (tug test), and verify absence of peridevice leak ($< 3	ext{ mm}$) before release.

## 3.2 The Standard Transoesophageal 3D Echo Examination</summary><div class="details-content">

Three-dimensional transoesophageal echocardiography (3D TOE) utilizes matrix-array transducers incorporating over 2,500 active piezoelectric elements. Real-time volumetric steering allows instant rendering of complex intracardiac spatial relationships without geometric assumptions.

#### 3D Acquisition Modalities
* **Live 3D (Real-Time 3D):** Continuous volumetric pyramid ($30^\circ 	imes 60^\circ$) rendered in real time without ECG gating. Frame rate $20	ext{–}30	ext{ Hz}$. Ideal for real-time catheter tracking, wire navigation, and initial orientation.
* **3D Zoom:** Focused, high-density volumetric box tailored tightly around a specific cardiac structure (mitral valve, aortic valve, tricuspid valve). Eliminates surrounding clutter to optimize spatial and temporal resolution ($20	ext{–}40	ext{ Hz}$).
* **Full-Volume (Multi-Beat ECG-Gated):** Stitches multiple smaller volume sectors (typically 4–6 consecutive cardiac cycles) during breath-hold or temporary ventilator pause to create a wide-angle ($90^\circ 	imes 90^\circ$) volumetric dataset. Achieves high temporal resolution ($> 50	ext{ Hz}$), required for left ventricular volumetry and complex congenital anomalies. Stitching artifacts can occur with arrhythmias or patient movement.
* **3D Colour Doppler:** Combines volumetric B-mode imaging with colour flow mapping to visualize the origin, trajectory, and vena contracta area ($VCA$) of complex, eccentric, or multiple regurgitant jets.

### 3.2.3 Multi-Planar Reconstruction (MPR) for Interventional Procedures</summary><div class="details-content">

Multi-Planar Reconstruction (MPR) slices volumetric 3D datasets along user-defined orthogonal planes for precise procedural sizing ([Fig. 3.2.8](#fig-03-16) and [Fig. 3.2.9](#fig-03-17)):
* **Aortic Annulus Sizing for TAVI:**
  * The anatomical aortic annulus is non-planar and oval; 2D sagittal measurements consistently underestimate true dimensions.
  * MPR aligns two orthogonal longitudinal planes through the lowest hinge points of all three aortic cusps.
  * The third orthogonal plane yields the true anatomical cross-sectional annular plane.
  * Quantitative planimetric parameters:
    * Annular Cross-Sectional Area ($CSA_{	ext{annulus}}$)
    * Annular Perimeter ($P_{	ext{annulus}}$)
    * Perimeter-Derived Diameter ($D_P = P / \pi$)
    * Area-Derived Diameter ($D_A = 2 	imes \sqrt{CSA / \pi}$)
    * Annular Eccentricity Index ($1 - [D_{\min} / D_{\max}]$)
* **Coronary Ostial Heights:**
  * Direct vertical measurement from the virtual basal annular plane to the inferior margin of the Left Main Coronary Artery (LMCA) and Right Coronary Artery (RCA) ostia ([Fig. 3.2.9](#fig-03-17)).
  * Ostial height $< 10	ext{ mm}$ (especially combined with narrow sinus of Valsalva $< 30	ext{ mm}$) indicates high risk of fatal acute coronary obstruction during TAVI, warranting coronary protection strategies.

#### Standardized Report Conclusion
The report must conclude with a succinct, numbered summary directly answering the clinical indication:
1. **Primary Diagnosis:** Clear statement addressing the referral question (e.g. "Severe P2 mitral valve prolapse with chordal rupture causing severe eccentric mitral regurgitation; excellent anatomy for surgical repair").
2. **Key Negative Findings:** Explicitly state critical exclusions (e.g. "No left atrial appendage thrombus; no evidence of endocarditis, abscess, or aortic dissection").
3. **Comparison:** Direct comparison with prior TTE/TOE studies noting progression or stability.
4. **Actionable Recommendations:** Urgent communication with the referring physician or Heart Team if critical unexpected findings are identified.
