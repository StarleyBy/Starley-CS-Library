## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">
Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) and comprehensive critical care echocardiography (CCE) provide bedside, real-time hemodynamic characterization and rapid differential diagnosis in the intensive care unit (ICU) and emergency department.

### Core Clinical Objectives
1. **Differentiating Mechanisms of Undifferentiated Shock**:
   - **Hypovolaemic**: Small, hyperdynamic LV with end-systolic cavity obliteration ("kissing walls"), flat/collapsing IVC ($\text{distensibility index} > 18\%$).
   - **Cardiogenic**: Dilated, poorly contracting LV ($\text{LVEF} < 30\%$, low $\text{VTI}_{\text{LVOT}} < 12\text{ cm}$), elevated filling pressures ($E/e' > 15$), multiple bilateral B-lines on lung ultrasound, plethoric IVC.
   - **Obstructive**: Acute cor pulmonale (dilated RV, McConnell's sign, 60/60 sign) from massive pulmonary embolism; or pericardial tamponade (RA/RV diastolic collapse, plethoric IVC); or tension pneumothorax (absent lung sliding and B-lines).
   - **Distributive (Septic)**: Hyperdynamic or variable LV/RV function, normal or low filling pressures, vasoplegia with low systemic vascular resistance (SVR), high cardiac output, variable volume responsiveness.
2. **Assessment of Fluid Responsiveness in Mechanical Ventilation**:
   - Respiratory variation of the Inferior Vena Cava ($\Delta \text{IVC} > 18\%$ distensibility index).
   - Respiratory variation of the Superior Vena Cava by TOE ($\Delta \text{SVC} > 36\%$, highly specific).
   - Aortic velocity-time integral variation ($\Delta \text{VTI}_{\text{LVOT}} > 12\text{--}15\%$).
   - Passive Leg Raising (PLR) test: Transient increase in stroke volume ($> 10\%$) during end-expiratory hold or PLR confirms preload dependence.
3. **Integrated Lung Ultrasound (LUS)**:
   - A-lines: Normal aerated lung surface reverberations.
   - B-lines ("lung rockets"): Vertical, hyperechoic laser-like artifacts originating from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen. Multiple diffuse bilateral B-lines indicate interstitial syndrome (cardiogenic pulmonary edema vs. ARDS).
   - Pneumothorax: Complete absence of lung sliding and B-lines; presence of the pathognomonic **Lung Point** (transition between sliding and absent sliding).
4. **Cardiac Arrest & Resuscitation (FEEL / FOCUS Protocols)**: Rapid ($< 10\text{ seconds}$) rhythm-check subcostal/parasternal imaging during pulse checks to differentiate true asystole from fine ventricular fibrillation or pseudo-PEA (organized cardiac contraction with profound hypotension), identifying reversible causes (cardiac tamponade, massive PE, severe hypovolemia, tension pneumothorax).
5. **Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS)**: Monitoring left ventricular assist devices (LVAD: cannula alignment, suction events, septal position, aortic valve opening) and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA/VV ECMO).
</div></details>

## 12.1 Differential Diagnosis of Acute Dyspnoea & Lung Ultrasound</summary><div class="details-content">
### The Integrated Lung Ultrasound Protocol
Lung ultrasound (LUS) has revolutionized bedside assessment of acute respiratory failure, outperforming supine chest radiography for pulmonary edema, pneumonia, pneumothorax, and pleural effusion.

### Table 12.1.1 Main Causes of Acute Dyspnoea on Integrated Cardiopulmonary Ultrasound
| Clinical Condition | Lung Ultrasound Findings | Echocardiographic Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** | Multiple, diffuse, bilateral, homogeneously distributed B-lines; preserved lung sliding; bilateral pleural effusions common | Elevated LV filling pressures ($E/e' > 15$, restrictive filling); depressed LVEF or severe diastolic dysfunction; dilated LA; plethoric IVC |
| **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)** | Heterogeneous/patchy B-lines; spared areas of normal parenchyma; subpleural microconsolidations; irregular, thickened pleural line; reduced/absent lung sliding | Normal or hyperdynamic LV; absence of elevated left atrial pressure ($E/e' < 8\text{--}10$); acute cor pulmonale in up to $25\%$ |
| **Pneumonia / Consolidation** | Tissue-like pattern (hepatization) with dynamic air bronchograms, irregular margins ("shred sign"), localized pleural effusion | Hyperdynamic circulation (sepsis phenotype), hyperdynamic LV |
| **Pneumothorax** | Complete absence of lung sliding; absence of B-lines; absence of lung pulse; presence of the **Lung Point** sign | Tachycardia, small hyperdynamic cardiac chambers; RV collapse in tension pneumothorax |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Normal aeration pattern (A-lines) or peripheral triangular subpleural infarcts; absence of pulmonary edema | Acute RV pressure overload: dilated RV, McConnell's sign, 60/60 sign, TR jet $> 2.8\text{ m/s}$, McConnell sign, mobile in-transit thrombi |
| **COPD / Asthma Exacerbation** | Pure A-lines with preserved lung sliding; absence of significant B-lines (dry lung) | RV hypertrophy or dilation secondary to chronic cor pulmonale; normal LV filling pressures |

## 12.2 Focused Ultrasound Approach to Shock & Hypotension</summary><div class="details-content">
### Diagnostic Protocol for Circulatory Failure

### Table 12.1.2 Focused Integrated Ultrasound Approach to Shock / Hypotension
| Shock Category | Left Ventricle (LV) | Right Ventricle (RV) | Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) | Lung Ultrasound | Primary Ultrasound Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypovolaemic Shock** | Small cavity, hyperdynamic, end-systolic cavity obliteration ("kissing walls") | Small, collapsed, hyperdynamic | Small, flat ($< 10\text{ mm}$), total inspiratory collapse | Dry lungs (A-lines), absence of B-lines | Hemoperitoneum, ruptured AAA, active hemorrhage |
| **Cardiogenic Shock** | Severely depressed LVEF, regional wall motion abnormalities, severe acute MR/AR | Variable (often secondary dysfunction) | Plethoric ($> 21\text{ mm}$), non-collapsible ($< 50\%$) | Wet lungs (diffuse bilateral B-lines) | Acute MI mechanical complications, acute valve rupture |

| **Obstructive: Massive PE** | Small, underfilled, hyperdynamic, D-shaped (systolic/diastolic shift) | Severely dilated, akinetic free wall with apical sparing (McConnell's sign) | Plethoric ($> 21\text{ mm}$), non-collapsible | Normal (A-lines) or subpleural wedge consolidation | McConnell's sign, 60/60 sign, in-transit thrombi |

### Volume Responsiveness in the Mechanically Ventilated Patient
In patients receiving positive pressure mechanical ventilation without spontaneous breathing efforts:
- **IVC Distensibility Index ($\Delta \text{IVC}$)**:
  $$\Delta \text{IVC} = \frac{\text{IVC}_{\max} - \text{IVC}_{\min}}{\text{IVC}_{\min}} \times 100\%$$
  A $\Delta \text{IVC} > 18\%$ predicts significant volume responsiveness (increase in cardiac output $\ge 15\%$ after $500\text{ mL}$ fluid challenge).
- **Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Collapsibility Index ($\Delta \text{SVC}$, via TOE)**:
  $$\Delta \text{SVC} = \frac{\text{SVC}_{\max} - \text{SVC}_{\min}}{\text{SVC}_{\max}} \times 100\%$$
  A $\Delta \text{SVC} > 36\%$ is highly sensitive and specific for volume responsiveness.
- **Aortic Velocity Time Integral Variation ($\Delta \text{VTI}_{\text{LVOT}}$)**:
  $$\Delta \text{VTI}_{\text{LVOT}} = \frac{\text{VTI}_{\max} - \text{VTI}_{\min}}{(\text{VTI}_{\max} + \text{VTI}_{\min}) / 2} \times 100\%$$
  A $\Delta \text{VTI}_{\text{LVOT}} > 12\text{--}15\%$ indicates that stroke volume is preload-dependent.

## 12.3 Focused Protocols in Cardiorespiratory Arrest (FEEL / FOCUS)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Ultrasound During Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
Focused Echocardiographic Evaluation in Life support (FEEL) integrates ultrasound into standard ACLS algorithms:
- **Timing**: Image acquisition must be performed strictly within the scheduled **$10\text{-second}$ pulse check window** between chest compression cycles. Chest compressions must never be delayed or prolonged for imaging.
- **Preferred Acoustic Window**: Subcostal four-chamber view (does not interfere with chest compressions, airway management, or defibrillation pad placement).
- **Core Clinical Questions**:
  1. Is there true mechanical standstill (asystole) or fine ventricular fibrillation / organized cardiac motion (pseudo-PEA)? Pseudo-PEA carries a dramatically higher survival rate with targeted resuscitation.
  2. Is there cardiac tamponade? (Immediate pericardiocentesis).
  3. Is there massive pulmonary embolism? (Massive RV dilation; indication for thrombolysis).
  4. Is there severe hypovolemia? (Completely collapsed, empty chambers; urgent volume loading).
  5. Is there tension pneumothorax? (Absent lung sliding; immediate decompression).

### Table 12.1.3 Focused Echo Protocols in Critically Ill Patients & Cardiac Arrest
| Protocol | Clinical Setting | Core Targets Evaluated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FEEL** (Focused Echocardiographic Evaluation in Life Support) | Cardiac arrest (PEA, asystole) | Differentiate true PEA from pseudo-PEA; rule in tamponade, hypovolemia, massive PE |
| **FATE** (Focus Assessed Transthoracic Echocardiography) | Emergency & perioperative screening | Cardiopulmonary screening: chamber dimensions, gross LV/RV function, pleura |
| **RUSH** (Rapid Ultrasound in Shock) | Undifferentiated shock / hypotension | "The Pump" (cardiac contractility, tamponade), "The Tank" (IVC, B-lines, free fluid), "The Pipes" (aorta, DVT) |
| **BLUE** (Bedside Lung Ultrasound in Emergency) | Acute respiratory failure | Algorithmic lung ultrasound profiles (A-profile, B-profile, C-profile, PLAPS) |

## 12.4 Echocardiography in Mechanical Circulatory Support (LVAD & ECMO)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Left Ventricular Assist Devices (LVAD)
Continuous-flow left ventricular assist devices (HeartMate II/III, HeartWare) require systematic echocardiographic monitoring:
- **Inflow Cannula Alignment**: Evaluated in apical views. The cannula should be oriented strictly toward the center of the LV cavity and aligned parallel to the long-axis of the ventricle, avoiding abutment against the interventricular septum or lateral free wall.
- **Interventricular Septal Position**:
  - Neutral / slight leftward shift: Indicates adequate LV decompression and balanced biventricular unloading.
  - Marked leftward shift (flattened/bowed into LV): Indicates LV over-pumping, suction risk, and secondary right ventricular failure.
  - Rightward shift: Indicates inadequate LV unloading (pump thrombosis, cannula malposition, or severe cannula obstruction).
- **Aortic Valve Dynamics**: Aortic valve opening should be evaluated on M-mode. Complete permanent closure of the aortic valve promotes cusp fusion, leaflet thrombosis, and progressive secondary aortic regurgitation. Ideally, the valve should open intermittently (every $2\text{ to }3$ beats).
- **De novo Aortic Regurgitation**: Closed-loop regurgitant volume circulating from the outflow graft back through the aortic valve into the LVAD inflow diminishes systemic net cardiac output.

### Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO)
- **Veno-Arterial (VA) ECMO**: Provides full biventricular and respiratory support. Echocardiography monitors for severe LV distension and afterload overload (due to retrograde femoral arterial flow), intramural stasis, spontaneous echo contrast ("smoke"), aortic valve non-opening, and intracardiac thrombus formation.
- **Veno-Venous (VV) ECMO**: Provides purely respiratory support in refractory ARDS. Echocardiography ensures correct bicaval cannula placement and monitors for secondary right ventricular failure.
