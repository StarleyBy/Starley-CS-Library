# Chapter 14. Cardiac Sources of Embolism & Cardiac Masses

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">
Echocardiography represents the frontline diagnostic imaging modality for investigating suspected cardiogenic embolism (responsible for $20\text{--}30\%$ of ischemic strokes) and characterizing intracardiac masses.

### Core Clinical Objectives
1. **Cardiac Source of Embolism (SOE)**:
   - High-Risk Sources: Left atrial appendage (LAA) thrombus, LV mural thrombus, cardiac myxoma, papillary fibroelastoma, prosthetic valve thrombosis/strands, infective endocarditis vegetations, complex aortic atheroma ($\ge 4\text{ mm}$ with mobile elements).
   - Low-Risk / Potential Sources: Patent foramen ovale (PFO), atrial septal aneurysm (ASA), spontaneous echo contrast (SEC), mitral annular calcification (MAC), Lambl's excrescences.
   - TOE is the reference standard for evaluating the LAA, pulmonary veins, and thoracic aorta. LAA peak emptying velocity $< 20\text{--}25\text{ cm/s}$ by PW Doppler denotes severe mechanical stasis.
2. **Differential Diagnosis of Intracardiac Masses**:
   - **Thrombi**: Predominantly in regions of stasis (akinetic LV apex, dilated LAA, trabeculations, prosthetic valves).
   - **Vegetations**: Irregular, mobile, attached on the upstream / low-pressure side of valves along the closure line, associated with valve destruction and regurgitation.
   - **Primary Benign Tumours ($75\%$)**:
     - *Myxoma ($50\%$)*: Arising predominantly from the fossa ovalis region of the left atrium ($75\%$), mobile, pedunculated, lobulated.
     - *Papillary Fibroelastoma ($10\text{--}15\%$)*: Small ($< 1\text{--}2\text{ cm}$), "sea-anemone" appearance with fine fronds, attached to downstream or upstream surfaces of valves.
     - *Lipoma*: Highly echogenic, encapsulated, immobile; lipomatous hypertrophy of the interatrial septum characteristically spares the fossa ovalis ("dumbbell" appearance).
     - *Fibroma & Rhabdomyoma*: Intramural masses, common in pediatric/young adult populations.
   - **Primary Malignant Tumours ($25\%$)**: Sarcomas (angiosarcoma most common, predominantly in right atrium, invasive with hemopericardium), lymphoma.
   - **Secondary / Metastatic Tumours**: 20 to 40 times more frequent than primary tumours (lung, breast, melanoma, renal cell carcinoma extending via IVC into RA).
3. **Normal Anatomical Variants & Echo Pseudotumours**:
   - Eustachian valve and Chiari network in right atrium.
   - Coumadin / Warfarin ridge (ligament of Marshall) separating LAA from left upper pulmonary vein.
   - False tendons in LV cavity, transverse sinus, and hiatal hernia compressing the left atrium.
</div></details>

### Left Atrial Appendage (LAA) Evaluation
In patients presenting with atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter, transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) is required prior to electrical or pharmacological cardioversion to rule out intracardiac thrombus:
- **Imaging Angles**: The LAA is systematically evaluated at multiplane angles ($0^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ, 135^\circ$) to interrogate all lobes and pectinate muscles.
- **Spontaneous Echo Contrast (SEC / "Smoke")**: Dynamic, swirling echogenic pattern resulting from erythrocyte rouleaux formation in low-shear flow states. Dense SEC independently predicts thromboembolism even in the absence of discrete thrombus.
- **Pulsed-Wave (PW) Doppler of LAA**:
  - Sample volume positioned $1\text{ cm}$ within the orifice of the LAA.
  - Normal peak emptying velocity $> 50\text{ cm/s}$.
  - **LAA Dysfunction**: Peak emptying velocity $< 20\text{--}25\text{ cm/s}$ indicates severe stasis and high risk of thrombus formation.

### Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) & Atrial Septal Aneurysm (ASA)
- **Paradoxical Embolism**: Passage of a venous thrombus into the systemic arterial circulation through an interatrial communication.
- **Agitated Saline Contrast Study**:
  - Performed at rest and during provocative maneuvers (standardized Valsalva release).
  - Positive study: Appearance of microbubbles in the left atrium within $3\text{ cardiac cycles}$ following right atrial opacification. Appearance after $4\text{--}5$ cycles suggests transpulmonary shunting (e.g., pulmonary arteriovenous malformation).
- **Atrial Septal Aneurysm (ASA)**:
  - Defined as saccular excursion of the fossa ovalis membrane into the RA or LA $\ge 10\text{ mm}$ (or total excursion $\ge 15\text{ mm}$) with a base width $\ge 15\text{ mm}$.
  - The combination of PFO and ASA carries a synergistic, substantially elevated risk of recurrent cryptogenic stroke.

## 14.2 Intracardiac Tumours (Benign & Malignant)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Differential Diagnosis of Cardiac Tumours

| Tumour Type | Prevalence / Demographics | Typical Location & Morphology | Echocardiographic Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiac Myxoma** | Most common primary tumor in adults ($50\%$) | $75\%$ in Left Atrium, $20\%$ in Right Atrium; attached to fossa ovalis by a narrow stalk | Mobile, lobulated, gelatinous, heterogeneous echogenicity; diastolic prolapse across mitral annulus causing "tumor plop" |
| **Papillary Fibroelastoma** | Second most common benign tumor ($10\text{--}15\%$) | Aortic valve ($40\%$), mitral valve ($35\%$); attached by a small pedicle to downstream side of leaflet | Small ($< 1.5\text{ cm}$), round/oval, shimmering "sea-anemone" appearance with fine mobile surface fronds |
| **Cardiac Lipoma** | Benign ($5\text{--}10\%$) | Subendocardial, subepicardial, or intramural | Homogeneous, bright hyperechoic, broad-based, non-mobile, well-circumscribed |
| **Cardiac Fibroma** | Common in children ($~5\%$) | Interventricular septum or LV free wall | Intramural, solid, sharply demarcated, bright homogeneous echogenicity; central calcification |
| **Cardiac Rhabdomyoma** | Most common pediatric tumor | Ventricular walls or septum; multiple in $> 90\%$ | Multiple nodular intramural masses; strong association with tuberous sclerosis; spontaneous regression |
| **Cardiac Angiosarcoma** | Most common primary malignant tumor | Right Atrium ($> 80\%$) and pericardium | Large, invasive, ill-defined, hemorrhagic mass infiltrating RA free wall, venae cavae, and pericardium; bloody effusion |
| **Metastatic Tumours** | 20–40× more frequent than primary | Pericardium, myocardium, or right heart cavities | Multiple nodules, pericardial effusion/tamponade, caval extension (renal cell carcinoma via IVC) |

## 14.3 Non-Neoplastic Masses & Pseudotumours (Normal Variants)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Thrombi vs. Non-Neoplastic Masses
- **Left Ventricular Thrombi**: Most commonly situated at the akinetic/dyskinetic apex in post-MI remodeling or dilated cardiomyopathy. Classified into mural (laminated) vs. protruding/pedunculated (high embolic risk).
- **Thrombi in Transit**: Elongated, serpentine, highly mobile clots trapped across the tricuspid valve, patent foramen ovale, or right heart chambers, originating from deep venous thrombosis.

### Normal Anatomical Variants & Echo Mimickers ("Pseudotumours")
Careful multiplane interrogation avoids misidentifying normal anatomical structures as intracardiac pathology:

| Normal Structure / Variant | Anatomical Location | Echocardiographic Appearance & Key Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eustachian Valve** | Valve of the IVC at junction with right atrium | Thin, undulating crescentic flap extending from IVC to interatrial septum; directs fetal blood to fossa ovalis |
| **Chiari Network** | Right atrium near IVC / coronary sinus | Highly mobile, fenestrated, web-like reticular structure with whip-like motion; benign congenital remnant |
| **Thebesian Valve** | Orifice of coronary sinus | Semicircular fold guarding coronary sinus opening |
| **Crista Terminalis** | Posterolateral wall of right atrium | Muscular ridge separating smooth and trabeculated RA; seen on cross-section as an apparent right atrial mass |
| **Coumadin / Warfarin Ridge** | Left atrium between LAA and left upper PV | Muscular fold containing the ligament of Marshall; prominent rounded ridge simulating an intra-atrial tumor |
| **False Tendons / Moderator Band** | Left or right ventricular cavities | Linear fibrous or muscular bands traversing cavity, inserting into septum/free wall rather than valve leaflets |
| **Lipomatous Hypertrophy of IAS** | Interatrial septum | Massive non-encapsulated fat accumulation ($> 15\text{ mm}$); characteristic sparing of the fossa ovalis ("dumbbell" shape) |
| **Hiatal Hernia** | Directly posterior to the left atrium | Extrinsic compression of the posterior LA wall; dynamic change with carbonated fluid ingestion |
| **Transverse Pericardial Sinus** | Superior to left atrium and behind ascending aorta | Echo-free fluid collection simulating aortic dissection or mediastinal cyst |
