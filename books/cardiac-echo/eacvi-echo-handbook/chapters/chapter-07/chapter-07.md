# Chapter 7. Heart Valve Disease & Prostheses

<details class="med-details" open><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">
Valvular heart disease (VHD) represents one of the most critical applications of diagnostic and interventional echocardiography. Comprehensive evaluation requires a multi-parametric approach combining 2D/3D morphological assessment, colour Doppler jet evaluation, continuous-wave (CW) and pulsed-wave (PW) spectral hemodynamics, and integration of upstream and downstream chamber consequences.

### Key Clinical Pillars in Valve Disease
1. **Aortic Stenosis (AS)**:
   - Primary criteria for severe AS: Peak aortic jet velocity $v_{\max} \ge 4.0\text{ m/s}$, mean transvalvular gradient $\Delta P_{\text{mean}} \ge 40\text{ mmHg}$, aortic valve area (AVA) $\le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ (indexed $\text{AVA}_i \le 0.6\text{ cm}^2/\text{m}^2$).
   - Doppler Velocity Index / Dimensionless Index ($\text{DVI} = \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}} / \text{TVI}_{\text{AV}} < 0.25$ indicates severe AS independent of LVOT diameter measurement error).
   - Low-flow, low-gradient AS:
     - Classical low-flow, low-gradient AS ($\text{LVEF} < 50\%$, $\text{SVi} < 35\text{ mL/m}^2$): Low-dose dobutamine stress echocardiography (DSE) differentiates true-severe AS (fixed $\text{AVA} \le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ as flow increases) from pseudo-severe AS ($\text{AVA} > 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ with normalization of gradient) and assesses contractile reserve ($\ge 20\%$ increase in stroke volume).
     - Paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS ($\text{LVEF} \ge 50\%$, $\text{SVi} < 35\text{ mL/m}^2$): Typically elderly, hypertensive, small hypertrophied LV; confirmed by multi-detector computed tomography (MDCT) aortic valve calcium scoring.
2. **Mitral Stenosis (MS)**:
   - Severe MS: Planimetric $\text{MVA} \le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ (normal $4.0\text{--}5.0\text{ cm}^2$), mean gradient $\ge 10\text{ mmHg}$, systolic pulmonary artery pressure ($\text{SPAP} > 50\text{ mmHg}$).
   - MVA calculation via Pressure Half-Time: $\text{MVA} = 220 / \text{PHT}$ (valid in pure rheumatic MS, invalidated immediately post-valvuloplasty or in high LV EDP).
   - Morphological scoring for percutaneous mitral commissurotomy (PMC): Wilkins score (leaflet mobility, thickening, calcification, subvalvular thickening; score $\le 8$ predicts procedural success) and Cormier anatomical score.
3. **Regurgitant Valve Lesions (AR, MR, TR, PR)**:
   - **Aortic Regurgitation (AR)**: Vena contracta width $> 6\text{ mm}$, holodiastolic flow reversal in descending thoracic/abdominal aorta, steep deceleration slope ($\text{PHT} < 200\text{ ms}$), PISA $\text{EROA} \ge 0.30\text{ cm}^2$, Regurgitant Volume ($\text{RVol} \ge 60\text{ mL}$).
   - **Mitral Regurgitation (MR)**:
     - Primary (Organic) MR: $\text{VC} \ge 7\text{ mm}$, $\text{EROA} \ge 0.40\text{ cm}^2$, $\text{RVol} \ge 60\text{ mL}$, pulmonary venous systolic flow reversal.
     - Secondary (Functional) MR: More restrictive cut-offs in prior guidelines ($\text{EROA} \ge 0.20\text{ cm}^2$, $\text{RVol} \ge 30\text{ mL}$) predict adverse outcome; current consensus harmonizes $\text{EROA} \ge 0.40\text{ cm}^2$ for severe MR while emphasizing that dynamic tethering in dilated ventricles carries high risk at lower thresholds.
   - **Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR)**: $\text{VC} \ge 7\text{ mm}$ (massive $> 14\text{ mm}$, torrential $> 21\text{ mm}$), $\text{PISA radius} > 9\text{ mm}$ at Nyquist $28\text{ cm/s}$, systolic flow reversal in hepatic veins, marked RV/RA dilatation.
4. **Prosthetic Heart Valves (PrV)**:
   - Routine baseline post-operative TTE (at 6–12 weeks) is mandatory.
   - Evaluation of high transprosthetic gradients: Differentiate pathological obstruction (thrombosis vs. pannus vs. degeneration) from high flow / hyperdynamic state and Patient-Prosthesis Mismatch (PPM: indexed $\text{EOA} \le 0.85\text{ cm}^2/\text{m}^2$ for aortic, $\le 1.20\text{ cm}^2/\text{m}^2$ for mitral).
   - Identification of paravalvular leaks (PVL) vs. transvalvular dehiscence and rocking motion.
5. **Infective Endocarditis (IE)**:
   - Major echocardiographic criteria (Modified Duke criteria): Vegetations (oscillating intracardiac mass), abscess / pseudoaneurysm, new partial dehiscence of prosthetic valve.
   - TTE vs. TOE: TOE sensitivity is $90\text{--}100\%$ (vs. $40\text{--}63\%$ for TTE); mandatory in prosthetic valves, intracardiac leads, suspected perivalvular complications, and negative TTE with high clinical suspicion.

## 7.1 Aortic Valve Stenosis (AS)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology and Morphology
Aortic valve stenosis is the most prevalent primary valve disease in Europe and North America.

| Etiology | Age Group | Pathological & Echocardiographic Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Calcific Degenerative** | Elderly ($> 65$ years) | Progressive fibrocalcific thickening and nodular calcification of the aortic side of the cusps, typically starting at the base and margins of attachment and progressing toward the free edges; commissural fusion is absent. |
| **Congenital Bicuspid (BAV)** | Young/Middle-aged ($30\text{--}60$ years) | Two operational cusps, usually with a raphe. Most common morphology: Right-Left cusp fusion (anterior-posterior opening; associated with coarctation and ascending aortopathy), followed by Right-Non-coronary fusion. Asymmetric mechanical stress leads to accelerated calcification. |
| **Rheumatic Heart Disease** | Endemic areas / Immigrants | Diffuse fibrous leaflet thickening with fusion of all three commissures, leaflet retraction, and characteristic thickening/calcification of free edges; almost universally associated with concomitant rheumatic mitral valve involvement. |
| **Congenital Unicuspid / Quadricuspid** | Infancy / Childhood | Severe early obstruction (unicuspid) or associated regurgitation. |

### Hemodynamic Quantification

#### 1. The Continuity Equation
Based on the conservation of mass, stroke volume (SV) traversing the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) equals the stroke volume traversing the stenotic aortic valve orifice:
$$\text{SV}_{\text{LVOT}} = \text{SV}_{\text{AV}}$$
$$\text{CSA}_{\text{LVOT}} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}} = \text{AVA} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{AV}}$$
$$\text{AVA} = \frac{\text{CSA}_{\text{LVOT}} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}}{\text{TVI}_{\text{AV}}} = \frac{\pi \times \left(\frac{D_{\text{LVOT}}}{2}\right)^2 \times \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}}{\text{TVI}_{\text{AV}}} = \frac{0.785 \times D_{\text{LVOT}}^2 \times \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}}{\text{TVI}_{\text{AV}}}$$

#### Technical Rules for Accurate Measurements:
1. **LVOT Diameter ($D_{\text{LVOT}}$)**:
   - Measured in parasternal long-axis (PLAX) view, mid-systole (when leaflet separation is maximal), inner edge-to-inner edge, parallel to the valve plane, strictly $3\text{ to }10\text{ mm}$ below the aortic annulus.
   - *Crucial Pitfall*: Because diameter is squared in the continuity equation, a 10% measurement error produces a 20% error in calculated AVA.
2. **LVOT PW Doppler Velocity & TVI**:
   - Recorded from apical 5-chamber or apical 3-chamber view with pulsed-wave (PW) Doppler sample volume placed $3\text{ to }5\text{ mm}$ apical to the aortic valve, exactly at the location of the diameter measurement.
   - The spectral envelope should show a smooth velocity curve with a clean closing click and no spectral broadening.
3. **Aortic Valve CW Doppler Velocity & TVI**:
   - Recorded using continuous-wave (CW) Doppler from multiple acoustic windows (apical 5-chamber, right parasternal window with Pedoff probe, suprasternal notch, subcostal).
   - The window yielding the highest peak velocity and dense envelope must be reported.

#### 2. Doppler Velocity Index (DVI) / Dimensionless Index
Eliminates the error inherent to LVOT diameter measurement:
$$\text{DVI} = \frac{\text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}}{\text{TVI}_{\text{AV}}} \quad \left(\text{or } \frac{v_{\text{LVOT}}}{v_{\text{AV}}}\right)$$
- Normal DVI $> 0.50$.
- Moderate AS: $0.25\text{--}0.50$.
- **Severe AS: $\text{DVI} < 0.25$**.

#### 3. Simplified Bernoulli Equation
$$\Delta P = 4 \times v^2$$
- Peak instantaneous gradient: $\Delta P_{\max} = 4 \times v_{\max}^2$.
- Mean gradient: Calculated by planimetry of the CW Doppler envelope ($\Delta P_{\text{mean}} = \frac{1}{T} \int 4v(t)^2 dt$).

### Low-Flow, Low-Gradient Aortic Stenosis
When $\text{AVA} < 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ but $v_{\max} < 4.0\text{ m/s}$ and $\Delta P_{\text{mean}} < 40\text{ mmHg}$:

1. **Classical Low-Flow, Low-Gradient AS with Reduced LVEF ($\text{LVEF} < 50\%$, $\text{SVi} < 35\text{ mL/m}^2$)**:
   - Perform **Low-Dose Dobutamine Stress Echocardiography (DSE)** (incremental infusion from $2.5\text{ to }20\text{ }\mu\text{g/kg/min}$).
   - **True-Severe AS**: Stroke volume increases by $\ge 20\%$ (presence of contractile / flow reserve), transaortic gradient rises ($\Delta P_{\text{mean}} \ge 40\text{ mmHg}$ or $v_{\max} \ge 4.0\text{ m/s}$) while $\text{AVA}$ remains fixed ($\le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$). Benefit from AVR is proven.
   - **Pseudo-Severe AS**: Flow increases, but calculated AVA increases significantly ($> 1.0\text{ cm}^2$) with only minor increase in gradient. Primary problem is myocardial failure; AVR does not improve outcome.
   - **Absence of Flow Reserve**: Inability to increase stroke volume by $\ge 20\%$. Carries high perioperative surgical mortality.
2. **Paradoxical Low-Flow, Low-Gradient AS with Preserved LVEF ($\text{LVEF} \ge 50\%$, $\text{SVi} < 35\text{ mL/m}^2$)**:
   - Small, concentric hypertrophied LV cavity with low compliance, marked afterload mismatch, and high systemic vascular resistance.
   - Workup: Verify strict blood pressure control (exclude hypertension at exam), assess non-contrast MDCT aortic valve calcium score (severe AS cut-offs: $\ge 2000\text{ AU}$ in men, $\ge 1200\text{ AU}$ in women).

## 7.2 Pulmonary Stenosis (PS)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Pulmonary stenosis is predominantly a congenital defect, occurring as an isolated valvular lesion or in complex syndromic conditions (Tetralogy of Fallot, Noonan syndrome). Acquired etiology includes carcinoid syndrome and extrinsic tumoral compression.

- **Valvular Anatomy**: Dome-shaped valve with preserved mobility at base and fused commissures; dysplastic pulmonary valve with thickened, immobile cusps; subvalvular (infundibular fibromuscular tunnel); supravalvular (discrete ridge or hypoplasia of pulmonary trunk).
- **Associated Features**: Right ventricular hypertrophy (RVH), post-stenotic dilatation of the main pulmonary artery, right atrial enlargement.

### Table 7.2.1 Grades of Pulmonary Stenosis Severity
| Grade of PS | Peak Jet Velocity ($v_{\max}$, m/s) | Peak Instantaneous Gradient (mmHg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild PS** | $< 3.0\text{ m/s}$ | $< 36\text{ mmHg}$ |
| **Moderate PS** | $3.0\text{--}4.0\text{ m/s}$ | $36\text{--}64\text{ mmHg}$ |
| **Severe PS** | $> 4.0\text{ m/s}$ | $> 64\text{ mmHg}$ |

## 7.3 Mitral Stenosis (MS)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology and Morphology
- **Rheumatic Fever**: Accounts for $> 90\%$ of clinical mitral stenosis worldwide. Characterized by commissural fusion, leaflet thickening (especially at tips), chordal shortening, fusion and thickening, leading to characteristic "hockey-stick" diastolic doming of the anterior mitral leaflet in PLAX.
- **Degenerative Calcific MS**: Increasingly prevalent in elderly populations; extensive calcification of the mitral annulus extending into the base of the leaflets without commissural fusion.

### Wilkins Score for Percutaneous Mitral Commissurotomy (PMC)
Evaluates four anatomical components on a 1 to 4 point scale (total score 4–16):

### Table 7.3.1 Wilkins Score (Echocardiographic Scoring for PMC Suitability)
| Grade | Leaflet Mobility | Valvular Thickening | Subvalvular Thickening | Valvular Calcification |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Highly mobile; only leaflet tips restricted | Near-normal thickness ($4\text{--}5\text{ mm}$) | Minimal thickening just below the valve | Single area of increased brightness |
| **2** | Mid and base portions have normal mobility | Normal thickness at base; thickened margin ($5\text{--}8\text{ mm}$) | Thickening of chordal structures up to upper 1/3 | Scattered areas of brightness confined to cusp margins |
| **3** | Valve moves forward in diastole mainly at base | Thickening extends through entire leaflet ($5\text{--}8\text{ mm}$) | Thickening extending to distal 1/3 of chordae | Brightness extending into the mid-portion of leaflets |
| **4** | No or minimal forward movement in diastole | Massive thickening of all leaflet tissue ($> 8\text{--}10\text{ mm}$) | Extensive thickening and fusion extending to papillary muscles | Extensive brightness throughout much of leaflet tissue |

- **Score $\le 8$**: Highly favorable anatomical profile for percutaneous balloon commissurotomy.
- **Score $> 11$**: Unfavorable morphology; higher risk of severe procedural MR and low success rate.

### Table 7.3.2 Cormier Anatomical Score for PMC
- **Group 1**: Pliable non-calcified mitral valve and subvalvular apparatus, thin chordae ($\le 10\text{ mm}$ fusion).
- **Group 2**: Pliable non-calcified valve with severely diseased subvalvular apparatus ($> 10\text{ mm}$ chordal fusion).
- **Group 3**: Calcified mitral valve of any degree, confirmed by fluoroscopy.

### Quantitative Hemodynamics

#### 1. Planimetry of Mitral Valve Area (MVA)
- Reference standard method for MVA.
- Performed in parasternal short-axis view at the exact tip of the leaflets during mid-diastole.
- 3D echocardiography with multiplanar reconstruction (MPR) avoids oblique slicing and ensures true orthogonal alignment with the smallest orifice.

#### 2. Pressure Half-Time (PHT) Method
$$\text{MVA} = \frac{220}{\text{PHT}}$$
- $\text{PHT}$ is the time (in milliseconds) required for the peak transmitral pressure gradient to decline by 50% ($v_{\text{PHT}} = v_{\max} / \sqrt{2} \approx 0.707 \times v_{\max}$).
- *Limitations*: Inaccurate immediately post-PMC (altered chamber compliance), severe aortic regurgitation (rapid LV pressure rise falsely shortens PHT and overestimates MVA), and significant diastolic LV stiffness.

#### 3. Transmitral Diastolic Pressure Gradient
Measured via CW Doppler across the mitral valve from the apical 4-chamber view:
- Mean gradient obtained by automated tracing of the diastolic velocity profile.

### Table 7.3.3 Recommendations for Classification of Mitral Stenosis Severity
| Parameter | Mild MS | Moderate MS | Severe MS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mitral Valve Area (MVA, $\text{cm}^2$)** | $> 1.5$ | $1.0\text{--}1.5$ | $< 1.0$ |
| **Mean Transvalvular Gradient (mmHg)** | $< 5$ | $5\text{--}10$ | $> 10$ |
| **Pressure Half-Time (PHT, ms)** | $< 150$ | $150\text{--}220$ | $> 220$ |
| **Systolic Pulmonary Artery Pressure (SPAP, mmHg)** | $< 30$ | $30\text{--}50$ | $> 50$ |

## 7.4 Tricuspid Stenosis (TS)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology and Hemodynamic Assessment
Tricuspid stenosis is rare in clinical practice, virtually always rheumatic in origin and accompanied by rheumatic mitral and aortic valve involvement. Non-rheumatic causes include carcinoid heart disease, endomyocardial fibrosis, pacemaker lead-induced fibrosis, and right atrial myxoma.

- **CW Doppler interrogation**: Recorded from the apical 4-chamber or RV inflow view.
- **Inflow Deceleration & Gradient**: Diastolic inflow velocity rarely exceeds $2.0\text{ m/s}$ due to lower systemic venous pressures; therefore, even modest gradients ($\Delta P_{\text{mean}} \ge 5\text{ mmHg}$) are clinically severe.

### Table 7.4.1 Findings Indicative of Severe Tricuspid Stenosis
| Diagnostic Parameter | Cut-off for Severe TS |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mean Transvalvular Diastolic Gradient** | $\ge 5\text{ mmHg}$ (at normal heart rates) |
| **Pressure Half-Time (PHT)** | $\ge 190\text{ ms}$ |
| **Tricuspid Valve Area (TVA by Continuity Equation)** | $\le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ |
| **Right Atrial Dilatation & Inferior Vena Cava** | Marked RA dilatation with dilated, non-collapsible IVC |

### Pathophysiology & Mechanisms: Carpentier Classification

### Table 7.5.1 Carpentier Functional Classification in Aortic Regurgitation
| Class | Mechanism | Anatomical & Pathological Causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

### Quantitative Grading of AR Severity

#### 1. Color Doppler Jet Dimensions & Vena Contracta
- **Vena Contracta (VC)**: Narrowest diameter of the regurgitant jet at or immediately below the aortic orifice. $\text{VC} > 6\text{ mm}$ indicates severe AR; $\text{VC} < 3\text{ mm}$ indicates mild AR.
- **Jet Width to LVOT Diameter Ratio**: Ratio $> 65\%$ measured in PLAX just below the aortic valve indicates severe AR.

#### 2. Flow Reversal in Descending Aorta
- Recorded with PW Doppler in the upper descending thoracic aorta (suprasternal view) or abdominal aorta (subcostal view).
- **Holodiastolic retrograde flow** with end-diastolic velocity $> 20\text{ cm/s}$ in descending thoracic aorta (and any holodiastolic reversal in abdominal aorta) is highly specific for severe AR.

#### 3. Continuous-Wave (CW) Doppler: Pressure Half-Time (PHT)
- In severe AR, rapid equalization of aortic diastolic and LV diastolic pressure produces a steep deceleration slope.
- $\text{PHT} < 200\text{ ms}$: Severe AR.
- $\text{PHT} > 500\text{ ms}$: Mild AR.

#### 4. PISA Method (Proximal Isovelocity Surface Area)
$$\text{Flow} = 2\pi r^2 \times v_{\text{alias}}$$
$$\text{EROA} = \frac{\text{Flow}}{v_{\max}} = \frac{2\pi r^2 \times v_{\text{alias}}}{v_{\max}}$$
$$\text{RVol} = \text{EROA} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{AR}}$$

### Table 7.5.2 Integrating Indices for Grading Aortic Regurgitation Severity
| Parameter | Mild AR | Moderate AR | Severe AR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vena Contracta Width (mm)** | $< 3.0$ | $3.0\text{--}6.0$ | $> 6.0$ |
| **Jet Width / LVOT Diameter (%)** | $< 25\%$ | $25\text{--}64\%$ | $\ge 65\%$ |
| **Pressure Half-Time (PHT, ms)** | $> 500$ | $200\text{--}500$ | $< 200$ |
| **Diastolic Flow Reversal in Descending Aorta** | Absent / Early diastolic | Brief intermediate | Holodiastolic (end-diastolic $v > 20\text{ cm/s}$) |
| **Effective Regurgitant Orifice Area (EROA, $\text{cm}^2$)** | $< 0.10$ | $0.10\text{--}0.29$ | $\ge 0.30$ |
| **Regurgitant Volume (RVol, mL/beat)** | $< 30$ | $30\text{--}59$ | $\ge 60$ |
| **Regurgitant Fraction (RF, %)** | $< 30\%$ | $30\text{--}49\%$ | $\ge 50\%$ |
| **LV Dilatation (LVEDD, mm)** | Normal | Mild/Moderate | Marked ($\text{LVEDD} > 65\text{ mm}$, $\text{LVESD} > 50\text{ mm}$) |

## 7.6 Mitral Regurgitation (MR)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology and Mechanisms: Primary vs. Secondary MR
- **Primary (Organic) MR**: Disease intrinsic to the mitral valve apparatus (leaflets, chordae tendineae, papillary muscles, annulus).
  - Degenerative mitral valve disease: Barlow's disease (diffuse myxomatous thickening, billowing, multsegment prolapse) vs. Fibroelastic deficiency (thin translucent leaflets, chordal rupture causing flail of a single segment, commonly P2).
  - Infective endocarditis (leaflet perforation, abscess, vegetations).
  - Rheumatic MR (leaflet retraction, thickening, poor coaptation).
- **Secondary (Functional) MR**: Structurally normal leaflets with regurgitation driven by left ventricular dysfunction and adverse remodelling.
  - Chronic ischaemic MR or dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM): Apical and lateral displacement of papillary muscles causing severe tethering (tenting) and annular dilatation.

### Carpentier Classification of Mitral Regurgitation
- **Type I**: Normal leaflet motion (annular dilatation or leaflet perforation).
- **Type II**: Excessive leaflet motion (leaflet prolapse or flail).
- **Type IIIa**: Restricted leaflet motion in both systole and diastole (rheumatic disease).
- **Type IIIb**: Restricted leaflet motion in systole (tethering in ischaemic or dilated cardiomyopathy).

### Quantitative Doppler Assessment: The PISA Method
$$\text{Regurgitant Flow} = 2\pi r^2 \times v_{\text{alias}}$$
$$\text{EROA} = \frac{\text{Flow}}{v_{\max}} = \frac{2\pi r^2 \times v_{\text{alias}}}{v_{\max}}$$
$$\text{RVol} = \text{EROA} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{MR}}$$

## 7.7 Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Etiology & Mechanics
- **Secondary (Functional) TR**: Represents $> 90\%$ of all significant TR cases. Caused by RV enlargement and annular dilation secondary to left heart disease, pulmonary hypertension, or atrial fibrillation.
  - Significant annular dilatation is defined as end-diastolic tricuspid annulus diameter $> 40\text{ mm}$ (or $> 21\text{ mm/m}^2$) in apical 4-chamber view.
- **Primary (Organic) TR**: Infective endocarditis (IV drug users), carcinoid heart disease (rigid, thickened, retracted leaflets fixed in open position), pacemaker lead impingement, Ebstein anomaly, blunt trauma.

## 7.9 Multiple and Mixed Valve Disease</summary><div class="details-content">
### Diagnostic Complexities & Pitfalls
Multiple valve disease (involvement of $\ge 2$ separate valves) and mixed valve disease (combination of stenosis and regurgitation on the same valve) present complex hemodynamic interactions where standard single-valve diagnostic indices can be misleading.

### Table 7.9.1 Main Diagnostic Caveats in Multiple and Mixed Valve Disease
| Valve Combination | Hemodynamic Interaction | Echocardiographic Pitfall & Diagnostic Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aortic Stenosis + Aortic Regurgitation** | Regurgitation increases total transaortic stroke volume; forward flow across the stenotic valve is markedly elevated. | High flow produces excessive transaortic gradients ($\Delta P_{\text{mean}}$ elevated disproportionate to true orifice area). Continuity equation AVA is more reliable than gradient alone. |
| **Mitral Stenosis + Aortic Stenosis** | MS restricts LV inflow, causing low transaortic stroke volume. | Low transvalvular flow produces low transaortic velocities and gradients, masking severe AS. Calculate AVA and look for leaflet calcification. |
| **Aortic Regurgitation + Mitral Regurgitation** | AR causes LV volume overload with elevated diastolic pressures; high LV systolic pressure augments MR driving force. | Severe AR regurgitant volume can be confused with forward stroke volume; use volumetric calculations from multiple non-regurgitant valves. |
| **Severe AR causing Austin Flint Murmur vs. Organic MS** | Regurgitant jet of severe AR impinges on anterior mitral leaflet, causing diastolic fluttering and premature closure. | Absence of commissural fusion, normal MVA on planimetry, and absence of subvalvular disease exclude true rheumatic MS. |

## 7.10 Prosthetic Valves (PrV)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Types of Prosthetic Valves
1. **Mechanical Prostheses**:
   - Bileaflet mechanical valves (St. Jude, Carbomedics): Two semicircular pivoting discs; lowest thrombogenicity among mechanical valves.
   - Tilting disc valves (Medtronic-Hall, Björk-Shiley): Single circular disc opening at an angle ($60\text{--}80^\circ$).
   - Caged-ball valves (Starr-Edwards): Historical; highly obstructive hemodynamics.
2. **Biological Prostheses (Bioprostheses)**:
   - Stented heterografts (porcine aortic or bovine pericardial mounted on a stent frame).
   - Stentless bioprostheses: Superior hemodynamics, lack of obstructive sewing ring.
   - Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation (TAVI): Balloon-expandable (Edwards SAPIEN) or self-expanding (Medtronic CoreValve / Evolut).

### Table 7.10.1 Essential Parameters in Comprehensive Evaluation of Prosthetic Valve Function
- Exact prosthesis type, manufacturer, labeled size, and implantation date.
- Transprosthetic peak velocity ($v_{\max}$) and mean pressure gradient ($\Delta P_{\text{mean}}$).
- Effective Orifice Area (EOA) by continuity equation:
  $$\text{EOA} = \frac{\text{CSA}_{\text{LVOT}} \times \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}}{\text{TVI}_{\text{PrV}}}$$
- Doppler Velocity Index ($\text{DVI} = \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}} / \text{TVI}_{\text{PrV}}$ for aortic; $\text{DVI} = \text{TVI}_{\text{PrV}} / \text{TVI}_{\text{LVOT}}$ for mitral).
- Presence, location, and severity of regurgitation (physiological washing jets vs. pathological transvalvular or paravalvular leaks).

### Differential Diagnosis: Obstruction vs. Patient-Prosthesis Mismatch vs. High Flow

### Table 7.10.5 Differential Diagnosis: Prosthetic Valve Pannus vs. Thrombosis
| Feature | Prosthetic Valve Thrombosis | Prosthetic Valve Pannus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset of Symptoms** | Acute or subacute ($< 1$ month), cardiogenic shock | Insidious, slowly progressive dyspnea ($> 1\text{--}5$ years) |
| **Anticoagulation Status** | Subtherapeutic INR or cessation of oral anticoagulants | Adequate, therapeutic INR |
| **Echo Visibility** | Soft, mobile or immobile mass on leaflets/occluders; size often $> 5\text{ mm}$ | Small, dense, highly echogenic fixed fibrous tissue at sewing ring |
| **Leaflet Motion** | Restricted or blocked leaflet motion | Reduced leaflet excursion without mass on occluder |
| **Preferred Management** | Urgent surgical reoperation or fibrinolysis | Surgical reoperation (pannus resection or re-replacement) |

| **Mean Transvalvular Gradient (mmHg)** | $< 20\text{ mmHg}$ | $20\text{--}35\text{ mmHg}$ | $> 35\text{ mmHg}$ |

  - Severe PPM: Indexed $\text{EOA} < 0.90\text{ cm}^2/\text{m}^2$.

## 7.11 Infective Endocarditis (IE)</summary><div class="details-content">
### Major Echocardiographic Findings in Infective Endocarditis

### Table 7.11.1 Echocardiographic Criteria for Infective Endocarditis (Modified Duke Criteria)
| Cardinal Finding | Definition & Echocardiographic Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vegetation** | An oscillating intracardiac mass on a valve, supporting structure, or implanted device, in the path of regurgitant jets, or on implanted material in the absence of an alternative anatomical explanation. |
| **Abscess** | Thickened perivalvular area with heterogeneous density, lacking communication with cardiac cavities; most frequent in aortic root. |
| **Pseudoaneurysm** | A pulsatile perivalvular echo-free space communicating directly with the cardiac cavity, demonstrating high-velocity color flow. |
| **Fistula** | Abnormal communication between two adjacent cardiac chambers or vessels with continuous Doppler flow. |
| **Leaflet Perforation** | Direct tissue defect through the leaflet body showing trans-leaflet color Doppler flow. |
| **New Dehiscence** | New rocking motion or $> 10\%$ paravalvular regurgitant detachment of a prosthetic valve sewing ring. |

### Diagnostic Performance: TTE vs. TOE
- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE)**: Sensitivity $40\text{--}63\%$ for native valve IE; lower for prosthetic valves ($< 35\%$). Excellent for global ventricular function, pericardial effusion, and hemodynamic consequences.
- **Transoesophageal Echocardiography (TOE)**: Sensitivity $90\text{--}100\%$ for native and prosthetic valve IE.
- **Repeat TOE**: If initial TOE is negative but high clinical suspicion persists, repeat TOE within $5\text{ to }7\text{ days}$.

### Indications for Urgent Surgery in Native and Prosthetic Valve IE

### Table 7.11.3 Indications for Urgent Surgery in Native Valve Infective Endocarditis
| Clinical Indication | Timing of Surgery | Rationale & Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heart Failure** | Emergency ($< 24\text{ h}$) or Urgent ($< 48\text{ h}$) | Severe acute AR or MR causing refractory pulmonary edema or cardiogenic shock. |
| **Uncontrolled Infection** | Urgent (within a few days) | Locally uncontrolled infection (abscess, pseudoaneurysm, fistula, enlarging vegetation despite optimal antimicrobial therapy). |
| **Prevention of Embolism** | Urgent (within a few days) | Large vegetation ($> 10\text{ mm}$) following $\ge 1$ embolic episode, or persistent very large vegetation ($> 15\text{ mm}$) with mobile characteristics. |

### Table 7.11.4 Indications for Urgent Surgery in Prosthetic Valve Infective Endocarditis (PVIE)
- Severe prosthetic valve dysfunction causing acute heart failure or shock.
- Perivalvular extension (abscess, dehiscence $> 10\%$, pseudoaneurysm, AV block).
- Persistent bacteremia or fever $> 7\text{--}10\text{ days}$ under appropriate antibiotic therapy.
- Infection caused by fungi or multiresistant organisms (*Staph. aureus*, non-HACEK Gram-negative bacteria).
