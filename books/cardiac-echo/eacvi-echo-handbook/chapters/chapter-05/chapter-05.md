# Chapter 5. Assessment of Diastolic Function

##### Comprehensive EACVI practical guide to left ventricular diastolic physiology, transmitral inflow, pulmonary venous Doppler, tissue Doppler annular velocities (e', E/e'), flow propagation velocity (Vp), and standardized multi-step grading algorithms for diastolic dysfunction and LV filling pressures.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Diastolic Physiology & Diagnostic Principles
* **Pathophysiological Core:** Diastole encompasses active, energy-dependent myocardial relaxation (early diastole) and passive viscoelastic compliance/stiffness (late diastole). Impairment in active relaxation occurs early in virtually all cardiac diseases, leading to compensatory elevation of left atrial and LV filling pressures (LVFP) to maintain cardiac output.
* **Core Interrogation Parameters:**
  * *Transmitral Flow (PW Doppler):* Peak early velocity ($E$), peak late velocity ($A$), $E/A$ ratio, and $E$-wave deceleration time ($EDT$ or $DT$).
  * *Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI):* Peak early diastolic annular velocity ($e'$ septal, $e'$ lateral, and $e'_{\text{average}}$), and the $E/e'$ ratio.
  * *Left Atrial Volume Index (LAVI):* Maximum volume at end-systole indexed to BSA; threshold for enlargement $> 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
  * *Tricuspid Regurgitation Peak Velocity ($TR\ v_{\text{max}}$):* Peak continuous-wave Doppler velocity across the tricuspid valve; threshold for elevated pulmonary artery systolic pressure $> 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
  * *Pulmonary Venous Flow:* Systolic ($S$), diastolic ($D$), and atrial reversal ($Ar$) velocities; $Ar$ duration minus mitral $A$ duration ($Ar_{\text{dur}} - A_{\text{dur}} > 30\text{ ms}$).
  * *Color M-Mode Flow Propagation Velocity ($V_p$):* $V_p < 45\text{ cm/s}$ and $E/V_p > 2.5$.

### Grading Diastolic Dysfunction (EACVI Algorithm)
* **Step 1: In Patients with Normal LVEF (Presence of Diastolic Dysfunction):**
  * Evaluates 4 key criteria:
    1. Average $E/e' > 14$ (or Septal $E/e' > 15$, Lateral $E/e' > 13$).
    2. Septal $e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$ or Lateral $e' < 10\text{ cm/s}$.
    3. $TR\ v_{\text{max}} > 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
    4. $LAVI > 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
  * *Diagnosis:* Dysfunction is present if $> 50\%$ ($3$ or $4$) criteria are positive. Normal if $< 50\%$ ($0$ or $1$) criteria are positive. Indeterminate if exactly $2$ criteria are positive.
* **Step 2: In Patients with Depressed LVEF or Myocardial Disease (Grading Severity & LVFP):**
  * *Grade I (Impaired Relaxation with Normal LVFP):* $E/A \le 0.8$ and peak $E \le 50\text{ cm/s}$.
  * *Grade III (Restrictive Filling with High LVFP):* $E/A \ge 2.0$, $DT < 150\text{ ms}$.
  * *Intermediate ($E/A \le 0.8$ with $E > 50\text{ cm/s}$, or $E/A\ 0.8\text{–}2.0$):* Requires evaluation of triad:
    1. Average $E/e' > 14$.
    2. $TR\ v_{\text{max}} > 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
    3. $LAVI > 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
    * If $2$ or $3$ are positive $\rightarrow$ **Grade II (Pseudonormal Filling with Elevated LVFP)**.
    * If $2$ or $3$ are negative $\rightarrow$ **Grade I (Normal LVFP)**.

## 5.1 Left Ventricle Diastolic Function</summary><div class="details-content">

Diastolic dysfunction contributes to symptoms and adverse outcomes across heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), arterial hypertension, and coronary artery disease.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 5.1.1 Phases of Diastole & Structural Substrates</summary><div class="details-content">

Ventricular diastole is partitioned into four distinct sequential phases ([Fig. 5-1](#fig-05-01)):

1. **Isovolumetric Relaxation Time (IVRT):**
   * Period between aortic valve closure and mitral valve opening.
   * LV pressure drops precipitously while LV cavity volume remains constant.
   * Energy-dependent process mediated by active sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium reuptake (SERCA2a).
2. **Early Rapid Filling Phase:**
   * Initiates upon mitral valve opening when LV pressure falls below left atrial (LA) pressure.
   * Generates the transmitral $E$-wave. Approximately $70\text{–}80\%$ of normal filling volume enters the LV during this phase.
3. **Diastasis:**
   * Period of low or negligible flow between early and late filling, during which LV and LA pressures equilibrate.
   * Duration depends directly on heart rate; shortens or disappears in tachycardia.
4. **Atrial Contraction (Late Filling):**
   * Active left atrial booster pump systole, generating the transmitral $A$-wave.
   * Contributes $15\text{–}20\%$ of LV stroke volume in healthy young adults, increasing up to $40\%$ in elderly patients or patients with impaired early relaxation.

* **Structural Substrates of Diastolic Dysfunction:**
  * Assessment of LV size and hypertrophy (LVMI and RWT) and left atrial enlargement ([Fig. 5-2](#fig-05-02)).
  * LA remodeling ($LAVI > 34\text{ mL/m}^2$) reflects chronically elevated LV end-diastolic filling pressure, excluding acute volume overload or high-output states.

### 5.1.2 Transmitral Inflow Pulsed-Wave Doppler</summary><div class="details-content">

Transmitral inflow interrogation assesses the pressure gradient between the left atrium and the left ventricle during diastole.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Technical Execution & Doppler Alignment</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Acquisition Protocol ([Fig. 5-3](#fig-05-03)):**
  * Apical 4-chamber view with the ultrasound beam aligned strictly parallel to transmitral blood flow.
  * Pulsed-wave (PW) Doppler sample volume ($1\text{–}3\text{ mm}$) positioned between the tips of the mitral leaflets during diastole.
  * Sweep speed set to $50\text{–}100\text{ mm/s}$ during end-expiratory apnea.
* **Measurement Rules:**
  * **Peak $E$ Velocity:** Maximal velocity of early transmitral filling ($cm/s$).
  * **Peak $A$ Velocity:** Maximal velocity of late filling during atrial contraction ($cm/s$).
  * **$E/A$ Ratio:** Ratio of peak early to late velocities.
  * **$E$-Wave Deceleration Time ($EDT$ or $DT$):** Time elapsed between peak $E$-wave velocity and the extrapolation of the deceleration slope to the zero-velocity baseline ([Fig. 5-3A](#fig-05-03) and [Fig. 5-4](#fig-05-04)).
  * **Mitral $A$-Wave Duration ($A_{\text{dur}}$):** Measured from the onset to the cessation of late transmitral flow at the leaflet annulus/tips level ($ms$).

#### Transmitral Inflow Patterns Across Disease States</summary><div class="details-content">

Progressive worsening of diastolic function manifests as four distinctive transmitral filling configurations ([Fig. 5-5](#fig-05-05) and [Fig. 5-6](#fig-05-06)):

1. **Normal Pattern:**
   * $E/A$ ratio between $1.0$ and $2.0$.
   * $DT$ between $150$ and $220\text{ ms}$.
   * Normal active LV relaxation with normal LA pressure.
2. **Impaired Relaxation (Grade I Diastolic Dysfunction):**
   * Slower LV pressure fall shifts filling to late diastole: $E/A < 0.8$, peak $E < 50\text{ cm/s}$, and prolonged $DT > 200\text{ ms}$.
   * LA pressure remains normal; common in aging, systemic hypertension, and stable CAD.
3. **Pseudonormal Filling (Grade II Diastolic Dysfunction):**
   * Superimposition of elevated LA pressure on impaired LV relaxation increases the early transvalvular gradient, masking the delayed relaxation pattern: $E/A\ 0.8\text{–}1.5$, $DT\ 160\text{–}200\text{ ms}$.
   * Differentiated from normal filling by Tissue Doppler ($e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$, $E/e' > 14$), pulmonary venous flow ($Ar - A > 30\text{ ms}$), or the **Valsalva Maneuver** ([Fig. 5-8](#fig-05-08)).
4. **Restrictive Filling (Grade III Diastolic Dysfunction):**
   * Severe reduction in LV compliance and markedly elevated LA pressure: $E/A > 2.0$, tall $E$-wave ($> 100\text{ cm/s}$), and very short $DT < 150\text{ ms}$ (or $< 130\text{ ms}$).
   * Reversible vs Fixed Restrictive: If the Valsalva maneuver reverses the restrictive pattern back to impaired relaxation, it is reversible; if unchanged, it is fixed, carrying a dismal prognosis.

#### Transmitral Flow in Arrhythmias</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) ([Fig. 5-9A](#fig-05-09)):**
  * Absence of the $A$-wave; irregular cycle lengths.
  * Peak $E$ velocity reflects instantaneous LA pressure; $DT \le 130\text{ ms}$ indicates markedly elevated filling pressure. Measurements should be averaged over $\ge 10$ consecutive cardiac cycles.
* **Atrial Flutter ([Fig. 5-9B](#fig-05-09)):**
  * Flutter waves appear as multiple regular saw-tooth low-velocity oscillations ($250\text{–}300/\text{min}$) throughout diastole.
* **First-Degree Atrioventricular Block ([Fig. 5-9C](#fig-05-09)):**
  * Prolonged PR interval shifts the $A$-wave earlier into mid-diastole, causing partial or complete fusion of $E$ and $A$ waves, preventing distinct $DT$ measurement.
* **Complete Heart Block ([Fig. 5-9D](#fig-05-09)):**
  * Dissociation between ventricular contraction and independent atrial $A$-waves occurring at random intervals during systole and diastole.

### 5.1.3 Pulmonary Venous Flow Doppler</summary><div class="details-content">

Pulmonary venous flow reflects left atrial pressure changes throughout the cardiac cycle, providing essential confirmation of elevated LV filling pressures when transmitral signals are ambiguous.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Technical Protocol & Waveform Components</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Acquisition Technique ([Fig. 5-10](#fig-05-10)):**
  * Apical 4-chamber view tilted superiorly to visualize the right superior pulmonary vein (RSPV) entering the posterolateral left atrium.
  * Color Doppler guidance; place the PW Doppler sample volume $1\text{–}2\text{ cm}$ into the pulmonary vein lumen.
* **Three Normal Velocity Waves ([Fig. 5-12](#fig-05-12) and [Fig. 5-13](#fig-05-13)):**
  1. **Systolic Wave ($S$):** Forward flow from pulmonary veins into the LA during LV systole. Frequently biphasic:
     * $S_1$: Early systolic wave related to atrial relaxation.
     * $S_2$: Late systolic wave related to mitral annular descent toward the apex.
  2. **Diastolic Wave ($D$):** Forward flow during ventricular diastole, occurring simultaneously with early rapid transmitral filling.
  3. **Atrial Reversal Wave ($Ar$):** Retrograde flow back into the pulmonary veins during left atrial contraction.
* **Severe Mitral Regurgitation Pitfall ([Fig. 5-11](#fig-05-11)):**
  * Severe MR jets entering the pulmonary veins cause systolic flow blunting or overt systolic flow reversal ($S < 0$), mimicking high LA pressure.

#### Diagnostic Analysis: S/D Ratio & Ar - A Duration Difference</summary><div class="details-content">

* **$S/D$ Velocity Ratio:**
  * In young healthy adults, $D$ may exceed $S$ ($S/D < 1.0$).
  * With normal aging and impaired relaxation, $S$ becomes dominant ($S > D$).
  * When LA pressure rises significantly, early systolic flow is impeded, causing $S$-wave blunting and diastolic dominance ($S/D < 1.0$), corroborating elevated filling pressures.
* **Atrial Reversal Duration Difference ($Ar_{\text{dur}} - A_{\text{dur}}$) ([Fig. 5-14](#fig-05-14)):**
  * When LV end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) is elevated, late diastolic LV compliance is reduced. Atrial contraction meets high resistance, expelling blood retrogradely into the pulmonary veins.
  * An atrial reversal duration exceeding mitral $A$-wave duration by $\ge 30\text{ ms}$ ($Ar_{\text{dur}} - A_{\text{dur}} \ge 30\text{ ms}$) provides definitive evidence of elevated LV end-diastolic pressure, even when the transmitral $E/A$ pattern appears normal.

### 5.1.4 Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI) of the Mitral Annulus</summary><div class="details-content">

Pulsed-wave Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI / DMI) records longitudinal myocardial tissue velocities at the mitral annulus, providing a relatively preload-independent measure of active LV relaxation.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Annular Velocity Interrogation & Waveforms</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Acquisition Technique ([Fig. 5-15](#fig-05-15)):**
  * Apical 4-chamber view with sample volume ($3\text{–}5\text{ mm}$) placed at the junction of the LV myocardium with the mitral annulus at both the **septal** and **lateral** sites.
  * Beam alignment parallel to longitudinal annular motion (angle $< 15^\circ$).
  * Nyquist velocity scale lowered to $\pm 15\text{–}20\text{ cm/s}$; sweep speed $100\text{ mm/s}$.
* **Three Characteristic Waves ([Fig. 5-16](#fig-05-16) and [Fig. 5-17](#fig-05-17)):**
  1. **$s'$ (or $S$):** Peak systolic velocity reflecting longitudinal LV contraction ($> 8\text{ cm/s}$ normal).
  2. **$e'$ (or $E'$):** Peak early diastolic velocity reflecting early active myocardial relaxation.
  3. **$a'$ (or $A'$):** Peak late diastolic velocity reflecting atrial contraction.
* **Morphological Shift Across Dysfunction ([Fig. 5-18](#fig-05-18)):**
  * A reduction in $e'$ is the earliest, most robust marker of diastolic dysfunction, appearing in all grades of dysfunction.
  * Unlike transmitral $E$, which pseudonormalizes with rising LA pressure, $e'$ remains depressed across all grades of disease.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### The E/e' Ratio & LV Filling Pressure Correlation</summary><div class="details-content">

Because transmitral peak $E$ is proportional to left atrial driving pressure, while annular $e'$ is inversely proportional to myocardial relaxation, the **$E/e'$ ratio** correlates directly with mean left atrial pressure and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP):

* **Annular Velocity Thresholds:**
  * *Septal $e':$* Lower normal limit $< 7\text{ cm/s}$.
  * *Lateral $e':$* Lower normal limit $< 10\text{ cm/s}$.
  * Lateral $e'$ is normally higher than septal $e'$ due to adjacent right ventricular interaction at the septum.
* **$E/e'$ Diagnostic Cut-Offs:**
  * **Average $E/e':$**
    * $E/e'_{\text{average}} < 10\rightarrow$ Normal LV filling pressure.
    * $E/e'_{\text{average}} > 14\rightarrow$ Undoubtedly elevated LV filling pressure.
    * $E/e'_{\text{average}}\ 10\text{–}14\rightarrow$ Intermediate/indeterminate gray zone; requires secondary non-invasive indices ($TR\ v_{\text{max}}$, $LAVI$, $Ar - A$).
  * **Septal $E/e':$** $< 8$ normal, $> 15$ elevated.
  * **Lateral $E/e':$** $< 8$ normal, $> 13$ elevated.
* **Limitations and Pitfalls of $E/e'$:**
  * Not reliable in significant mitral annular calcification (MAC), surgical mitral rings, prosthetic mitral valves, and acute severe mitral regurgitation.
  * Unreliable in constrictive pericarditis due to the "annulus paradoxus" phenomenon (normal/elevated $e'$ despite marked filling pressure elevation).

| **$E/e'\ 8\text{–}15$** | Intermediate zone; requires corroboration with secondary echo parameters. |

</div></details>

### 5.1.5 Color M-Mode Flow Propagation Velocity (Vp)</summary><div class="details-content">

Color M-mode flow propagation velocity ($V_p$) measures the speed at which the early diastolic transmitral jet propagates from the mitral plane to the LV apex ([Fig. 5-19](#fig-05-19)).

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Acquisition Methodology & Clinical Utility</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Acquisition Technique ([Fig. 5-19](#fig-05-19)):**
  * Apical 4-chamber view magnified to encompass the mitral valve and LV cavity.
  * Color Doppler flow map aligned across the mitral valve inflow into the LV apex.
  * M-mode cursor passed through the center of the color inflow column.
  * Sweep speed set to $100\text{ mm/s}$.
  * The color baseline is shifted down to lower the Nyquist limit, creating a sharp central blue aliasing contour.
  * $V_p$ is calculated as the slope of the first aliasing velocity from the mitral valve plane to approximately $4\text{ cm}$ distally into the LV cavity ($cm/s$).
* **Diagnostic Interpretation ([Fig. 5-20](#fig-05-20) and [Fig. 5-21](#fig-05-21)):**
  * *Normal $V_p$:* $> 55\text{ cm/s}$ ($> 50\text{ cm/s}$ in elderly). Reflects vigorous early diastolic suction.
  * *Impaired Relaxation:* $V_p < 45\text{ cm/s}$. Suction is lost; flow propagation slows down ([Fig. 5-21B](#fig-05-21)).
  * **$E/V_p$ Ratio:**
    * In patients with depressed LVEF, an $E/V_p > 2.5$ predicts elevated pulmonary capillary wedge pressure ($PCWP > 15\text{ mmHg}$) with high specificity.
* **Interplay of Mechanics ([Fig. 5-22](#fig-05-22)):**
  * Integrates the complex interplay between left atrial driving pressure, active relaxation, and passive viscoelastic compliance.

### 5.1.6 Comprehensive EACVI Grading Algorithms & Special Populations</summary><div class="details-content">

The diagnosis and grading of diastolic dysfunction follows a standardized, validated two-step algorithmic framework.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Step 1: Algorithm for Patients with Normal LVEF</summary><div class="details-content">

In patients with preserved LVEF ($\ge 50\%$), the primary clinical question is whether diastolic dysfunction is present or absent ([Fig. 5-23](#fig-05-23)):

* **The Four Evaluated Criteria (Thresholds):**
  1. **Average $E/e' > 14$** (or Septal $E/e' > 15$, Lateral $E/e' > 13$).
  2. **Annular $e'$ Velocity:** Septal $e' < 7\text{ cm/s}$ or Lateral $e' < 10\text{ cm/s}$.
  3. **Tricuspid Regurgitation Peak Velocity ($TR\ v_{\text{max}}$):** $> 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
  4. **Left Atrial Volume Index (LAVI):** $> 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
* **Classification Rules:**
  * **Normal Diastolic Function:** If $< 50\%$ ($0$ or $1$) criteria are positive.
  * **Diastolic Dysfunction Present:** If $> 50\%$ ($3$ or $4$) criteria are positive.
  * **Indeterminate:** If exactly $2$ criteria are positive and $2$ are negative.

<span id="fig-05-23"></span>![](fig-05-23.jpg)

**FIGURE 5-23** EACVI/ASE diagnostic scheme for determining the presence and severity of LV diastolic dysfunction in patients with normal LVEF (STEP 1).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Step 2: Algorithm for Patients with Depressed LVEF or Known Myocardial Disease</summary><div class="details-content">

In patients with reduced LVEF ($< 50\%$) or proven structural heart disease, diastolic dysfunction is assumed present, and the clinical goal is to estimate LV filling pressure and assign a severity grade ([Fig. 5-24](#fig-05-24)):

* **Primary Stratification by Transmitral $E/A$:**
  * **If $E/A \le 0.8$ and Peak $E \le 50\text{ cm/s}$:**
    * $\rightarrow$ **Grade I Diastolic Dysfunction (Normal LV Filling Pressure)**.
  * **If $E/A \ge 2.0$ (with $DT < 150\text{ ms}$):**
    * $\rightarrow$ **Grade III Diastolic Dysfunction (Elevated LV Filling Pressure / Restrictive Filling)**.
  * **If $E/A \le 0.8$ with Peak $E > 50\text{ cm/s}$, OR $E/A$ is between $0.8$ and $2.0$:**
    * Interrogate the secondary triad:
      1. Average $E/e' > 14$.
      2. $TR\ v_{\text{max}} > 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
      3. $LAVI > 34\text{ mL/m}^2$.
    * *If $2$ or $3$ criteria are positive:* $\rightarrow$ **Grade II Diastolic Dysfunction (Elevated LV Filling Pressure / Pseudonormal)**.
    * *If $2$ or $3$ criteria are negative:* $\rightarrow$ **Grade I Diastolic Dysfunction (Normal LV Filling Pressure)**.
    * *If only $1$ criterion is evaluable or contradictory:* $\rightarrow$ Indeterminate.

<span id="fig-05-24"></span>![](fig-05-24.jpg)

**FIGURE 5-24** EACVI/ASE diagnostic algorithm for grading LV filling pressure and diastolic dysfunction severity in patients with depressed LVEF or myocardial disease (STEP 2).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Diastolic Evaluation in Special Populations</summary><div class="details-content">

Standard parameters require modified thresholds in specific clinical settings ([Fig. 5-25](#fig-05-25)):

1. **Atrial Fibrillation:**
   * Transmitral $A$-wave and $LAVI$ criteria are invalidated.
   * Elevated LVFP is established if:
     * Peak $E$ deceleration time $\le 130\text{ ms}$.
     * Average $E/e' \ge 11$ (or septal $E/e' \ge 11$).
     * $TR\ v_{\text{max}} > 2.8\text{ m/s}$.
     * IVRT $\le 65\text{ ms}$.
2. **Mitral Valve Disease (MAC, MS, MR, Prosthetic Valves):**
   * Annular velocities and $E/e'$ are unreliable.
   * In severe primary MR: $IVRT \le 60\text{ ms}$ and $Ar - A \ge 30\text{ ms}$ denote elevated LVEDP.
   * In mitral stenosis: Mean transmitral gradient and pulmonary artery pressure reflect MS severity rather than intrinsic LV relaxation.
3. **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM):**
   * Asymmetrical septal hypertrophy causes profound septal $e'$ depression ($< 4\text{ cm/s}$) without reflecting global LAP.
   * Lateral $e'$ or average $E/e'$, along with $LAVI$, $TR\ v_{\text{max}}$, and $Ar - A$, must be utilized.
4. **Constrictive Pericarditis vs Restrictive Cardiomyopathy:**
   * *Constriction:* Displays **"Annulus Paradoxus"** (medial $e' > 8\text{ cm/s}$, often higher than lateral $e'$, and inverse correlation between $E/e'$ and PCWP) and hepatic vein diastolic flow reversal on expiration.
   * *Restriction:* Displays uniformly severely depressed annular velocities (septal and lateral $e' < 4\text{ cm/s}$) and marked hepatic vein diastolic flow reversal on inspiration.
