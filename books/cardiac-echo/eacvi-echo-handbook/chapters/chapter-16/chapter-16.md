# Chapter 16. Stress Echocardiography

<details class="med-details" open><summary>
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Clinical Scope & Modalities**: Stress echocardiography evaluates inducible myocardial ischemia, myocardial viability, valvular hemodynamics, and exertional diastolic dysfunction. Modalities include physiological exercise (treadmill or semi-recumbent bicycle ergometer, the primary choice) and pharmacological stress (dobutamine or coronary vasodilators like dipyridamole and adenosine) when physical exercise is unfeasible or contraindicated.
- **Ischemic Cascade & Diagnostics**: Wall motion abnormalities precede ECG ST-segment shifts and chest angina along the ischemic cascade. Diagnostic criterion for ischemia is new or worsening regional wall motion abnormality (RWMA) in $\ge 2$ contiguous segments (or $\ge 1$ segment in apical views), failure of end-systolic volume (ESV) to decrease, or an increase in Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI) $\ge 0.20$.
- **Four Equations of Stress Echo**:
- **Normal**: Rest Normokinesis + Stress Normo/hyperkinesis = Normal myocardium
- **Ischemia**: Rest Normokinesis + Stress Hypo/a/dyskinesis = Inducible ischemia
- **Viability**: Rest Akinesis + Stress Hypo/normokinesis (low-dose dobutamine) = Viable myocardium
- **Necrosis (Scar)**: Rest A/dyskinesis + Stress A/dyskinesis = Non-viable scar
- **Biphasic Response**: Low-dose improvement followed by high-dose worsening/ischemia — pathognomonic of viable hibernating myocardium with critical epicardial stenosis, carrying the highest predictive value for functional recovery after revascularization.
- **Valvular Stress Echocardiography**:
- *Low-Flow, Low-Gradient Aortic Stenosis (LFLG AS)*: Low-dose dobutamine ($5–20$ $\mu\text{g/kg/min}$) differentiates true severe AS (flow reserve present with $\Delta\text{SV} \ge 20\%$, AVA remains $\le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$, and mean gradient rises to $\ge 40\text{ mmHg}$) from pseudo-severe AS (AVA expands to $> 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ while mean gradient remains $< 40\text{ mmHg}$).
- *Secondary Mitral Regurgitation*: Exercise-induced dynamic worsening ($\Delta\text{EROA} \ge 13\text{ mm}^2$, $\Delta\text{RegVol} \ge 20\text{ mL}$) or exercise $\text{PASP} > 60\text{ mmHg}$ identifies high mortality and pulmonary edema risk.
- *Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)*: Unmasks latent LVOT dynamic gradient (exercise gradient $\ge 50\text{ mmHg}$ despite resting gradient $< 30\text{ mmHg}$).
- *Diastolic Stress (HFpEF)*: Exertional $E/e' > 14$ and peak TR velocity $> 3.4\text{ m/s}$ confirm elevated LV filling pressures.

## 16.0 Indications, Principles & The Ischemic Cascade</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Stress echocardiography is an established non-invasive imaging modality combining 2D and Doppler echocardiography with physical, pharmacological, or electrical stress. It possesses a high diagnostic sensitivity ($80–85\%$) and specificity ($85–90\%$) for coronary artery disease (CAD), superior to standard exercise ECG testing.

### The Ischemic Cascade
During progressive myocardial oxygen supply-demand mismatch, pathological events occur in a strict, predictable chronological sequence known as the **ischemic cascade**:

1. **Perfusion Heterogeneity & Malperfusion**: Imbalance at the capillary/microvascular level (detectable by myocardial contrast echocardiography or nuclear perfusion).
2. **Metabolic Alterations**: Cellular ATP depletion, anaerobic glycolysis, and intracellular acidosis.
3. **Diastolic Dysfunction**: Impaired active relaxation and increased LV diastolic stiffness (detectable by tissue Doppler imaging, increased $E/e'$).
4. **Regional Systolic Dysfunction**: Impaired regional myocardial thickening and endocardial excursion (detectable by 2D wall motion analysis and strain imaging).
5. **Global LV Cavity Dilation & Ejection Fraction Decline**: Increase in end-systolic volume and decrease in LVEF.
6. **Electrocardiographic Changes**: ST-segment depression or elevation, T-wave inversion.
7. **Clinical Angina Pectoris**: Symptomatic chest pain or angina equivalents (dyspnea, fatigue).

Because regional mechanical wall motion abnormalities occur significantly earlier than ST-T wave changes and symptoms, stress echocardiography has substantially higher sensitivity and localization accuracy than conventional exercise treadmill ECG testing.

### Clinical Indications
- **Diagnosis of CAD**:
  - Intermediate pre-test probability of CAD.
  - Uninterpretable resting ECG: left bundle branch block (LBBB), ventricular paced rhythm, pre-excitation (Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome), resting ST depression $> 1.0$ mm, digoxin therapy, or left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH).
  - Inability to perform adequate physical exercise.
  - Equivocal or borderline exercise treadmill test.
- **Prognostic Stratification in Known CAD**:
  - Risk assessment before major non-cardiac vascular surgery in high-risk patients with reduced functional capacity.
  - Post-acute coronary syndrome or post-revascularization (PCI/CABG) with recurrent or changed symptoms.
  - Assessment of functional significance of intermediate coronary lesions identified on invasive angiography or coronary CT.
- **Myocardial Viability Assessment**:
  - Evaluation of dysfunctional myocardium (hibernating or stunned) in ischemic cardiomyopathy prior to revascularization.
- **Valvular & Non-Ischemic Cardiac Diseases**:
  - Low-flow, low-gradient aortic stenosis.
  - Asymptomatic severe primary mitral regurgitation or severe aortic stenosis.
  - Dynamic secondary mitral regurgitation.
  - Latent LVOT obstruction in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
  - Unexplained exertional dyspnea (HFpEF diastolic stress test).

## 16.1 LV Segmentation, Polar Plot & Coronary Perfusion Territories</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Accurate territorial assessment requires standardized anatomical segmentation of the left ventricle in accordance with the ASE/EACVI 16- and 17-segment models.

### Polar Plot Display (Bull's Eye) & 17-Segment Model
The left ventricle is divided into three circular transverse levels and an apical cap:
- **Basal Level (Segments 1–6)**: Basal anterior (1), basal anteroseptal (2), basal inferoseptal (3), basal inferior (4), basal inferolateral / posterior (5), and basal anterolateral (6).
- **Mid-Cavity Level (Segments 7–12)**: Mid anterior (7), mid anteroseptal (8), mid inferoseptal (9), mid inferior (10), mid inferolateral / posterior (11), and mid anterolateral (12).
- **Apical Level (Segments 13–16)**: Apical anterior (13), apical septal (14), apical inferior (15), and apical lateral (16).
- **Apex (Segment 17)**: The apical cap / true apex (evaluated in 17-segment model, omitted in 16-segment model).

### Coronary Artery Perfusion Territories
1. **Left Anterior Descending (LAD) Artery**:
   - Supplies the anterior wall, anteroseptum, apical septum, apical anterior wall, and the true apex (Segments 1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14, 17).
2. **Right Coronary Artery (RCA)**:
   - In a right-dominant system ($85\%$), supplies the basal and mid inferoseptum, inferior wall, and apical inferior wall (Segments 3, 4, 9, 10, 15).
3. **Left Circumflex (LCx) Artery**:
   - Supplies the anterolateral, inferolateral (posterior), and apical lateral walls (Segments 5, 6, 11, 12, 16).

### Wall Motion Scoring & Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI)
Each visible segment is assigned a numerical score based on systolic thickening and endocardial excursion:
- **Score 1 = Normal or Hyperkinetic**: Systolic wall thickening $\ge 40\%$ (normal) or $> 60\%$ (hyperkinetic).
- **Score 2 = Hypokinetic**: Reduced systolic wall thickening ($< 40\%$) and diminished endocardial inward motion.
- **Score 3 = Akinetic**: Complete absence of systolic wall thickening ($< 10\%$) and absence of inward motion.
- **Score 4 = Dyskinetic**: Paradoxical systolic wall thinning and outward systolic bulging (aneurysmatic expansion during systole).
- **Score 5 = Aneurysmal**: Diastolic and systolic outward deformity with marked wall thinning and fibrosis.

The global **Wall Motion Score Index (WMSI)** is calculated as:
$$\text{WMSI} = \frac{\sum \text{Score of all visualized segments}}{\text{Total number of visualized segments}}$$

A normal resting and stress WMSI equals **1.00**. A peak stress WMSI $> 1.00$ indicates inducible ischemia or pre-existing scar. An increase in WMSI from rest to peak stress of $\ge 0.20$ is highly indicative of physiologically significant ischemia.

## 16.2 Stress Modalities, Protocols & Comparison</summary>
<div class="details-content">

| Stress Modality | Equipment & Methodology | Standard Infusion / Exercise Protocol | Mechanism of Stress | Main Advantages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Exercise** (Semi-recumbent Bicycle or Treadmill) | Ergometer cycle or motorized treadmill | 25 W increments every 2 minutes (bicycle); standard Bruce protocol (treadmill) | Physiological: combined $\uparrow$ heart rate, $\uparrow$ blood pressure, $\uparrow$ myocardial contractility | Evaluates functional capacity, blood pressure response, symptoms, and valvular hemodynamics |
| **Dobutamine** | Precision infusion pump | 5 $\mu\text{g/kg/min}$, then 10, 20, 30, and 40 $\mu\text{g/kg/min}$ in 3-minute stages. Atropine ($0.25–0.5\text{ mg}$ up to $1.0–2.0\text{ mg}$) added if target HR not reached | $\beta_1$-adrenergic inotropic and chronotropic stimulation; increases myocardial oxygen demand | Excellent for ischemia detection and viability assessment (low dose) |
| **Dipyridamole** | Syringe infusion pump | $0.56\text{ mg/kg}$ over 4 min, followed by 4 min rest; if negative, additional $0.28\text{ mg/kg}$ over 2 min (total $0.84\text{ mg/kg}$ in 6 or 10 min) $\pm$ atropine | Adenosine reuptake inhibition; coronary microvascular arteriolar vasodilation; induces "coronary steal" | High safety profile; suitable for patients with severe hypertension or ventricular arrhythmias |
| **Adenosine** | Continuous infusion pump | $140\ \mu\text{g/kg/min}$ continuously over 4 to 6 minutes | Direct $A_{2A}$ purinergic receptor stimulation; maximal coronary hyperemia | Very short half-life ($< 10$ seconds); rapid recovery; assesses Coronary Flow Velocity Reserve (CFVR) |
| **Atrial Pacing** | Transesophageal or transvenous pacemaker | Increment of $10\text{ bpm}$ every 2 minutes from $100\text{ bpm}$ up to target heart rate | Isolated tachycardia without catecholamine surges or hyperdynamic contraction | Useful in patients with implanted pacemakers or specific hemodynamic conditions |

### Exercise vs. Pharmacological Stress Echo: Clinical Selection

| Parameter | Physical Exercise | Pharmacological Stress |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Intravenous line required** | No | Yes |
| **Diagnostic utility of heart rate & BP response** | High | Limited / drug-dependent |
| **Feasibility in deconditioned / frail patients** | Low | High |
| **Feasibility in severe orthopedic / neurological limits** | Low | High |
| **Level of echocardiographic imaging difficulty** | High (respiratory motion, chest excursion) | Low (stable acoustic windows) |
| **Overall safety profile** | Very High | Moderate (arrhythmias, hypotension, bronchospasm) |
| **Clinical role in valvular heart disease** | Primary (gold standard) | Secondary (except low-dose dobutamine in AS) |
| **Clinical role in exertional pulmonary hypertension** | Primary | Negligible |
| **Fatigue and dyspnea symptom reproduction** | Yes (physiological) | No (artificial pharmacological symptoms) |

## 16.3 Contraindications, Termination Criteria & Safety Management</summary>
<div class="details-content">

### Absolute Contraindications
1. **General / All Modalities**:
   - Acute myocardial infarction ($< 48$ hours).
   - High-risk unstable angina with ongoing rest pain.
   - Uncontrolled, hemodynamically significant cardiac arrhythmias (sustained ventricular tachycardia, rapid atrial fibrillation).
   - Acute symptomatic pericarditis, myocarditis, or endocarditis.
   - Acute pulmonary embolism or pulmonary infarction.
   - Acute aortic dissection or severe aortic aneurysm ($> 60$ mm).
   - Severe systemic arterial hypertension (resting BP $> 220/120$ mmHg).
2. **Dobutamine-Specific**:
   - Severe dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction.
   - Known hypersensitivity to dobutamine.
3. **Dipyridamole / Adenosine-Specific**:
   - Severe bronchial asthma, active chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with bronchospasm.
   - High-degree atrioventricular block (second- or third-degree AV block) or sick sinus syndrome without a functioning permanent pacemaker.
   - Concomitant use of methylxanthines (theophylline, aminophylline, caffeine) within the preceding 24 hours.
   - Ongoing dipyridamole therapy within 24 hours.
   - Severe carotid stenosis with transient ischemic attacks.
4. **Atropine-Specific**:
   - Angle-closure glaucoma.
   - Severe prostatic hypertrophy with urinary retention.
   - Myasthenia gravis.

### Test Termination Criteria
The stress test must be stopped immediately upon occurrence of any of the following:
- **Achievement of Target Heart Rate**: Heart rate reaches $\ge 85\%$ of the age-predicted maximal heart rate ($[220 - \text{age}] \times 0.85$).
- **Definite Echocardiographic Ischemia**: New or worsening wall motion abnormality in $\ge 2$ contiguous segments.
- **Maximal Protocol Reached**: Completion of highest dose stage (e.g., dobutamine $40\ \mu\text{g/kg/min} + \text{atropine } 1\text{ mg}$).
- **Severe Angina Pectoris**: Intolerable typical chest pain.
- **Electrocardiographic Criteria**:
  - ST-segment elevation $\ge 1.0$ mm (in leads without baseline Q-waves).
  - Horizontal or downsloping ST-segment depression $> 2.0$ mm.
  - Development of significant cardiac arrhythmias (sustained or non-sustained VT, frequent polymorphic ventricular premature beats, new atrial fibrillation, high-degree AV block).
- **Hemodynamic Instability**:
  - Excessive blood pressure rise (systolic BP $> 240$ mmHg or diastolic BP $> 120$ mmHg).
  - Symptomatic blood pressure drop: decrease in systolic BP $> 20$ mmHg below baseline or below preceding stage.
- **Intolerable Side Effects**: Severe dizziness, presyncope, severe bronchospasm, or severe headache.

### Reversal Agents & Complications Management
- **Dobutamine Reversal**: Short intravenous half-life ($t_{1/2} \approx 2$ minutes). Immediate cessation of infusion is usually sufficient. In cases of refractory severe tachycardia, angina, or hypertension, administer short-acting IV $\beta$-blockers (e.g., Esmolol $0.5\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 1 min, or Metoprolol $2.5–5\text{ mg}$ IV slowly).
- **Dipyridamole / Adenosine Reversal**: Slow IV injection of **Aminophylline** ($100–240\text{ mg}$ over $2–3$ minutes) reverses receptor antagonism rapidly.
- **Atropine Reversal**: Atropine toxicity is managed by cessation, hydration, and, in severe refractory delirium/glaucoma, physostigmine ($0.5–1.0\text{ mg}$ IV).

## 16.4 Diagnostic Response Patterns: Ischemia vs. Viability</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Echocardiographic images are acquired and displayed side-by-side in quad-screen format: **Rest**, **Low-Dose / Intermediate Stress**, **Peak Stress**, and **Recovery**.

<figure class="med-figure" id="fig-16-04">
  <img src="images/fig-16-04.jpg" alt="Stress echocardiography diagnostic patterns" class="med-image" loading="lazy" /
  <figcaption class="med-caption">Fig. 16.1.4: Echocardiographic response patterns during stress: normal hyperkinesia with reduced end-systolic volume (upper row), ischemic wall motion abnormality with failure of systolic thickening (middle row), and biphasic response denoting viable hibernating myocardium (lower row).</figcaption>
</figure>

### The Four Diagnostic Equations of Stress Echocardiography

| Rest Motion | Stress Motion | Final Diagnosis | Clinical Implications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normokinesis** | **Normo- / Hyperkinesis** | **Normal Myocardium** | Normal coronary perfusion reserve; excellent $< 1\%$/year cardiac event prognosis |
| **Normokinesis** | **Hypo-, A-, or Dyskinesis** | **Inducible Ischemia** | Hemodynamically significant epicardial coronary stenosis |
| **Akinesis / Severe Hypokinesis** | **Hypo- or Normokinesis** (at low dose) | **Myocardial Viability** (Stunned / Hibernating) | Contractile reserve present; high likelihood of functional recovery after revascularization |
| **A- or Dyskinesis** | **A- or Dyskinesis** (unchanged at all doses) | **Myocardial Scar / Necrosis** | Transmural scar; revascularization unlikely to recover regional function |

### Response Patterns in Myocardial Viability
1. **Biphasic Response (Contractile Reserve followed by Ischemia)**:
   - Improvement of regional thickening at low-dose dobutamine ($5–10\ \mu\text{g/kg/min}$), followed by worsening of wall motion (akinesis/hypokinesis) at high-dose dobutamine ($20–40\ \mu\text{g/kg/min}$) or exercise.
   - **Pathophysiology**: Indicates viable, chronically underperfused (hibernating) myocardium subtended by a critical epicardial coronary artery stenosis.
   - **Clinical Value**: Most predictive pattern for functional ejection fraction recovery and survival benefit following surgical or percutaneous revascularization.
2. **Sustained Improvement (Uncompromised Viability)**:
   - Regional thickening improves at low dose and remains hyperdynamic at peak stress.
   - **Pathophysiology**: Stunned myocardium (post-infarction or post-ischemic) or non-transmural infarction without critical residual stenosis.
3. **No Contractile Reserve (Fixed Scar)**:
   - Persistent akinesia/dyskinesia with marked diastolic wall thinning ($< 6$ mm) and hyperechogenicity. Revascularization does not improve regional function.

## 16.5 Stress Echocardiography in Valvular Heart Disease</summary>
<div class="details-content">

Beyond coronary artery disease, stress echocardiography provides indispensable hemodynamic data for clinical decision-making in valvular heart disease:

### 1. Low-Flow, Low-Gradient Aortic Stenosis with Reduced LVEF
Characterized by aortic valve area (AVA) $\le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ ($\le 0.6\text{ cm}^2/\text{m}^2$), mean transvalvular gradient $< 40\text{ mmHg}$, and $\text{LVEF} < 50\%$.
- **Protocol**: Low-dose dobutamine infusion ($5$, $10$, $15$, up to $20\ \mu\text{g/kg/min}$ maximum without atropine).
- **Assessment of Flow Reserve**: Defined as an increase in forward stroke volume (SV) by $\ge 20\%$.
- **Diagnostic Categories**:
  - **True Severe Aortic Stenosis**: As stroke volume increases by $\ge 20\%$, the mean transvalvular pressure gradient increases to $\ge 40\text{ mmHg}$ (or peak velocity $V_{\max} \ge 4.0\text{ m/s}$), while the aortic valve area remains severely restricted ($\text{AVA} \le 1.0\text{ cm}^2$). Surgical or transcatheter aortic valve replacement (AVR / TAVI) is indicated (Class I).
  - **Pseudo-Severe Aortic Stenosis**: As stroke volume increases, the rigid valve is forced open by the increased flow, resulting in an increase of valve area to $> 1.0\text{ cm}^2$ (often $> 1.2\text{ cm}^2$) with only a modest increase in mean gradient ($< 40\text{ mmHg}$). The primary abnormality is myocardial dysfunction; valve replacement is not indicated.
  - **Absence of Flow Reserve (Indeterminate AS)**: Stroke volume fails to increase by $\ge 20\%$. Operative mortality is elevated ($20–30\%$), but non-contrast CT calcium scoring (Agatston score $\ge 2000$ in men, $\ge 1200$ in women confirms true severe AS) is required to guide intervention.

### 2. Secondary (Functional) Mitral Regurgitation
In ischemic or dilated cardiomyopathy, mitral regurgitation is dynamic and sensitive to exertional afterload, preload, and geometric distortion.
- **Protocol**: Semi-recumbent exercise echocardiography.
- **High-Risk Exertional Criteria**:
  - Increase in effective regurgitant orifice area by $\Delta\text{EROA} \ge 13\text{ mm}^2$.
  - Increase in regurgitant volume by $\Delta\text{RegVol} \ge 20\text{ mL}$.
  - Exertional systolic pulmonary artery pressure $\text{PASP} > 60\text{ mmHg}$.
  - Identifies patients prone to acute decompensated pulmonary edema, hospital readmission, and high cardiovascular mortality.

### 3. Asymptomatic Severe Primary Mitral Regurgitation
- Exercise echocardiography unmasks true symptom limitation in patients who subconsciously restrict physical activity.
- Failure of LV contractile reserve: failure of LVEF to increase by $> 5\%$ or worsening of global longitudinal strain (GLS) during exercise indicates early subclinical myocardial decompensation.
- Exercise-induced pulmonary hypertension ($\text{PASP} \ge 60\text{ mmHg}$) is a Class IIa indication for mitral valve repair in asymptomatic patients with preserved resting LVEF.

### 4. Mitral Stenosis with Discordant Symptoms
In patients with moderate rheumatic mitral stenosis at rest (valve area $1.0–1.5\text{ cm}^2$, resting mean gradient $< 10\text{ mmHg}$) who complain of severe exertional breathlessness:
- Exercise echocardiography demonstrating a rise in mean transvalvular gradient to $> 15\text{ mmHg}$ or exercise $\text{PASP} > 60\text{ mmHg}$ confirms hemodynamically significant obstruction and justifies percutaneous mitral commissurotomy.

### 5. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)
Approximately one-third of HCM patients have resting obstruction ($\ge 30\text{ mmHg}$), one-third have non-obstructive disease, and one-third have **latent dynamic obstruction**.
- Exercise echocardiography (treadmill or bicycle) is the modality of choice (dobutamine is contraindicated in HCM due to non-physiological cavity obliteration).
- Development of an exercise-induced peak dynamic LVOT gradient $\ge 50\text{ mmHg}$ identifies symptomatic obstructive physiology and guides septal reduction therapy (myectomy or alcohol septal ablation).

### 6. Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF) / Diastolic Stress Test
- Evaluates unexplained exertional dyspnea when resting echocardiography is inconclusive.
- Diagnostic criteria for exercise-induced diastolic dysfunction:
  - Average $E/e' > 14$ (or septal $E/e' > 15$) at peak exercise.
  - Peak tricuspid regurgitant velocity $V_{\text{TR}} > 3.4\text{ m/s}$ (corresponding to exertional $\text{PASP} > 45–50\text{ mmHg}$).

## 16.6 Appropriateness Criteria & Quality Standards</summary>
<div class="details-content">

EACVI and ACC/AHA appropriateness criteria define the clinical utility of stress echocardiography:

| Clinical Scenario | Appropriate (A) | Uncertain (U) | Inappropriate (I) | Evidence Class |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Uninterpretable ECG, inability to exercise, or submaximal exercise ECG** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Uncertain hemodynamic significance of intermediate coronary stenosis** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Recurrent or changed symptoms following revascularization (PCI / CABG)** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Pre-operative risk stratification in high-risk surgery with poor functional capacity** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Viability evaluation in ischemic cardiomyopathy before revascularization** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Low-flow, low-gradient aortic stenosis with reduced LVEF** | **✓** | — | — | **Class I** |
| **Asymptomatic patients $> 5$ years post-CABG or $> 2$ years post-PCI** | — | **✓** | — | **Class IIb** |
| **Routine screening in asymptomatic low-risk individuals** | — | — | **✓** | **Class III** |
| **Low pre-test probability, normal interpretable ECG, able to exercise** | — | — | **✓** | **Class III** |
| **Pre-operative evaluation before intermediate-risk surgery with good exercise capacity** | — | — | **✓** | **Class III** |
| **Asymptomatic patients $< 5$ years post-CABG or $< 2$ years post-PCI** | — | — | **✓** | **Class III** |

### Quality Assurance & Technical Recommendations
- **Digital Quad-Screen Archiving**: Simultaneous synchronized display of rest, low-dose/intermediate, peak, and recovery loops at matched heart rates.
- **Left Ventricular Opacification (Ultrasound Contrast Agents)**: Indicated whenever two or more contiguous LV myocardial segments cannot be adequately visualized on fundamental or harmonic 2D imaging. Contrast significantly improves endocardial border delineation, reader confidence, and diagnostic accuracy.
