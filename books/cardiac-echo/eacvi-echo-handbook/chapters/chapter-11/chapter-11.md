# Chapter 11. Cardiac Transplants

<details class="med-details" open><summary>📌 Executive Summary: Echocardiography in Heart Transplantation</summary><div class="details-content">
Echocardiography is the cornerstone for non-invasive serial surveillance of heart transplant (HT) recipients, playing four critical roles:
1. **Comprehensive Baseline Evaluation**: Establishing patient-specific reference parameters (chamber dimensions, ventricular function, suture line anatomy) soon after surgery (at 2–4 weeks), which serves as the essential benchmark for all subsequent serial comparisons.
2. **Detection of Acute Allograft Rejection**: Monitoring for multi-parametric indicators of acute cellular or antibody-mediated rejection (progressive wall thickening $> 4\text{ mm}$, myocardial hyperechogenicity, new restrictive filling, $> 10\%$ drop in LVEF or $e'$, new/expanding pericardial effusion).
3. **Surveillance for Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy (CAV)**: Non-invasive screening for accelerated coronary panarteritis via annual dobutamine stress echocardiography (DSE) and 2D speckle-tracking strain.
4. **Guidance of Endomyocardial Biopsy (EMB)**: Real-time 2D ultrasound guidance of bioptome positioning at the right ventricular septum, preventing tricuspid valve laceration and free wall perforation.
</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>11.0 Surgical Techniques in Heart Transplantation</summary><div class="details-content">
### Surgical Anatomy
- **Orthotopic Transplantation**: The native diseased recipient heart is completely excised and the donor heart is positioned in the normal anatomical mediastinal location:
  - **Biatrial Anastomosis (Lower technique)**: Recipient posterior right and left atrial cuffs are preserved and anastomosed to donor atria. Associated with significant atrial geometric distortion, biatrial enlargement, functional tricuspid/mitral regurgitation, and sinus node dysfunction.
  - **Bicaval Anastomosis (Current standard)**: Individual end-to-end anastomoses of the superior and inferior vena cava (IVC/SVC) and a single left atrial cuff. Preserves normal right atrial geometry, reduces tricuspid regurgitation, and lowers the incidence of atrial arrhythmias.
- **Heterotopic Transplantation ("Piggyback")**: The donor heart is placed in the right hemithorax alongside the recipient's native failing heart. Anastomoses between systemic and pulmonary venous and arterial circulations allow blood to transit through either or both hearts simultaneously. Used selectively in severe fixed pulmonary hypertension or major donor-recipient size mismatch.

<details class="med-details"><summary>11.1 Normal Post-Transplant Echocardiographic Findings</summary><div class="details-content">
### Distinguishing Normal Post-Surgical Findings from Pathology
The denervated, surgically altered allograft exhibits unique physiological characteristics that must not be mistaken for acute rejection or graft failure:

| Normal Finding in Transplanted Heart | Morphological & Physiological Mechanism | Clinical Caveat |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biatrial Dilatation** | Enlarged recipient-donor combined atrial cuffs (especially biatrial technique) | Do not interpret as elevated filling pressures in isolation |
| **Suture Line Hyperechogenicity** | Dense fibrous surgical ridge at the atrial anastomosis | Must not be confused with intra-atrial thrombus or vegetation |
| **Paradoxical / Flat Septal Motion** | Post-pericardiotomy altered anterior translation and altered bundle activation | Common normal variant; does not imply left bundle branch block |
| **Decreased Interventricular Thickening** | Non-specific post-cardiotomy septal hypokinesis | Lateral and posterior walls show compensatory normal or hyperdynamic thickening |
| **Mild Increase in Wall Thickness & Mass** | Ischemia-reperfusion injury, donor-recipient size mismatch, denervation hypertrophy | Baseline reference must be established to monitor future changes |
| **Right Ventricular Dilatation** | Elevated pre-existing recipient pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) | Usually regresses over first $3\text{--}6\text{ months}$; baseline TAPSE is often low ($14\text{--}16\text{ mm}$) |
| **Tricuspid & Mitral Regurgitation** | Mild-to-moderate central regurgitation due to altered annular geometry | Progressive severe TR suggests chordal injury from repeated biopsies |
| **Beat-to-Beat Inflow Variation** | Independent donor and recipient sinus node activity in biatrial transplants | Dissociated $P$-waves and variable filling patterns are benign |
| **Small Pericardial Effusion** | Post-operative serosanguinous fluid | Common up to 6 months; rapid enlargement suggests rejection or infection |

<details class="med-details"><summary>11.2 Echocardiographic Indicators of Allograft Rejection</summary><div class="details-content">
### Detection of Acute Cellular & Antibody-Mediated Rejection
Because acute rejection may be clinically silent until catastrophic graft failure develops, serial echocardiography is utilized as a screening tool:

| Echocardiographic Indicator | Threshold for Suspicion of Acute Rejection | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wall Thickness Increase** | Progressive increase in diastolic thickness $> 4\text{ mm}$ (IVS or PWT) | Interstitial myocardial edema and inflammatory cell infiltration |
| **Myocardial Echogenicity** | Diffuse increased brightness / granular sparkling texture | Interstitial edema and acute myocyte necrosis |
| **Diastolic Filling Pattern** | Transition to restrictive filling ($E/A > 2$, $\text{EDT} < 140\text{ ms}$, IVRT drop $> 20\%$) | Marked compliance loss and elevated left ventricular filling pressures |
| **Tissue Doppler Velocities** | Decrease in mitral annular $e'$ velocity $> 10\%$ | Impaired active myocardial relaxation |
| **Global Systolic Function** | Decrease in $\text{LVEF} > 10\%$ (or absolute $\text{LVEF} < 50\%$) | Extensive myocyte injury and contractile depression |
| **Deformation Imaging (GLS)** | Significant worsening of global longitudinal strain ($> 15\%$ relative drop) | Sensitive marker of subclinical rejection preceding LVEF decline |
| **Pericardial Effusion** | New appearance or rapid expansion of pericardial effusion | Epimyocardial inflammatory exudation |
| **Valvular Regurgitation** | New or worsening significant mitral or tricuspid regurgitation | Acute annular dilation or papillary muscle dysfunction |

### Clinical Practice Rules:
- **Multi-Parametric Rule**: A combination of $\ge 2$ abnormal parameters strongly indicates acute rejection and mandates urgent endomyocardial biopsy.
- **Negative Predictive Rule**: Normal echocardiographic parameters do not completely exclude low-grade cellular rejection (ISHLT Grade 1R) or early antibody-mediated rejection (pAMR).

### Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy (CAV)
- Accelerated diffuse, concentric, obliterative coronary intimal hyperplasia affecting epicardial vessels and microvasculature.
- **Dobutamine Stress Echocardiography (DSE)**: Annual DSE has high negative predictive value ($> 95\%$) for excluding severe CAV and predicting adverse cardiovascular events.
