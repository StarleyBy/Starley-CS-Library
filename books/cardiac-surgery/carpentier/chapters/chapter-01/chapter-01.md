## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Historical Evolution of Valvular Surgery
* **1960's Paradigm:** Predominance of congenital malformations and rheumatic disease; diagnostic reliance on catheterization; early palliative repairs, valvular homografts, and mechanical ball-valve prostheses (Starr-Edwards).
* **1970's Paradigm:** Dominance of mechanical prostheses and advent of the concept of "nonthrombogenic valve surgery" through bioprostheses and early reconstructive techniques.
* **1980's Paradigm:** Epidemiological shift toward degenerative mitral valve disease; widespread adoption of Doppler echocardiography; introduction of functional valve analysis and systematic reconstructive surgery across Europe and North America.
* **1990's Paradigm:** Formal validation of long-term superiority of valve repair; expansion into functional, ischemic, and cardiomyopathy-related mitral regurgitation; integration of surgical ablation for atrial fibrillation (Cox-Maze).

### The Landmark 1984 Comparative Evidence
* **Perier & Carpentier Study:** First concomitant comparative series of four cohorts of 100 patients undergoing surgery for mitral regurgitation (repair vs. porcine bioprosthesis vs. Starr-Edwards vs. Bjork-Shiley).
* **Survival and Morbidity Advantages:** Reconstructive surgery demonstrated statistically superior freedom from valve-related death, valve-related morbidity, systemic thromboembolism, and reoperation ([Fig. 1-1](#fig-01-01)).

### The Triad of Reconstructive Principles
* **Definition:** Reconstructive valve surgery is a comprehensive system combining functional valve analysis and targeted techniques based on three fundamental tenets:
  1. **Preserve or restore full leaflet motion:** Ensuring physiological mobility and eliminating restrictions or prolapse.
  2. **Create a large surface of coaptation:** Providing an extensive coaptation zone (typically 8–10 mm height) to ensure lasting competence under systemic ventricular pressure.
  3. **Remodel and stabilize the entire annulus:** True prosthetic annuloplasty restores physiological annular configuration, supports leaflet coaptation, and prevents late redilation.
* **Curative Objective:** Unlike valve replacement which trades native valve pathology for prosthesis-related disease, successful reconstruction provides a lifelong definitive cure without the requirement for chronic anticoagulation.

### Structure of the Reconstructive System
* **Section I (Fundamentals):** Pathophysiological triad (etiology, lesions, dysfunction), functional classification, perioperative and operative principles.
* **Section II (Mitral Valve Reconstruction):** Pathophysiology, "one lesion, one technique" strategy for Type I, Type II, Type IIIa, Type IIIb dysfunctions, systolic anterior motion (SAM), and left atrial remodeling.
* **Section III (Tricuspid Valve Reconstruction):** Surgical anatomy, functional assessment, and reconstructive annuloplasty and leaflet techniques.
* **Section IV (Aortic Valve Reconstruction):** Cusp and root reconstructive methods in perspective with autografts, homografts, and bioprostheses.
* **Section V (Disease-Specific Approach):** Etiology-specific strategies for degenerative, rheumatic, ischemic, endocarditis, cardiomyopathy, and congenital malformations.
* **Section VI (Transfer of Knowledge):** Multidisciplinary team integration and systematic step-by-step educational pathway for cardiac surgeons.

## EVOLUTION OF VALVE SURGERY</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Early Decades: 1960's to 1970's</summary><div class="details-content">

Since the onset of open-heart surgery, remarkable changes have occurred in the epidemiology, diagnosis, and surgical treatment of valvular diseases. At the beginning, in the 1960's, congenital malformations and rheumatic valvular diseases prevailed. The diagnosis of valvular disease was based on clinical investigation and catheterization. The surgical procedures comprised palliative valve repair techniques and valve replacement using valvular homografts or ball-valve prostheses.

In the 1970's rheumatic valve disease was predominant and generally treated by various types of mechanical valve prostheses. "Nonthrombogenic valve surgery" evolved with the contemporary development of valvular bioprostheses and reconstructive valve surgery.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Expansion of Functional Analysis: 1980's to 1990's</summary><div class="details-content">

The 1980's were marked by an increased incidence of degenerative valvular diseases, the extensive use of echocardiography, and the gradual incorporation of "functional valve analysis and reconstructive valve surgery" both in the United States and in Europe.

In the 1990's the surgical benefits of reconstructive valve surgery were strongly established, and attention turned to ischemic and cardiomyopathy-related valvular diseases with specific techniques of valve reconstruction. The contemporary development of surgical techniques to treat atrial fibrillation resulted in an additional valuable contribution to the concept of nonthrombogenic valve surgery.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Landmark Comparative Evidence</summary><div class="details-content">

Each decade led to an increasing number of valve procedures, which had a direct impact on both the survival and the quality of life of patients. The respective benefits and drawbacks of the different procedures were assessed and discussed to establish the best indications for each individual patient.

However, it was not until 1984 that the first comparative analysis of four different valve procedures outlined the superiority of reconstructive valve surgery over mechanical and biological valve replacement in mitral and tricuspid valve diseases ([Fig. 1-1](#fig-01-01)).

## CARPENTIER’S RECONSTRUCTIVE PHILOSOPHY</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Definition and Curative Objectives</summary><div class="details-content">

Today, essentially all patients with degenerative valvular diseases and the majority of patients with other types of valvular diseases enjoy the benefits of reconstructive valve surgery.

Reconstructive valve surgery is a comprehensive system of valve analysis and related techniques based on three basic principles: restoring or preserving the full mobility of the leaflets, creating a large surface of leaflet coaptation, and remodelling the annulus to provide an optimal and stable orifice area.

The objective of this book is to describe this system in detail, and to facilitate the transfer of knowledge to surgeons who want to acquire an expertise in reconstructive valve procedures that can cure a patient for the rest of his life.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Core Principles of Reconstruction</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### <span id="box-01-01"></span>BOX 1-1: Carpentier’s Principles of a Reconstructive Valve Operation</summary><div class="details-content">

* **Preserve or restore full leaflet motion:** Physiological leaflet mobility is mandatory to prevent functional stenosis and ensure unobstructed transvalvular flow.
* **Create a large surface of coaptation:** Adequate height and depth of leaflet apposition provide a durable hydraulic seal resistant to high intraventricular systolic pressures.
* **Remodel and stabilize the entire annulus:** Prosthetic ring annuloplasty restores physiological annular geometry, prevents progressive dilatation, and secures the repair against late failure.

## STRUCTURE OF THE COMPREHENSIVE SYSTEM</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Organization of the Six Sections</summary><div class="details-content">

This book is organized into the following six sections:

* **Section I (Fundamentals):** Describes the fundamentals of reconstructive valve surgery regardless of the valve involved or its pathology. Particularly important in this section is the chapter on valve analysis, which will help the reader become familiar with the pathophysiological triad, the functional classification, and the basic principles of valve reconstruction.
* **Section II (Mitral Valve Reconstruction):** Describes the techniques of mitral valve reconstruction in each functional group based on their pathological features and the "one lesion, one technique" principle.
* **Section III (Tricuspid Valve Reconstruction):** Describes the techniques of tricuspid valve reconstruction using the same functional classification as a guideline.
* **Section IV (Aortic Valve Reconstruction):** Explains the current status of aortic valve reconstruction in light of other nonthrombogenic alternatives such as autograft, homograft, or bioprosthetic valve replacement.
* **Section V (Disease-Specific Approach):** Presents a clinical review of the various congenital and acquired valvular diseases. For each etiology, a specific strategic approach is described and the indications and results are presented.
* **Section VI (Transfer of Knowledge):** Stresses the importance of teamwork by cardiologists, echocardiographists, anesthesiologists, and surgeons and addresses the issue of training and transfer of knowledge, particularly how to proceed step by step to acquire expertise in valve reconstruction.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### Multidisciplinary Teamwork and Transfer of Knowledge</summary><div class="details-content">

The sixth and last section stresses the importance of teamwork by cardiologists, echocardiographists, anesthesiologists, and surgeons, and addresses the issue of training and transfer of knowledge, particularly how to proceed step by step to acquire expertise in valve reconstruction.

Every effort has been made to provide comprehensive information and guidelines to all specialists involved in order to develop a successful reconstructive valve practice.
