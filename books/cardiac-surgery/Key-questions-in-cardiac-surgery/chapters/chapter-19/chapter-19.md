## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Atrial Fibrillation: Classification and Epidemiology

*   **Atrial fibrillation (AF):** Uncoordinated atrial activity at a rate of 300–500 bpm.
*   **Classification:** Isolated AF (single episode); recurrent AF (≥2 episodes) — paroxysmal (<7 days, spontaneous termination), persistent (requires drugs or DC cardioversion), permanent (does not revert to sinus rhythm).
*   **Incidence:** 1% of general population; increases with age — 0.2–0.3% at 25–35 years, 3–4% at 55–65 years, 6–9% at 65–90 years. Most common arrhythmia (33% of all arrhythmias).

### Causes and Risk Factors

*   **Common causes:** Idiopathic, mitral valve disease (→ LA dilation), ischemic heart disease, hypertension, post-cardiac surgery, alcohol, thyrotoxicosis.
*   **Post-operative AF risk factors:** Age, β-blocker withdrawal, electrolyte imbalance (K⁺, Mg²⁺), hypoxia, ischemia, pericardial effusion, infection.

### Pathophysiology

*   **Triggers:** Focal areas of automaticity (mainly around pulmonary veins)
*   **Substrate:** Atrial abnormality (inflammation/fibrosis)
*   **Modulating factors:** Autonomic nervous system
*   **Maintenance:** Multiple macro re-entry circuits within both atria
*   **Treatment principle:** Paroxysmal AF → pulmonary vein isolation (targets triggers); Persistent AF → Cox-maze procedure (targets macro re-entry circuits)

### Sequelae of AF

*   Palpitations and anxiety
*   Hemodynamic compromise — 30% loss of cardiac output; long-term may cause cardiomyopathy
*   Thromboembolism — stasis → stroke or pulmonary embolism

### Anticoagulation Guidelines (AHA Class I Indications)

| Clinical Scenario | Anticoagulation |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Prosthetic mechanical valves | Warfarin (INR 2.5–3.5) |
| 1 high-risk factor | Warfarin (INR 2.0–3.0) |
| >1 moderate-risk factor | Warfarin (INR 2.0–3.0) |
| 1 moderate-risk factor | Aspirin 81–325 mg or Warfarin (INR 2.0–3.0) |
| Low-risk factors | Aspirin 81–325 mg |

*   **High-risk factors:** Prior thromboembolism (stroke, TIA, systemic embolism), rheumatic mitral stenosis
*   **Moderate-risk factors:** Age >75 years, hypertension, heart failure, LVEF <35%, diabetes mellitus
*   **Low-risk factors:** Female gender, age 65–75 years, CAD, thyrotoxicosis
*   **Lone AF age <60 years:** No anticoagulation recommended

### Treatment Options for AF

*   **Pharmacological:** Chemical cardioversion and rate control (amiodarone, digoxin, β-blockers, calcium channel blockers)
*   **DC cardioversion**
*   **Catheter ablation:** 60% success rate; complications include pulmonary vein stenosis, systemic embolization, pericardial effusion, tamponade, phrenic nerve paralysis
*   **Surgical ablation:** 75–80% success rate

### Surgical Ablation Modalities

| Modality | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| **Cut and sew** | Surgical incisions (Cox maze III lesion set) |
| **Radiofrequency ablation** | Alternating current 350 kHz–1 MHz, 70–80°C for 1 minute, 3–6 mm lesion; transmurality assessed by conductance/impedance |
| **Microwave** | High-frequency electromagnetic radiation → water molecule oscillation |
| **Cryoablation** | Nitrous oxide at –60°C for 2 minutes → visible "iceball" |
| **Ultrasound** | High-frequency sound waves (2–20 MHz) → thermal heating and membrane disruption |
| **Laser** | Monochromatic, phase-coherent beam → heating and cellular destruction |

### Maze Procedure

*   **Principle:** Creates blind alleys with one entrance and one exit, directing electrical impulse from SA node to AV node by interrupting conduction routes and re-entrant pathways.
*   **Advantage over Guiraudon corridor procedure:** Better maintenance of atrial transport.
*   **Left atrial appendage excision:** Often performed to reduce blood stasis and thrombus formation.
*   **Predictors of failure:** Large left atrium (>5 cm) or longstanding AF (>5 years).

### Left Atrial Lesion Set (for mitral valve surgery)

*   Around right superior and inferior pulmonary veins
*   Around left superior and inferior pulmonary veins (divide ligament of Marshall)
*   From LSPV to LAA (LAA then excluded with suture)
*   From RIPV to LIPV
*   From connecting lesion between LIPV/RIPV to posterior mitral annulus
*   From left atriotomy to posterior mitral annulus

### Right Atrial Lesion Set (for full maze)

*   Right atrial free wall → SVC
*   Right atrial free wall → IVC
*   Right atrial free wall → anteroposterior tricuspid commissure
*   Right atrial appendage → anteroseptal tricuspid commissure
*   Right atrial appendage → right atrial free wall (leaving 2–3 cm gap for conduction)

### Atrial Flutter

*   **Mechanism:** Single macro re-entry circuit
*   **Presentation:** Variable block (2:1, 3:1, or 4:1) — atrial rate 300 bpm → ventricular response 150, 100, or 75 bpm
*   **Isthmus:** From IVC opening along Eustachian valve and coronary sinus to tricuspid valve
*   **Treatment:** Amiodarone, calcium channel blockers, DC cardioversion, catheter ablation, or surgical ablation of the flutter isthmus

### Vaughan-Williams Classification of Antiarrhythmic Drugs

| Class | Mechanism | Drugs |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **I** | Fast sodium channel blockade | Ia: quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide; Ib: lidocaine, phenytoin; Ic: flecainide, propafenone |
| **II** | β-sympathetic blockade | β-blockers, bretylium, guanethidine |
| **III** | Potassium channel blockade | amiodarone, sotalol, bretylium |
| **IV** | Slow calcium channel blockade | verapamil, diltiazem, adenosine |

## 1. Classification of Atrial Fibrillation</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Definition

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is defined as uncoordinated atrial activity at a rate of **300–500 bpm**.

#### Classification

*   **Isolated AF:** Single episode
*   **Recurrent AF (≥2 episodes):**
    *   **Paroxysmal AF:** Lasts less than 7 days and reverts spontaneously
    *   **Persistent AF:** Does not terminate spontaneously and requires drugs or DC cardioversion to restore sinus rhythm
    *   **Permanent AF:** Does not revert to sinus rhythm using drugs or DC cardioversion

## 5. Pathophysiology of Atrial Fibrillation</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Mechanism

Atrial fibrillation is **induced by focal areas of automaticity** (mainly around the pulmonary veins) and is **maintained by multiple macro re-entry circuits** within both atria.

![](_page_2_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 1. Pathophysiology of atrial fibrillation.** Macro re-entrant circuits (red) maintain the uncoordinated activity of the electric wavelets (yellow). RAA = right atrial appendage; LAA = left atrial appendage; SA node = sino-atrial node; AV node = atrioventricular node; PV = pulmonary vein; SVC = superior vena cava; IVC = inferior vena cava.

#### Requirements for AF

*   **Triggers:** Atrial ectopic foci, changes in atrial wall tension
*   **Substrate:** Atrial abnormality (inflammation/fibrosis)
*   **Modulating factors:** Autonomic nervous system

#### Treatment Implications Based on Pathophysiology

*   **Paroxysmal AF** — requires stopping the induction pathways (focal areas of automaticity) by **pulmonary vein isolation**
*   **Persistent AF** — requires eliminating the maintaining pathways (macro re-entry circuits) using the **Cox-maze procedure**

## 9. Principles of the Maze Operation</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Concept

A "maze" is created with a set of blind alleys with **one entrance and one exit** for atrial electrical activation, thereby directing the electrical impulse along one specified route from the sino-atrial node to the atrioventricular node by interrupting conduction routes and re-entrant pathways.

![](_page_5_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 3. Mechanism of atrial ablation.** A) Two parallel lines of fibrous tissue (scar tissue) interrupt the myocardial continuity and avoid the propagation of the electrical wave. The lines are close enough to stop the macro re-entrant circuit. The scar lesion can be obtained by healing of a surgical incision or by alternative sources of energy that create transmural myocardial injury. B) A pre-determined set of lesions creates a "maze", which allows the linear propagation of the electrical impulse through a series of "channels" (red and yellow arrows).

#### Advantages

Using the maze procedure (as opposed to the Guiraudon "corridor" procedure), **atrial transport can be better maintained**.

#### Adjunctive Procedure

**Left atrial appendage excision** is often performed in association with the maze procedure to reduce the area of blood stasis and subsequent thrombus formation.

#### Predictors of Failure

The maze procedure is less likely to be successful in patients with:
*   Large left atria (>5 cm)
*   Longstanding AF (>5 years)

## 10. Lesion Sets for the Maze Procedure (When Performed with Mitral Valve Surgery)</summary><div class="details-content">

The full maze procedure consists of lesion sets involving the left and right atrium. In certain patients, however, only a **left heart maze** is carried out using a cryoablation or radiofrequency ablation clamp or probe device.

#### Left Atrial Lesion Set (A–F)

*   **A:** Around the right superior and inferior pulmonary veins
*   **B:** Around the left superior and inferior pulmonary veins
    *   *To gain access to the left pulmonary veins, the heart needs to be retracted cephalad and to the right. Often the **ligament of Marshall** (black arrow), between the left superior pulmonary vein (LSPV) and the left pulmonary artery (LPA), also needs to be divided*
*   **C:** From the left superior pulmonary vein to the left atrial appendage, which is then excluded from the atrial cavity with a direct suture
*   **D:** From the right inferior pulmonary vein to the left inferior pulmonary vein
*   **E:** From the connecting lesion between the left and right inferior pulmonary veins to the middle of the posterior mitral valve annulus
*   **F:** From the left atriotomy to the middle of the posterior mitral valve annulus

#### Right Atrial Lesion Set (G–K)

For completion of the full maze procedure, the right atrial lesion set is performed. Initially, incisions in the right atrium (1, 2, and 3) are made to allow insertion of the ablation device to create lesions from the:

*   **G:** Right atrial free wall to the superior vena cava
*   **H:** Right atrial free wall to the inferior vena cava
*   **I:** Right atrial free wall to the anteroposterior commissure of the tricuspid valve
*   **J:** Right atrial appendage to the anteroseptal commissure of the tricuspid valve
*   **K:** Right atrial appendage to the right atrial free wall, ensuring a **2–3 cm gap is left** to allow for electrical conduction

## 11. Atrial Flutter: Mechanism and Management</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Mechanism

Atrial flutter is caused by a **single macro re-entry circuit**.

#### Clinical Presentation

Patients usually present with a **variable block** (e.g., 2:1, 3:1, or 4:1), which represents:
*   Atrial rate: **300 bpm**
*   Ventricular response: **150, 100, or 75 bpm**, respectively

#### Anatomy of the Flutter Isthmus

The atrial flutter isthmus runs from the **inferior vena caval opening** along the **Eustachian valve** and the **coronary sinus** to the **tricuspid valve**.

![](_page_9_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 5. Macro re-entrant circuit of atrial flutter.** The electric impulse crosses the isthmus between the orifices of the inferior vena cava (IVC) and the coronary sinus (CS). An ablation lesion is placed at this point, effectively blocking the electric loop. RAA = right atrial appendage; TV = tricuspid valve; SVC = superior vena cava.

#### Treatment Options

*   Amiodarone
*   Calcium channel blockers
*   DC cardioversion
*   Catheter ablation
*   Surgical ablation of the flutter isthmus
