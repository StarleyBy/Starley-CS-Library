# Tricuspid Valve Disease

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR) — Classification
*   **Normal leaflet motion** — annular dilation (secondary to pulmonary hypertension, RV failure, mitral valve disease), infective endocarditis.
*   **Excessive leaflet motion** — myxomatous degeneration.
*   **Restricted leaflet motion** — rheumatic fever, Ebstein’s anomaly.

### Ebstein’s Anomaly (Figure 1)
*   Dysplasia of tricuspid valve leaflets with:
    - Fused/perforated/absent leaflets, abnormal chordae
    - **Apical displacement** of septal leaflet into RV body
    - Thin **atrialised portion** of RV
    - Severe RV dysfunction and dilation
    - TR
    - RVOT obstruction (displaced leaflets)
*   Associated rhythm disturbances: SVT, VT, WPW syndrome.

### Symptoms of TR
*   Dyspnoea, fatigue (↓ cardiac output)
*   Peripheral oedema, ascites (systemic venous congestion)

### Signs of TR
*   ↑ JVP with prominent **V wave**
*   Peripheral oedema, ascites
*   **Pan-systolic murmur** at left sternal edge, ↑ with inspiration (Carvallo’s sign)
*   S₃ (gallop rhythm)
*   RV heave
*   Jaundice (hepatic congestion)

### Quantification of TR (Echocardiography)
*   **Severe TR:** vena contracta **>0.7 cm** (AHA)
*   Systolic flow reversal in hepatic veins
*   Additional: colour-flow jet area (relative to RA), PISA radius, RA size

### Tricuspid Stenosis (TS) — Causes
*   Rheumatic fever (most common)
*   Carcinoid heart disease
*   Congenital TS

### Symptoms of TS
*   Dyspnoea, fatigue (↓ cardiac output)
*   Peripheral oedema, ascites (systemic venous congestion)

### Signs of TS
*   ↑ JVP with prominent **A wave**
*   Peripheral oedema, ascites
*   Tricuspid opening snap
*   **Diastolic rumbling murmur** at left sternal edge, ↑ with inspiration
*   Split S₁

### Quantification of TS (Echocardiography)
*   **Severe TS:** tricuspid valve area **<1.0 cm²** (AHA)
*   Mean tricuspid diastolic pressure gradient
*   TVA calculation: continuity equation or planimetry

### Indications for Tricuspid Valve Surgery (AHA)

**In absence of mitral valve disease:**
*   **Class IIa:** severe TR in symptomatic patients
*   Considerations: annulus size (>40 mm), RV function/size, pulmonary hypertension (>50 mmHg rest, >60 mmHg exercise), RA size, AF

**In patients undergoing mitral valve surgery:**
*   **Class I:** severe TR
*   **Class IIb:** less than severe TR with pulmonary hypertension or dilated annulus (>40 mm)

### Surgical Options for Annular Dilation
*   **Bicuspidisation/plication** — running suture along posterior annulus to shorten/eliminate posterior leaflet
*   **De Vega annuloplasty (Figure 2):** two arms of running suture woven along annulus from anteroseptal to posteroseptal commissures, tightened over Hegar dilator
*   **Ring annuloplasty (Figures 3–4):** interrupted mattress sutures, sized ring implanted
*   **Avoid** AV node and Bundle of His (triangle of Koch)

### Structures at Risk During Tricuspid Valve Surgery (Figure 5)
*   AV node (centre of triangle of Koch) and Bundle of His (apex of triangle of Koch)
*   Aortic valve (non-coronary cusp)
*   Coronary sinus
*   Right coronary artery
*   Atrioventricular nodal artery

### Tricuspid Valve Annulus Sizing (Figure 6)
*   Annuloplasty sizers measuring anterior leaflet
*   Fixed sizes: 30–32 mm (women), 32–34 mm (men)
*   Ring has **gap** at apex of triangle of Koch to avoid suturing conducting system

## 2. What is Ebstein’s anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

![](_page_1_Picture_2.jpeg)

![](_page_1_Picture_3.jpeg)

**Figure 1. Ebstein’s anomaly. The septal leaflet (SL) is hypoplastic and plastered to the interventricular septum (IVS), and the posterior leaflet is absent. - It is defined as dysplasia of the tricuspid valve leaflets (Figure 1) resulting in:
  - **a) Fused, perforated or absent leaflets** with abnormal chordae
  - **b) Apical displacement of the septal leaflet** into the body of the right ventricle
  - **c) A thin atrialised portion** of the right ventricle
  - **d) Severe right ventricular dysfunction and dilation**
  - **e) Tricuspid regurgitation**
  - **f) Right ventricular outflow tract obstruction** caused by the displaced leaflets
- Ebstein’s anomaly is also associated with **rhythm disturbances**, including supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia and Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome.

## 3. What are the symptoms of tricuspid regurgitation?</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Dyspnoea and fatigue** — due to reduced cardiac output
- **Peripheral oedema and ascites** — due to back pressure on the systemic venous circulation

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## 4. What are the signs of tricuspid regurgitation?</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Increased jugular venous pressure** with a prominent **V wave**
- **Peripheral oedema and ascites**
- **Pan-systolic murmur** prominent at the left sternal edge, increased with inspiration (**Carvallo’s sign**)
- **Third heart sound (S₃)** — gallop rhythm
- **Right ventricular heave**
- **Jaundice** — secondary to hepatic congestion in severe TR

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## 5. How is tricuspid regurgitation quantified by echocardiography?</summary><div class="details-content">

- According to the American Heart Association (AHA) guidelines, the main criteria is a **vena contracta** (narrowest part of regurgitant jet) width **>0.7 cm** for severe TR.
- **Severe TR** is also associated with **systolic flow reversal in the hepatic veins**.
- Additional methods used to quantify tricuspid regurgitation include using colour-flow Doppler to measure the size of the regurgitant jet in relation to the right atrium, **PISA (proximal isovelocity area)** radius and right atrial size.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## 9. How is tricuspid stenosis quantified by echocardiography?</summary><div class="details-content">

- According to the AHA guidelines, the main criteria is a **tricuspid valve area (TVA) <1.0 cm²** for severe TS.
- The TVA can be calculated by the **continuity equation** or **planimetry**.
- The **mean tricuspid diastolic pressure gradient** can also be used to quantify tricuspid stenosis.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## 10. What are the indications for tricuspid valve surgery in the absence of mitral valve disease?</summary><div class="details-content">

### AHA guidelines

- **Class IIa:** severe TR in **symptomatic patients**
- **Other considerations** include:
  - Tricuspid valve annulus size **>40 mm**
  - Right ventricular function and size
  - Pulmonary hypertension (**>50 mmHg at rest, 60 mmHg on exercise**)
  - Right atrial size
  - Presence of atrial fibrillation

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## 11. What are the indications for tricuspid valve repair in patients undergoing mitral valve surgery?</summary><div class="details-content">

### AHA guidelines

- **Class I:** **severe TR**
- **Class IIb:** **less than severe TR** with pulmonary hypertension or a dilated tricuspid valve annulus (**>40 mm**)

</div></details>

## 12. What are the surgical options for tricuspid valve annular dilation?</summary><div class="details-content">

![](_page_4_Picture_7.jpeg)

**Figure 2. De Vega tricuspid valve annuloplasty.**

![](_page_5_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 3. Tricuspid valve ring annuloplasty.**

![](_page_5_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 4. Operative image of the tricuspid valve following ring annuloplasty. AL = anterior leaflet of the tricuspid valve; SL = septal leaflet of the tricuspid valve; PL = posterior leaflet of the tricuspid valve.**

- **Bicuspidisation/plication of the tricuspid valve** — a running suture along the posterior TV annulus to shorten or eliminate the posterior leaflet resulting in a bicuspid valve
- **De Vega annuloplasty (Figure 2)** — two arms of a running suture weaving along the TV annulus from the anteroseptal to posteroseptal commissures. The first arm of the weaved suture is started at the anteroseptal commissure, superficially and close to the annulus, to avoid the non-coronary cusp of the aortic valve. This is then continued clockwise to the posteroseptal commissure. The suture is then weaved back counter-clockwise and secured with Teflon® pledgets at both ends (Figure 2A). The stitch is tightened over an appropriately sized Hegar dilator (Figure 2B)
- **Ring annuloplasty (Figures 3 and 4)** — using interrupted mattress sutures, an appropriately sized tricuspid valve ring is implanted into the tricuspid valve annulus
- During tricuspid valve surgery it is important to **avoid the atrioventricular node and Bundle of His**, which are located in the **triangle of Koch**.

</div></details>
