# Atrial Septal Defects

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Определение и классификация
*   **Atrial septal defect (ASD):** Врождённое сообщение между левым и правым предсердиями с шунтированием крови.
*   **Истинные ASD (эмбриологически):** только дефекты в области fossa ovalis (ostium secundum, PFO, vestibular defect) — 75–76%.
*   **Хирургически к ASD относят:** ostium primum (15–20%, частичный AVSD), sinus venosus (5–10%, чаще у SVC), coronary sinus (1%, unroofed coronary sinus).
*   **Ostium secundum:** вызывается аномальной резорбцией septum primum или неполным перекрытием septum secundum.
*   **Ostium primum:** неполное сращение septum primum с эндокардиальными подушками.
*   **Sinus venosus:** неполное слияние sinus venosus с межпредсердной перегородкой.

### Эпидемиология и ассоциации
*   7% всех врождённых пороков сердца (2-е место после двустворчатого аортального клапана).
*   Соотношение ж:м = 2:1. Чаще при синдроме Дауна.
*   В 10 раз чаще встречается с другими аномалиями, чем изолированно.

### Патофизиология и естественное течение
*   Шунт слева направо (из-за разницы в растяжимости желудочков) → объёмная перегрузка правых отделов → дилатация ПП, ПЖ, лёгочной артерии.
*   При нелеченном ASD — укорочение продолжительности жизни, осложнения: предсердные аритмии, дисфункция ПЖ, лёгочная гипертензия, инсульт (парадоксальная эмболия), редко — Eisenmenger.

### Диагностика
*   **ЭКГ:** неполная блокада ПНПГ (rSR' в V1). Ostium secundum — правый тип отклонения оси; primum и sinus venosus — левый тип.
*   **Рентген:** кардиомегалия (ПП+ПЖ), усиление лёгочного рисунка.
*   **Эхокардиография:** золотой стандарт — тип дефекта, шунт (допплер), последствия (дилатация ПЖ), ассоциированные аномалии.
*   **Bubble test:** при внутривенном введении взболтанного физраствора появление пузырьков в левом предсердии указывает на ASD/PFO.
*   **Катетеризация:** step-up O₂ на уровне ПП (таблица 1). Qp:Qs > 1.5 — гемодинамически значимый шунт.

### Расчёт шунта (Qp:Qs)
*   По принципу Фика: 
    $Qp:Qs = \frac{(Ao\ sats - RA\ sats)}{(PV\ sats - PA\ sats)}$
*   RA sats = (3×SVC sats + 1×IVC sats)/4.
*   Клинически значимый: Qp:Qs > 1.5; показание к закрытию: Qp:Qs > 2.

### Показания и противопоказания к закрытию
*   **Показания:** Qp:Qs > 2; предсердная аритмия; обратимые ЛГ; дисфункция ПЖ; PFO с право-левым шунтом и рецидивирующими парадоксальными эмболиями.
*   **Противопоказания:** асимптомный Qp:Qs < 1.5; фиксированная ЛГ (PASP > 60 мм рт.ст., PVR > 12 WU, шунт справа налево).

### Методы закрытия
*   **Чрескожное (device closure):** только ostium secundum, rim ≥5 мм до клапанов/вен, дефект <40 мм. Устройства: AMPLATZER™, CardioSEAL®, HELEX®.
*   **Хирургическое:** все типы ASD. Доступы: срединная стернотомия, мини-торакотомия, торакоскопия/робот.

### Особенности хирургии по типам
*   **Ostium secundum:** правая атриотомия, ушивание или заплата (аутоперикард).
*   **Ostium primum:** заплата на гребень МЖП с обходом AV-узла и пучка Гиса + пластика митрального клапана (closure of cleft).
*   **Sinus venosus:** перикардиальный baffle для перенаправления аномальных лёгочных вен в ЛП; возможен Warden procedure (отсечение и реимплантация ВПВ).
*   **Coronary sinus:** восстановление крыши КС + закрытие дефекта второй заплатой.

### Риски и исходы
*   **Летальность:** дети <1%; взрослые до 10% (факторы риска — сопутствующие пороки, ЛГ, ФП).
*   **Осложнения:** AV-блокада (ostium primum, coronary sinus), митральная регургитация (ostium primum), обструкция системного венозного возврата (sinus venosus), остаточный шунт.
*   **Долгосрочные исходы:** при закрытии в возрасте <25 лет — выживаемость как в популяции. Пожилой возраст и предоперационная СН III–IV (NYHA) — независимые предикторы поздней смертности.

## 1 What is an atrial septal defect (ASD)?</summary><div class="details-content">

An atrial septal defect is a congenital lesion that results in communication and therefore shunting of blood between the left and right atria.

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 2 How is the atrial septum formed embryologically?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Septum Primum</summary><div class="details-content">

The cavity of the primitive atrium is initially subdivided into right and left chambers by the **septum primum**, which is a crescentic fold of endocardial tissue that begins to develop at day 28 *in utero* (Figure 1).

![](_page_0_Picture_8.jpeg)

**Figure 1. Development of the septum primum.**

The septum primum grows downwards to close the primary interatrial foramen by fusing with the endocardial cushions by approximately day 35, leaving an opening below its free margin (**ostium primum**).

By programmed cell death (apoptosis), a second opening (**ostium secundum** or **foramen ovale**) develops in the upper part of the septum primum at day 33. This opening persists until birth.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Septum Secundum and Fossa Ovalis</summary><div class="details-content">

At a similar time (day 33), a second endocardial fold (**septum secundum**), semilunar in shape, develops and descends towards but never reaches the endocardial cushions, thereby forming the **fossa ovalis** (Figure 2).

![](_page_1_Picture_5.jpeg)

**Figure 2. Development of the septum secundum.**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Closure After Birth</summary><div class="details-content">

Shortly after birth, the foramen ovale closes following fusion of the septum secundum with the septum primum (Figure 3).

![](_page_2_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 3. Closure of the foramen ovale resulting in separation of the left and right atrial chambers.**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Components of the True Interatrial Septum</summary><div class="details-content">

The component parts of the final true atrial septum (Figure 4) include:

*   **a)** floor of the fossa ovalis — which is derived from the septum primum;
*   **b)** anteroinferior muscular rim of the fossa ovalis.

![](_page_2_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 4. Components of the true interatrial septum (in white) and surrounding structures.**

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 3 How are atrial septal defects classified?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Defects of the Interatrial Septum (Fossa Ovalis Defects) — 75–76%</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **i) Patent foramen ovale (Vieussens valve)** — superiorly placed.
*   **ii) Ostium secundum defect** — centrally placed, can be single or multi-fenestrated (Figure 5B).
*   **Vestibular defect** (<1%) — muscular defect in the anteroinferior rim of the fossa ovalis, outside the triangle of Koch (Figure 5C).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Defects Outside the Interatrial Septum — 6–11%</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Sinus venosus defect** (5–10%) — usually occurs at the SVC–right atrial junction (superior defect), but may also occur at the IVC–right atrial junction (inferior defect) (Figure 5D).
*   **Coronary sinus defect** (1%) — represents an unroofed coronary sinus (Figure 5E).
*   **Other types:** cor triloculare biatriatum (<1%).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Defects of the Atrioventricular Septum — 15–20%</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Ostium primum defect** (15–20%) — also known as a partial atrioventricular septal defect (Figure 5F).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Embryologic vs Surgical Definition</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Embryologically:** only lesions in the fossa ovalis (ostium secundum defects, patent foramen ovale and vestibular defects) are considered true atrial septal defects.
*   **Surgically:** all interatrial communications, including ostium primum defects, sinus venosus defects and coronary sinus defects, as well as the fossa ovalis lesions, are referred to as atrial septal defects.

</div></details>

![](_page_4_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 5. Atrial septal defects:** A) normal anatomy of the right atrial cavity; B) multi-fenestrated ostium secundum defect; C) vestibular defect; D) sinus venosus defect (superior and inferior type) and patent foramen ovale; E) multi-fenestrated coronary sinus defect; F) ostium primum defect.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 4 What is the aetiology of atrial septal defects?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Ostium Secundum Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

Caused by abnormal reabsorption of the septum primum or incomplete overlap of the septum secundum with the septum primum.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Ostium Primum Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

Caused by incomplete fusion of the septum primum with the endocardial cushions, resulting in a gap at the level of the atrioventricular valves. The left atrioventricular valve is usually displaced, whereas the right atrioventricular valve is rarely involved.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Sinus Venosus Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

Caused by incomplete fusion of the embryological sinus venosus with the atrial septum, most commonly near the junction with the superior vena cava.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO)</summary><div class="details-content">

Caused by incomplete fusion of the septum primum with the septum secundum.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Iatrogenic ASD</summary><div class="details-content">

Atrial septal defects can also have an iatrogenic aetiology, either during catheter-based procedures (such as percutaneous mitral balloon valvotomy) or cardiac surgery (such as atrial myxoma excision or left atriotomy for mitral valve surgery).

</div></details>

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 5 What abnormalities are often associated with atrial septal defects?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Trifoliate left atrioventricular valve with or without regurgitation (associated with ostium primum defects).
*   Anomalous pulmonary venous connections (partial and total, associated with sinus venosus defects or fossa ovalis defects).
*   Left-sided superior vena cava (associated with coronary sinus defects).
*   Asplenia.
*   Atrial septal defects are **ten times more likely to occur in association with other anomalies** than as isolated defects.

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 6 What is the epidemiology of atrial septal defects?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Account for **7% of all congenital cardiac abnormalities** (second only to bicuspid aortic valve).
*   **Female to male ratio is 2:1**.
*   Increased incidence in patients with **Down's syndrome**.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 7 What is the pathophysiology of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Direction and Timing of Shunt</summary><div class="details-content">

Due to the difference in compliance between the left and right ventricles, blood usually flows across the defect as a **left-to-right shunt**, predominantly in late systole and during diastole.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Determinants of Shunt Magnitude</summary><div class="details-content">

The magnitude of the shunt depends on:

*   **a)** size of the defect;
*   **b)** compliance of the left and right ventricles;
*   **c)** systemic and pulmonary vascular resistance.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Haemodynamic Consequences</summary><div class="details-content">

This results in **volume overload of the right-sided chambers**, with subsequent enlargement of the:
*   Right atrium
*   Right ventricle
*   Pulmonary artery

Initially, the shunt is well tolerated whilst the pulmonary vascular resistance remains low, but if left uncorrected, **atrial arrhythmias, right ventricular failure and possibly pulmonary hypertension** eventually ensue.

</div></details>

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 8 What is the natural history of atrial septal defects?</summary><div class="details-content">

Although most patients with an untreated defect usually survive into adulthood, their life expectancy is **not normal** in comparison to an age-matched population.

Survival from a cardiac standpoint is influenced by the clinical sequelae of:

*   **a)** atrial arrhythmias;
*   **b)** right ventricular dysfunction;
*   **c)** pulmonary hypertension;
*   **d)** stroke;
*   **e)** shunt reversal and Eisenmenger syndrome (in rare cases).

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 9 What are the symptoms of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

The timing of the clinical presentation depends on the degree of the left-to-right shunt and subsequent haemodynamic consequences. **Most patients are hence asymptomatic.**

Patients may eventually present with:

*   **a)** reduced exercise tolerance and fatigue;
*   **b)** recurrent respiratory infections;
*   **c)** palpitations (secondary to atrial arrhythmias);
*   **d)** syncope;
*   **e)** stroke (secondary to paradoxical embolism);
*   **f)** congestive cardiac failure.

</div></details>

---

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 10 What are the signs of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Signs of Left-to-Right Shunt</summary><div class="details-content">

The physical signs elicited depend on the haemodynamic consequences and relate to the:
*   **a)** size of the defect;
*   **b)** degree of the left-to-right shunt;
*   **c)** relative compliance of the left and right ventricles;
*   **d)** systemic and pulmonary vascular resistances.

*   **Parasternal heave** of the right ventricle (due to increased diastolic filling and subsequent increased right ventricular stroke volume).
*   **Split S1** (caused by increased intensity of T1 due to delayed closure of the tricuspid valve).
*   **Fixed splitting of the 2nd heart sound** (which does not vary during respiration).
*   **Pansystolic flow murmur** at the left sternal edge in the 2nd intercostal space (due to increased blood flow across the pulmonary valve and not due to flow across the ASD).
*   **Soft mid-diastolic murmur** at the left sternal edge in the 4th intercostal space (due to the increased flow through the tricuspid valve).
*   An **apical pansystolic murmur** of mitral regurgitation may be heard in patients with an ostium primum defect.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Signs of Pulmonary Hypertension</summary><div class="details-content">

If the patient develops pulmonary hypertension, further signs include:

*   **a)** early diastolic murmur (Graham Still murmur, representing pulmonary regurgitation);
*   **b)** disappearance of the split 2nd heart sound, pansystolic flow murmur and the mid-diastolic rumble.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cyanosis</summary><div class="details-content">

Cyanosis and clubbing are only present in patients with **shunt reversal (Eisenmenger syndrome)**.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 14 Describe the bubble test</summary><div class="details-content">

Sterile saline is agitated by passing it between two syringes to produce tiny bubbles. Following intravenous injection of the saline, echocardiographic images of the left and right atrium are monitored.

![](_page_12_Figure_5.jpeg)

**Figure 13. Bubble test demonstrated on serial transthoracic apical four-chamber images:** A) the right atrium (RA) and ventricle (RV) are filled with bubbles; B) during a Valsalva manoeuvre, the bubbles fill the left atrium (LA) first; and then C) the left ventricle (LV).

*   If the atrial septum is intact, the bubbles should remain on the right side.
*   **Bubbles within the left atrium indicate a communication between the atria**, suggestive of an atrial septal defect.

## 16 How do you calculate the shunt fraction (Qp:Qs)?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### The Fick Principle</summary><div class="details-content">

Flow $= \frac{O_2 \text{ consumption}}{\text{arteriovenous } O_2 \text{ concentration difference}}$

Flow $= \frac{O_2 \text{ consumption}}{(\text{arteriovenous } O_2 \text{ sats difference} \times \text{Hb} \times 1.34 \times 10)}$

**Pulmonary flow** $= \frac{O_2 \text{ consumption}}{[(\text{PV sats} - \text{PA sats}) \times \text{Hb} \times 1.34 \times 10]}$

**Systemic flow** $= \frac{O_2 \text{ consumption}}{[(\text{Ao sats} - \text{RA sats}) \times \text{Hb} \times 1.34 \times 10]}$

As $O_2$ consumption and Hb are the same in both equations:

**Pulmonary flow : systemic flow ratio (shunt ratio)**

$$Qp:Qs = \frac{(\text{Ao sats} - \text{RA sats})}{(\text{PV sats} - \text{PA sats})}$$

**RA sats** $= \frac{(3 \times \text{SVC sats}) + (1 \times \text{IVC sats})}{4}$

PV = pulmonary vein; PA = pulmonary artery; Ao = aorta; RA = right atrium; SVC = superior vena cava; IVC = inferior vena cava; Hb = haemoglobin; sats = oxygen saturations.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Example Calculation (Figure 14)</summary><div class="details-content">

![](_page_15_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 14. Shunt calculation based on the Fick principle.**

RA sats = ([3 × 64] + [1 × 68]) / 4 = 65%

Measured PA saturations = 82%

Measured PV saturations = 99%

Assuming aortic and PV saturation are equal:

$Qp:Qs = \frac{(99 - 65)}{(99 - 82)} = \frac{34}{17} = 2:1$

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 17 What are the therapeutic options for a patient with an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Conservative Management</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Infants with an ostium secundum defect **<8 mm** (as 66% close within 18 months);
*   **b)** Adults with a **Qp:Qs ratio of <1.5**;
*   **c)** Patients with severe pulmonary vascular resistance, shunt reversal and fixed pulmonary hypertension (PASP >60 mmHg, PVR >12 WU).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Medical Therapy</summary><div class="details-content">

To optimise patients with atrial arrhythmias and right ventricular volume overload prior to intervention.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Interventional Options</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Percutaneous closure
*   Surgery

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 18 What are the indications for therapeutic closure of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   A **Qp:Qs ratio of >2**
*   **Atrial arrhythmia**
*   **Reversible pulmonary hypertension**
*   **Right ventricular dysfunction**
*   **Patent foramen ovale** in patients with a demonstrated right-to-left shunt (by saturations or bubble study) and recurrent paradoxical cerebral emboli

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 19 What are the contraindications for therapeutic closure of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Asymptomatic patient with a clinically insignificant atrial septal defect (**Qp:Qs <1.5**)
*   **Shunt reversal with fixed pulmonary hypertension** (PASP >60 mmHg, PVR >12 WU)

</div></details>

## 20 What are the principles of device closure for an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Indications for Device Closure</summary><div class="details-content">

Device closure is only suitable in patients with:

*   **a)** Ostium secundum defects (within the fossa ovalis);
*   **b)** **5 mm rim** from the defect to the valves and major veins;
*   **c)** Defect **<40 mm** in size.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Procedure</summary><div class="details-content">

*   General anaesthesia or local anaesthesia with sedation.
*   Femoral venous access with fluoroscopic and transoesophageal guidance.
*   The diameter of the defect is measured both by echocardiography and sizing balloon.
*   Deployment of the occluder device.

## 24 What are the principles of surgery for an ostium primum defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Repair of an ostium primum defect follows the principles of repair of an **atrioventricular septal defect**, where no ventricular component is detected.
*   A patch is attached to the **crest of the ventricular septum** at the junction of the left and right atrioventricular valves.
*   It is important to avoid the **atrioventricular node and bundle of His**, which are displaced inferiorly and posteriorly, close to the coronary sinus.
*   The ostium of the coronary sinus can lie on either the right or left side of the patch.
*   **Concomitant left atrioventricular valve repair**, including closure of the anterior cleft with or without annuloplasty, should be performed in all cases.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 25 What are the principles of surgery for a sinus venosus defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   The anomalous pulmonary blood is redirected back into the left atrium using an autologous or heterologous **pericardial baffle**, closing the defect at the same time.
*   To avoid possible stenosis at the cavoatrial junction, the superior vena cava can be detached and reimplanted on the atrial appendage (**Warden procedure**). Alternatively, a posterior atriotomy extending into the superior vena cava is then repaired with a second patch.
*   It is important to avoid damage to the **sinoatrial node and its nutrient artery**.

![](_page_22_Picture_2.jpeg)

A concomitant ostium secundum defect is also present; B) a baffling patch of autologous pericardium is used to redirect the pulmonary venous drainage across the sinus venosus defect into the left atrium; C) after completion of the sinus venosus repair, the concomitant ostium secundum defect is repaired primarily; D) a second patch of autologous pericardium is used to enlarge the cavo-atrial junction and the remaining portion of the atriotomy is closed primarily. ## 26 What are the principles of surgery for a coronary sinus defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

An **unroofed coronary sinus** is invariably found in association with a coronary sinus defect.

The principles of repair in these patients includes:

*   **a)** **Re-roofing** of the coronary sinus in its course along the posterior aspect of the mitral valve annulus;
*   **b)** Closure of the interatrial defect using a **second patch** of autologous or heterologous pericardium.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 27 What are the complications of surgery following repair of an atrial septal defect?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### General Complications</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Thromboembolism
*   Atrial arrhythmias
*   Congestive heart failure
*   Pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular dysfunction
*   Infective endocarditis (during the first 6 months following surgery)
*   Residual shunts

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Mortality</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Surgical mortality in children is **<1%**. The type of ASD does not represent a significant risk factor.
*   Overall mortality in adults can reach **10%**. Coexistent malformations, pulmonary hypertension and atrial fibrillation represent significant risk factors.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Specific Complications by Defect Type</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Atrioventricular block** and the need for a permanent pacemaker (in ostium primum and coronary sinus defects).
*   **Mitral regurgitation** and the need for reintervention (in ostium primum defects).
*   **Systemic venous return obstruction** (in sinus venosus defects).

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 28 What are the long-term outcomes of surgery?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Surgery in adults **<25 years old** results in long-term survival comparable to an age-matched population.
*   In older patients with pre-operative atrial arrhythmias, right ventricular dysfunction and pulmonary hypertension, long-term survival following surgery is **reduced compared to age-matched controls**, but **improved compared to medically treated patients**.
*   **Older age at repair** and pre-operative **New York Heart Association Class III or IV heart failure** are independent predictors of late mortality.

</div></details>
