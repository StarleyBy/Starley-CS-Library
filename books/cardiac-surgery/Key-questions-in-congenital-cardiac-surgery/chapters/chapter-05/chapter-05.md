## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Principles of Imaging Modalities

| Modality | Principles | Risks |
|----------|------------|-------|
| **CT** | X-ray attenuation; iodinated contrast enhances blood pool | Ionising radiation (IRMER: ALARP); allergic reaction (24/1000); renal impairment (<2%) |
| **MRI** | Magnetic field + radiofrequency; protons generate signal; gadolinium contrast | Metal implant risks; NSF (renal failure); anaphylaxis rare (1:10,000) |

### Coronary Artery Anomalies

*   **Modality of choice:** ECG-gated CT angiography (origin, course, termination, fistulae, myocardial bridges)
*   **MRI role:** Ventricular function, myocardial viability, valvular function, stress ischaemia detection, post-repair baffle assessment
*   **Key anomalies:** Anomalous origin from opposite sinus (ALCAPA, retro-aortic course), interarterial course, myocardial bridging

### Atrial Septal Defect (ASD)

*   **CXR:** Cardiomegaly, RA dilatation, prominent central pulmonary arteries, increased vascular markings
*   **MRI:** Poor echo windows, anatomical assessment, shunt quantification (phase-contrast Qp/Qs), ventricular volumes/function
*   **CT:** Associated anomalies (anomalous pulmonary veins), pre-op planning (vascular access, defect size/shape for device), coronary assessment

### Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)

*   **CXR:** Dilated central pulmonary arteries, increased vascularity, increased cardiothoracic ratio
*   **MRI:** Gold standard for LV function/size, shunt quantification, scar localisation (late gadolinium) for arrhythmia ablation
*   **CT:** Complementary anatomy (type, location, size, number), outflow obstruction, coronary assessment, surgical planning

### Atrioventricular Septal Defect (AVSD)

*   **CXR:** Cardiomegaly, large proximal/small peripheral PAs ("peripheral pruning"), prominent pulmonary veins (if AV valve regurgitation)
*   **CT/MRI:** Anatomical evaluation (when echo inconclusive), outflow obstruction, coarctation, heterotaxy; MRI for post-op quantification (MR, LV function, residual shunt)

### Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)

*   **CT:** RVOT morphology, pulmonary arteries, aorta, collaterals (MAPCAs), re-do surgery planning, coronary patency, sternal proximity
*   **MRI:** Ventricular size/function, pulmonary regurgitation quantification (phase-contrast), residual VSD, fibrosis (late gadolinium) — guides timing of pulmonary valve replacement

### Double-Outlet Right Ventricle (DORV)

*   **MRI:** Intracardiac anatomy, biventricular function, surgical feasibility, pulmonary arteries, aortic arch
*   **CT:** Great artery relationship, VSD location, outflow obstruction, coronary anomalies (RCA from LMCA, duplicated LAD), 3D reconstruction for surgical planning

### Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA)

*   **CXR:** Narrow mediastinum (parallel great arteries)
*   **Atrial switch (Mustard/Senning):** MRI for RV function, baffle anatomy, leaks; CT preferred if pacemaker (baffle obstruction, leaks, vena cava obstruction)
*   **Rastelli:** MRI for ventricular function, conduit function; CT for conduit calcification/stenosis, LVOT obstruction
*   **Arterial switch (Jatene):** MRI for ventricular volumes, aortic regurgitation, branch PA stenosis, coronary anatomy, myocardial perfusion/viability; CT for anatomical delineation, coronary stenosis

### Congenitally Corrected TGA (ccTGA)

*   **CXR:** Dextrocardia or mesocardia
*   **MRI:** Anatomy, ventricular function, ischaemia/scarring, RV size/function, tricuspid regurgitation severity
*   **CT:** Anatomical assessment, great vessels, coronary arteries, cardiac function

### Cavopulmonary Circulation (Fontan)

*   **Pre-op:** CT for branch PA stenosis, pulmonary venous return; MRI for ventricular function, valvular regurgitation, collateral flow quantification
*   **Post-op:** CT for connection stenosis/thrombosis, pulmonary vein compression, AV fistulae; MRI for ventricular function, connection evaluation, flow through fenestrations/leaks, collaterals, thrombosis, fibrosis

### Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Drainage (TAPVD)

*   **Modality of choice:** CT and MRI (echo difficult unless vein drains close to cavoatrial junction)
*   **CXR:** Scimitar sign, pulmonary artery dilatation, cardiomegaly
*   **MRI:** Phase-contrast Qp/Qs quantifies shunt significance

### Coarctation of the Aorta

*   **CXR:** Rib notching (Roesler sign), figure of 3 sign
*   **MRI:** Preferred (no radiation) — severity, collateral vessels, associated anomalies, recoarctation, aneurysm
*   **CT:** Stented coarctation (metal artefact limits MRI), site/morphology, arch hypoplasia, collaterals, post-stenting complications (dissection, leak, thrombus, migration, fracture), coronary evaluation

### Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome (HLHS)

*   **Prenatal:** Ultrasound (>22 weeks)
*   **Post-op:** CMR for Glenn/Fontan suitability (ventricular size/function, pulmonary arteries, neo-aorta); CT for associated anomalies, sizing

### Proximal Aortic Pathology

*   **Indications:** Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, Loeys-Dietz, bicuspid aortic valve
*   **CTA:** Most precise for aortic diameter measurement
*   **MRI:** Preferred for follow-up (young patients) — 3D SSFP, cine images, flow mapping for valve disease (mitral prolapse, aortic regurgitation)

### Ebstein's Anomaly

*   **CXR:** Cardiomegaly, narrow aortic pedicle, decreased pulmonary vascular markings
*   **CT/MRI:** Apical displacement of septal/posterior tricuspid leaflet, atrialised RV, severe RA enlargement, associated anomalies (ASD, PS, subaortic stenosis, coarctation)
*   **MRI:** RV size/function quantification, tricuspid regurgitation severity, Qp/Qs, fibrosis, LV non-compaction assessment; post-op surveillance

### Transcatheter Procedure Planning

*   **CT/Echo:** Suitability, device size, vascular access, coronary proximity, complications
*   **PET:** Device infection

### Vascular Slings and Rings

*   **CXR:** Tracheal narrowing, unilateral aeration
*   **Upper GI:** Oesophageal indentation
*   **CTA/MRA:** Vascular anatomy, tracheal compression, intracardiac anomalies

### Aortopulmonary Window

*   **CT:** Abnormal connection between aorta and pulmonary trunk, associated pulmonary hypertension (mosaic perfusion, PA dilatation, hepatic vein dilatation)

## 1. Principles of Computed Tomography and Magnetic Resonance Imaging</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Principles of Computed Tomography (CT)

A CT scan combines a series of X-ray images taken from different projections; attenuation data are used to reconstruct cross-sectional images. Different tissues and organs are differentiated by their inherent variation in attenuation:
*   Bone: high attenuation
*   Air: lowest attenuation
*   Water: intermediate attenuation

Iodinated contrast agents enhance visualisation of the blood pool (vessels, body organs). CT plays an important role in characterising congenital cardiovascular anomalies and complications post-corrective surgery.

**Risks:**
*   **Ionising radiation:** Potential cancer risk. IRMER regulations require benefits to outweigh risks and radiation dose kept "as low as reasonably practicable" (ALARP).
*   **Iodinated contrast:**
    *   Allergic reaction: anaphylaxis in 24 per 1000
    *   Renal impairment: <2%; screen renal function before CT (especially kidney disease, diabetes, elderly); adequate hydration reduces risk

#### Principles of Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

MRI uses a strong magnetic field and radiofrequency waves to produce images. Signal is generated from magnetisation of atoms with odd numbers of protons or neutrons (mainly protons).

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) plays an important role in detecting congenital anomalies and associated lesions, allowing non-invasive assessment of ventricular function and shunt flow. CMR is widely used for follow-up of ventricular function, size, and shunt studies in repaired and unrepaired congenital lesions, and for timing of surgery.

**Risks:**
*   **Magnetic field:** Risk in patients with metal and non-conditional implantable devices
*   **Gadolinium contrast:**
    *   Anaphylaxis: rare (1:10,000)
    *   Nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF): in renal failure patients; alternative contrast agents often used; incidence has significantly declined

## 2. Coronary Artery Anomalies</summary><div class="details-content">

#### CT Assessment (Figures 1–4)

**ECG-gated CT angiography** is the non-invasive modality of choice for detecting the origin, course, and termination of coronary artery anomalies (Figures 1, 2), with the ability to define related structures and coronary artery fistulae (Figure 3). CT is also reliable for detecting myocardial bridges (Figure 4).

![](_page_3_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 2. Anomalous left coronary artery from pulmonary artery (ALCAPA): A,B) CT angiography volume-rendered reconstructions showing tortuous dilated right coronary artery (white arrow) and dilated collateral feeding anomalous LAD retrogradely (black arrow); C) CT short-axis double oblique multiplanar reconstruction showing dilated coronary arteries and subendocardial calcification (arrow) — previous infarction in LAD territory; D) CMR showing late gadolinium enhancement indicating subendocardial infarction in anterior wall due to ischaemia from steal phenomenon.**

![](_page_4_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 3. CT angiography in coronary-pulmonary artery fistula: A) 3D reconstruction showing conus branch (arrow) leading to network of dilated tortuous vessels communicating with pulmonary artery (PA); B) axial image showing non-dilated pulmonary artery (25 mm).**

![](_page_4_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 4. CT coronary angiography demonstrating bridging of mid-left anterior descending coronary artery (arrow) on: A) 3D reconstruction; B) coronal section. Bridged segment is 5 mm at maximum depth and 3 cm in length.**

#### MRI Assessment

Cardiac MRI enables assessment of ventricular function, myocardial viability, and valvular function. However, assessing coronary anomalies themselves is challenging due to limited spatial resolution and data acquisition time. Navigated 3D whole heart magnetic resonance angiography may allow adequate visualisation, though ostial narrowing is difficult to comment on. Simple SSFP or black blood imaging usually shows origin and proximal course.

**Post-repair follow-up:** MRI is useful for evaluating baffles (Takeuchi technique) or direct reimplantation procedures — assessing baffle confluency, anastomotic site, and reimplantation to aortic root.

**Stress MRI:** With adenosine, helpful for detecting ischaemia in coronary anomalies and postoperative patients.

## 3. Atrial Septal Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Chest Radiograph

In adults with haemodynamically significant ASD: cardiac enlargement, right atrial dilatation, prominent central pulmonary arteries, and increased pulmonary vascular markings.

#### MRI (Figure 20C)

Indicated for:
*   Poor echocardiographic acoustic windows
*   Anatomical assessment of surrounding structures and associated anomalies
*   Non-invasive shunt quantification (phase-contrast MRI for systemic and pulmonary blood flow)

MRI also allows accurate assessment of ventricular volumes and function.

#### CT (Figure 5)

![](_page_5_Picture_8.jpeg)

**Figure 5. ECG-gated CT angiography demonstrating large secundum ASD (arrow), with left-to-right shunting causing contrast mixing, and dilated hypertrophied right ventricle with flattening of interventricular septum. Shunts should be suspected when dense contrast is seen in all heart chambers on CT angiography (unless technically intended).**

**Complementary role of CT:**
*   Assessment of associated anomalies (e.g., anomalous pulmonary venous return)
*   Pre-operative planning: vascular access, defect size/location/shape for device selection
*   Assessment of coronary arteries in older patients

**Incidental findings:** CT may incidentally diagnose ASD or PFO. Equal opacification of right and left heart chambers on CT may hinder diagnosis of significant shunt.

</div></details>

## 4. Ventricular Septal Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Chest Radiograph

Dilatation of central pulmonary arteries, increased pulmonary vascularity, increased cardiothoracic ratio.

#### MRI

*   Modality of choice for pulmonary and systemic blood flow measurement (Qp/Qs ratio — haemodynamic significance)
*   Gold standard for assessing LV function and size → helps decision-making for VSD closure
*   Late gadolinium MRI can localise scar in patients with post-repair arrhythmias for subsequent ablation

#### CT (Figures 6, 7)

![](_page_6_Picture_10.jpeg)

**Figure 6. ECG-gated CT angiography demonstrating: A) membranous VSD with contrast entering from LV to relatively normal-sized RV; B) significant pulmonary artery dilatation.**

![](_page_7_Picture_3.jpeg)

**Figure 7. ECG-gated CT angiography demonstrating membranous VSD (arrow): A) pre-operatively; B) following implantation of closure device.**

**Complementary role of CT:**
*   Type, location, size, and number of VSDs
*   Chamber sizes
*   Right or left ventricular outflow tract obstruction
*   Coexisting cardiac abnormalities (e.g., tetralogy of Fallot)
*   Assessment of coronary arteries and proximity of VSD to surrounding valves and atrioventricular conduction axis (surgical planning)

VSD is sometimes identified incidentally on CT.

## 12. Coarctation of the Aorta and Arch Hypoplasia</summary><div class="details-content">

Imaging defines the coarctation lesion and identifies coexisting abnormalities: bicuspid aortic valve (up to 85%), VSD, mitral valve abnormalities, intracranial aneurysms (Berry aneurysm of circle of Willis in 3–5%).

#### Chest Radiograph (Figure 22)

![](_page_23_Picture_6.jpeg)

**Figure 22. A) Chest radiograph and B) magnified inset in coarctation demonstrating subcostal bone erosion (rib notching) from intercostal artery dilatation.**

Rib notching (Roesler sign) from dilated intercostal collaterals eroding inferior rib margins. "Figure of 3" sign from pre- and post-stenotic dilatation.

#### MRI (Figure 23)

![](_page_24_Picture_3.jpeg)

**Figure 23. Volume-rendered contrast-enhanced MRA demonstrating: A) tight coarctation at junction of distal arch and descending aorta (arrow); B) dilated internal mammary and intercostal arteries bridging stenotic aorta on MIP reconstruction.**

**MRI advantages:**
*   Preferred modality (no radiation)
*   Assess severity, relationship to neighbouring structures, associated anomalies, shunt quantitation, collateral vessels
*   Detect complications: recoarctation, aneurysm formation

#### CT (Figure 24)

![](_page_25_Picture_3.jpeg)

**Figure 24. Imaging after endovascular coarctation repair: A) double oblique MPR CT angiography showing patent stent in distal arch position and dilated descending aorta; B) phase-contrast MRI showing peak velocity 1.4 m/s at stent site (arrow) — no significant residual coarctation. Asc Ao = ascending aorta; Desc Ao = descending aorta.**

**CT advantages:**
*   Preferred in stented coarctation (metallic artefacts limit MRI)
*   Children who cannot tolerate MRI
*   Defines site, morphology, arch hypoplasia, collateral circulation, associated anomalies
*   Post-intervention: residual stenosis, recoarctation, collateral formation, post-stenting complications (dissection, leaks, in-stent thrombus, stent migration/fracture)
*   Preferred for coronary artery evaluation

**Limitation:** CT does not provide functional data (gradient across coarctation).

## 13. Interrupted Aortic Arch</summary><div class="details-content">

Both CT and MRI can differentiate interrupted aortic arch with severe coarctation from aortic arch hypoplasia (may not be possible by echocardiography). CTA and CMR delineate anatomical features of different subtypes and assess associated anomalies (VSD, subaortic stenosis, other complex lesions). Multiplanar and volume-rendering reconstructions demonstrate anatomical configurations and relationships to facilitate surgical planning. CT and MRI have important roles in postoperative follow-up.

</div></details>

## 14. Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Prenatal

Ultrasound detects HLHS after week 22, when ventricular size difference becomes more apparent.

#### Postnatal

Echocardiography confirms diagnosis.

#### Post-operative

*   **CMR:** After Norwood procedure, determines suitability for Glenn and subsequent Fontan — precisely delineates ventricular size/function, pulmonary arteries, and neo-aorta
*   **CT:** Before and after Norwood — detects associated anomalies, sizing of neo-aorta, biventricular size, and pulmonary arteries

## 15. Proximal Aortic Pathology</summary><div class="details-content">

3D imaging should be incorporated with 2D echocardiography for detecting and monitoring aortic root and ascending aorta in connective tissue disorders (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, Loeys-Dietz) or anomalies (bicuspid aortic valve with concomitant aortic root dilatation).

*   **CTA:** Most precise technique for aortic diameter measurement
*   **MRI:** Preferred for follow-up (young population)
    *   Non-contrast 3D SSFP with multiplanar reconstruction for aortic root measurement
    *   SSFP cine images for measurement
    *   Cine images + flow velocity mapping for concomitant valve disease: mitral valve prolapse (Marfan), aortic valve regurgitation (bicuspid aortic valve)

</div></details>

## 16. Ebstein's Anomaly</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Chest Radiograph

Severe form: cardiomegaly, narrow aortic pedicle, decreased pulmonary vascular markings.

#### CT and MRI (Figure 25)

![](_page_27_Figure_5.jpeg)

**Figure 25. Radiological imaging of Ebstein's anomaly showing apical displacement of septal tricuspid leaflet (arrow), atrialised RV, severe RA enlargement on: A) axial CT; B,C) axial SSFP MRI; D) sail-like anterior tricuspid leaflet in four-chamber SSFP MRI.**

**Key findings:**
*   Apical displacement of septal or posterior tricuspid valve leaflet
*   Atrialised right ventricle
*   Severe right atrial enlargement
*   Associated anomalies: ASD, pulmonary valve stenosis, subaortic stenosis, aortic coarctation

#### MRI

*   Accurate quantitative measurements: RV size and function, tricuspid regurgitation severity
*   Shunt detection and haemodynamic significance (Qp:Qs ratio)
*   Late gadolinium enhancement: fibrotic changes in atrialised ventricle
*   Left ventricular non-compaction (LVNC) cardiomyopathy assessment (associated with Ebstein)
*   Post-operative surveillance: RV function, repaired (cone) or replaced tricuspid valve

#### CT

Helpful when:
*   Poor echocardiographic acoustic windows for RV visualisation
*   MRI challenging (lack of breath-hold capability)
*   RV function can be quantified with retrospective gating (increased radiation)

## 17. Imaging for Transcatheter Procedure Planning and Follow-up</summary><div class="details-content">

Echocardiography and CT identify patient suitability and feasibility for transcatheter cardiovascular procedures (Figure 26). Non-invasive imaging has significantly improved procedure safety:
*   Identification of correct device size
*   Avoiding injury to adjacent structures

![](_page_29_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 26. CT images used to plan transcatheter valve implantation for TOF patient with significant pulmonary homograft regurgitation. CT helps choose appropriate device size/type, measure homograft length/diameter, determine homograft location relative to coronary arteries, and map venous access (especially when venous stenosis/thrombosis suspected).**

**CT role:**
*   Evaluates adequacy and patency of arterial and venous access
*   Identifies related complications

**Infection assessment:** Echocardiography, CT, and PET identify device infection and its source.

## 18. Vascular Slings and Rings</summary><div class="details-content">

Patients often present with respiratory symptoms.

#### Initial Imaging

*   **Chest radiograph:** Tracheal narrowing, unilateral aeration
*   **Upper GI fluoroscopy** (dysphagia): Oesophageal indentation on lateral view
