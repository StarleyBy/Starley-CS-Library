# Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connections

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Definition and Spectrum
*   **Anomalous pulmonary venous connections:** Congenital defects of the pulmonary veins including PAPVC, TAPVC, atresia of the common pulmonary vein, cor triatriatum, and abnormal absorption of the common pulmonary vein.
*   **Partial anomalous pulmonary venous connection (PAPVC):** One or more (but not all) pulmonary veins drain into the right atrium or systemic veins.
*   **Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC):** All four pulmonary veins drain into the right atrium or systemic veins. Subtypes: supracardiac (most common), cardiac, infracardiac, mixed.
*   **Scimitar syndrome:** Right pulmonary vein draining into the IVC (often with right lung hypoplasia and dextrocardia).
*   **Cor triatriatum:** Accessory atrial chamber receiving pulmonary veins, communicating with the left atrium through a membrane.
*   **Pulmonary vein stenosis:** Congenital or acquired (postoperative) narrowing of individual pulmonary veins.

### Embryology
*   Common pulmonary vein arises from left atrial outpouching at 27–29 days, bifurcates twice to form four pulmonary veins.
*   Lung buds drain to splanchnic venous system; mesodermal plexus meets developing pulmonary veins at week 5.
*   Common pulmonary vein gradually incorporated into left atrial wall → all four veins enter posterior LA separately.

### Epidemiology and Associations
*   **PAPVC:** Incidence 0.6–0.7%, more common in females, occurs in 10% of patients with ASD. Associated with ASD (90% — usually sinus venosus type), heterotaxia, Scimitar syndrome.
*   **TAPVC:** 1.5% of congenital heart defects; infracardiac type 3× more common in males. Associated with ASD, PFO, PDA.
*   **Cor triatriatum:** Associated with TOF, DORV, aortic coarctation, VSD/AVSD, asplenia/polysplenia.

### Pathophysiology
*   **PAPVC:** Qp:Qs proportional to number of anomalous veins. Right heart dilatation → arrhythmias, RV failure, pulmonary hypertension.
*   **TAPVC:** All pulmonary venous return to RA → RV volume overload. Obstruction or restrictive ASD → pulmonary hypertension, decreased cardiac index.
*   **Cor triatriatum:** Pulmonary venous obstruction → pulmonary venous hypertension, reduced CO (may be compensated by ASD).
*   **Pulmonary vein stenosis:** Severity depends on number of veins involved. Bilateral arteriopathy can occur even with unilateral stenosis.

### Natural History
*   Uncorrected TAPVC with obstruction → death within first month of life.
*   Three or four stenotic pulmonary veins → mortality >80% (pulmonary hypertensive crisis, infection, haemoptysis).
*   PAPVC prognosis depends on shunt magnitude and sequelae.

### Diagnosis
*   **ECG:** May be normal; right atrial enlargement (tall P waves), RV hypertrophy (rSR pattern, right axis deviation), arrhythmias (AF/flutter, SVT).
*   **CXR:** Cardiomegaly, prominent hilum, widened mediastinum, pulmonary oedema, Scimitar sign (curvilinear opacity to IVC).
*   **MRI:** Broken ring sign (PAPVC — breach of mediastinal fat ring around SVC). Defines anatomy and haemodynamics.
*   **Echocardiography:** Diagnostic — identifies anomalous connections, PV dimensions, cor triatriatum membrane, associated defects. Colour Doppler: PV stenosis suggested by monophasic flow or velocity >1.6 m/s.
*   **Catheterisation:** Not routine for PAPVC. For TAPVC/cor triatriatum/stenosis — defines anatomy, pressures, wedge pressures.

### Pulmonary Vein Stenosis Score (Table 1)
| Score | Echo 2D | Mean Doppler gradient | Operative diameter |
|-------|---------|----------------------|--------------------|
| 0 | No stenosis | <2 mmHg, biphasic flow | Normal |
| 1 | Mild–moderate | <5–6 mmHg | 3–4 mm |
| 2 | Severe (pinpoint ostium) | >7 mmHg | <2 mm |
| 3 | Occlusion/atresia | None | No lumen |

### Therapeutic Options
*   **Medical (TAPVC with obstruction):** Sedation, hyperventilation (100% O₂, pCO₂ <30 mmHg), PGE₁, bicarbonate, haematocrit optimisation.
*   **Percutaneous:** Atrial septostomy (restrictive PFO), stenting of obstructed vertical vein, balloon dilatation of cor triatriatum membrane (limited success).
*   **Surgery:** Definitive treatment.

### Indications for Surgery
*   **PAPVC:** All patients (low morbidity/mortality); or Qp:Qs ≥2, or entire lung drains anomalously.
*   **TAPVC with obstruction:** Medical emergency — immediate surgery after optimisation.
*   **TAPVC without obstruction:** Elective surgery at 2–4 months.
*   **Contraindications:** Fixed pulmonary hypertension (PAPVC); ASD closure not suitable if pulmonary pressure >⅔ systemic.

### Structures at Risk During Surgery
*   Conduction system, sinoatrial node and its artery, left phrenic nerve.

### Surgical Techniques — PAPVC
*   **Baffle repair (double or single patch):** Glutaraldehyde-treated autologous pericardial patch redirects anomalous veins to LA and closes ASD.
*   **Warden operation:** SVC transected and reanastomosed to right atrial appendage; baffle created from anomalous veins to ASD and LA.

### Surgical Techniques — TAPVC
*   **Supracardiac:** Vertical vein ligated; incision in pulmonary venous confluence anastomosed to posterior LA (large, unobstructed anastomosis).
*   **Cardiac:** Unroof coronary sinus (between CS and fossa ovalis) → large ASD; patch reconstructs atrial septum.
*   **Infracardiac:** Similar to supracardiac; vertical/Y-shaped LA incision; some surgeons leave vertical vein unligated as low-pressure relief.
*   **Mixed:** Combination of above; sutureless technique (pericardium around PVs anastomosed to LA) may reduce postoperative stenosis.

### Surgical Techniques — Pulmonary Vein Stenosis
*   **Sutureless technique (atrial marsupialisation):** Complete resection of stenotic segments; atrial wall anastomosed directly to pericardium.
*   **Atrial pulmonary venoplasty, pericardial patch venoplasty, anastomotic revision.**
*   **Pneumonectomy:** For unilateral stenosis with haemoptysis or recurrent infection (not for CHF).

### Complications
*   Bleeding, thromboembolism, atrial arrhythmias, congestive heart failure, pulmonary hypertension and RV dysfunction, recurrent pulmonary vein stenosis, AV block (pacemaker), death.

### Long-Term Outcomes
*   **TAPVC:** Hospital mortality 2–20% (risk factors: poor pre-op status, obstruction, high PVR, young age, small LV, single ventricle). Pulmonary venous obstruction in up to 15% (usually at anastomosis). Bilateral occlusion → poor prognosis.
*   **PAPVC:** Excellent long-term prognosis. Early ASD closure eliminates arrhythmia risk. Diffuse stenosis rare.
*   **Scimitar PAPVC:** Better prognosis than PV stenosis; restenosis 3–10%. Survival depends on associated lung hypoplasia.
*   **Pulmonary vein stenosis:** High mortality; few surgical options.

## 1 What is an anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

Pulmonary venous anomalies represent congenital defects of the pulmonary veins and comprise a spectrum of abnormalities including:

*   **a)** partial anomalous pulmonary venous connection (PAPVC);
*   **b)** total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC);
*   **c)** atresia of the common pulmonary vein;
*   **d)** cor triatriatum;
*   **e)** abnormal absorption of the common pulmonary vein into the left atrium.

</div></details>

## 2 Describe the embryological formation of the pulmonary veins</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Common Pulmonary Vein</summary><div class="details-content">

The common pulmonary vein arises from an outpouching of the left atrium at **27–29 days' gestation**. It then bifurcates twice to produce four pulmonary veins, which grow towards the developing lungs.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Connection with Splanchnic System</summary><div class="details-content">

The lung buds develop from the foregut and drain to the **splanchnic venous system** of the embryo (Figure 1). A plexus of veins from the mesoderm envelop the bronchial buds and meet with the developing pulmonary veins to form a connection at the **fifth week of gestation**.

The incorporated pulmonary veins form the smooth posterior wall of the left atrium, whilst the trabeculated portion of the left atrium forms the more ventral aspect.

*   **a) Accessory atrial chamber receiving all pulmonary veins communicating with LA:**

    *   ii) other anomalous connections (connection to RA; TAPVC)
*   **b) Accessory atrial chamber receiving all pulmonary veins but NOT communicating with LA:**

    *   ii) supracardiac or infracardiac TAPVC
*   **c) Subtotal cor triatriatum:**
    *   i) accessory chamber receiving part of PVs connecting to LA (remaining PVs normal or anomalous — partial cor triatriatum with PAPVC)

## 4 What is the aetiology of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Primary anomalies of pulmonary venous connection** occur if the common pulmonary vein becomes **atretic** (partially or totally) whilst the pulmonary to venous connections persist → PAPVC or TAPVC respectively.
*   **Atresia of the common pulmonary vein** occurs if the common pulmonary vein fails to develop in the absence of pulmonary to systemic venous connections.
*   **Cor triatriatum** results from **stenosis of the common pulmonary vein**.
*   **Pulmonary vein stenosis** is the only pulmonary venous anomaly that may also be **acquired**, usually postoperatively.

</div></details>

*   Ventricular septal defect
*   Atresia of the aortic and pulmonary valves
*   Asplenia
*   Facial dysmorphia

</div></details>

*   Tetralogy of Fallot
*   Double-outlet right ventricle
*   Aortic coarctation
*   Ventricular or atrioventricular septal defect
*   Left-sided superior vena cava with coronary sinus atrial septal defect
*   Asplenia or polysplenia

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

*   Atrial or ventricular septal defect

## 6 What is the epidemiology of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Partial anomalous pulmonary venous connections:** Incidence **0.6–0.7%**, more common in females, occur in **10% of patients with an atrial septal defect**.
*   **Total anomalous pulmonary venous connections:** Occur in **1.5% of patients with congenital cardiac abnormalities**. The **infracardiac type is three times more common in males**.
*   Other pulmonary venous anomalies are rare.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 7 What is the pathophysiology of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Qp:Qs is predominantly determined by and proportional to the **number of anomalous pulmonary venous connections**.
*   **b)** The source of the anomalous pulmonary veins is also important — pulmonary blood flow is greatest to the middle and lower lobes in an upright patient, hence anomalous drainage to the RA will be greatest if arising from those lobes.
*   **c)** Any associated ASD may also exacerbate the left-to-right shunt.
*   **d)** Progressive right heart dilatation can eventually lead to **arrhythmias, right heart failure and pulmonary hypertension**.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Since all pulmonary venous return is to the right atrium, **right heart volume overload** occurs.
*   **b)** Right heart pressure increases if there is **pulmonary venous obstruction** or **restriction of flow across the atrial septum**. Increased pulmonary blood flow may result in pulmonary hypertension.
*   **c)** The **cardiac index** may also reduce due to decreased left-sided blood flow.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cor Triatriatum</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Pathophysiological changes depend on the extent of communication between the proximal and distal chambers of the left atrium.
*   **b)** Obstruction of pulmonary venous return results in **pulmonary venous hypertension and reduced cardiac output**. This can be compensated by an ASD allowing pulmonary venous return to the proximal chamber offloading into the RA (but this may result in right-sided volume overload).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Pulmonary Vein Stenosis</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** The extent of pulmonary venous hypertension depends on the **number of pulmonary veins involved** and the **degree of stenosis** (from discrete stenosis to generalised hypoplasia).
*   **b)** **Bilateral pulmonary hypertensive arteriopathy** may occur, even in the presence of unilateral pulmonary vein stenosis.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 8 What is the natural history of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Pulmonary venous anomalies may be an incidental finding following detection of a murmur. Prognosis is influenced by the sequelae of **arrhythmias, right heart failure and pulmonary hypertension**.
*   **Uncorrected total anomalous pulmonary venous connection with pulmonary venous obstruction** results in **death within the first month of life**.
*   Mortality of patients with **three or four stenotic pulmonary veins** may be over **80%** (compared with 0% in patients with only one or two stenotic veins), due to pulmonary hypertensive crisis, intercurrent pulmonary infection or haemoptysis.

</div></details>

## 9 What are the symptoms of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Symptoms depend on the extent of pulmonary venous involvement and the magnitude of the atrial level shunt.
*   **b)** Most patients are usually **asymptomatic**.
*   **c)** Dyspnoea and reduced exercise tolerance may occur in patients with right-sided volume overload.
*   **d)** Palpitations may occur due to arrhythmias associated with right atrial dilatation.
*   **e)** Haemoptysis may represent pulmonary vascular disease.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Patients with pulmonary venous obstruction develop **respiratory distress and cyanosis in early life**.
*   **b)** Patients without pulmonary venous obstruction may present with **failure to thrive, reduced exercise tolerance and recurrent respiratory infections**.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Atresia of the Common Pulmonary Vein</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Cyanosis, respiratory distress and symptoms of congestive cardiac failure.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cor Triatriatum</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Failure to thrive and respiratory distress.
*   **b)** Symptoms of pulmonary venous obstruction and palpitations (secondary to atrial enlargement-induced arrhythmias).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Pulmonary Vein Stenosis</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Symptoms depend on the number of veins involved and the degree of stenosis.
*   **b)** Respiratory distress and recurrent pneumonia may occur.
*   **c)** Patients may develop symptoms of pulmonary hypertension, pulmonary oedema and haemoptysis.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 10 What are the signs of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Clinical examination can be normal if only one pulmonary vein is involved.
*   **b)** **Parasternal heave** (secondary to RV and pulmonary artery dilatation).
*   **c)** **Ejection systolic murmur** over the pulmonary area (increased flow across pulmonary valve) and a **widely split 2nd heart sound** (usually with respiratory variation).
*   **d)** Soft **mid-diastolic murmur** at the lower left sternal edge (increased flow across tricuspid valve).
*   **e)** Signs of right-sided heart failure (raised JVP, peripheral oedema, hepatomegaly, ascites) and pulmonary hypertension (early diastolic murmur, disappearance of split S2, mid-diastolic rumble) may develop.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **With obstruction:** Cyanosis, respiratory distress, prominent cardiac impulse, increased P2. May have systolic murmur over pulmonary area and murmur of tricuspid regurgitation.
*   **Without obstruction:** RV heave, widely split S2, systolic murmur over pulmonary area, sometimes a diastolic tricuspid murmur.
*   Signs of pulmonary hypertension may also develop.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cor Triatriatum</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Signs of pulmonary venous obstruction and pulmonary hypertension.
*   RV heave and loud P2.
*   Early diastolic murmur of pulmonary regurgitation.
*   Soft continuous murmur due to flow across the membrane.
*   Poor weight gain and signs of low cardiac output (pallor, tachypnoea, weak peripheral pulses).

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Pulmonary Vein Stenosis</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Tachypnoea and signs of pneumonia.
*   Signs of pulmonary hypertension develop as the disease progresses.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 14 What are the cardiac catheterisation features of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **PAPVC:** Routine cardiac catheterisation is not necessary. If performed — normal right heart pressures in paediatric patients; higher oxygen saturations in RA compared to SVC (suggests anomalous pulmonary venous drainage to RA in absence of ASD).
*   **Selective right and left pulmonary artery angiography** delineates the anatomy of anomalous connections.
*   **Cor triatriatum:** Pulmonary hypertension without left-to-right shunt, high pulmonary artery wedge pressures, normal left atrial pressures. Pulmonary artery angiography reveals opacified pulmonary venous chamber draining into LA.
*   **Pulmonary vein stenosis:** No antegrade flow of contrast dye through veins; retrograde flow into arteries draining into less stenotic veins. Non-occluded veins visualised directly via transseptal catheterisation.

</div></details>

## 16 What are the therapeutic options for patients with anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Medical Management</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **TAPVC with pulmonary venous obstruction:** Sedation, ventilation/hyperventilation with 100% oxygen (pCO₂ <30 mmHg), prostaglandin E₁ (maintain PDA), aggressive correction of systemic acidosis with sodium bicarbonate, optimisation of haematocrit.
*   **TAPVC without pulmonary venous obstruction:** Mild inotropic support, diuresis, low levels of inspired oxygen.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Percutaneous Intervention</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Atrial septostomy** — for TAPVC with restrictive foramen ovale (if surgical repair delayed).
*   **Stenting of obstructed vertical vein** — prior to surgery for TAPVC.
*   **Balloon dilatation of cor triatriatum membrane** — reported but limited.
*   **Balloon dilatation or stenting of stenotic pulmonary veins** — usually results in restenosis; stenting may limit future surgical correction.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Surgery</summary><div class="details-content">

Surgery is the definitive treatment (see sections 20–22).

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 17 What are the indications for surgery for anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** All patients with PAPVC may be appropriate for surgical repair due to low associated morbidity and mortality.
*   **b)** Otherwise, once **Qp:Qs ≥2** or if **an entire lung drains anomalously**.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a) Obstructed TAPVC** is a **medical emergency** requiring surgical correction for definitive treatment.
*   **b)** Medical optimisation prior to surgery: intubation, hyperventilation with 100% oxygen, prostaglandin infusion, aggressive correction of acidosis and metabolic dysfunction, inotropic support.
*   **c) Elective surgery** for unobstructed TAPVC can be performed at **2–4 months of age**.

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 18 What are the contraindications for surgery for anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   There are **no specific contraindications** for repair of TAPVC.
*   The surgical risk in patients with PAPVC and **fixed pulmonary hypertension** may preclude surgery.
*   Closure of an ASD in patients with PAPVC may not be suitable where the **pulmonary pressure is more than two thirds of systemic pressure**.

</div></details>

## 21 What are the principles of surgery for a patient with total anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Supracardiac TAPVC</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Vertical vein usually ligated next to its connection to the systemic vein.
*   **b)** Access to pulmonary venous confluence: posterior pericardium (retract heart right/anteriorly); superior approach (between ascending aorta and SVC); or right pericardial fossa (vertical incision through RA, IAS, LA).
*   **c)** Incision created in pulmonary venous confluence to match corresponding incision in posterior LA (extending into left atrial appendage).
*   **d)** Pulmonary confluence anastomosed to LA with fine sutures to minimise intimal hyperplasia and restenosis.
*   **e)** Aim: **large, unobstructed anastomosis**.
*   **f)** Deep hypothermic arrest rarely necessary; short period of low-flow CPB may be desirable.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cardiac TAPVC</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Coronary sinus unroofed through an incision between the CS and fossa ovalis → large ASD.
*   **b)** Pulmonary vein ostia visualised through CS and its connection with the confluence.
*   **c)** Patch reconstructs atrial septum, leaving pulmonary venous drainage to flow through unroofed CS into LA.
*   **d)** When anomalous veins drain directly into RA, the IAS is detached posteriorly and reattached to the right of the right-sided pulmonary vein ostia.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Infracardiac TAPVC (Figure 18)</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Technique similar to supracardiac TAPVC.
*   **b)** Pulmonary venous confluence tends to be oriented vertically (typically Y-shaped) and is **extrapericardial**. LA incision is relatively vertical or Y-shaped.
*   **c)** Some surgeons do not ligate the vertical vein (provides low-pressure relief if LA is small and restrictive). Others ligate the connecting vein immediately above the diaphragm and use the intrapericardial portion to produce a larger anastomosis.

![](_page_23_Picture_6.jpeg)

**Figure 18. Surgical repair of an infracardiac total anomalous pulmonary venous connection.** IVC = inferior vena cava; VV = vertical vein; LV = left ventricle; LA = left atrium; RLPV = right lower pulmonary vein; LLPV = left lower pulmonary vein; LUPV = left upper pulmonary vein; RUPV = right upper pulmonary vein; LPA = left pulmonary artery; MPA = main pulmonary artery; RPA = right pulmonary artery; Ao = aorta; SVC = superior vena cava; PFO = patent foramen ovale.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Mixed TAPVC</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Surgical correction depends on exact anatomy and site of connections.
*   **b)** Combination of aforementioned procedures usually required.
*   **c)** **Sutureless technique** (Figure 19): pericardium around pulmonary veins anastomosed to LA, avoiding direct suture of pulmonary veins. May reduce incidence of postoperative pulmonary vein stenosis.

![](_page_24_Picture_6.jpeg)

</div></details>

## 23 What are the complications of surgery following repair of anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   Bleeding
*   Thromboembolism
*   Atrial arrhythmias
*   Congestive cardiac failure
*   Pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular dysfunction
*   Recurrent pulmonary vein stenosis
*   Atrioventricular block and requirement of a permanent pacemaker
*   Death

## 24 What are the long-term outcomes of surgery?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Hospital mortality ranges from **2–20%**.
*   **b)** Risk factors: poor pre-operative status, obstruction, high PVR, young age, small left ventricle, single-ventricle anatomy.
*   **c)** Long-term prognosis is favourable.
*   **d)** Pulmonary venous obstruction may arise in as many as **15%** of patients after repair (usually at anastomosis). Repeat surgery produces generally favourable results.
*   **e)** In some patients, this process may progress to almost complete vein occlusion.
*   **f)** When the lesion is bilateral, the prognosis is poor.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Long-term prognosis is **excellent**.
*   **b)** Early closure of ASD almost eliminates the risk of atrial arrhythmias.
*   **c)** Pre-operative arrhythmias beget persistent arrhythmias and mortality.
*   **d)** Patients should be followed up for evidence of stenosis at the site of pulmonary venous repair.
*   **e)** Diffuse stenosis described after repair of TAPVC is rare.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Scimitar Partial Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Long-term prognosis is better than pulmonary vein stenosis; successful repair offers excellent survival rates.
*   **b)** Survival depends on severity of associated lesions (lung hypoplasia, airways problems).
*   **c)** Restenosis rates are higher than in PAPVC, ranging from **3% to 10%**.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Pulmonary Vein Stenosis</summary><div class="details-content">

*   This is a **serious condition with high mortality** due to progression of pulmonary vein stenosis and pulmonary hypertension, and few surgical options.

</div></details>
