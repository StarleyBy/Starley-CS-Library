# Univentricular Heart

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Definition
- **Univentricular heart (functionally single ventricle):** Both atria connected entirely or almost entirely to a single functional ventricle
- Refers to **functional**, not anatomical condition (second rudimentary ventricle usually present; solitary ventricle exceedingly rare)
- ISNPCHD definition: "Spectrum of cardiovascular malformations where ventricular mass may not readily lend itself to partitioning that commits one ventricular pump to systemic circulation and another to pulmonary circulation"

### Common Causes (Figure 1)
- Hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS)
- Hypoplastic right heart (HRH)
- Tricuspid atresia (TA)
- Pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PA-IVS)
- Double-inlet left ventricle (DILV)
- Double-inlet right ventricle (DIRV)
- Unbalanced atrioventricular septal defect
- Heterotaxy syndrome
- Mitral atresia
- Solitary undetermined ventricle

### Epidemiology
- HLHS: ~2.5 per 10,000 live births; Tricuspid atresia: ~1 per 10,000 live births
- Proportion undergoing Fontan: **3.2%** of CHD
- Recurrence risk: **2–5%** with affected sibling or parent
- Congenital heart lesions in up to **33%** of siblings with HLHS

### Pathophysiology (Figure 2)
- Single ventricle supplies both systemic and pulmonary beds **in parallel** (not in series)
- Flow distribution depends on **vascular resistance** and anatomical restrictions
- **Low PVR:** Preferential flow to pulmonary bed → ↑ O₂ sat, ↑ lactate, ↓ coronary perfusion
- **High PVR:** Increased systemic flow → ↓ O₂ sat, ↓ lactate, ↑ myocardial perfusion
- **Unrestricted PBF → heart failure**
- **Restricted PBF → cyanosis**
- **Systemic outflow obstruction → shock after PDA closure (ductus-dependent)**

### Diagnostic Imaging
| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **CXR** | Assess pulmonary blood flow: oligaemic (limited) vs plethoric (unrestricted) (Figure 3) |
| **CT** | Anatomical delineation of vascular structures (arch, aberrant branching, distal PAs, PV connections) |
| **MRI** | Volume measurements (MV, AoV, LV sizes for biventricular repair candidacy) |
| **3D rapid heart modelling** | Hollow heart/blood pool models for complex anatomy and surgical planning (Figure 4) |

### Investigation Findings
- **ECG:** Varies by pathology; two distinct P waves (right atrial isomerism); no P waves (left atrial isomerism)
- **Echocardiography:** Most important tool — sequential segmental analysis, myocardial/valvar function, obstructive lesions
- **Cardiac catheterisation:** Essential for anatomical + haemodynamic info; routinely before Stage II and III to assess PAs, PA pressure, PVR (Figure 5)

### Management Principles (Figure 6)
- Foetal diagnosis → cardiology consultation + pregnancy planning
- Early complete illustration of all therapeutic options (including termination)
- Support/comfort care part of multidisciplinary approach
- **Surgical palliation** = strategy of choice in most centres
- Heart transplant: can be considered at any stage (primary therapy in some centres)

### Staged Surgical Palliation (Fontan circulation)
| Stage | Age | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage I** | Neonatal | Achieve balanced circulation: Norwood, BTS, RV-RA conduit, or PA banding |
| **Stage II** | 4–6 months | Bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt (BCPS) / Glenn / SVC to PA connection |
| **Stage III** | 3–4 years | Fontan / total cavopulmonary connection (TCPC) |

Some patients with balanced circulation may skip Stage I and go directly to Stage II.

### Stage I Palliation Objectives
- Unobstructed systemic blood flow from dominant ventricle
- Unobstructed pulmonary venous return
- Controlled PBF → balanced circulation

**Procedures (Figures 7–9):**
- **Increase PBF:** BTS (Figure 7), PDA stenting
- **Decrease PBF:** PA banding (Figure 8)
- **Hybrid:** PDA stenting + bilateral PA banding (Figure 9)
- **Systemic outflow obstruction:** DKS procedure ± arch repair
- **Pulmonary venous obstruction:** Address ASAP; atrial septostomy/septectomy for inadequate atrial communication

### Stage II Palliation (BCPS/Glenn) (Figure 10)
- Connect SVC to ipsilateral PA
- **Objective:** Unload ventricle, deliver in-series secure PBF
- Preceded by diagnostic catheterisation (PA pressure, PA size) — CT alternative if pressure data not needed
- Address associated problems: systemic AV valve regurgitation, PA stenosis

### Stage III Palliation (Fontan/TCPC) (Figure 11)
- **Aim:** Normalise volume/pressure work of single ventricle; systemic venous blood completely bypasses heart into lungs
- **Procedures:** Extracardiac conduit, lateral tunnel; may include fenestration between Fontan pathway and atrium

### Most Serious Long-term Complications After Fontan
- **Protein-losing enteropathy** and **plastic bronchitis** — poorest prognosis, high mortality (elevated venous pressure + lymphatic congestion)
- Other: arrhythmias, thromboembolism, hepatic dysfunction, worsening cyanosis, reduced exercise tolerance

### Long-term Outcomes
- **20-year freedom from death or transplantation after Fontan:** 60–85%
- **Prognostic factors:** Underlying anatomy, elevated atrial pressure, protein-losing enteropathy

### Heart Transplantation and VADs
- Primary transplantation decreasing worldwide
- **Transplantation option** for failing palliation (high-risk group; significant waiting list mortality)
- **Risk factors:** Pre-transplant comorbidities, pre-operative sensitisation, surgical complexity, prolonged ischaemic time, higher graft failure
- **5-year survival post-transplant:** ~70%
- VADs and total artificial hearts: promising, limited experience

### Pregnancy in Univentricular Heart
- Successful pregnancy reported (low maternal risk)
- **Must be planned early** with comprehensive risk stratification and multidisciplinary care
- **High-risk features:** Prior cardiovascular events, cyanosis, poor functional class, systemic outflow tract obstruction, systemic ventricle EF <40%

## 1. Definition and Common Causes</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 1. What is the definition of a univentricular heart?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Univentricular heart** is a term used to describe a wide range of congenital heart lesions where both atria are connected entirely or almost entirely to a **single functional ventricle**. It is also known as a single-ventricle heart or a functionally single ventricle.

**Key points:**
- In vast majority of cases, refers to a **functional**, rather than anatomical condition (a second rudimentary ventricle is usually present)
- Exceedingly rare to have only one (solitary) ventricle
- In some instances, two well-developed ventricles exist but cannot be rendered into a normal biventricular configuration (e.g., large VSD with straddling AV valves)

**ISNPCHD definition:** "A spectrum of cardiovascular malformations in which the ventricular mass may not readily lend itself to partitioning that commits one ventricular pump to the systemic circulation, and another to the pulmonary circulation."

</div></details>

- Tricuspid atresia (TA)
- Pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PA-IVS)
- Double-inlet left ventricle (DILV)
- Double-inlet right ventricle (DIRV)
- Unbalanced atrioventricular septal defect
- Heterotaxy syndrome

## 3. Pathophysiology</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 4. What is the pathophysiology of a univentricular heart (Figure 2)?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Key concept:** The single ventricle supplies both systemic and pulmonary beds **in parallel** (as opposed to in series in a normal two-ventricle setting).

Flow to either system depends on:
- Vascular resistance
- Presence/absence of anatomical restrictions

**Clinical consequences:**

- **Systemic outflow tract obstruction** → shock after PDA closure (ductus-dependent)

## 5. Management Principles</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 7. What are the principles of management in a patient with a univentricular heart (Figure 6)?</summary><div class="details-content">

- Diagnosis usually identified during foetal scanning → paediatric cardiology consultation + pregnancy planning
- Complete illustration of all therapeutic options as early as possible (including termination, according to parents' wishes)
- Support and comfort care (including end-of-life) part of modern multidisciplinary approach

**If postnatal intervention contemplated:**
- **Surgical palliation** = strategy of choice in most centres
- **Heart transplant:** Some centres offer as primary therapy; can be considered at any stage (e.g., with single ventricle failure)

## 6. Staged Surgical Palliation</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 8. What are the principles of surgical palliation in a patient with a univentricular heart?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Goal:** Construction of circulation where systemic venous blood flow reaches lungs by completely bypassing cardiac chambers (**Fontan circulation**)

**Three stages:**

| Stage | Age | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage I** | Neonatal | Achieve balanced circulation (Norwood, BTS, RV-RA conduit, or PA banding) |
| **Stage II** | 4–6 months | Bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt (BCPS) / Glenn procedure |
| **Stage III** | 3–4 years | Fontan procedure / total cavopulmonary connection (TCPC) |

**Note:** Some patients with balanced circulation may not require Stage I and can directly undergo Stage II during infancy.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 9. What are the principles of Stage I palliation in a patient with a univentricular heart (Figures 7–9)?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Timing:** Neonatal period

**Objectives:**
1. Unobstructed systemic blood flow from dominant ventricle
2. Unobstructed pulmonary venous return
3. Controlled pulmonary blood flow → balanced circulation

**Surgical procedures (Figures 7–9):**

| Goal | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Increase PBF** | BTS (Figure 7), PDA stenting |
| **Decrease PBF** | PA banding (Figure 8) |
| **Hybrid** | PDA stenting + bilateral PA banding (Figure 9) |

![](_page_8_Picture_4.jpeg)

**FIGURE 7.** Stage I for pulmonary atresia + hypoplastic RV: BTS with Gore-Tex® interposition graft between right subclavian artery and RPA. *Image courtesy of Ms. Norah Al-Otaibi.*

![](_page_9_Picture_2.jpeg)

**FIGURE 8.** Stage I for unbalanced AVSD + hypoplastic LV: PA banding. *Image courtesy of Ms. Norah Al-Otaibi.*

![](_page_9_Picture_4.jpeg)

**FIGURE 9.** Hybrid procedure: Banding of both PAs (white arrows) + PDA stenting (red arrow).

**Additional procedures:**
- **Systemic outflow obstruction:** DKS procedure ± arch repair (coarctation/arch hypoplasia)
- **Pulmonary venous obstruction:** Address ASAP (prevent venous hypertension-mediated lung injury)
- **Inadequate atrial communication + LA hypertension:** Atrial septostomy/septectomy

**Choice depends on underlying anatomy and resultant physiology:**
- Native pulmonary stenosis → may not need intervention for excessive PBF
- Unobstructed pathway → PA banding
- Limited PBF → PDA stenting or BTS

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 10. What are the principles of Stage II palliation in a patient with a univentricular heart (Figure 10)?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Timing:** 4–6 months of age

**Also known as:** Bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt (BCPS), Glenn procedure, superior cavopulmonary connection

**Procedure:** Connect SVC to ipsilateral pulmonary artery (Figure 10)

**Objective:** Unload the ventricle while delivering an **in-series secure source** of pulmonary blood flow

**Pre-operative assessment:**
- Routinely preceded by **diagnostic catheterisation** (PA pressure, PA size adequacy)
- CT scan alternative when pressure data not required

**Address associated problems during this stage:**
- Systemic atrioventricular valve regurgitation
- Pulmonary artery stenosis

![](_page_11_Picture_2.jpeg)

**FIGURE 10.** Stage II palliation: Bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt. *Image courtesy of Ms. Norah Al-Otaibi.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 11. What are the principles of Stage III palliation in a patient with a univentricular heart (Figure 11)?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Timing:** 3–4 years of age

**Also known as:** Fontan procedure, total cavopulmonary connection (TCPC)

**Aim:** Normalisation of volume and pressure work of single functional ventricle — systemic venous blood completely bypasses heart into lungs; ventricle pumps fully saturated blood to systemic circulation

**Procedures (Figure 11):**
- Extracardiac conduit
- Lateral tunnel procedure
- May include **fenestration** between Fontan pathway and atrium

## 8. Heart Transplantation, VADs, and Pregnancy</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 14. What is the role of heart transplantation or ventricular assist devices in the management of a univentricular heart?</summary><div class="details-content">

- Numbers of patients offered **primary transplantation** worldwide are **decreasing**

**Transplantation for failing palliation:**
- Option for patients with failing palliative strategy
- Constitutes **high-risk group** with significant mortality even on waiting list

**Major risk factors:**
- Pre-transplant comorbidities
- Pre-operative sensitisation
- Surgical complexity (central PA or arch reconstruction)
- Prolonged graft ischaemic times
- Higher incidence of graft failure

**5-year survival post-transplantation:** ~70%

**VADs and total artificial hearts:** Promising technical advances; limited experience in some centres

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 15. Is pregnancy possible in a patient with a univentricular heart?</summary><div class="details-content">

- Successful pregnancy after Stage III palliation reported in numerous patients with **low maternal risk**

**Critical requirements:**
- Must be **planned early**
- Comprehensive **risk stratification**
- **Multidisciplinary care**

**High-risk features:**
- Prior cardiovascular events
- Cyanosis
- Poor functional class
- Systemic outflow tract obstruction
- Systemic ventricle ejection fraction **<40%**

</div></details>
