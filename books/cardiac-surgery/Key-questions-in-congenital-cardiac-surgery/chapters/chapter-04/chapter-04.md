# Congenital Echocardiography

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Principles and Modalities

*   **Sequential segmental analysis:** Systematic approach to describe congenitally malformed hearts based on three morphological segments (atria, ventricles, arterial trunks) and their connections.
*   **Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE):** Handheld transducer on chest/abdomen; commonest modality.
*   **Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE):** Probe passed into oesophagus; detailed valve assessment, intra-operative imaging, guidance for transcatheter procedures.
*   **Epicardial echocardiography:** Probe placed directly on epicardium with chest open; intra-operative or on PICU.
*   **B-mode:** Live moving 2D image.
*   **M-mode:** Time-motion display; assesses LV function, fractional shortening, myocardial thickness.
*   **Doppler:** Assesses direction and velocity of blood flow; colour flow uses BART (Blue Away, Red Towards).
*   **3D echocardiography:** Demonstrates cardiac morphology, valves, septal structures; guides interventions.
*   **Z-score:** Number of standard deviations from the mean; used to normalise measurements for body surface area.

### Key Echocardiographic Views

| View | Structures Visualised |
|------|----------------------|
| **Abdominal short-axis (situs)** | Situs, IVC, aorta, hepatic veins |
| **Subcostal** | Atrial septum, AV valves, ventricular septum |
| **Apical four-chamber** | All four chambers, AV valves |
| **Apical five-chamber** | Additional aortic root |
| **Parasternal long-axis** | LVOT, aortic valve, mitral valve, aorta |
| **Parasternal short-axis** | RVOT, pulmonary valve, pulmonary arteries, aortic valve (en face) |
| **Suprasternal** | Aortic arch, PDA |
| **Left subclavicular ("crab view")** | Pulmonary veins |

### Atrial Septal Defects (ASD)

| Type | Characteristics |
|------|-----------------|
| **PFO** | Small communication (<4 mm) in central septum; bubble contrast may be needed |
| **Secundum** | Central septal communication; assess rims for device closure |
| **Primum** | Defect at junction of primum septum and AV valves; no offsetting of tricuspid/mitral valves |
| **Sinus venosus** | Proximity to SVC/pulmonary veins; high right parasternal view |
| **Coronary sinus** | Unroofing of coronary sinus |

### Ventricular Septal Defects (VSD)

| Type | Echocardiographic Features |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Perimembranous** | Fibrous continuity between aortic and tricuspid valves; parasternal short-axis |
| **Muscular** | Multiple; sweep entire septum with low Nyquist limit |
| **Doubly-committed subarterial** | Fibrous continuity between aortic and pulmonary valves; subcostal short-axis |

### Left-to-Right Shunt Assessment

| Defect | Dilated Chambers | Key Features |
|--------|------------------|--------------|
| **ASD** | RA, RV | Assess rims for device closure |
| **VSD** | LA, LV | Assess aortic valve regurgitation |
| **PDA** | LA, LV | Continuous L→R flow; estimate PA pressure |

### Obstructive Lesions

*   **Aortic stenosis:** Assess valve morphology, number of cusps, peak velocity (Doppler), gradient; best alignment: high right parasternal, apical five-chamber, subcostal short-axis.
*   **Coarctation:** Suprasternal view shows juxtaductal narrowing with shelf; CW Doppler shows high peak systolic velocity with diastolic tail; abdominal aortic PW shows low-velocity continuous flow with prolonged time to peak.
*   **Tetralogy of Fallot:** Dual-profile CW Doppler (fixed parabolic + dynamic dagger-shaped); assess coronary artery crossing RVOT (LAD from RCA in 3%).

### Cyanotic Congenital Heart Disease

| Condition | Key Echocardiographic Features |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| **TAPVD** | Supracardiac: vertical vein to innominate vein; Cardiac: coronary sinus dilation ("tail of whale"); Infracardiac: descending vein (most prone to obstruction) |
| **TGA** | Parallel great vessels ("gun-barrel"); aorta from RV, PA from LV |
| **ccTGA** | AV discordance + VA discordance; reverse offsetting of AV valves (tricuspid more apical on left) |
| **Truncus arteriosus** | Solitary vessel from base; classify by pulmonary artery origin |
| **DORV** | Aorta overrides septum >50%; aorto-mitral discontinuity |
| **PA-IVS** | Assess tricuspid valve Z-score, RV size, RV sinusoids (50%), coronary fistulae |
| **PA-VSD** | Hypoplastic branch PAs; MAPCAs; high parasternal views |
| **Tricuspid atresia** | Thick band at AV groove; absent right AV connection |
| **Double-inlet ventricle** | Dominant LV with rudimentary RV (DILV commonest) |
| **HLHS** | Hypoplastic LV, aortic/mitral atresia/stenosis, restrictive ASD, RV function (TAPSE, FAC) |
| **Ebstein's anomaly** | Apical displacement of septal/posterior tricuspid leaflets; atrialised RV; assess anterior leaflet (may cause RVOT obstruction) |
| **ALCAPA** | LCA from pulmonary artery; retrograde flow (steal), dilated RCA, endocardial fibroelastosis |

### Atrioventricular Septal Defect (AVSD)

*   **Complete AVSD:** Common AV valve at single level; no offsetting; assess Rastelli classification (subcostal short-axis en face).
*   **Associated lesions:** PDA, coarctation, secundum ASD, additional VSDs, tetralogy of Fallot.

### Isomerism

| Type | Echocardiographic Features |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Right isomerism** | Absent coronary sinus, TAPVD, AVSD, DORV with PS/PA, bilateral SVC, left-handed topology |
| **Left isomerism** | Interrupted IVC with azygos continuation, AVSD, bilateral SVC |

## 1. Principles and Modalities of Echocardiography</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Principles of Echocardiography for Congenital Heart Disease

Hearts with congenital malformation present with extraordinary variability of lesions. Moreover, these can undergo important modification over time, given the progression of the disease and the natural compensatory adaptation of the heart to the resulting physiological changes.

Echocardiography remains the mainstay of diagnosis, pre-operative work-up, intra-operative assessment of correction and medical follow-up.

It is therefore of paramount importance that a **systematic approach** to scanning congenitally malformed hearts is adopted. This fundamental approach uses **sequential segmental analysis**, which is applied to the description of all congenitally malformed hearts. It relies on the recognition of the three morphological cardiac segments (atria, ventricles and arterial trunks), and how the segments are connected to each other. Whenever possible, this principle should be adopted in all scans, although focused interrogations are used in specific circumstances.

#### Types of Echocardiography

| Modality | Description | Uses |
|----------|-------------|------|
| **Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE)** | Handheld transducer containing piezoelectric crystal applied to chest/abdomen | Commonest type; routine diagnostic imaging |
| **Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE)** | Specialised probe passed into oesophagus and stomach; adult, paediatric, and neonatal sizes | Detailed valve morphology (pre- and intra-op), cardiac function, intracardiac air post-bypass, guide transcatheter procedures |
| **Epicardial echocardiography** | Smallest TTE probe in sterile sleeve placed directly on epicardium with chest open | Intra-operative or on PICU when chest open |

#### Modes of Two-Dimensional Echocardiography

**B-mode echocardiography:** Multiple ultrasound reflections processed to produce a live moving 2D image of the structure of interest.

**M-mode echocardiography (Figure 1):** A single chosen ultrasound line displayed as a time-motion display of that specific region. Most often used to assess left ventricular function (fractional shortening) and myocardial thickness.

![](_page_2_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 1. M-mode image of the left ventricle taken from a parasternal short-axis view. IVSd = interventricular septum in diastole; LVIDd = left ventricular internal diameter in diastole; LVPWd = left ventricular posterior wall in diastole; IVSs = interventricular septum in systole; LVIDs = left ventricular internal diameter in systole; LVPWs = left ventricular posterior wall in systole.**

**Doppler echocardiography:** Utilises the Doppler effect to assess direction and velocity of blood flow. Colour flow Doppler uses **BART nomenclature (Blue Away, Red Towards)** — blue = flow away from transducer, red = flow towards transducer (Figure 2). Fastest velocities appear cyan (away) or yellow (towards). Waveform display shows flow towards transducer above baseline, away below baseline. Blood flow velocity (m/s) estimates pressure gradients (mmHg).

![](_page_2_Figure_4.jpeg)

**Figure 2. Colour flow Doppler map. Slow velocity away from the probe appears blue and towards is red. The fastest velocity away from the probe appears cyan and towards the probe is yellow.**

#### Three-Dimensional Imaging

Modern machines use specialised probes and software to obtain 3D echocardiographic images. Commonly used to demonstrate cardiac morphology, heart valves, septal structures or complex lesions to aid interventional or surgical planning. 3D functional assessment is challenging for dilated ventricles or functionally single ventricles; interpretation with caution. Live 3D echo guides interventions (e.g., device closure of septal defects).

#### Z-Score

The **Z-score** is the number of standard deviations (SD) of a measurement from the mean. A measurement 2 SD above the mean has Z-score of +2 (97.7th percentile); Z-score of –2 indicates 2 SD below the mean (2.3rd percentile). Measurements of cardiac structures are normalised with Z-scores relative to body surface area.

## 3. Atrial Septal Defects</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) (Figure 8)

Small communication in central portion of atrial septum on subcostal view, usually <4 mm. Blood flow left→right appears red on TTE (colours reversed on TOE). May require bubble contrast echocardiography in adults.

![](_page_6_Figure_5.jpeg)

**Figure 8. Subcostal images demonstrating a: A) patent foramen ovale (PFO, red arrow); and B) left-to-right blood flow on the corresponding colour flow Doppler image. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; RV = right ventricle; LV = left ventricle.**

#### Secundum ASD (Figure 9)

Communication in central portion of atrial septum on subcostal view. For large defects, assess rims in multiple views to determine suitability for transcatheter device closure.

![](_page_7_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 9. Subcostal images with colour flow Doppler demonstrating a: A) secundum atrial septal defect (blue arrow); and B) fenestrated atrial septal defect with two defects (white crosses). RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium.**

#### Primum ASD (Figure 10)

Defect at junction of primum atrial septum and atrioventricular valves on subcostal and apical views. **Absence of usual offsetting** of tricuspid and mitral valves — both at same level.

![](_page_7_Figure_4.jpeg)

**Figure 10. Subcostal view demonstrating a: A) primum atrial septal defect (green asterisk); and B) left-to-right blood flow across the defect (white arrow) on the corresponding colour flow Doppler image. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; RV = right ventricle; LV = left ventricle.**

#### Sinus Venosus Defect

Defect in close proximity to pulmonary veins and superior vena cava on high right parasternal view.

#### Coronary Sinus Defect

Defect secondary to unroofing of coronary sinus tissue on subcostal view.

#### Associated Findings and Rim Assessment

Right atrial and right ventricular dilatation from volume overload best seen on apical four-chamber view (inadequate for ASD size — beam perpendicular causes dropout and overestimation).

**Rims assessed on multiple views:**
*   Subcostal four-chamber: rim towards SVC junction and AV valves
*   Subcostal short-axis: SVC and IVC junctions with RA
*   Parasternal short-axis: anterior-posterior relationship from aortic root, especially aortic rim
*   High right parasternal (probe in superior-inferior orientation): sinus venosus defect

## 4. Patent Ductus Arteriosus</summary><div class="details-content">

Echocardiographic assessment best from **suprasternal notch and left subclavicular views**.

**Key features to assess (Figure 11):**
*   Course and size of PDA
*   Velocity and direction of shunt (Doppler, colour flow)
*   Estimation of pulmonary artery pressure using peak systolic velocity + simultaneous non-invasive systolic BP
*   Left atrial and ventricular size (dilated with significant PDA) and LV function
*   Arch-sidedness and branching pattern of head/neck vessels
*   Presence of coarctation or hypoplasia
*   Branch pulmonary artery stenosis

![](_page_9_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 11. A) 2D image of a parasternal short-axis view showing a dilated main pulmonary artery (MPA) dividing into right and left pulmonary arteries (RPA; LPA) and a patent ductus arteriosus (PDA). B) Corresponding colour image showing red flow in the PDA passing from the aorta into the pulmonary artery. C) Doppler showing continuous left-to-right flow in the PDA.**

## 5. Ventricular Septal Defects</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Perimembranous VSD (Figure 12)

Best assessed in short-axis view where fibrous continuity between aortic and tricuspid valves is noted. Delineate any muscular extension and margins by sweeps of septum in parasternal and apical views. Assess for aortic valve incompetence.

![](_page_10_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 12. Echocardiographic images demonstrating a large perimembranous ventricular septal defect (\*) on: A) apical four-chamber view; B) corresponding colour flow Doppler image; C) parasternal short-axis view (green arrow = VSD, red arrow = aortic-tricuspid valve continuity); and D) corresponding colour flow Doppler image. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; RV = right ventricle; LV = left ventricle. Ao = aorta; PA = pulmonary artery; TV = tricuspid valve.**

#### Muscular VSD (Figure 13)

Best assessed by short-axis sweep of ventricular septum and posterior-to-anterior sweep in apical view. Assess whole septum with low Nyquist limit. May be small, large, or multiple.

![](_page_11_Figure_2.jpeg)

**Figure 13. Echocardiographic images demonstrating multiple muscular ventricular septal defects (arrows) on the: A) apical four-chamber view; and B) corresponding colour flow Doppler image. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; RV = right ventricle; LV = left ventricle.**

#### Doubly-Committed Subarterial VSD (Figure 14)

Best assessed in subcostal short-axis view where fibrous continuity between aortic and pulmonary valves is seen. Assess for aortic or pulmonary valve incompetence.

![](_page_11_Figure_4.jpeg)

**Figure 14. Echocardiographic image demonstrating a doubly-committed subarterial ventricular septal defect (\*) with evidence of continuity (red line) between the aortic and pulmonary valves. Ao = aorta; PA = pulmonary artery.**

#### Pre-operative Assessment of VSD

*   Whole interventricular septum to identify all defects
*   Degree of ventricular volume overload and function
*   Septal attachment of tricuspid valve and evidence of straddling
*   Aortic valve for regurgitation
*   RV outflow tract muscle bundles

#### Postoperative Assessment Following VSD Repair

*   Repair integrity; patch leak
*   Left ventricular function
*   Tricuspid valve function; regurgitation
*   Doppler gradient across pulmonary artery if banded

## 7. Mitral Valve Disease</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Views for Mitral Valve Assessment

**TTE views:** Parasternal long-axis, apical four-chamber (Figure 18), apical two-chamber, subcostal.

![](_page_15_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 18. Apical four-chamber view demonstrating turbulent colour flow through a severely stenotic mitral valve, an enlarged left atrium and bowing of the interatrial septum into the right atrium. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium.**

#### Papillary Muscles

Praecordial short-axis views show number and arrangement of papillary muscles (normally at 3 o'clock and 8 o'clock). Absence, hypoplasia, or fusion (parachute mitral valve) may be demonstrated.

#### Specific Mitral Valve Abnormalities

| Abnormality | Echocardiographic Features |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Isolated cleft** | Subcostal/parasternal short-axis; cleft "points" towards outflow tract; assess for outflow tract obstruction (chordal attachments to septum) |
| **Arcade mitral valve** | Short-axis view |
| **Double-orifice mitral valve** | Short-axis view |
| **Supramitral ring** | Subtle findings; ring may be fused with valve leaflets; additional valvular/subvalvular pathology present |

#### Important Considerations

*   In isolated mitral regurgitation, **exclude anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary artery (ALCAPA)**
*   Colour flow and continuous wave Doppler assess severity of regurgitation/stenosis
*   **3D echocardiography** demonstrates mitral valve leaflets and subvalvular apparatus; can display surgical view

## 8. Cor Triatriatum</summary><div class="details-content">

Cor triatriatum is separation of blood flow between pulmonary veins and mitral valve due to membranous partition within left atrium. Best seen in apical or subcostal four-chamber views (Figure 19).

![](_page_16_Figure_9.jpeg)

**Figure 19. Subcostal four-chamber view demonstrating: A) a cor triatriatum membrane (green arrow) above the level of the mitral valve leaflets (white arrows); with B) a narrow jet of flow passing through the membrane on colour Doppler. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; LV = left ventricle.**

**Assessment:**
*   Position of membrane and any restriction to blood flow
*   Atrial septum most often intact (ASD may be present in some cases)
*   Communicating orifice within membrane allows varying degrees of blood flow

## 9. Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Drainage</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Normal Pulmonary Veins (Figure 20)

Best assessed in left subclavicular view with reduced Nyquist limit — characteristic "crab view" (legs = pulmonary veins, claws = right SVC and left atrial appendage).

![](_page_17_Picture_6.jpeg)

**Figure 20. Left subclavicular ('crab') view demonstrating the pulmonary veins joining the left atrium. LUPV = left upper pulmonary vein; RUPV = right upper pulmonary vein; LLPV = left lower pulmonary vein; RLPV = right lower pulmonary vein.**

#### Supracardiac TAPVC (Figure 21)

Pulmonary venous confluence drains via vertical vein superiorly into left innominate vein, SVC, or azygos vein, then right atrium. Left subclavicular view assesses venous confluence; suprasternal view assesses connections via vertical vein and innominate vein.

![](_page_18_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 21. Suprasternal view demonstrating supracardiac total anomalous pulmonary venous connection with vertical vein (VV) draining confluence of right (RPVs) and left pulmonary veins (LPVs). Blood ascends to innominate vein then SVC. Ao = aorta; PA = pulmonary artery.**

#### Cardiac TAPVC (Figure 22)

Pulmonary veins join confluence to coronary sinus, then drain to right atrium. Subcostal view shows pulmonary veins draining into severely dilated coronary sinus with characteristic **"tail of the whale"** appearance. Associated ASD with obligate right-to-left shunt (blue flow from RA to LA).

![](_page_19_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 22. Subcostal view demonstrating: A) intracardiac total anomalous pulmonary venous connection with RPVs and LPVs draining into coronary sinus (CS), creating "tail of whale" appearance (outlined in green); and B) small left atrium (LA) filled by blood from right atrium (RA) via secundum ASD (blue flow with arrow). LV = left ventricle; RV = right ventricle.**

#### Infracardiac TAPVC (Figure 23)

Pulmonary venous confluence drains via vertical descending vein inferiorly to portal vein or hepatic veins, then IVC and RA. **Most prone to obstruction** — requires emergency surgery.

![](_page_19_Figure_5.jpeg)

**Figure 23. Echocardiographic images demonstrating: A) infracardiac total anomalous pulmonary venous connection with descending vein (DV, outlined in green) from high left chest; and B) continuous high-velocity flow due to obstruction on Doppler in descending vein.**

**Critical:** In all cases of TAPVC, carefully rule out obstruction — indication for immediate surgical intervention.

## 11. Tetralogy of Fallot</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Key Views (Figure 26)

**Subcostal anterior oblique view** demonstrates subpulmonary infundibulum, pulmonary valve, main pulmonary artery, and ventricular septum. Excellent alignment for Doppler assessment of RVOT gradient.

![](_page_22_Figure_6.jpeg)

**Figure 26. Echocardiographic images of tetralogy of Fallot: A) subcostal right anterior oblique view showing VSD (\*) from anterocephalad deviation of outlet septum (OS); B) RVOT obstruction (turbulent yellow/cyan flow) on colour Doppler; C) parasternal long-axis view showing aorta overriding VSD (red arrow). LPA = left pulmonary artery; RPA = right pulmonary artery; PT = pulmonary trunk; LA = left atrium; RA = right atrium; RV = right ventricle; TV = tricuspid valve; Ao = aorta.**

#### Doppler Assessment (Figure 27)

Dual-profile waveform:
*   **Smooth ellipse** = fixed obstruction at pulmonary valve level
*   **Triangular dagger-shape** = dynamic obstruction from muscular infundibulum

![](_page_23_Figure_3.jpeg)

**Figure 27. Continuous wave Doppler across RVOT demonstrating peak gradient >100 mm Hg with dual-profile waveform (fixed parabolic + dynamic dagger-shaped).**

#### Critical Pre-operative Assessment

**Coronary artery crossing RVOT** alters surgical approach. LAD from RCA in **3%** of cases. Best imaged in parasternal short-axis view. If coronary anatomy unclear, CT scan indicated.

#### Postoperative Assessment by Repair Type

| Repair Type | Findings |
|-------------|----------|
| **Valve-sparing** | Unobstructed forward flow; limited pulmonary regurgitation |
| **Transannular patch** | Unobstructed forward flow; free pulmonary regurgitation |
| **RV-PA conduit** | Unobstructed forward flow; good branch PA flow; obstruction may develop over time (increased flow velocity) |

## 12. Pulmonary Atresia with Intact Ventricular Septum</summary><div class="details-content">

Two distinct types: **hypoplastic RV** (common) or **dilated RV** (less common).

#### PA-IVS with Hypoplastic RV — Assessment

| Structure | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Tricuspid valve annulus** | Measured in apical view; Z-score quantification vs. BSA |
| **Tricuspid regurgitation** | Colour Doppler in same view |
| **RV size** | Apical and parasternal short-axis views; compare to LV; assess RV hypertrophy, endocardial fibroelastosis (bright myocardium) |
| **RV sinusoids** | Communications between RV cavity and coronary arteries (50% of cases); apical and short-axis windows with colour Doppler |
| **RV-PA luminal discontinuity** | Lack of forward flow on colour Doppler of RVOT; subcostal short-axis/oblique, parasternal long/short-axis views |
| **Pulmonary valve atresia** | Seen in almost all patients |
| **Main and branch PA size** | Usually normal; parasternal short-axis and high parasternal views |

#### PA-IVS with Dilated RV — Assessment

| Structure | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Tricuspid regurgitation severity** | Apical view with colour Doppler |
| **RA and RV dilatation** | Multiple views (apical, long-axis, short-axis) |
| **Pulmonary valve atresia** | Membranous atresia usually seen |
| **Main and branch PA size** | Usually normal |

</div></details>

## 13. Pulmonary Atresia with Ventricular Septal Defect</summary><div class="details-content">

In pulmonary atresia with VSD, branch pulmonary arteries are commonly **hypoplastic** (unlike PA-IVS where they are normal). Branch PAs are usually supplied by **multiple major aortopulmonary collateral arteries (MAPCAs)** rather than a single duct.

**Echocardiographic assessment:** High parasternal short-axis and suprasternal notch views (Figure 28). When multiple collaterals present, **CT** is useful to delineate vessel course and guide surgical planning.

![](_page_25_Picture_7.jpeg)

## 14. Transposition of the Great Arteries</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Diagnostic Features

*   Pulmonary artery arises from LV and bifurcates into RPA and LPA
*   Aorta arises from RV with branching of head/neck vessels
*   Classic view: **parasternal long-axis showing parallel great vessels ("gun-barrel appearance")** (Figure 29)

![](_page_26_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 29. Parasternal long-axis view demonstrating transposition of the great arteries with parallel great vessels: pulmonary artery (PA) from left ventricle (LV), aorta (Ao) from right ventricle (RV). LA = left atrium.**

*   Parasternal short-axis: both aortic and pulmonary valves seen en face; aorta typically anterior and rightward (side-by-side possible)

#### Pre-operative Assessment

| Feature | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| **Interatrial communication** | Size and flow velocity |
| **VSD** | Presence and location |
| **Coronary artery anatomy** | Parasternal short-axis, apical, subcostal views; Leiden convention: LCA from sinus 1, RCA from sinus 2 (commonest). Intramural course = higher intra-operative risk |
| **Outflow tract obstruction** | Presence |
| **PDA** | Evidence, size, flow pattern |

#### Postoperative Assessment After Arterial Switch

| Feature | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| **Myocardial function** | Key indicator of coronary artery function |
| **Neo-aortic root** | Dilatation; neo-aortic valve regurgitation |
| **RVOT and branch PAs** | Stenosis; branch PAs seen in suprasternal view after Le Compte manoeuvre |

## 15. Coarctation of the Aorta</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Echocardiographic Views (Figure 30)

| View | Findings |
|------|----------|
| **Suprasternal long-axis with ductal cuts** | Localised discrete juxtaductal narrowing with shelf from posterior wall just distal to left subclavian artery; long segment or arch hypoplasia possible. ![](_page_28_Figure_2.jpeg)

![](_page_29_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Clues Suggestive of Neonatal Coarctation

*   High left atrial pressure
*   Disproportionate ventricles (smaller LV, right heart dilatation)
*   Mitral stenosis, aortic stenosis, or LV hypertrophy
*   Impaired LV systolic function
*   Increased distance between left subclavian and left common carotid arteries (>50% greater than distance between innominate and left common carotid arteries)

## 16. Truncus Arteriosus</summary><div class="details-content">

Truncus arteriosus arises as solitary arterial vessel from base of heart, giving rise to aorta, pulmonary arteries, and coronary arteries. Best seen in subcostal and apical five-chamber views (Figure 32).

![](_page_30_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 32. Echocardiography images demonstrating Type I truncus arteriosus: A) branching of truncus into aorta and pulmonary artery, dysplastic truncal valve (arrow); B) truncal valve regurgitation (arrow) and forward flow into branch pulmonary arteries. Ao = aorta; PA = pulmonary artery; TA = truncus arteriosus; RV = right ventricle; LV = left ventricle; RPA = right pulmonary artery; LPA = left pulmonary artery.**

#### Echocardiographic Assessment

| Feature | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| **VSD** | Size and location |
| **Truncal valve** | Structure, number of leaflets (parasternal short-axis), degree of regurgitation |
| **Type** | Type I most common (50–65%) |
| **Coronary arteries** | Variation; single coronary artery frequent |

**Important:** Distinguish truncus arteriosus from aortopulmonary window or aortic atresia with hypoplastic ascending aorta.

## 17. Congenitally Corrected Transposition of the Great Arteries</summary><div class="details-content">

ccTGA = atrioventricular (AV) discordance + ventriculo-arterial (VA) discordance.

#### Echocardiographic Features (Figure 33)

*   Ventricles switched: morphological RV on left, morphological LV on right
*   Morphological RV identified by attachment to tricuspid valve (offset apically — more inferior attachment on interventricular septum than mitral valve) — **reverse offsetting**

![](_page_31_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 33. Echocardiographic images of congenitally corrected transposition (ccTGA): A) pre-operative: reverse offsetting of mitral (green arrow) and tricuspid valves (blue arrow) — tricuspid more inferior on left, VSD (\*); B) post-double-switch: left atrial baffle (green asterisk) to right-sided mitral valve and morphological LV; right atrial baffle (blue asterisk) to morphological RV on left; C) colour Doppler post-double-switch: pulmonary venous blood through baffle to right side.**

#### Coronary Artery Anatomy

Coronary arteries inverted: morphological LCA from right-sided sinus, morphological RCA from left-sided sinus. Common variations: single sinus origin of both main coronary arteries, or LCA from RCA. CT/MRI/angiography may be required.

#### Associated Anomalies

| Anomaly | Features |
|---------|----------|
| **VSD** | Usually perimembranous outflow subpulmonary defect; with large VSD + inlet extension, normal reverse offsetting may be absent |
| **LV outflow tract obstruction** | Subpulmonary or pulmonary valve level stenosis |
| **Tricuspid valve abnormalities** | Frequently Ebstein's anomaly; seen on left-sided morphological RV in apical four-chamber view |

#### Long-Term Follow-up Assessment

| Feature | Importance |
|---------|------------|
| **Systemic RV function** | Most patients develop progressive RV dysfunction (as early as first decade) |
| **PA band gradient** | In patients who underwent PA banding to train morphological LV; assess branch PA distortion |
| **Biventricular function** | Guide need for resynchronisation therapy |
| **Tricuspid valve function** | Degree of regurgitation; need for replacement |

</div></details>

## 19. Vascular Rings and Isomerism</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Vascular Rings (Figure 36)

Diagnosed with suprasternal echocardiographic views; often confirmed by CT or MRI.

![](_page_34_Picture_4.jpeg)

Vascular rings can be diagnosed by foetal sonographers (e.g., right aortic arch with aberrant left subclavian artery and left ductus arteriosus).

#### Isomerism

Isomerism refers to **atrial appendages**, not entire atria. Situs inferred from arrangement of aorta and IVC (or azygos/hemiazygos) on subcostal view (Figure 37).

![](_page_35_Figure_2.jpeg)

![](_page_35_Figure_3.jpeg)

|------|---------------------------|

## 20. Tricuspid Atresia and Double-Inlet Ventricle</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Tricuspid Atresia (Figure 38)

Anatomically: imperforate valve or absent connection. Echocardiographically: thick band of atrioventricular groove separating atrium from ventricle. Associated with rudimentary RV; left atrium connected to dominant LV. Ventriculo-arterial connections may be concordant or discordant.

![](_page_36_Picture_6.jpeg)

**Figure 38. Apical four-chamber view demonstrating tricuspid atresia: A) thick band between right atrium (RA) and rudimentary right ventricle (RV) — absent right connection; B) flow across open mitral valve but no flow across absent right connection on colour Doppler. LV = left ventricle; LA = left atrium.**

#### Double-Inlet Ventricle (Figure 39)

Echocardiography uses relationship of rudimentary ventricle to main ventricle (not trabecular pattern) to distinguish three types:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Double-inlet LV (DILV)** — commonest | Rudimentary anterosuperior RV on left or right of main ventricle |
| **Double-inlet RV** | Rudimentary posteroinferior LV |
| **Double-inlet to solitary, indeterminate ventricle** | Rare |

**Holmes heart:** DILV with rudimentary RV and ventriculo-arterial concordance.

![](_page_37_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 39. Apical view (adult orientation with apex up) demonstrating double-inlet to dominant left ventricle (LV). TV = tricuspid valve; MV = mitral valve.**

## 23. Anomalous Left Coronary Artery from Pulmonary Artery</summary><div class="details-content">

Anomalous left coronary artery typically arises from left inferolateral aspect of main pulmonary artery just beyond pulmonary valve. Best seen in parasternal short-axis view (Figure 42). Courses toward interventricular groove and branches into LAD and LCX.

![](_page_40_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 42. Parasternal short-axis view demonstrating: A) anomalous left coronary artery from pulmonary artery (ALCAPA); B) ALCAPA from pulmonary artery (PA) dividing into left anterior descending (LAD) and left circumflex (LCX) arteries on colour Doppler. Ao = aorta.**

**Key findings:**
*   Retrograde flow from left coronary artery to main pulmonary artery — **steal phenomenon**
*   Right coronary artery often dilated
*   Endocardial fibroelastosis (bright LV myocardium, especially papillary muscles)
*   Left heart dilation with functional mitral regurgitation (apical four-chamber view)

## 24. Ebstein's Anomaly</summary><div class="details-content">

Best seen on apical (Figure 43) and subcostal four-chamber views.

![](_page_41_Figure_8.jpeg)

**Figure 43. Apical four-chamber view of Ebstein's anomaly demonstrating: A) inferior displacement of septal tricuspid leaflet (green arrow) compared to normally positioned anterior tricuspid leaflet (red arrow) and septal mitral leaflet (blue arrow) — creating atrialised RV (aRV) while functional RV (fRV) is small; and B) severe tricuspid regurgitation on colour Doppler. RA = right atrium; LA = left atrium; LV = left ventricle.**

#### Key Features

*   **Inferior displacement** of proximal attachments of septal and posterior tricuspid valve leaflets
*   **Rightward and anterior rotation** → atrialisation of proximal RV → reduced functional RV size + tricuspid regurgitation
*   **Secundum ASD** — frequent association
*   **Non-compaction of LV** — known association

#### Outflow Views (Subcostal Right Anterior Oblique, Parasternal Short-Axis)

*   Anterior tricuspid leaflet often redundant and curtain-like — may cause RV outflow obstruction
*   Presence of anatomical or functional pulmonary atresia

#### Echocardiographic Score (Neonates)

Ratio of combined area of right atrium + atrialised RV to functional RV + left heart in four-chamber view at end-diastole — prognostic implications.

</div></details>
