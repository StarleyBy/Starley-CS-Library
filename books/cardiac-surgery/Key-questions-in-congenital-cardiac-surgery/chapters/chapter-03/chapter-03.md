## 1. Antithrombotic Agents</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Aspirin

**Class of drug:** Cyclo-oxygenase inhibitor.

**Mechanism of action:** Aspirin irreversibly inhibits thromboxane A₂ production in platelets via acetylation of cyclo-oxygenase. Inhibition of thromboxane A₂ production reduces the adhesiveness of platelets.

**Indications:**
*   Prophylaxis against clot formation after cardiac surgery
*   Prophylaxis of stroke in children with high risk
*   Kawasaki syndrome

**Cautions:**
*   Asthma, peptic ulcer disease, renal impairment
*   Concomitant use of NSAIDs or anticoagulants (warfarin)

**Side effects:**
*   Gastrointestinal effects: nausea, vomiting, bleeding, epigastric distress
*   Bronchospasm, respiratory depression, hyperthermia (toxic doses)
*   Hypersensitivity
*   Reye syndrome (children <16 years) — fulminant hepatitis with cerebral oedema

**Dose (oral):**
*   Neonate: 1–5 mg/kg once daily
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 1–5 mg/kg once daily (usual maximum 75 mg)
*   Child (12–18 years): 75 mg once daily

---

#### Clopidogrel

**Class of drug:** Platelet ADP (adenosine diphosphate) receptor antagonist.

**Mechanism of action:** Clopidogrel irreversibly modifies the platelet ADP receptor, thereby directly inhibiting the binding of ADP and subsequent ADP-mediated activation of the glycoprotein IIb/IIIa complex. Platelets exposed to clopidogrel are ineffective for the remainder of their lifetime (5–7 days).

**Indications:**
*   Primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease (CAPRIE trial)
*   Dual therapy with aspirin for acute coronary syndrome (CURE trial)
*   Long-term anti-thrombotic prophylaxis for drug-eluting stents

**Cautions:** Active bleeding.

**Side effects:** Haemorrhage, neutropenia.

**Dose:**
*   Child (0–24 months): 0.2 mg/kg/day (maximum 75 mg per dose)
*   Child (2–17 years): 0.2–1 mg/kg/day (maximum 75 mg per dose)

---

#### Heparin

**Mechanism of action:** Heparin is a mucopolysaccharide that:
*   Inactivates activated factor X
*   Inhibits conversion of prothrombin to thrombin
*   Prevents fibrin formation from fibrinogen

**Indications:**
*   Treatment and prophylaxis of DVT and PE
*   Unstable angina
*   Anticoagulation during cardiopulmonary bypass
*   Temporary anticoagulation for prosthetic valve patients

**Cautions:** Active bleeding.

**Side effects:** Haemorrhage, hyperkalaemia, osteoporosis, thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity.

**Dose:**
*   **Prophylactic:** 100 units/kg BD subcutaneously (max 5000 U per dose)
*   **Therapeutic:**
    *   Neonate (up to 35 weeks corrected GA): 50 units/kg load, then 25 units/kg/hr IV
    *   Neonate (term): 75 units/kg load, then 25 units/kg/hr IV
    *   Child (1–11 months): 75 units/kg load, then 25 units/kg/hr IV
    *   Child (1–17 years): 75 units/kg load, then 20 units/kg/hr IV
*   **Full cardiopulmonary bypass:** 300 units/kg bolus
*   **Minimal extracorporeal circulation (MECC):** 200 units/kg

**Reversal:** Protamine sulphate.

---

#### Protamine

**Mechanism of action:** Protamine antagonises the anticoagulant effects of heparin.

**Indications:** Reversal of overdose with intravenous unfractionated or low-molecular-weight heparin.

**Cautions:**
*   Excessive doses can have an anticoagulant effect
*   Increased risk of allergic reaction (previous protamine or protamine insulin, fish allergy, infertile adolescent males)

**Side effects:** Hypersensitivity (angioedema, anaphylaxis), dyspnoea (pulmonary oedema), flushing, bradycardia, hypotension.

**Dose (child 1 month–18 years):** By IV injection (rate ≤5 mg/min) to neutralise each 100 units of unfractionated heparin:
*   <30 minutes since overdose: 1 mg
*   30–60 minutes: 500–750 μg
*   60–120 minutes: 375–500 μg
*   >120 minutes: 250–375 μg
*   Maximum: 50 mg

---

#### Low-Molecular-Weight Heparins (LMWH)

**Mechanism of action:** Similar to heparin, with differences:
*   Longer half-life
*   No monitoring required (APTT unaffected)
*   Greater anti-factor Xa activity
*   Lower incidence of thrombocytopenia and osteoporosis

**Indications:** Acute coronary syndrome (FRISC, TIMI IIB trials), DVT prophylaxis.

**Cautions:** Active bleeding.

**Side effects:** Haemorrhage, hyperkalaemia, osteoporosis, thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity.

**Dalteparin (Fragmin®):**
*   **Therapeutic:**
    *   Neonate: 100 units/kg BD
    *   Child (1 month–11 years): 100 units/kg BD
    *   Child (12–17 years): 200 units/kg OD (maximum 18,000 units)
*   **Prophylactic:**
    *   Neonate: 100 units/kg OD
    *   Child (1 month–11 years): 100 units/kg OD
    *   Child (12–17 years): 2500–5000 units OD

**Enoxaparin (Clexane®):**
*   **Therapeutic:**
    *   Neonate: 1.5–2 mg/kg BD
    *   Child (1–2 months): 1.5 mg/kg BD
    *   Child (2 months–17 years): 1 mg/kg BD
*   **Prophylactic:**
    *   Neonate: 750 μg/kg BD
    *   Child (1–2 months): 750 μg/kg BD
    *   Child (2 months–17 years): 500 μg/kg BD (maximum 40 mg/day)

---

#### Warfarin

**Mechanism of action:** Coumarin derivative that interferes with vitamin K metabolism. Vitamin K is a cofactor in hepatic production of coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X.

**Indications:** Prophylaxis and treatment of thromboembolism (DVT, PE, AF, mechanical prosthetic valves, LV thrombus, TIA).

**Cautions:** Hepatic impairment, peptic ulcer disease; interacts with amiodarone, antibiotics (rifampicin), anticonvulsants, NSAIDs, statins.

**Side effects:** Haemorrhage.

**Dose (induction):**
*   **Neonate:** 200 μg/kg on day 1, then 100 μg/kg once daily for 3 days; subsequent doses adjusted to INR. Omit dose if INR >3.5.
*   **Child:** 200 μg/kg (max 10 mg) on day 1, then 100 μg/kg (max 5 mg) once daily for 3 days; subsequent doses adjusted to INR. Omit dose if INR >3.5.

**Reversal:** Vitamin K, fresh frozen plasma, or Beriplex® (human prothrombin complex concentrate).

---

#### Vitamin K

**Mechanism of action:** Cofactor in hepatic production of coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X.

**Indications:** Bleeding due to vitamin K deficiency (neonates/infants), warfarin-induced bleeding.

**Cautions:** Allergic reactions, risk of vascular collapse (give slowly).

**Side effects:** Allergic reactions, haemolytic anaemia, cytotoxicity in liver cells.

**Dose:**
*   **Neonatal hypoprothrombinaemia/vitamin K deficiency bleeding:** 1 mg IV, repeated 8-hourly if necessary
*   **Reversal of coumarin anticoagulation (continued anticoagulation required or no significant bleeding, child 1 month–18 years):** 15–30 μg/kg (max 1 mg) single IV dose, repeated as necessary
*   **Reversal of coumarin anticoagulation (continued anticoagulation not required or significant bleeding; treatment of vitamin K deficiency haemorrhage, child 1 month–18 years):** 250–300 μg/kg (max 10 mg) single IV dose

---

#### Tranexamic Acid

**Class of drug:** Lysine analogue antifibrinolytic agent.

**Mechanism of action:** Binds to plasminogen, thereby inhibiting fibrinolysis.

**Indications:** Prophylaxis to reduce bleeding and use of blood products following cardiac surgery; massive haemoptysis; haemophiliacs.

**Cautions:** Renal impairment, disseminated intravascular coagulation, thromboembolic disease.

**Side effects:** Renal impairment, graft patency concerns.

**Dose:**
*   Loading: 30 mg/kg over 5 minutes (max 2 g) IV
*   Maintenance: 5–10 mg/kg/hr IV infusion
*   Added to CPB prime: 30 mg/kg

## 2. Inotropes and Vasopressors</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Dopamine

**Mechanism of action:** Activates α- and β-adrenergic receptors (dose-dependent):
*   Very high doses: α₁ activation → vasoconstriction
*   Moderate doses: β₁ stimulation → inotropic and chronotropic effects (in older children; in neonates, moderate doses reduce CO)
*   Low doses: Dopaminergic receptor activation → renal and splanchnic vasodilation

**Indications:** Cardiogenic shock, septic shock.

**Cautions:** Tachyarrhythmias, neonatal pulmonary hypertension, phaeochromocytoma, MAO inhibitors.

**Side effects:** Tachycardia, vasoconstriction, hypotension (rarely hypertension), dyspnoea.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 3–20 μg/kg/min IV infusion
*   Child (1 month–18 years): 5–20 μg/kg/min IV infusion

---

#### Dobutamine

**Mechanism of action:** Direct-acting catecholamine; β₁ receptor agonist (inotropic, chronotropic).

**Indications:** Inotropic support after cardiac surgery, increased cardiac output in acute heart failure.

**Cautions:** Tachyarrhythmias, phaeochromocytoma, marked obstruction of cardiac ejection (e.g., HOCM).

**Side effects:** Tachyarrhythmias, bronchospasm, nausea, hypertension.

**Dose:** Initially 5 μg/kg/min IV infusion, adjusted to 2–20 μg/kg/min.

---

#### Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

**Mechanism of action:**
*   β₁: Positive inotropic and chronotropic effects
*   β₂ (low dose): Systemic vasodilation, bronchodilation
*   α (high dose): Vasoconstriction

**Indications:** Low cardiac output syndrome, CPR, anaphylaxis, acute hypotension, bradycardia (unresponsive to atropine).

**Cautions:** Obstructive cardiomyopathy, aortic stenosis, occlusive vascular disease, arrhythmias, hypertension, phaeochromocytoma, glaucoma, diabetes mellitus.

**Side effects:** Tachycardia, arrhythmias, hypertension, metabolic acidosis, CNS disturbances (anxiety, fear, headache, tremor).

**Dose:** Initially 100 ng/kg/min IV infusion, titrate up to 1.5 μg/kg/min.

---

#### Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)

**Mechanism of action:**
*   Potent α₁ agonist: intense vasoconstriction → ↑peripheral resistance
*   β₁ agonist: ↑contractility and heart rate (minimal in vivo; α effects predominate at therapeutic doses)

**Indications:** Shock with low SVR (sepsis, vasodilation, anaphylaxis), low CO following cardiac surgery.

**Cautions:** Peripheral vascular thrombosis, hyperthyroidism, diabetes mellitus, extravasation (necrosis), angle-closure glaucoma, uncorrected hypovolaemia.

**Side effects:** Hypertension, tachyarrhythmias, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, tremor, psychosis.

**Dose:** 20–100 ng/kg/min IV infusion, titrate to response (max 1 μg/kg/min).

---

#### Vasopressin

**Mechanism of action:**
*   V₂ receptor (kidney): ↑water permeability and reabsorption (ADH effect)
*   V₁ receptor (vascular smooth muscle): Vasoconstriction

**Indications:** Diabetes insipidus, bleeding from oesophageal varices or colonic diverticula.

**Cautions:** Coronary artery disease, epilepsy, asthma, heart failure, conditions aggravated by water retention.

**Side effects:** Vascular disease, chronic nephritis, fluid retention, peripheral ischaemia, hypersensitivity.

**Dose (acute massive GI haemorrhage, child 1 month–18 years):**
*   Initially: 0.3 units/kg (max 20 units) over 20–30 minutes
*   Then: 0.3 units/kg/hour IV infusion, titrate to response (max 1 unit/kg/hour)

---

#### Phenylephrine

**Mechanism of action:** Selective α₁-adrenergic receptor agonist → systemic vasoconstriction.

**Indications:** Acute hypotension; maintain systemic BP on cardiopulmonary bypass.

**Cautions:** Severe hyperthyroidism.

**Side effects:** Hypertension, peripheral ischaemia, headache.

**Dose:** 500 μg–1 mg IV bolus during CPB.

---

#### Levosimendan

**Class of drug:** Calcium sensitizer.

**Mechanism of action:** Increases myocyte sensitivity to calcium → ↑contractility without increasing intracellular calcium.

**Indications:** Severe congestive heart failure.

**Cautions:** Hepatic and renal impairment.

**Side effects:** Dysrhythmias, hypokalaemia, myocardial ischaemia.

**Dose:** 0.05–0.2 μg/kg/min IV infusion.

---

#### Enoximone

**Class of drug:** Phosphodiesterase type 3 inhibitor.

**Mechanism of action:** Inodilator: ↑intracellular cAMP → ↓SVR and PVR + moderate positive inotropic effects.

**Indications:** Low CO following cardiac surgery, especially with pulmonary hypertension or RV failure.

**Cautions:** Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, stenotic/obstructive valvular disease, aortic stenosis, hypotension.

**Side effects:** Tachyarrhythmias, hypotension, thrombocytopenia, ectopic beats, VT, SVT.

**Dose:**
*   Loading: 500 μg/kg slow IV injection
*   Maintenance: 5–20 μg/kg/min IV infusion over 24 hours (max 24 mg/kg/24 hr)

---

#### Nitric Oxide

**Mechanism of action:** Potent and selective pulmonary vasodilator; relaxes pulmonary artery smooth muscle via cGMP.

**Indications:** Persistent neonatal pulmonary hypertension, other forms of pulmonary arterial hypertension.

**Cautions:** Monitor methaemoglobin, especially in neonates.

**Side effects:** Haemorrhage risk (inhibits platelet aggregation), methaemoglobinaemia.

**Dose:** 20 ppm inhaled.

## 3. Pulmonary Vasodilators</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Sildenafil

**Class of drug:** Phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor.

**Mechanism of action:** Inhibits cGMP-specific PDE5, preventing cGMP degradation → pulmonary arterial relaxation.

**Indications:** Pulmonary arterial hypertension.

**Cautions:** Hepatic/renal impairment, concurrent nitric oxide donors, hypotension, hereditary retinal disorders, LV outflow obstruction, pulmonary veno-occlusive disease.

**Side effects:** Headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, colour vision disturbances, photophobia.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate–1 year: 250–500 μg/kg every 4–8 hours (max 30 mg/day)
*   Child (1–18 years):
    *   Body weight <20 kg: 10 mg TDS
    *   Body weight >20 kg: 20 mg TDS

---

#### Bosentan

**Class of drug:** Endothelin-1 competitive antagonist.

**Mechanism of action:** Competitively binds to endothelin-1 receptor → prevents vasoconstriction.

**Indications:** Primary pulmonary hypertension.

**Cautions:** Hypotension, hepatotoxic (monitor liver enzymes), contraindicated in acute porphyria.

**Side effects:** Liver failure, peripheral oedema, pulmonary oedema, anaemia.

**Dose (oral, 2–8 years):**
*   Body weight 10–20 kg: 31.25 mg OD for 4 weeks, then BD
*   Body weight 20–40 kg: 31.25 mg OD for 4 weeks, then 62.5 mg BD

## 4. Antiarrhythmics</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Amiodarone

**Class:** III antiarrhythmic.

**Mechanism of action:** Increases action potential duration and refractory period; blocks outward K⁺ current.

**Indications:** Supraventricular and ventricular tachyarrhythmias.

**Cautions:** Hepatic impairment, thyroid dysfunction, hypokalaemia, bradycardia; interacts with digoxin and warfarin.

**Side effects:** Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis, GI intolerance, hypotension (IV), photosensitivity, slate-grey skin, thyroid dysfunction, neuropathy, hepatic dysfunction, muscle weakness, tremor, ataxia.

**Dose:**
*   **Oral:**
    *   Neonate: 5–10 mg/kg BD for 7–10 days, then 5–10 mg/kg OD
    *   Child (1 month–12 years): 5–10 mg/kg (max 200 mg) BD for 7–10 days, then 5–10 mg/kg (max 200 mg) OD
    *   Child (12–18 years): 200 mg TDS for 1 week, then 200 mg BD for 1 week, then 200 mg OD
*   **IV infusion:**
    *   Neonate: 5 mg/kg over 30 minutes, then 5 mg/kg over 30 minutes every 12–24 hours
    *   Child (1 month–18 years): 5–10 mg/kg over 20 min–2 hours, then 300 μg/kg/hr continuous infusion (max 1.2 g/24 hr)

---

#### Sotalol

**Class:** III antiarrhythmic + non-selective β-blocker.

**Mechanism of action:** Blocks outward K⁺ current (prolongs QT) + β-blockade.

**Indications:** Arrhythmias, prevention of recurrence.

**Cautions:** Heart block, poor LV function, asthma, peripheral vascular disease.

**Side effects:** QT prolongation (torsade de pointes), bradycardia, heart block, postural hypotension.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 1 mg/kg BD, increase every 3–4 days to max 4 mg/kg BD
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 1 mg/kg BD, increase every 2–3 days to max 4 mg/kg BD (max 80 mg BD)
*   Child (12–18 years): 80 mg OD or 40 mg BD, increase to 80–160 mg BD; 480–640 mg daily for life-threatening VT

---

#### Flecainide

**Class:** Ic antiarrhythmic.

**Mechanism of action:** Blocks Na⁺ channels → slows phase 0 depolarisation → slows conduction in all cardiac tissues; reduces automaticity.

**Indications:** Paroxysmal AF/flutter, SVT, VT, resistant re-entry SVT, WPW syndrome.

**Cautions:** Negative inotropic effects may aggravate heart failure.

**Side effects:** Dizziness, blurred vision, headache, nausea; can aggravate pre-existing arrhythmias or induce life-threatening VT.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 2 mg/kg BD or TDS, adjust according to response and plasma concentration
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 2 mg/kg BD or TDS (max 8 mg/kg/day or 300 mg daily)
*   Child (12–18 years): Initially 50–100 mg BD; max 300 mg daily (400 mg for VT in heavily built children)

---

#### Lidocaine

**Class:** Ib antiarrhythmic.

**Mechanism of action:** Blocks Na⁺ channels → shortens phase 3 repolarisation in ventricular muscle. Little effect on atrial or AV junction arrhythmias.

**Indications:** Ventricular arrhythmias, digoxin toxicity.

**Cautions:** 2nd/3rd degree heart block, severe sinoatrial block, WPW, acute porphyria, severe myocardial depression.

**Side effects:** Bradycardia, arrhythmias, hypotension, CNS effects (drowsiness, slurred speech, paraesthesia, agitation, confusion, convulsions, respiratory depression).

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 0.5–1 mg load, then 0.6–3 mg/kg/hr infusion
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 0.5–1 mg load, then 0.6–3 mg/kg/hr infusion
*   Child (12–18 years): 50–100 mg load, then 120 mg over 30 min, then 240 mg over 2 hours, then 60 mg/hr; reduce if infusion >24 hr

---

#### Procainamide

**Class:** Ia antiarrhythmic.

**Mechanism of action:** Blocks Na⁺ channels → slows phase 0 depolarisation → widens QRS.

**Indications:** Supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias, WPW syndrome.

**Cautions:** Renal failure.

**Side effects:** Reversible lupus-like syndrome (chronic use), asystole, torsade de pointes, CNS effects (depression, hallucination, psychosis), hypersensitivity (fever, agranulocytosis).

**Dose:**
*   IM: 20 mg/kg/day divided into 4 doses (max 4 g/day)
*   IV: Loading 3–6 mg/kg over 5 minutes (max 15 mg/kg/dose); Maintenance 0.02–0.08 mg/kg/min (max 2 g/24 hr)

---

#### Adenosine

**Mechanism of action:** Naturally occurring nucleoside; at high doses reduces conduction velocity, prolongs refractory period, decreases automaticity in AV node.

**Indications:** Acute SVT termination.

**Cautions:** 2nd/3rd degree heart block, sick sinus syndrome, decompensated heart failure, long QT syndrome, severe hypotension.

**Side effects:** Arrhythmias (asystole, bradycardia), flushing, chest pain, hypotension, metallic taste, light-headedness.

**Dose (rapid IV push):**
*   Neonates: 150 μg/kg; repeat every 1–2 min increasing by 50–100 μg/kg until termination or max 300 μg/kg
*   Child (1 month–1 year): 150 μg/kg; repeat every 1–2 min increasing by 50–100 μg/kg until termination or max 500 μg/kg
*   Child (1–12 years): 100 μg/kg; repeat every 1–2 min increasing by 50–100 μg/kg until termination or max 500 μg/kg (12 mg)
*   Child (12–18 years): 3 mg initially; if necessary, 6 mg after 1–2 min, then 12 mg after further 1–2 min

---

#### Atropine

**Mechanism of action:** Competitive muscarinic acetylcholine receptor blocker:
*   Low dose: Blocks M₁ receptors → bradycardia
*   Higher dose: Blocks M₂ receptors on SA node → ↑HR

**Indications:** Bradycardia, anti-secretory premedication.

**Cautions:** Tachyarrhythmias.

**Side effects:** SVT/VT, dry mouth, blurred vision, dilated pupils, urinary retention, constipation, nausea, dizziness, restlessness, confusion, hyperthermia.

**Dose:**
*   **Bradycardia (IV):**
    *   Neonate: 10–20 μg/kg
    *   Child (1 month–12 years): 10–20 μg/kg
    *   Child (12–18 years): 300–600 μg (larger doses in emergency)
*   **Premedication:**
    *   Neonate: 10–20 μg/kg
    *   Child (1 month–18 years): 20–40 μg/kg (max 900 μg)

---

#### Calcium Gluconate

**Mechanism of action:** Increases threshold potential of cardiac myocytes, restores normal gradient with resting membrane potential (abnormally elevated in hyperkalaemia).

**Indications:** Cardioprotective agent in hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia.

**Cautions:** Rapid injections may cause vasodilation, cardiac arrhythmias, hypotension.

**Side effects:** Nausea, constipation; with rapid injection: cardiac arrhythmias, hypotension, bradycardia.

**Dose:**
*   Acute hypocalcaemia, hyperkalaemia (arrhythmia prevention): 0.11 mmol/kg (0.5 mL/kg of calcium gluconate 10%) as single dose

## 5. Beta-Blockers and ACE Inhibitors</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Beta-Blockers

**Mechanism of action:** β-adrenoreceptor blockers → negative inotropic and chronotropic effects → ↓myocardial O₂ demand; membrane stabilizing effect (antiarrhythmic).

**Indications:** Arrhythmias, hypertension, primary/secondary prevention of cardiovascular events, heart failure; esmolol/propranolol for cyanotic spells in tetralogy of Fallot.

**Cautions:** Heart block, poor LV function, asthma, peripheral vascular disease.

**Side effects:** Bradycardia, heart block, postural hypotension, peripheral vasoconstriction, bronchospasm.

**Atenolol (oral):**
*   Neonate: 0.5–2 mg/kg once daily (may be divided BD)
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 0.5–2 mg/kg once daily (may be divided BD)
*   Child (12–18 years): 25–50 mg once daily (may be divided BD); arrhythmias: 50–100 mg BD

**Carvedilol (oral, heart failure):**
*   Child (2–18 years): Initially 50 μg/kg (max 3.125 mg) BD, double dose every ≥2 weeks up to 350 μg/kg (max 25 mg) BD

**Esmolol (tetralogy spells):**
*   Neonate: 600 μg/kg IV over 1–2 min, then 300–900 μg/kg/min IV infusion

---

#### Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (Enalapril)

**Mechanism of action:** Inhibits conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II; ↓bradykinin inactivation → vasodilation.

**Indications:** Hypertension, severe LV dysfunction, secondary prevention of cardiovascular events.

**Cautions:** Renal impairment, renal artery stenosis, severe aortic stenosis, concurrent K⁺-sparing diuretics; children <20 kg not licensed for hypertension.

**Side effects:** Postural hypotension, renal insufficiency, hyperkalaemia, persistent dry cough, angioedema.

**Dose (enalapril oral):**
*   Neonate: 10 μg/kg once daily, increase up to 500 μg/kg daily in 1–3 divided doses
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 100 μg/kg once daily, increase up to 1 mg/kg daily (OD or BD)
*   Child (12–18 years): 2.5 mg once daily; maintenance 10–20 mg daily (OD or BD); max 40 mg daily if >50 kg

</div></details>

## 6. Diuretics</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Furosemide

**Mechanism of action:** Inhibits Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in ascending limb of loop of Henle → inhibits reabsorption of sodium, potassium, chloride, and water.

**Indications:** Pulmonary oedema, heart failure, hyperkalaemia, peripheral oedema, oliguria with renal impairment, hypercalcaemia.

**Cautions:** Hypovolaemia, hypotension, hepatorenal syndrome; some liquid preparations contain alcohol (caution in neonates).

**Side effects:** Electrolyte disturbances (hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia), pancreatitis, hepatic encephalopathy, postural hypotension, bone marrow suppression, visual disturbances.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 0.5–2 mg/kg every 12–24 hours orally; 0.5–1 mg/kg every 12–24 hours IV
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 0.5–2 mg/kg BD/TDS orally (max 12 mg/kg/day); 0.5–1 mg/kg IV repeated every 8 hours as needed
*   Child (12–18 years): 20–40 mg OD orally (increase to 80–120 mg in resistant oedema); 20–40 mg TDS IV
*   Continuous infusion (1 month–18 years): 0.1–2 mg/kg/hour; post-cardiac surgery: 100 μg/kg/hour initially, double every 2 hours until urine output >1 mL/kg/hr

---

#### Spironolactone

**Mechanism of action:** Aldosterone antagonist → inhibits aldosterone-mediated Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion in distal tubule → promotes Na⁺ and water loss, preserves K⁺.

**Indications:** Oedema and ascites (cardiac failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome), Conn's syndrome, prevention of hypokalaemia.

**Cautions:** Potassium supplements, concurrent ACEI, acute porphyria, Addison's disease.

**Side effects:** Electrolyte disturbances (hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia), postural hypotension, acute renal failure, hepatotoxicity, agranulocytosis.

**Dose:**
*   Neonate: 1–2 mg/kg daily in 1–2 divided doses (up to 7 mg/kg in resistant ascites)
*   Child (1 month–12 years): 1–3 mg/kg daily in 1–2 divided doses (up to 9 mg/kg daily in resistant ascites)
*   Child (12–18 years): 50–100 mg daily in 1–2 divided doses (up to 9 mg/kg daily, max 400 mg daily)

</div></details>

## 7. Ductus Arteriosus Modulators</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Alprostadil (Prostaglandin E₁)

**Class of drug:** Prostaglandin E₁ (PGE₁).

**Mechanism of action:** Vasodilation of the ductus arteriosus.

**Indications:** Maintain patency of the ductus arteriosus.

**Cautions:** History of haemorrhage, weakening of duct wall following prolonged usage.

**Side effects:** Apnoea (be prepared for intubation), DIC, hypokalaemia.

**Dose:** 5–100 ng/kg/min IV infusion.

---

#### Indomethacin

**Mechanism of action:** NSAID; reversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase → anti-inflammatory, analgesic, anti-pyretic.

**Indications:** Closure of PDA in neonates, juvenile arthritis, pericarditis.

**Cautions:** Concurrent warfarin or other NSAIDs, liver/renal impairment, thrombocytopenia, coagulation defects.

**Side effects:** GI bleeding, ulceration, perforation.

**Dose:**
*   **Symptomatic PDA (IV over 20–30 min):** Neonate: 100–200 μg/kg single dose, then 100 μg/kg at 12–24 hour intervals for 2–3 doses
*   **Pain relief/anti-inflammatory (1 month–18 years):** 0.5 mg/kg twice daily; higher doses under specialist supervision

## 9. Anaesthetic and Sedative Agents</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Propofol

**Mechanism of action:** Short-acting IV sedative/hypnotic; potentiates GABA_A receptor activity, Na⁺ channel blocker. Induction within 30–40 seconds.

**Indications:** Induction and maintenance of anaesthesia, sedation of ventilated patients.

**Cautions:** Respiratory depression (with benzodiazepines), hypotension.

**Side effects:** Pain at injection site, vasodilation (hypotension), transient apnoea, dystonia, propofol infusion syndrome, CNS depression, occasional excitatory phenomena.

**Dose:**
*   **Induction (0.5% or 1% injection):**
    *   Child (1 month–17 years): 2.5–4 mg/kg slow IV
    *   Child (17–18 years): 1.5–2.5 mg/kg at 20–40 mg every 10 seconds until response
*   **Maintenance (1% injection continuous IV):**
    *   Child (1 month–17 years): 9–15 mg/kg/hr
    *   Child (17–18 years): 4–12 mg/kg/hr

---

#### Midazolam

**Class of drug:** Benzodiazepine (rapid onset).

**Mechanism of action:** Enhances affinity of GABA for its receptor.

**Indications:** Comfort during stressful procedures, sedation during ventilation, control of agitation and seizures.

**Cautions:** When used with other sedatives.

**Side effects:** In neonates: respiratory depression, hypotension, ↓cerebral blood flow; myoclonus, paradoxical agitation.

**Dose:**
*   Sedation: 50–400 μg/kg/hr IV infusion
*   Ventilation: 200 μg/kg bolus, then 60 μg/kg/hr infusion

---

#### Sevoflurane

**Class of drug:** Volatile anaesthetic.

**Mechanism of action:** Interferes with neurotransmitter release/reuptake at postsynaptic terminals; alters ionic conductance.

**Indications:** Induction and maintenance of anaesthesia.

**Cautions:** Drowsiness, fever, hypothermia; uncommonly: asthma, AV block, confusion.

**Dose (inhalation):**
*   **Induction:**
    *   Neonate: up to 4%, using calibrated vaporiser
    *   Child: 0.5–1% initially, increase gradually up to 8%
*   **Maintenance:**
    *   Neonate: 0.5–2%
    *   Child: 0.5–3%

---

#### Opiates (Morphine, Codeine)

**Mechanism of action:** Act on µ-opioid receptors in CNS → analgesia, sedation.

**Indications:** Severe pain.

**Cautions:** Respiratory depression, urinary retention, bowel obstruction, ileus, biliary colic (relative contraindications).

**Side effects:** Respiratory depression, constipation, addiction, tolerance, withdrawal syndrome.

**Morphine (Oramorph® immediate release):**
*   Child (1 month–11 years): 200 μg/kg every 4–6 hours (max 5 mg/dose)
*   Child (12–17 years): 5 mg every 4–6 hours

**Morphine PCA:**
*   Neonate/infant <5 kg: bolus 10 μg/kg, lockout 20 min
*   Child <50 kg: bolus 10–20 μg/kg, background 0–5 μg/kg/hr, lockout 5–10 min
*   Child >50 kg: 1 mg demand bolus, background 0–0.2 mg/hr, lockout 5–10 min

**Codeine (child 12–17 years):** 30–60 mg QDS orally

---

#### Remifentanil

**Class of drug:** µ-opioid agonist.

**Mechanism of action:** Ultra-short acting with rapid onset and peak effect.

**Indications:** Analgesia and enhancement of anaesthesia at induction, assisted ventilation, pain control.

**Cautions:** Not licensed for children <1 year.

**Side effects:** Respiratory depression, CNS effects (confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, headache), hypotension, dry mouth, arrhythmias, constipation, pupillary constriction, nausea/vomiting, urinary retention.

**Dose:**
*   Child (1 month–12 years) for assisted ventilation: 0.1–1 μg/kg over 30 seconds, then 3–78 μg/kg/hr IV infusion
*   Child (12–18 years) for assisted ventilation: 0.1–1 μg/kg over 30 seconds, then 3–120 μg/kg/hr IV infusion

---

#### Muscle Relaxants

**Mechanism of action:** Specific blockade of neuromuscular junction.

**Non-depolarising agents:** Compete with acetylcholine at receptor sites; reversed by anticholinesterases (neostigmine).
*   **Aminosteroids:** Pancuronium, rocuronium, vecuronium
*   **Benzylisoquinoliniums:** Atracurium, cisatracurium, mivacurium

**Depolarising agents (suxamethonium):** Non-competitive binding → depolarisation; rapid onset (ideal for intubation), short duration, not reversible; neonates/young children require higher dose.

**Suxamethonium (IV):**
*   Neonate: 2 mg/kg
*   Child (1–11 months): 2 mg/kg
*   Child (1–17 years): 1 mg/kg

**Rocuronium:**
*   Initially 600 μg/kg IV, then 300–600 μg/kg/hr IV infusion

## 10. Antibiotics</summary><div class="details-content">

#### Cefuroxime

**Class of drug:** Second-generation cephalosporin.

**Mechanism of action:** Inhibits synthesis of peptidoglycan layer of bacterial cell wall; less sensitive to penicillinases than penicillins.

**Indications:** Surgical prophylaxis; infections caused by Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms (S. pneumoniae, S. aureus, N. gonorrhoeae, H. influenzae, E. coli).

**Cautions:** Renal impairment, hypersensitivity to other β-lactams.

**Side effects:** Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, GI disturbance, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, transient hepatitis.

**Dose:**
*   **Surgical prophylaxis:** 50 mg/kg IV (max 1.5 g) up to 30 min pre-op, then 30 mg/kg IV TDS for up to 3 doses if required
*   **Oral:**
    *   Child (3 months–1 year): 10 mg/kg BD (max 125 mg/dose)
    *   Child (2–11 years): 15 mg/kg BD (max 250 mg/dose)
    *   Child (12–17 years): 250 mg BD
*   **IV:**
    *   Child: 20 mg/kg TDS (max 750 mg/dose); increase to 50–60 mg/kg every 8 hours (max 1.5 g/dose) in severe infections

---

#### Gentamicin

**Class of drug:** Aminoglycoside.

**Mechanism of action:** Crosses bacterial cell wall → inhibits ribosome activity → prevents protein synthesis.

**Indications:** Gram-negative organisms, particularly Pseudomonas.

**Cautions:** Renal impairment, hearing impairment, myasthenia gravis.

**Side effects:** Ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity.

**Dose:**
*   **IV infusion:** Initially 7 mg/kg once daily; subsequent doses adjusted to serum concentration
*   **Slow IV injection:**
    *   Child (1 month–11 years): 2.5 mg/kg TDS (over ≥3 minutes)
    *   Child (12–17 years): 2 mg/kg TDS (over ≥3 minutes)

---

#### Vancomycin

**Class of drug:** Glycopeptide.

**Mechanism of action:** Inhibits cell wall synthesis by preventing formation and cross-linking of structural polymer chains.

**Indications:** Surgical prophylaxis (MRSA carriers), endocarditis, serious MRSA infection, Clostridium difficile colitis.

**Cautions:** Renal impairment.

**Side effects:** Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, hypersensitivity, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia.

**Dose:**
*   **Severe infections (IV):**
    *   Child (1 month–11 years): 10–15 mg/kg QDS, adjust to plasma concentration
    *   Child (12–17 years): 15–20 mg/kg IV BD or TDS (max 2 g/dose), adjust to plasma concentration
*   **Clostridium difficile infection (oral):**
    *   Child (1 month–11 years): 10 mg/kg QDS for 10 days
    *   Child (12–17 years): 125 mg QDS for 10 days; increase to 500 mg QDS if necessary

</div></details>
