# Ebstein's Anomaly

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Definition and Pathoanatomy
*   **Ebstein's anomaly (EA):** Malformation of the right heart characterised by failure of delamination of the tricuspid valve (TV) leaflets from the underlying right ventricular (RV) myocardium.
*   **Classic findings:** Apical displacement of functional TV hinge points (most prominent in septal leaflet, then posterior, then anterior); dilatation of 'atrialised' RV; anterior leaflet fenestrations/redundancy/tethering; right atrioventricular junction dilatation.
*   **Atrialised RV:** Portion of RV between anatomical AV junction and functional TV annulus; functionally associated with RA rather than RV; may account for >50% of RV volume in severe cases.

### Classification (Carpentier)
*   **Type A:** Adherent septal and posterior leaflets, normal RV
*   **Type B:** Atrialised RV, mobile anterior leaflet
*   **Type C:** Stenotic anterior leaflet
*   **Type D:** Extensive atrialisation of RV

### Associated Anomalies and Epidemiology
*   Interatrial communication (PFO or ASD) — **80–90%**
*   VSD, RVOT obstruction (functional/anatomical pulmonary atresia, pulmonic stenosis), PDA, ccTGA
*   Left heart abnormalities: mitral valve prolapse, bicuspid aortic valve, LV non-compaction
*   **Epidemiology:** 1% of all congenital heart lesions (~1 in 20,000 live births). Maternal lithium use associated with relative risk 1.2–7.7.

### Pathophysiology
*   RV contractile dysfunction (myopathy + enlargement) + severe tricuspid regurgitation → inefficient forward flow → progressive RA and RV dilatation → further TV annular dilatation → worsening TR.

### Natural History
*   **Neonatal:** Mortality approaches 100% for severely symptomatic neonates.
*   **Beyond neonatal period:** Cumulative survival at 1, 5, 10, 15, and 20 years: 89%, 81%, 76%, 53%, and 41% respectively.
*   **Predictors of cardiac-related death:** Cardiothoracic ratio ≥0.65, younger age at diagnosis, increased TV displacement severity, cyanosis, severe TR.

### Symptoms
*   **Neonates:** Severe cyanosis or heart failure, functional/anatomical pulmonary atresia.
*   **Children:** Early fatigue or mild cyanosis (depends on heart failure degree).
*   **Adults:** May be asymptomatic; frequently present with arrhythmia (AF, atrial flutter, ectopic atrial tachycardia).

### Neonatal Echocardiographic Predictors of Mortality
*   Gestational age <32 weeks (OR 8.6, p<0.001)
*   Pulmonary regurgitation (OR 2.9, p<0.001)
*   Pericardial effusion (OR 2.5, p=0.04)
*   Increased TV annulus diameter z-score (OR 1.3, p=0.001)

### Great Ormond Street Echocardiography (GOSE) Score
*   **Index = (RA + aRV) / (RV + LV + LA)** on apical four-chamber view in end-diastole.
*   **Score 1 (<0.5):** 0% mortality; **Score 2 (0.5–0.99):** 10% mortality; **Score 3 (1–1.49):** 44–100% mortality; **Score 4 (≥1.5):** 100% mortality.

### Diagnosis
*   **CXR:** Globular cardiomegaly (RA enlargement + RVOT displacement); normal/diminished pulmonary vascularity; massive 'wall-to-wall' heart in severely symptomatic neonates.
*   **ECG:** RBBB, right atrial enlargement, pre-excitation (LBBB pattern with dominant S waves in right precordial leads), low voltage in right precordial leads, SVT, prolonged PR interval.
*   **Echocardiography:** Apical displacement of septal/posterior leaflet ≥8mm/m² BSA; failure of anterior leaflet delamination; severe TR; possible right-to-left shunt if interatrial communication present; RV enlargement/dysfunction with paradoxical septal motion.
*   **MRI:** Defines AV junction and TV leaflet attachments; quantifies RV size and function (anatomical RV volume includes aRV; functional RV volume excludes aRV). Most adults have pre-op EDV 150–250mL/m².
*   **CT:** Useful for surgical re-entry planning and vessel assessment for peripheral CPB.

### Arrhythmias
*   **Atrial tachycardia (AF, flutter, ectopic atrial):** 25–65%
*   **Accessory pathways (WPW):** 10–45% (multiple pathways in 6–36%)
*   **AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT):** 10%

### Indications for Pre-operative Electrophysiological Study
*   Evidence of pre-excitation on ECG
*   History of SVT, wide-complex tachycardia, or syncope
*   Prior to operative intervention (routine in children, usually in adults)

### Surgical Approaches to Arrhythmia Management
*   **Paroxysmal AF/flutter:** Modified right-sided maze + cavotricuspid isthmus ablation or selective biatrial maze
*   **Continuous AF:** Biatrial maze
*   **Modalities:** Cryoablation, radiofrequency ablation, or traditional cut-and-sew

### Indications for Surgical Repair
*   **Neonatal period:** Inability to wean from ventilator, severe heart failure, persistent metabolic abnormalities, persistent cyanosis, prostaglandin dependence, GOSE score 3–4 with mild cyanosis, cardiothoracic ratio >80%.
*   **After neonatal period:** Symptoms or deteriorating exercise capacity, cyanosis, paradoxical embolus, progressive RV dilatation or dysfunction.
*   Early intervention considered if successful TV repair likely with low morbidity/mortality.

### Neonatal Medical Therapy
*   Prostaglandin E₁ (for functional pulmonary atresia — discontinue if PR develops)
*   Nitric oxide (reduce PVR)
*   Milrinone
*   Endotracheal intubation and ventilation
*   Cautious inotropes (epinephrine may exacerbate arrhythmias)

### Neonatal Surgical Options
*   **Single-ventricle (Starnes procedure):** TV patch closure (fenestrated), atrial septectomy, systemic-to-PA shunt (modified BT shunt).
*   **Biventricular repair:** TV repair, fenestrated ASD closure, reduction atrioplasty.
*   Early mortality when neonatal operation required: ~25%.

### Tricuspid Valve Repair Categories
*   **Monocusp repair:** Mobilisation of anterior leaflet to coapt with ventricular septum.
    *   Danielson method — TV remains in place in RV.
    *   Carpentier method — anterior leaflet mobilised, delaminated, reattached to anterior annulus + RV plication + ring annuloplasty.
*   **Cone repair:** Delamination and recruitment of all leaflet tissue → rotation → creation of 360° leaflet tissue → reanchoring at anatomical annulus.

### Principles of Cone Repair
*   Near anatomical repair with 360° of TV leaflet tissue
*   Neo-TV hinge point at true TV annulus (AV junction)
*   Selective plication of atrialised RV (eliminate dyskinetic areas)
*   Excision of redundant right atrium

### Adjunct Manoeuvres for TV Repair
*   TV annuloplasty band or reinforced annular plication
*   Anterior leaflet augmentation (elliptical patch for height; triangular patch for circumference) — autologous pericardium preferred
*   Neo-chordae (autologous or Gore-Tex®)
*   Modified Sebening stitch (approximates anterior papillary muscle to ventricular septum)

### Relative Contraindications to TV Repair
*   Older patients (>60 years)
*   Severe RV or TV annular dilatation
*   Pulmonary hypertension (uncommon)

### Surgical Approaches Outside Neonatal Period
*   Biventricular pathway
*   1.5-ventricle pathway (addition of bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt — BCPS) for severe RV dilatation/dysfunction

### Indications for Bidirectional Cavopulmonary Shunt (BCPS)
*   **RV offloading:** decreases RV volume by 35–45%.
*   **Specific indications:** RV EDV >250mL/m², RV EF <25%, cardiothoracic ratio >65%, small/D-shaped LV, post-repair RA pressure/LA pressure >1.5:1, post-repair low cardiac output, TV stenosis with mean gradient >8–10mmHg.
*   **Contraindications:** Mean PAP >20mmHg, PVR >4 Woods units, LVEDP/LA pressure >12mmHg, significant pulmonary artery hypoplasia.

### Atrial Septal Defect Subclosure/Non-closure
*   Intermediate option to bypass RV when function poor (less unloading than BCPS).
*   **Use:** Routinely in neonate/infant; selectively in child/young adult; rarely in adult (risk of paradoxical emboli).

### Tricuspid Valve Replacement
*   **Indications:** Older patients (>60 years), pulmonary hypertension, significant LV dysfunction.
*   **Prosthesis choice:** Bioprosthesis preferred (good durability in TV position, fewer thromboembolic complications).
*   **Technical note:** Suture line on atrial side of membranous septum and AV node (marked by vein of D). Tie sutures with heart beating to identify conduction injury.

### Perioperative Care
*   **Volume:** Tachycardia (HR 100–120, atrial pacing if needed), relative hypovolaemia (RA pressure <12mmHg), post-bypass ultrafiltration for patients ≤20kg.
*   **Ventilation:** Minimise PEEP and mean airway pressure; extubate when metabolic parameters normalise.
*   **Cardiac support:** Epinephrine + milrinone early (wean to avoid tachyarrhythmias); selective pulmonary vasodilators (24–48 hours).
*   **Arrhythmia prevention**

### Postoperative Discharge Medications
*   Beta-blocker and/or ACE inhibitor
*   Sildenafil for 6–8 weeks (severe RV dysfunction or high altitude residence)
*   Amiodarone for 2–3 months (transient arrhythmias) or 3–6 months (large RV plication)
*   Warfarin for 3–6 months → aspirin (biological TV replacement)

### Long-Term Outcomes
*   **Historical:** Late survival at 10 years — 93% after repair, 86% after replacement.
*   **Cone repair (recent data):** Overall survival 98% at 6 years; freedom from reoperation 98% at 6 years.

## 1 What are the characteristics of Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

Ebstein's anomaly (EA) is a malformation of the right heart and exemplifies anatomical heterogeneity in congenital heart disease. The classic findings include:

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### a) Failure of Delamination</summary><div class="details-content">

Failure of delamination of the tricuspid valve (TV) leaflets from the underlying right ventricular (RV) myocardium.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### b) Apical Displacement</summary><div class="details-content">

Apical (downward) displacement of the functional tricuspid annular hinge points. The displacement is most prominent in the septal leaflet, followed by the posterior leaflet and then the anterior leaflet (Figure 1).

![](_page_0_Picture_8.jpeg)

This results in an atrialised portion of the right ventricle that lies between the anatomical atrioventricular junction (dotted line) and the tricuspid annulus (dashed line). RA = right atrium; aRV = atrialised right ventricle; TRV = true right ventricle. *Copyright © Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### c) Atrialised RV Dilatation</summary><div class="details-content">

Dilatation of the 'atrialised' portion of the RV, with variable degrees of hypertrophy and thinning of the RV wall.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### d) Anterior Leaflet Abnormalities</summary><div class="details-content">

Anterior leaflet fenestrations, redundancy and tethering.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### e) Right Atrioventricular Junction Dilatation</summary><div class="details-content">

Right atrioventricular junction dilatation.

</div></details>

## 3 What cardiac abnormalities are commonly associated with Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Interatrial communication** — patent foramen ovale (PFO) or secundum atrial septal defect (ASD) (80–90%)
*   **Ventricular septal defect**
*   **Right ventricular outflow tract obstruction** — anatomical or functional pulmonary atresia, pulmonic valve stenosis
*   **Patent ductus arteriosus**
*   **Congenitally corrected transposition of the great arteries (ccTGA)**
*   Less commonly, **left heart abnormalities**, including mitral valve prolapse, bicuspid aortic valve or left ventricular non-compaction

## 5 What is the pathophysiology of Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### RV Contractile Dysfunction</summary><div class="details-content">

The pathophysiology of Ebstein's anomaly principally relates to dysfunction of right ventricular contractility, secondary to myopathy and enlargement, and tricuspid regurgitation.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Atrialised RV</summary><div class="details-content">

The atrialised portion of the RV separates the true TV annulus from the functional TV annulus. This variably large portion of the posteroseptal wall does not have trabeculae and is functionally associated with the right atrium (RA) rather than the RV, thereby contributing to the paradoxical effect on the tricuspid valve, resulting in increased regurgitation. It may also account for more than half the volume of the RV, thus reducing the true RV volume to the RVOT only, in severe cases.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Vicious Cycle</summary><div class="details-content">

Inefficient forward flow further contributes to dilatation of the RA and RV, further dilating the TV annulus, and worsening TV regurgitation.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 9 What is the Great Ormond Street Echocardiography (GOSE) score?</summary><div class="details-content">

The GOSE score (also known as the Celermajer index) is an echocardiographic score that correlates with outcome in neonates with Ebstein's anomaly (Figure 3).

$$ \text{GOSE score index} = \frac{\text{Area of (RA + Atrialised RV)}}{\text{Area of (RV + LV + LA)}} $$

RA = right atrium; RV = right ventricle; LA = left atrium; LV = left ventricle.

These values are calculated on the **apical four-chamber echocardiographic view in end-diastole**.

![](_page_4_Picture_2.jpeg)

**Figure 3. Apical four-chamber echocardiographic view used to measure the area of the right atrium (RA), atrialized right ventricle (aRV), right ventricle (RV), left atrium (LA) and left ventricle (LV).**

As the GOSE score increases, the likelihood of mortality increases (Table 1).

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Table 1. GOSE score and associated risk of mortality</summary><div class="details-content">

| GOSE score | Index (RA + aRV) / (RV + LA + LV) | Risk of mortality |
|------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | <0.5 | 0% |
| 2 | 0.5–0.99 | 10% |
| 3 | 1–1.49 | 44–100% |
| 4 | ≥1.50 | 100% |

</div></details>

#### Features Associated with Successful TV Repair</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Large, mobile anterior leaflet** with a free leading edge
*   **Presence of any inferior leaflet**
*   **Presence of a septal leaflet**

**Difficult TV repair** is more likely in patients with:
*   **a)** Absence of the septal leaflet
*   **b)** Severe muscularisation of the leaflet tissue
*   **c)** Adherence of the leaflet edge directly to the underlying myocardium
*   **d)** TV leaflet tissue in the RVOT
*   **e)** Severe RV or TV annular enlargement
*   **f)** Pulmonary hypertension (which is rare in Ebstein's anomaly)

## 13 What are the radiological features of Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Cardiac MRI</summary><div class="details-content">

Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is typically more useful than computed tomography (CT) due to the ability to obtain both functional and anatomical information.

*   Axial orientation helps delineate the atrioventricular junction and attachments of the abnormal tricuspid valve leaflets.
*   Useful for quantitative assessment of RV size and function (Figure 8).

![](_page_9_Picture_4.jpeg)

**Figure 8. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging in a patient with Ebstein's anomaly:** A) short-axis view at the mid-ventricular level; B) four-chamber view; and C) right ventricular inflow-outflow view. The atrialised right ventricle (aRV) lies between the atrioventricular junction (dotted line) and the functional tricuspid valve orifice (dashed line). The functional right ventricle (fRV) is the part of the right ventricle between the functional tricuspid valve orifice and the pulmonary valve. The anatomical right ventricle is a combination of both aRV and fRV. The functional orifice of the tricuspid valve is rotated apically and superiorly toward the right ventricular outflow tract. Ao = aorta; LV = left ventricle; PA = pulmonary artery; RA = right atrium.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### RV Volume Assessment</summary><div class="details-content">

Stack slices imaging can provide accurate volumetric assessment of the RV. End-diastolic and end-systolic areas are traced in each slice and multiplied by slab thickness to generate volume.

**RV volume can be determined in two ways:**
*   **a) Anatomical RV volumes** — atrialised RV is included (Mayo Clinic practice). This approach better predicts postoperative RV performance, as the atrialised RV is incorporated into the functional RV after TV (cone) repair.
*   **b) Functional RV volume** — volume between the TV and pulmonary valve is measured; atrialised portion is ignored.

Most adults presenting for surgery have pre-operative end-diastolic RV volumes of **150–250mL/m²**. Currently, there is no criteria of RV size or function to trigger referral for surgery.

TV regurgitation can be quantified by phase contrast (no contrast required); **regurgitant fraction >50%** = severe.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### CT</summary><div class="details-content">

Cross-sectional imaging with CT is helpful for **planning surgical re-entry** and **assessment of vessels** for possible peripheral cardiopulmonary bypass.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 18 What are the indications for surgical repair in patients with Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Neonatal Period</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Inability to wean from the ventilator
*   **b)** Severe heart failure
*   **c)** Persistent metabolic abnormalities
*   **d)** Persistent cyanosis
*   **e)** Prostaglandin dependence
*   **f)** GOSE score of **3–4** with mild cyanosis
*   **g)** Cardiothoracic ratio **>80%**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### After the Neonatal Period</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Symptoms or deteriorating exercise capacity
*   **b)** Cyanosis
*   **c)** Paradoxical embolus
*   **d)** Progressive RV dilatation or dysfunction

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### General Principle</summary><div class="details-content">

If a successful TV repair is likely with low morbidity and mortality, **early operative intervention** can be considered.

</div></details>

</div></details>

## 19 What are the medical therapies commonly used in neonates presenting with Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Prostaglandin E₁**, specifically with functional pulmonary valve atresia. It should be discontinued if pulmonary regurgitation develops.
*   **Nitric oxide** to reduce pulmonary vascular resistance
*   **Milrinone**
*   **Endotracheal intubation and ventilation**
*   **Cautious use of inotropes**, such as epinephrine, as this may exacerbate arrhythmias

</div></details>

## 22 What are the general categories of TV repair in Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Monocusp Repair</summary><div class="details-content">

Requires mobilisation of the anterior TV leaflet to coapt with the ventricular septum:

*   **a) Danielson method** — TV remains in place in the RV.
*   **b) Carpentier method** — anterior leaflet mobilised, delaminated and reattached to the anterior annulus, followed by plication of the atrialised portion of the RV and ring annuloplasty (Figures 17 and 18).

#### Cone Repair</summary><div class="details-content">

Consists of **delamination and recruitment of all leaflet tissue**, followed by **rotation** and creation of **360° of leaflet tissue** with reanchoring at the anatomical annulus.

*   **Selective plication** of atrialised RV to eliminate areas of dyskinesis
*   **Excision of redundant right atrium**

## 27 When is a bidirectional cavopulmonary shunt (BCPS) for a 1.5-ventricle pathway more likely to be required?</summary><div class="details-content">

Generally, BCPS should be considered in those with severe right heart failure to allow for RV volume and pressure offloading, as it decreases the volume to the RV by **35–45%**.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Specific Indications</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** RV end-diastolic volume **>250mL/m²**
*   **b)** RV ejection fraction **<25%**
*   **c)** Cardiothoracic ratio **>65%**
*   **d)** Small and **D-shaped left ventricle**
*   **e)** Post-repair right atrial pressure to left atrial pressure ratio **>1.5:1**
*   **f)** Post-repair low cardiac output (failure to separate from CPB, persistent metabolic acidosis, low urine output, increasing creatinine, poor peripheral perfusion, mean arterial pressure <50mmHg)
*   **g)** Non-heart failure indications: reduction of tension on a complex TV repair and post-repair TV stenosis with mean gradient **>8–10mmHg**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Contraindications</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **a)** Mean pulmonary artery pressure **>20mmHg**
*   **b)** Pulmonary arteriolar resistance **>4 Woods units**
*   **c)** Left ventricular end-diastolic pressure or left atrial pressure **>12mmHg**
*   **d)** Significant pulmonary artery hypoplasia

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

## 28 When should atrial septal defect subclosure or non-closure be considered?</summary><div class="details-content">

Residual interatrial communication is an intermediate option to bypass the RV in situations with poor RV function, although this does not provide as significant RV unloading as with BCPS.

**It should be used:**
*   **a)** Routinely in the **neonate/infant**
*   **b)** Selectively in the **child/young adult**
*   **c)** Rarely in the **adult**, especially when there is an increased risk of paradoxical emboli

</div></details>

## 29 When should tricuspid valve replacement be considered in the surgical management of Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Indications</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Older patients** (>60 years of age)
*   **Pulmonary hypertension**
*   **Significant LV dysfunction**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Prosthesis Choice</summary><div class="details-content">

A **bioprosthesis** is preferred to a mechanical prosthesis due to the good durability of a biological valve in the TV position and fewer thromboembolic complications.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

#### Technical Considerations</summary><div class="details-content">

The valve suture line is placed on the **atrial side of the membranous septum and AV node** (generally marked with the vein of D) to avoid conduction injuries. The sutures should be tied with **heart beating** to readily identify injury to the conduction system (Figure 23).

</div></details>

## 30 What are the special considerations for the perioperative care of patients with Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

RV preload and RV afterload must be optimised by:

*   **a) Adjusting intravascular volume** — using tachycardia (HR 100–120 with atrial pacing if needed) and relative hypovolaemia (RA pressures <12mmHg), as well as occasionally using post-bypass ultrafiltration in patients ≤20kg
*   **b) Ventilation** — minimising end-expiratory pressure, decreasing mean airway pressure and extubating when metabolic parameters normalise
*   **c) Cardiac support** — using epinephrine and milrinone in the early postoperative period but weaned to avoid potential tachyarrhythmias, and selective use of pulmonary vasodilators (generally for 24–48 hours)
*   **d) Prevention of arrhythmias**

</div></details>

## 31 What are typical postoperative discharge medications following surgical repair of Ebstein's anomaly?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Beta-blocker and/or ACE inhibitor**
*   **Sildenafil** for 6–8 weeks in those with severe RV dysfunction or residence at high altitudes
*   **Amiodarone** — for 2–3 months if transient atrial or ventricular arrhythmias occurred during hospitalisation, or selectively for 3–6 months if a large RV plication was performed
*   **Warfarin** (for 3–6 months) followed by aspirin if a biological tricuspid valve replacement was performed

</div></details>

## 32 What are the late outcomes for patients with Ebstein's anomaly following surgical intervention?</summary><div class="details-content">

*   **Historical:** Late survival at 10 years is **93% following repair** and **86% following replacement**.
*   **Cone repair (recent data):** Overall survival **98% at 6 years**; freedom from reoperation **98% at 6 years**.
