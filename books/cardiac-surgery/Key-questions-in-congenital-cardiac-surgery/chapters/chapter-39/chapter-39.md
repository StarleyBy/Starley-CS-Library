# Surgery for Cardiac Arrhythmias

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Atrial Re-entry Tachycardia (Atrial Flutter)
- **Mechanism:** Macro-re-entrant circuit requiring unidirectional block + area of slowed conduction (cavo-tricuspid isthmus) (Figure 1)
- **Most common in right atrium** (scar of tricuspid isthmus near IVC entrance)
- **Highest incidence in:** Ebstein's anomaly, Fontan, ASD, TOF, TGA with atrial switch
- **Ablation:** Cryoablation at inferior isthmus → transmural scar (cavo-tricuspid isthmus involved in >60% of circuits)

### Accessory Connection-Mediated Tachycardia (WPW)
- **Manifest (pre-excitation):** Antegrade conduction over accessory connection (delta wave) (Figure 2)
- **Concealed:** Retrograde conduction only (ventricle → atrium)
- **Re-entrant circuit:** Atria + accessory connection + ventricles + AV node
- **Orthodromic (90%):** Atrium → AV node → ventricle → accessory connection → atrium (Figure 3)
- **Antidromic (<5%):** Atrium → accessory connection → ventricle → AV node → atrium (wide QRS)
- **Pre-excited atrial tachycardia:** AF/AFL conducts antegrade via accessory connection → VF/sudden death risk
- **Digoxin contraindicated** (accelerates accessory connection conduction)
- **Ablation:** Transcatheter ablation of accessory connection (four locations: left-sided, right free-wall, anteroseptal, posteroseptal); high success rate

### AV Nodal Re-entry Tachycardia (AVNRT)
- **Mechanism:** Macro-re-entrant circuit using fast pathway (anterior) and slow pathway (posterior between CS and tricuspid valve) (Figure 4)
- **More common in:** TGA, AVSD, heterotaxy syndrome
- **Ablation:** Slow pathway modification (cryoablation/radiofrequency from posteroinferior CS os to IVC); surgical ablation limited to Mustard/Senning reoperations (avoids retrograde catheter approach)

### Focal Atrial Tachycardia
- **Mechanism:** Rapidly firing discrete atrial focus (automatic, micro-re-entrant, or triggered); inhibits normal sinus function (Figure 5)
- **Common locations:** Right atrium (crista terminalis, atrial appendages)
- **Ablation:** Cryoablation + limited atrial resection; wider areas require atrial wall isolation; refractory cases → PV isolation, LA/RA isolation, His bundle cryoablation + pacemaker

### Atrial Fibrillation (AF)
- **Mechanism:** Uncoordinated atrial activation (multiple independent re-entrant wavelets, rapidly firing foci, rotors); trigger often from pulmonary vein orifices
- **Common in CHD:** ASD (repaired/unrepaired), single ventricle, atrial baffle (TGA), Ebstein's anomaly
- **Note:** ASD closure without arrhythmia intervention → high AF recurrence
- **Ablation — Cox maze III/IV:** Creates transmural scars forming "corridors" from SA node to AV node; >90% success in adults with concomitant MV repair/ASD closure/CABG; successful in Fontan conversion, ASD closure, MV repair. **Right atrial only surgery ineffective for AF**

### Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)
- **Definition:** HR >120 bpm (adolescents/adults) or >150 bpm (children); usually re-entrant; sustained (>30 sec) vs non-sustained
- **In CHD:** Macro-re-entrant circuit may involve ventriculotomy scar or VSD patch (TOF, DORV); also in univentricular physiology, Ebstein's anomaly, post-Sano
- **Management:** Haemodynamic improvement may decrease non-sustained VT; sustained VT requires direct therapy ± ICD
- **Ablation:** No standard lesion set; individualised (ischaemic, fibrotic, oncogenic); transcatheter preferred; intra-operative cryoablation for concomitant surgery (e.g., TOF with PV insertion + cryoablation around VSD patch and ventriculotomy)

### Candidates for Intra-operative Cryoablation (Atrial Re-entry)
- Refractory to transcatheter ablation
- Requiring repair of associated intracardiac defects
- **Success rate (modified right atrial maze):** >95%
- **Atrial tachycardia incidence post-repair:** Mustard/Senning 20–50%; Fontan 40–50%; TOF 34%

### Therapeutic Cryoablation Lesions — Two Ventricles (Figure 6)
**Four techniques:**
1. Cavo-tricuspid isthmus ablation (typical AFL)
2. Modified right atrial maze (multiple re-entrant circuits)
3. Cox maze IV (± modifications) for AF
4. Atrial pacemaker (bradycardia prevention or anti-tachycardia)

**Mustard/Senning:** Catheter ablation possible but requires retrograde aortic approach (AI risk); if reoperation needed → direct surgical cryoablation

**Modified right atrial maze + modified Cox maze III** for multiple circuits in complex CHD with rare atrial anomalies (juxtaposed appendages, TAPVC, separate hepatic vein entry, persistent LSVC, absent CS) (Figure 7)

### Fontan Conversion Cryoablation (Figure 8)
- Fontan patients have hypertrophied, scarred, enlarged right atria with multiple circuits; transcatheter ablation has high early recurrence
- Combined approach: repair associated lesions + Fontan conversion + arrhythmia surgery
- Anatomical variances requiring maze alteration: tricuspid atresia, mitral atresia, common AV valve (AVSD)
- Pre-existing atriopulmonary Fontan with patch covering TV and CS → remove patch, identify anatomy, place cryoablation lesions, re-cover TV with new patch (Figure 9)

### AF Cryoablation in Fontan Conversion
- AF often preceded by atrial re-entry tachycardia; right atrial re-entry may degenerate into AF
- Treatment requires **biatrial maze** (all right-sided lesions + left-sided lesions modified for single ventricle) (Figures 7–8)

### AF Cryoablation — Two Ventricles
- Combined right + left atrial maze
- Key lesion: from pulmonary vein confluence to P3 scallop of posterior mitral annulus (prevents re-entrant circuit causing AF recurrence)
- Epicardial coronary sinus lesion complements mitral annulus lesion

### Right-sided Lesions on Beating Heart?
- Possible if **no atrial or ventricular communications** (otherwise air embolism risk)
- Pre-operative/intra-operative echocardiography to detect shunts
- Many centres use cardioplegic arrest for all right-sided maze (avoid air embolism, apply lesions in motionless heart)

### Is Biatrial Maze Always Required for AF?
- Adult acquired AF often attributed to pulmonary vein confluence re-entry → some advocate PV isolation alone (not universally accepted; Cox maze III/IV gives better results)
- **Adult CHD:** AF occurs with right atrial dilatation, fibrosis, pre-existing atrial tachycardia
- **Anatomical substrates:** Old atriopulmonary Fontan, untreated ASD (± PAPVC), longstanding untreated Ebstein's anomaly
- **Recommendation:** Biatrial maze for comprehensive long-term ablation

### Prophylactic Arrhythmia Surgery
**Rationale:** High subsequent arrhythmia incidence in selected subsets:
- Ebstein's anomaly, Fontan, reoperation for TOF, ASD closure >40 years

**Protocols:**
- Limited to highest-risk patients
- Tailored to anatomical variants
- Very low risk of procedure-induced arrhythmias
- Low incidence of harm to conducting tissue

### Prophylactic Lesion Sets
**Standard set (safe from AV node injury) for:**
- Secundum ASD, Ebstein's anomaly, single ventricle, post-TOF
- **Not safe** for partial AVSD or anomalies with displaced AV nodes

**Lesion set (Figure 10):** Cavo-tricuspid isthmus ablation (TV at septal/posterior commissure → inferior CS os → posterior IVC os); extend medial atriotomy to anterior IVC os

**For redo patients with atrial scarring (Figure 11):** Additional lesion from fossa ovalis across crista terminalis to posterior atrial incision

**Additional lesion (Figure 12):** Base of RAA to anterior TV annulus

**For ASD closure >40 years (high AF/AFL risk) (Figure 13):** More aggressive right-sided + left-sided lesions; additional cryoablation connecting SVC to IVC across interatrial lesion through crista terminalis

**Left atrial lesion set (Figure 14):**
- Circumferential PV confluence isolation
- Connect PV confluence to P3 of posterior MV annulus
- Connect PV confluence to base of LAA (LAA resection to prevent thrombus)

**Note:** These lesion sets proposed but not universally accepted; need prospective multi-institutional study

**Principles:** Simple to perform, minimal complications, intended to solve long-term haemodynamic aberrations

## Atrial Re-entry Tachycardia (Atrial Flutter)</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 1. What is the mechanism of action that causes postoperative atrial tachycardia?</summary><div class="details-content">

The most common mechanism of late postoperative supraventricular tachycardia is **macro-re-entrant atrial tachycardia** (atrial re-entry tachycardia or atrial flutter).

**Requirements for re-entrant tachycardia:**
- Circuit with **unidirectional block**
- Area of **slowed conduction** that can be initiated and terminated with pacing
- Circuit can be macro-re-entrant (large) or focal (small)

**In postoperative patients:**
- Macro-re-entrant circuit most commonly in **right atrium**
- Area of slow conduction: atrial scar of the **tricuspid isthmus** (near IVC entrance)

**Highest incidence in:**
- Ebstein's anomaly
- Univentricular heart with prior Fontan repair
- Atrial septal defect (ASD)
- Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)
- Transposition of the great arteries (TGA) with prior atrial switch

**Typical scenario:**
1. Premature atrial contraction → electrical wave front encounters unidirectional block near atrial septum
2. Wave front redirected inferiorly by alternative route to right atrial free wall
3. Wave front meets area of slow conduction (**cavo-tricuspid isthmus** — between IVC, tricuspid valve, coronary sinus) → electrophysiological delay
4. Delay allows area of unidirectional block to recover conduction
5. Wave front enters opposite area → re-entrant circuit → tachycardia (Figure 1)

**Multiple areas of slow conduction** may exist from prior incisions, patches, or wall stress.

![](_page_1_Picture_3.jpeg)

**FIGURE 1.** Cavo-tricuspid isthmus dependent macro-re-entrant atrial tachycardia. AV = atrioventricular; CS = coronary sinus; SA = sinoatrial. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 2. Describe how ablation lesions terminate atrio-re-entrant tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

- Arrhythmia circuit often interrupted at the **inferior isthmus** by cryoablation or other energy sources → produces **transmural scar** across myocardium
- **Cavo-tricuspid isthmus** involved in **>60%** of atrial re-entry circuits in CHD patients
- Cryoablation transforms area of slow conduction → area of **no conduction** (scar tissue) → eliminates circuit

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Accessory Connection-Mediated Tachycardia (WPW)</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 3. What is the cause of accessory connection-mediated tachycardia?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Accessory connections:** Bridges of electrical conductivity along the atrioventricular annulus

**Types:**
- **Manifest (pre-excitation):** Conduction from atria to ventricle antegrade over accessory connection (delta wave) (Figure 2)
- **Concealed:** Conducts only retrograde (ventricle → atrium)

**Re-entrant circuit:** Atria + accessory connection + ventricles + AV node

**Most common CHD with manifest accessory connections:**
- Ebstein's anomaly
- Congenitally corrected transposition of the great arteries (ccTGA)

**Orthodromic reciprocating tachycardia (most common, ~90%):**
- Atrium → normal AV node → ventricle → re-enters atrium via accessory connection (Figure 3)

**Antidromic reciprocating tachycardia (<5%):**
- Atrium → accessory connection → ventricle → re-enters atrium retrogradely over normal AV node
- Pre-excited, wide QRS

**Pre-excited atrial tachycardia:**
- Atrial fibrillation/flutter conducts antegrade via accessory connection (faster than AV node would allow)
- Rapid conduction to ventricle may precipitate **ventricular fibrillation** and **sudden cardiac arrest**

**Digoxin contraindicated** in patients with pre-excitation (accelerates accessory connection conduction)

![](_page_2_Picture_5.jpeg)

**FIGURE 2.** AV reciprocating tachycardia in manifest WPW syndrome. AV = atrioventricular; CS = coronary sinus; SA = sinoatrial. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

![](_page_3_Picture_2.jpeg)

**FIGURE 3.** Orthodromic reciprocating tachycardia (most common). Conduction blocked in accessory connection → delta wave lost ("concealed"). Circuit: atria → AV node → ventricle → accessory connection → atrium. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 4. Describe how ablation lesions terminate atrioventricular re-entry tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

**Therapeutic options:** Ablation of offending connection

**Four locations:**
- Left-sided connections
- Right free-wall connections
- Anteroseptal connections
- Posteroseptal connections

**Approaches:**
- Epicardial or endocardial techniques (different approach per location)
- **Transcatheter ablation** (most common) — can often be performed without fluoroscopy
- **High success rate** — benefit of recatheterisation/reablation if initial failure

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## AV Nodal Re-entry Tachycardia (AVNRT)</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 5. What is the mechanism of action that causes atrioventricular nodal re-entry tachycardia?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Macro-re-entrant circuit involving:**
- **Fast pathway:** Typical anterior approach to AV node in atrial septum
- **Slow pathway:** Posterior approach in anatomical area between coronary sinus and AV node (Figure 4)

**Mechanism:**
1. AV conduction encounters **unidirectional block** in fast pathway fibres superior to AV node
2. Wave front redirected to **slow pathway fibres** in atrial isthmus (between coronary sinus and tricuspid valve)
3. After impulse exits isthmus → re-enters fast pathway anteriorly and superiorly → establishes re-entrant circuit

**More common in:**
- Transposition of the great arteries (TGA)
- Atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD)
- Heterotaxy syndrome

![](_page_5_Figure_7.jpeg)

**FIGURE 4.** Slow/fast ("typical") AV nodal re-entry tachycardia. Conduction to ventricles not relevant to circuit. AV = atrioventricular; CS = coronary sinus; SA = sinoatrial. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 6. Describe how ablation lesions terminate atrioventricular nodal re-entry tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

**Historical approach:** Purposeful AV nodal ablation → heart block → pacemaker

**Modern approach (slow pathway modification):**
- Cryoablation near AV node can interrupt circuit while preserving AV conduction
- Anatomical approach: lesions applied inferior and anterior to coronary sinus os, with slow pullback from tricuspid annulus toward IVC

**Transcatheter techniques (current standard):**
- Linear lesion from posteroinferior rim of CS os to IVC
- If right-sided AV valve present: linear lesion from valve to posterior CS os
- PR interval prolongation **not necessary**

**Surgical cryoablation:**
- Requires <3 minutes to perform
- Limited to **Mustard or Senning reoperations** (avoids retrograde catheter approach to pulmonary venous atrium)

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Focal Atrial Tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 7. What is the mechanism of action that causes focal atrial tachycardia?</summary><div class="details-content">

- Originates in **rapidly firing discrete area** of atrium → conducts centrifugally throughout atria
- Electrophysiological mechanism: automatic, micro-re-entrant, or triggered
- Tachycardia occurs **independently of normal sinus function** (which is inhibited) (Figure 5)
- More common in **right atrium** — often localised to **crista terminalis** or **atrial appendages**

![](_page_7_Picture_7.jpeg)

**FIGURE 5.** Right focal atrial tachycardia. AV = atrioventricular; CS = coronary sinus; SA = sinoatrial. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 8. Describe how ablation lesions terminate atrial tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Cryoablation + limited atrial resection** → obliterate or isolate "hot spot"
- Wider areas → **atrial wall isolation**
- Multiple ectopic foci → more extensive techniques: pulmonary vein isolation, left atrial isolation, right atrial isolation, His bundle cryoablation + pacemaker
- Automatic atrial foci often localised to **right atrial appendage** → direct resection
- Refractory cases **rare** → individualised therapeutic plan for accurate diagnosis and localised ablation

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Atrial Fibrillation (AF)</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 9. What is the mechanism of action that causes atrial fibrillation?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Definition:** Uncoordinated atrial activation with worsening of atrial mechanical function

**ECG:** Normal P waves replaced by rapid oscillations/fibrillatory waves (variable amplitude, shape, timing) → irregular, frequently rapid ventricular response

**Proposed mechanisms:**
- Multiple independent re-entrant wavelets
- Rapidly firing foci
- Rotors with spiral re-entrant wave circuits

**Trigger:** Single rapid focus may originate in **orifice of pulmonary veins**
**Perpetuation:** Atrial fibrosis (haemodynamic changes) contributes to multiple re-entrant wavelets

**Clinical conditions:** Markedly dilated left atria (mitral regurgitation, valve abnormalities, ventricular dysfunction)
**Contributing factors:** Inflammation, genetics; multiple mechanisms may coexist

**Common in CHD:**
- ASD (repaired or unrepaired)
- Single-ventricle physiology
- Atrial baffle repair of TGA
- Ebstein's anomaly

**Even with ASD closure, atrial fibrillation will recur in a high percentage of patients following surgery if direct arrhythmia intervention is not performed**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 10. Describe how ablation lesions terminate atrial fibrillation</summary><div class="details-content">

**Cox maze III/IV procedure:**
- Designed to eliminate AF while preserving intact AV nodal conduction and atrial contractility
- Creates **transmural lesions** across atrial wall → full-thickness scars
- Scar tissue non-conducting → creates "corridors" guiding coordinated electrical activity from SA node to AV node

**Success:**
- >90% in adults with concomitant MV repair, ASD closure, or CABG
- Successful in complex CHD repairs: Fontan conversion, ASD closure, MV repair (no AF recurrence, no antiarrhythmic medications)

**Advantages of intra-operative linear cryoablation/radiofrequency:**
- Substantially shortens surgical time vs extensive atrial incisions

**Right atrial only arrhythmia surgery is ineffective in averting recurrence of atrial fibrillation**

**Transvenous catheter approach:** Subject of intense effort; pulmonary vein stenosis is uncommon but serious complication

</div></details>

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 11. What is the mechanism of action that causes ventricular tachycardia?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Definition:** Abnormal ventricular rhythm with increased heart rate:
- >120 bpm (adolescents/adults)
- >150 bpm (children)

**Characteristics:**
- Usually **re-entrant** in nature
- Can originate from various sites in left and/or right ventricles
- **Sustained** (>30 seconds) vs **non-sustained**

**In CHD:**
- Macro-re-entrant circuit may involve **scar of ventriculotomy** or **VSD patch** (TOF, DORV)
- Also in: univentricular physiology, Ebstein's anomaly, prior Sano procedures (progressive myocardial fibrosis/dysfunction)

**Non-sustained VT:** May reflect progression of haemodynamic sequelae of prior repairs or ventricular dysfunction
- **Haemodynamic improvement may decrease non-sustained VT**

**Sustained VT:** Requires additional direct therapy and/or **implanted defibrillator (ICD)**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 12. Describe how ablation lesions terminate ventricular tachycardia</summary><div class="details-content">

**No standard lesion set** — therapy must be individualised to underlying aetiology (ischaemic, fibrotic, oncogenic)

**Current approach:** Transcatheter techniques (if amenable)

**Intra-operative cryoablation:** Reserved for patients undergoing concomitant surgical therapy

**TOF with pulmonary insufficiency and VT:**
- Pulmonary valve insertion + cryoablation lesions:
  - From superior portion of VSD patch to superior extension of right ventriculotomy
  - From right medial portion of VSD patch to right portion of ventriculotomy near AV groove
- Endocardial resection of previous ventriculotomy area as adjunct

**VSD with VT (rare):**
- Jet lesion opposite VSD site in RV inlet (whitish, fibrous plaque)
- Local resection + cryoablation → may cure arrhythmia
- Close VSD after ablation

**Oncogenic site (epicardial or intramyocardial):**
- Combination of resection, cryoablation, or electrical isolation if transcatheter unsuccessful

</div></details>

</div></details>

## Patient Selection and Therapeutic Cryoablation</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 13. Which patients with atrial re-entry tachycardia and two ventricles are candidates for intra-operative cryoablation?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Candidates:**
- Refractory atrial re-entry tachycardia (failed transcatheter ablation)
- Atrial re-entry tachycardia requiring repair of associated intracardiac defects

**Success rate (modified right atrial maze):** >95%

**Special cases:**
- Neonate with incessant tachycardia + structural heart disease (transcatheter high risk) → surgical ablation (excellent results, but requires operation)
- Structurally normal heart → uncommon; usually treatable with radiofrequency catheter ablation

**Atrial tachycardia incidence post-repair:**
- Mustard or Senning procedure: **20–50%**
- Fontan procedure: **40–50%**
- Tetralogy of Fallot repair: **34%**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 14. Describe the standard therapeutic cryoablation lesions used to treat atrial re-entry tachycardia in patients with two ventricles (Figure 6)</summary><div class="details-content">

**Four highly effective techniques:**
1. **Cavo-tricuspid atrial ablation** for typical atrial flutter (isthmus)
2. **Modified right atrial maze procedure** for multiple atrial re-entrant circuits
3. **Cox maze IV procedure** (± modifications) for atrial fibrillation
4. **Atrial pacemaker implantation** (bradycardia prevention or anti-tachycardia device)

**Mustard/Senning:**
- Catheter ablation possible but requires **retrograde aortic approach** (risk of aortic insufficiency)
- If reoperation needed for haemodynamic problems (e.g., baffle leak repair) → direct surgical cryoablation between anatomical barriers (minimal prolongation)

**Right atrial maze procedure (Figure 6):**
- Cryoablation lesions of standard Cox maze IV in right atrium
- Some surgeons elect to perform only isthmus lesions to prevent the possibility of sinus node dysfunction (especially relevant in infants/young patients — cryoablation lesion width large for anatomy, may extend to neighbouring structures, harming normal conduction system)

![](_page_13_Picture_2.jpeg)

**FIGURE 6.** Ablative therapeutic lesions for normal hearts. *Reproduced with permission from Elsevier.*

**Modified right atrial maze + modified Cox maze III** for multiple circuits in variable anatomy, complex CHD, rare atrial anomalies (Figure 7):
- Juxtaposition of atrial appendages
- Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC)
- Separate atrial entry of hepatic veins
- Persistent left superior vena cava (LSVC)
- Absence of coronary sinus

**The surgical team should be aware of the potential areas of slow conduction** (e.g., between IVC and separate hepatic vein entry) to plan comprehensive cryoablation without injuring normal AV conduction mechanism.

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 15. What are the standard cryoablation lesions used to treat atrial re-entry tachycardia in patients undergoing a Fontan conversion?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Fontan patient characteristics:** Hypertrophied, scarred, enlarged right atria with **multiple** atrial macro-re-entrant circuits

**Transcatheter ablation:** High rate of early recurrence; does not address coexisting haemodynamic abnormalities

**Combined approach (favourable results):** Repair associated lesions + Fontan conversion + arrhythmia surgery

**Anatomical variances requiring alteration of right-sided maze (Figure 8):**
- Tricuspid atresia
- Mitral atresia
- Common AV valve (AVSD)

**Example (tricuspid atresia):** No lesion can be placed from tricuspid annulus to IVC (obvious reasons) (Figure 8A)

**Pre-existing atriopulmonary Fontan with patch covering TV and CS:**
- Patch camouflages coronary sinus (important landmark for cryoablation)
- **Remove patch** → identify anatomy → place cryoablation lesions → re-cover TV with new patch (Figure 9)

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 16. What are the standard cryoablation lesions used to treat atrial fibrillation in patients undergoing Fontan conversion?</summary><div class="details-content">

- Fontan patients with AF often have antecedent history of atrial re-entry tachycardia
- Right atrial re-entry may **degenerate into AF** or coexist with AF (due to right atrial anatomical abnormalities)

**Treatment:** Based on arrhythmia progression → requires **biatrial maze**
- All right-sided cryoablation lesions
- Left-sided lesions modified for single ventricle (Figures 7–8)

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 17. Describe the standard therapeutic cryoablation lesions used to treat atrial fibrillation in patients with two ventricles</summary><div class="details-content">

- **Combined right + left atrial maze** performed together
- Left-sided maze lesions debated (e.g., lesion from PV confluence to base of LAA) — prospective studies lacking

**Key lesions:**
- PV confluence isolation
- **Lesion from PV confluence to P3 scallop of posterior mitral annulus** (very important to prevent re-entrant circuit causing AF recurrence)
- **Epicardial coronary sinus lesion** (complements mitral annulus lesion) → adds to success rate

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
## Technical Considerations and Prophylactic Arrhythmia Surgery</summary><div class="details-content">

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 18. Can right-sided lesions be performed on a beating heart without cardioplegic arrest?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Yes, if no atrial or ventricular communications exist**

**If not detected or ignored:** Shunting of air to left side → air embolism → stroke, organ dysfunction

**Approaches:**
- Careful pre-operative + intra-operative echocardiography to detect shunts → if detected, use cardioplegic arrest
- Others do not rely on any of the diagnostic tools and use cardioplegic arrest under all circumstances (avoid air embolism, apply lesions in motionless heart)

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 19. Is a biatrial maze always required for atrial fibrillation?</summary><div class="details-content">

**Adult acquired AF:** Often attributed to re-entrant circuit originating in pulmonary vein confluence → some advocate PV isolation alone (not universally accepted; Cox maze III/IV gives better results)

**Adult CHD with AF:** AF occurs most often with:
- Right atrial dilatation
- Right atrial fibrosis
- Pre-existing atrial tachycardia before AF onset

**Anatomical substrates:**
- Old atriopulmonary Fontan connections
- Untreated ASD (± partial anomalous pulmonary venous connections)
- Longstanding, untreated Ebstein's anomaly

**While there are no prospective studies, it makes sense to perform a biatrial maze in adult congenital heart disease patients with significant atrial arrhythmias for a more comprehensive chance of arrhythmia ablation in the long term**

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>
  
#### 20. Describe the principles of prophylactic arrhythmia surgery</summary><div class="details-content">

**Definition:** Arrhythmia surgery performed concomitantly with congenital heart repairs **even when clinical arrhythmias not present**

**Rationale:** High incidence of subsequent arrhythmia in selected subsets:
- Ebstein's anomaly of tricuspid valve
- Fontan operation
- Reoperation for tetralogy of Fallot
- Atrial septal defect closure over 40 years of age

**Protocols (no universal agreement):**
- Limited to **highest-risk patients**
- **Tailored to anatomical variants** with specific lesions
- Associated with **very low risk of procedure-induced arrhythmias**
- Associated with **low incidence of harm to conducting tissue**

#### 21. Describe the lesion sets used for prophylactic arrhythmia surgery (Figures 10–14)</summary><div class="details-content">

**Challenge:** CHD patients have multiple anatomical anomalies that may confound lesion sets (e.g., conduction system not in expected location → unwanted injury)

**Standard lesion set (safe from AV node injury) — consensus group:**
- Safe for: secundum ASD, Ebstein's anomaly, single-ventricle physiology, post-TOF
- **Not safe** for partial AVSD or anomalies with displaced AV nodes

**Cavo-tricuspid isthmus ablation (Figure 10):**
- One continuous cryoablation lesion: TV at commissure of septal/posterior leaflets → inferior CS os → posterior IVC os
- This specific lesion can be omitted if there is any question that the conduction system may extend to the tricuspid annulus at the commissure of the septal and posterior leaflets
- Additional lesion: extend medial atriotomy to anterior IVC os

**These lesion sets have been proposed but have not been universally accepted at the present time. It has been proposed that these lesion sets should be standardised for therapeutic measures, as well as for prophylactic applications for patients with congenital heart disease undergoing repair or re-repair and should be tested in a prospective multi-institutional study.**

**Principles to preserve:**
- Simple to perform
- Minimal complications
- Intended to solve potential long-term haemodynamic aberrations
