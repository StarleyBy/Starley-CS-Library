# Mitral Valve Replacement

##### The mitral valve is a complex structure comprised of an anterior and posterior leaflet that is connected to the left ventricle via attachments to papillary muscles through the chordae tendineae. It is anchored to the mitral annulus, which is in close relation to the circumflex coronary artery laterally, coronary sinus medially, and aortic valve anteriorly. Shown in Fig. 19.1 is the mitral annulus in a lateral view, along with view of the mitral valve from the top of the heart showing its proximity to the aortic and tricuspid valves.

## Executive Summary</summary><div class="details-content">

### Surgical Anatomy
* **Mitral valve components:** Anterior and posterior leaflets, chordae tendineae, papillary muscles, mitral annulus.
* **Annular relations:** Circumflex coronary artery (laterally), coronary sinus (medially), aortic valve (anteriorly).

### Access to the Mitral Valve
* **Left atriotomy via Sondergaard's groove:** Dissect interatrial plane to separate right atrium overhanging left atrium toward septum. Extend superiorly toward left atrial roof, inferiorly anterior to inferior pulmonary veins (posterior to IVC).
* **Extended vertical transseptal biatriotomy:** Open right atrium from RAA toward IVC. Incise interatrial septum to fossa ovalis, extend cephalad onto dome of left atrium. Particularly useful for reoperative valve surgery with aortic prosthesis in place.
* **Khonsari biatriotomy:** Extend from RAA toward right superior pulmonary vein to expose interatrial septum, then incise transversely through fossa ovalis.

### Suture Placement Techniques
* **Continuous suture (3-0 Prolene or Gore-Tex):** For tough, fibrous annulus without significant calcification. Advantage: surgical speed (useful in robotic/minimally invasive surgery).
* **Interrupted sutures without pledgets (3-0 Ethibond):** For mitral annular calcification or reoperative replacement after failed prosthesis removal. Advantage: sewing cuff seats precisely within annular plane without distortion.
* **Interrupted sutures with pledgets:** Can be placed atrium-to-ventricle (pledgets on atrial surface) or ventricle-to-atrium (pledgets on ventricular surface). For mechanical valves, place pledgets on atrial surface (minimizes risk of pledget embolization into LV if suture tears).

### Prosthesis Orientation
* **Bioprosthesis:** Largest leaflet cusp should face left ventricular outflow tract (prevents outflow obstruction).
* **Mechanical prosthesis (bileaflet):** Position in antianatomic orientation with pivot guards oriented in an anterior-posterior direction.

### Chordal Preservation
* **Rationale:** Maintenance of ventricular-annular continuity associated with more favorable ventricular remodeling and better survival vs. non-chordal-sparing replacement.

* **Posterior leaflet preservation (most common):** Complete resection of anterior leaflet, retain posterior leaflet. Replacement sutures through annulus and through portion of posterior leaflet. Alternative: resect central portion of posterior leaflet, reattach free edge with valve replacement sutures.

* **Anterior leaflet preservation:**
  * Excise central trapezoidal segment of anterior leaflet; remaining tissue taken by valve suture.
  * Alternative: reapproximate remaining leaflet tissue to annulus with Prolene sutures before valve suture placement.
  * Complete detachment of anterior leaflet with resection of middle portion; reapproximate remaining tissue to anterolateral and posteromedial commissures.
  * Reattachment of detached anterior leaflet to posterior annulus (initially described for tilting disk mechanical prosthesis).

* **Khonsari I technique (for rheumatic valves with destroyed primary chordae and thickened subvalvular apparatus):** Preserve second-order chords in bundles, reattach radially to annulus in anatomic position using pledgeted valve sutures.

* **Neochordae placement:** Polytetrafluoroethylene sutures placed on papillary muscles and attached to mitral annulus at 2, 5, 7, and 10 o'clock positions.

### Annular Reconstruction
* **Indications:** Destruction of posterior annulus, secondary endocarditis, or after radical débridement for severe annular calcification.
* **Technique:** Sew pericardial patch into LV cavity with Prolene sutures. Secure valve sutures to patch, then reapproximate atrial surface.

### Mitral Annular Calcification (MAC)
* **Challenge:** Common and difficult cardiac lesion.
* **Younger patients (degenerative disease):** Calcification tends to involve posterior annulus; dense calcification can be removed en bloc with subsequent annular reconstruction.
* **Patients with renal dysfunction (dialysis), mediastinal irradiation, or advanced age:** Calcification may be friable with extension into ventricular cavity.
* **Explant strategy:** Begin with anterior leaflet resection (facilitates visualization of subvalvular structures and identification of annulus).
* **Débridement:** Sharp dissection with no. 11 blade + blunt with rongeur.
* **Suture strategy:** Interrupted suture placement allows prosthesis to seat within annulus and mitigates tension (prevents calcium fracturing during tying).
* **Impenetrable calcium (12 to 3 o'clock positions):** Place sutures across interatrial septum via right atrium.
* **Impenetrable calcium (9 to 12 o'clock positions):** Place sutures externally on left atrial roof onto mitral annulus.
* **Gandjbakhch technique (intraatrial insertion):** Sew Dacron collar onto valve prosthesis. Place inner row of interrupted pledgeted valve sutures (pledgets on atrial surface). Sew free end of collar to left atrium with running Prolene sutures.

## Step 2. Access to the Mitral Valve</summary><div class="details-content">

Exposure of the mitral valve must be optimized to facilitate efficient and effective surgery. Although not described in detail, complete drainage of the right atrium must be achieved prior to arresting the heart and opening the left atrium.

**Left atriotomy via Sondergaard's groove:** The interatrial plane is dissected to separate a portion of the right atrium that overhangs the left atrium toward the septum. This incision is extended superiorly toward the left atrial roof. It is extended inferiorly anterior to the inferior pulmonary veins, but posterior to the inferior vena cava ([Fig. 19.2](#fig-19-2)).

## Step 3. Suture Placement for Mitral Valve Replacement</summary><div class="details-content">

**Continuous suture:** This approach to anchoring a prosthesis is typically performed when the mitral annulus is tough and fibrous without much annular calcification. The major advantage of this technique relates to surgical speed, which may be advantageous in robotic or minimally invasive mitral surgery. This is performed with a 3-0 Prolene or Gore-Tex sutures ([Fig. 19.5](#fig-19-5)).

![](_page_11_Picture_2.jpeg)

<span id="fig-19-5"></span>**Figure 19.5** Continuous suture technique for mitral valve replacement.

**Interrupted sutures without pledgets:** This technique is performed in the setting of mitral annular calcification or following failed prosthesis removal at the time of reoperative mitral replacement. The major advantage of this technique is that the sewing cuff of the mitral prosthesis will be seated precisely within the plane of the mitral annulus, without any distortion. This is performed with 3-0 Ethibond sutures ([Fig. 19.6](#fig-19-6)).

![](_page_13_Picture_2.jpeg)

<span id="fig-19-6"></span>**Figure 19.6** Interrupted sutures without pledgets.

**Interrupted sutures with pledgets:** These sutures can be placed from the atrium toward the ventricle (pledgets sitting on the atrial surface of the mitral valve) or vice versa (pledgets sitting on the ventricular surface of the mitral valve; [Fig. 19.7](#fig-19-7)).

Although both approaches can be applied for bioprosthetic or mechanical valve replacement, it is advantageous to place pledgets on the atrial surface when using a mechanical valve. This minimizes the risk of pledget embolization into the left ventricle if a suture tears during tying.

## Step 5. Chordal Preservation</summary><div class="details-content">

Maintenance of the ventricular-annular continuity at the time of mitral valve replacement has been associated with more favorable ventricular remodeling and better survival compared to non-chordal-sparing valve replacement.

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 1. Preservation of the Posterior Leaflet</summary><div class="details-content">

The most common approach to chordal preservation involves complete resection of the anterior leaflet in which the posterior leaflet is retained. Replacement sutures are placed through the annulus and through a portion of the posterior leaflet ([Fig. 19.10](#fig-19-10)).

Some have also described resection of the central portion of the posterior leaflet, with reattachment of the posterior leaflet free edge with the valve replacement sutures.

![](_page_19_Picture_2.jpeg)

<span id="fig-19-10"></span>**Figure 19.10** Posterior leaflet preservation technique. Replacement sutures through annulus and through portion of posterior leaflet.

</div></details>

<details class="med-details"><summary>

### 2. Anterior Leaflet Preservation</summary><div class="details-content">

This has been described with excision of a central trapezoidal segment of the anterior leaflet. The remaining tissue is taken by the valve suture. Others have described using Prolene sutures to reapproximate the remaining leaflet tissue to the annulus before valve suture placement ([Fig. 19.11A](#fig-19-11)).

Others have also described complete detachment of the anterior leaflet with resection of the middle portion of the leaflet. The remaining leaflet tissue is then reapproximated to the anterolateral and posteromedial commissures, respectively.

Initially described for implantation of a tilting disk mechanical prosthesis, reattachment of the detached anterior leaflet to the posterior annulus has been used by some.

The Khonsari I technique is applied for rheumatic valves in which the primary chordae are destroyed and subvalvular apparatus are thickened. In this technique, second-order chords are preserved in bundles and then reattached radially to the annulus in their anatomic position using pledgeted valve sutures.

## Step 7. Mitral Annular Calcification</summary><div class="details-content">

Mitral annular calcification is common and represents a challenging cardiac lesion.

Mitral annular calcification in younger patients with degenerative disease tends to involve the posterior annulus ([Fig. 19.14](#fig-19-14)). In these patients, the calcification is dense and can be removed en bloc, with subsequent reconstruction of the annulus, described previously.

![](_page_23_Picture_4.jpeg)

<span id="fig-19-14"></span>**Figure 19.14** Mitral annular calcification involving posterior annulus.

In patients with a history of renal dysfunction requiring renal replacement therapy, or mediastinal irradiation, or those of advanced age, the calcification of the mitral annulus may be friable, with extension into the ventricle cavity.

In these patients, valve explant may be challenging. I recommend that resection begin with the anterior leaflet, thereby facilitating visualization of the subvalvular structures. Importantly, this aids in the identification of the mitral annulus, which may not be obvious. Débridement of the annulus is performed with sharp dissection using a no. 11 blade and bluntly with a rongeur. Mitral replacement with interrupted suture placement is useful because it allows the mitral prosthesis to be seated within the mitral annulus. Also, interrupted suture placement may mitigate tension on the annulus, which can result in calcium fracturing when tying.

For impenetrable calcium involving the portion of the mitral annulus from the 12 to 3 o'clock positions, sutures may be placed across the interatrial septum via the right atrium. Impenetrable calcium involving the mitral annulus from the 9 to 12 o'clock positions may require suture placement externally on the left atrial roof onto the mitral annulus ([Fig. 19.15](#fig-19-15)).

Another strategy for mitral prosthesis implantation involves the intraatrial insertion of the prosthesis, as described by Gandjbakhch. In this technique, a Dacron collar is sewn onto a valve prosthesis. An inner row of interrupted pledgeted valve sutures (pledgets on the atrial surface) are placed before the free end of the collar is sewn to the left atrium with running Prolene sutures.
